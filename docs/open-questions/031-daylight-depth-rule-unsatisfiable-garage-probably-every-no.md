# OQ 31 — the daylight-depth rule is unsatisfiable for a garage, and probably for every non-habitable room

*Status: RULED 24 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the garage (WP-4.3, 24 Aug 2026)*

**RULED 24 Aug 2026 — a room-level opt-out.** Option (b): a `daylight.depth_governs: false` flag on the room record. It keeps the decision in the data where this corpus keeps such things, is explicit per room rather than sweeping a whole function_class, and gives the corpus its first vocabulary for *judged, where the judgment does not apply* — which is what this question turned out to be about. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — the daylight-depth rule is unsatisfiable for a garage, and probably for every non-habitable room.** The validator checks a room's depth against the reach of its own window head (`depth_multiplier` from the room record, relaxed by half when cross-lit), which is exactly right for a room people sit in and wrong for one cars sit in. A two-car garage is 20 ft × 22 ft at the *shallow* end of `rooms/garage.json`'s own dimension band — a 16 ft car plus clearance — and no arrangement of windows in a 0-10% glazing band reaches its back wall. So every correctly-sized, correctly-glazed garage the composer produces reports one `serious` daylight finding that cannot be designed away, only ignored. The composer states this in its decision log rather than suppressing it, and the garage is deliberately *not* distorted (made shallower than a car, or over-glazed past the room's own band) to silence it. The question is where the fix belongs, and it is a judgment rather than an engineering call: (a) exempt `function_class: "work"` and `"service"` rooms from the depth rule, which is simple but would also stop flagging a genuinely unlit workshop or laundry, both of which people do occupy; (b) let a room record opt out with an explicit `daylight.depth_governs: false`, which keeps the decision in the data where the rest of this corpus keeps such things, at the cost of a schema field; or (c) leave it, on the argument that a finding a human dismisses in one second is cheaper than a rule that quietly stops looking. Related but distinct from the "unjudged is not passed" principle — this is *judged, and the judgment does not apply*, which the corpus has no vocabulary for yet. Surfaced by WP-4.3; nothing was changed on the strength of it.
