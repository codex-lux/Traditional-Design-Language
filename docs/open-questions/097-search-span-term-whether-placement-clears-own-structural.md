A defect reported SMALLER than it is, which is the OQ 52 family wearing the safe-looking sign. Fixed first, because a charge on a wrong measurement would have taught the search to aim at partitions.
**BUILT.** `geometry.SPAN_W = 20.0`, charged in proportion to how far over capacity the span is (`SPAN_W * span/capacity`, the ratio rather than the absolute so a hand-framed 20 ft bay and a light-frame joist table compare on one scale; only over-capacity spans are charged, so the ratio exceeds 1 by construction), and a half-reified penalty in `geometry_cp.py` -- affordable there because on the model's 1-ft integer grid the only positions within `bearing_lines`' 0.75 ft of a bay multiple ARE the multiples, so the candidate bearing set is seven lines rather than a continuum. A sweep that stopped at "small weights are safe" would have shipped the worst setting. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — the search has no span term, so whether a placement clears its own structural capacity is luck.** Found by WP-7.1 breaking a test that turned out to be pinning that luck. `build/structure.py` computes every clear span and checks it against the style's framing capacity — 20 ft for a hand-framed timber-bay house — and `spans_exceeding_capacity` reports what fails. **Nothing upstream of that check knows spans exist.** `level_score`, `exterior_score`, `adjacency_score`, `vertical_score` and the rest never look at clear span, so the search cannot prefer a placement that frames and cannot avoid one that does not; it clears the capacity or fails it by accident, and any change to the search reshuffles the accident. **The question is whether span capacity should become a search term at all, and at what force.** It is a genuine structural fact and the corpus already computes it, which argues for a charge; but `build_section` is a whole derivation per candidate and putting it inside a 250-candidate loop is the 25 s × N cost that `build_section`'s own docstring exists to avoid. A cheap proxy — the widest unbroken run in each candidate, which the slicer knows for free — may be enough, and measuring whether it moves anything before building it is the WP-6.3 discipline.
