# OQ 92 — furniture is placed for wet rooms and the kitchen, and for nothing else

*Status: HALF CLOSED 27 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the plan-semantics program (WP-6.1 / WP-6.2, 26 August 2026)*

**HALF CLOSED 27 Aug 2026 (WP-7.2) — the SIZING half is REFUSED and the ARRANGEMENT half is built to exactly what the corpus states.** Lucas's ruling, verbatim: *"room sizing should not be furniture driven - that's the tail wagging the dog - however furniture arrangements that suit the program/room name and room size should be placed accordingly."* Written into `docs/model.md`. A room's size comes from its programme and its catalogue band; the furniture is arranged into the room as given. The `critical_dimension` prose stays prose — it is a designer's argument for where a band's floor sits, addressed to a person, and making it a constraint would let a derived number overrule an authored one (the OQ 52 class).
**What the measurement found before anything was built.** The dataset splits in two and the register overstated one half: **all 278 furniture items carry a real `footprint_in` and `clearance_in` across 59 of 60 rooms**, so ARRANGEMENT has complete data — but **only 4 items in 3 rooms are marked `essential`**, so anything keyed on that field would have been inert for 57 of 60 rooms. And `schema/room.schema.json` had declared a `placement` field (`freestanding|against-wall|corner|built-in`) that **0 of 278 items carried**, so `plan_check` inferred it from the item's NAME with a regex — calling 84 items against-wall where the authored data calls 159. A guess in code where the schema has a field.
**BUILT.** (1) The door placer and the fixture placer can see each other. `openings.place`'s docstring said fixtures ran first *"because a door has to know what is against the wall it would swing into"* and `occupied` never held a fixture — so the code did not do the thing its own comment gave as the reason, and drew a door through the principal bath's double vanity (2.70 ft) and the powder room's basin (1.92 ft) on `spec-builder-colonial`. The ORDER was inverted rather than the map patched, because the precedence is what matters: a door is AUTHORED and a fixture layout is DERIVED, so the openings take their runs first and a fixture that cannot clear them is reported `unplaced` with a reason. (2) A fixture is tried on every wall, not only the longest — the powder room's WC and basin were refused on an 11 ft wall 9 ft spoken for while its east wall stood empty, which is a false "cannot fit". Both shipped plans now place every fixture with zero collisions. (3) All 278 `placement` values authored, the regex deleted, and `check_rooms.py` now requires the field: furniture findings on the two shipped plans 44 → 34, ten false accusations removed. (4) `needs_uninterrupted_wall_ft`, authored where a furniture item's own note states a run in words — **"exactly one does" WAS WRONG AND WP-7.4 CORRECTED IT TO FIVE** (`kitchen`'s range, *"a range needs 60 in of wall minimum and wants 72"*; `living-room`'s sofa, *"needs 132 in of wall with the end tables"*; `bedroom`'s desk, *"It needs 6 ft of wall"*; `study`'s camera wall, *"5 ft of wall BEHIND the sitter that is not a window"*; and the sideboard below). These are READINGS -- the number is in the sentence. **A sixth was a judgment and the new basis test caught it**: `keeping-room`'s hearth was authored at 9.0 ft from *"its chimney breast is 9 to 10 ft; on a 14 ft wall that leaves almost nothing either side"*, which is a band explaining why first-period halls are wide rather than a run the hearth requires, and the figure was withdrawn rather than the test loosened. The check went from 13 placed rooms of one type to **61 across five**, still 0 findings on the 16 plans, and the population is now asserted before the clean run is believed. Nothing in the repo had referenced the field at all. The original text follows: `dining-room`'s sideboard (*"Needs an uninterrupted wall of at least 6 ft. This is what the second window usually kills."*) — measured by the drawn layer against the openings this programme placed. The number sits beside the sentence in the same record so the two cannot drift.
**WHAT REMAINS OPEN.** Arrangement beyond a stated wall run: seating that makes a group rather than a line, a bed away from the door swing, a table centred under the ceiling fixture. The corpus states 8 such rules in prose and none of them with a figure, so each would be an editorial call rather than a reading. Also unaddressed: **some `furniture` entries are not objects at all** — "door swing", "towel positions", "clear route from the street", "a standing person" — they are clearance reservations wearing an item's shape, and they now carry a `placement` that means nothing for them. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — furniture is placed for wet rooms and the kitchen, and for nothing else.** WP-6.2 joined the two vocabularies that had never met: `room.fixtures` was a list of strings and the room catalogue carried each item's `footprint_in` and `clearance_in`, so a bathroom could be checked for whether it COULD hold a tub and never for whether the tub, the WC, the lavatory and the door swing fit together. `build/openings.py::fixture_pass` now packs them along a wall and reports what will not fit. **It stops at `sanitary` and `service` by ruling, and the reported symptom was wider than that** — *"There are no furniture layouts taken into consideration."* All 60 room records carry furniture with real footprints, including the ones that decide a room's size: `dining-room`'s table for eight with 36 in of chair pull and 18 in of passage, `drawing-room`'s twenty-four linear feet of seating, `bedroom`'s bed clearances. `plan_check` already checks whether each fits the room's short dimension (`plan_check.py:463`), which is a fit test and not an arrangement. What is unbuilt is arrangement proper: a table centred under the ceiling fixture, a sideboard on an uninterrupted 6 ft of wall (`dining-room`'s own note: *"This is what the second window usually kills"*), a bed away from the door swing, seating that makes a group rather than a line. That is a real design problem rather than a packing one, it interacts with the window and door positions this package just created, and doing it badly would produce confident nonsense of exactly the kind this program exists to remove. Wants a ruling on how far the corpus should go before it hands the room to the architect.
