# OQ 65 — `projection_parts` means two different things in this corpus, split by order, and nothing in a pack says which

*Status: CLOSED 26 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the adversarial audit of the legibility pass (26 August 2026)*

**CLOSED 26 Aug 2026 — the pack declares its datum, and the declaration is verified against the pack's own geometry rather than trusted.** Ruled as proposed: `projection_datum: "naked" | "axis"` joins the pack at `schema/proportion-pack.schema.json`, and every authority's published figures stay verbatim — a Gibbs number still checks against Gibbs's own plate, which is the whole reason normalising the twelve was refused. **Seven declarations cover all twenty-six**: the five Vignola bases (Doric and Tuscan `naked`, Ionic, Corinthian and Composite `axis`) plus the two standalone packs, `greek-doric` and `benjamin-doric`, both `naked`; the other nineteen are overlays and inherit through `resolve()`, which now carries the field and lets an overlay state its own where it genuinely differs. None does. `moorish-arch` declares nothing because it publishes no projections at all. Neither of the two standalone packs publishes a shaft body, so their reading was not taken from the Doric pattern — it was taken from the capital, whose widest member is under half the radius in both, and an abacus cannot stand INSIDE the shaft. **The declaration is checked, not believed**: `build/check_orders.py::check_projection_datum` dimensions every resolved pack and compares the declared value against what the geometry reads (a shaft body at 0 or at the semidiameter; failing that, the capital). Declaring the wrong datum on one Vignola base errors on that pack **and on all four overlays that inherit it**, which is verified in `tests/test_drawn_labels.py`. **The consumer that was wrong is fixed**: `build/orders_template.html::buildGeometry` added the naked to both readings, so `dist/orders.html` drew Vignola's Ionic **2.25× too wide** and Gibbs's Corinthian 1.41×, with every Ionic, Corinthian and Composite shaft narrower than its own apophyge; measured before and after, the eleven `naked` packs are unchanged to the pixel, as they should be. **The zeros half is ruled too — unrecorded, and marked.** Eight assemblies in the `axis` packs record every member's projection as 0, and under that reading 0 cannot be a statement: a cornice at radius 0 is at the axis. They are treated as an ABSENT figure, drawn at their naked, and counted on the sheet — the workbench plate says how many of a pack's own edges it does not give, rather than drawing a cornice that recedes behind its own column. That is a reading of a silence, not a fact, and the packs' own sources would settle whether those figures were never transcribed or were meant as flush; that part stays with OQ 7–11 and OQ 18's source half, which are environment-blocked. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — `projection_parts` means two different things in this corpus, split by order, and nothing in a pack says which.** Found by measuring all 26 order packs at a 36-inch diameter after the workbench plate started drawing them as a half section: **thirteen record a member's projection as an offset FROM ITS OWN NAKED** (Gibbs's Doric shaft body: `projection_in` 0, its base plinth 6.0 against a semidiameter of 18) **and twelve record it as an absolute radius FROM THE AXIS** (Vignola's Ionic shaft body: exactly 18.0, its base plinth 25.0). There is no third case and no ambiguous one — the shaft body is either 0 or exactly the semidiameter in every pack that publishes one. The split runs by ORDER and not by authority: every Doric and every Tuscan is an offset, every Ionic, Corinthian and Composite is a radius, across all five authorities. That is the signature of two data-entry passes, not of an architectural distinction, and `check_orders.py` has nothing to say about it because both readings are internally consistent. **What it costs today:** a consumer that adds the naked to the figure draws the twelve at two radii — the shaft's own apophyge, astragal and fillet standing a whole semidiameter clear of the shaft they sit on, which is the fault the plate was fixed for and which `dist/orders.html` (`buildGeometry`, `datum(a.id, m.y0) + m.projIn`) **still commits**, since it is where the rule was copied from. The workbench plate now reads the convention off each pack's own shaft — whose outer face at its foot IS the column's radius, so whichever reading puts it at r0 is that pack's reading — and `tests/test_drawn_labels.py` fails if any pack ever lands between the two. That is a reader working around the data, not the data being right. **The choice for whoever rules.** (a) Normalise the corpus to one convention and migrate the twelve (or the thirteen), which is a 26-file data change plus a re-check of every consumer, and settles it permanently. (b) Add an explicit `projection_datum: "naked" | "axis"` to the pack, which costs a schema bump, keeps both sets of published figures verbatim as their authorities give them, and makes every consumer's reading a stated one rather than a derived one. (c) Leave it and let each consumer derive it, which is what happens now and is one careless consumer away from the same fault again. A further thing to settle under (a) or (b): **eight assemblies across the radius-convention packs record every member's projection as 0** — `palladio-corinthian`'s whole cornice, `palladio-ionic`'s base, architrave, frieze and cornice, several friezes and pedestals elsewhere. Under the offset reading 0 is a statement (flush with the naked); under the radius reading it cannot be one, and the plate treats it as an absent figure and says so on the sheet rather than drawing a cornice that recedes behind its own column. Whether those are genuinely unrecorded or are zeros meaning flush is not decidable from the data, and the packs' sources would settle it.
