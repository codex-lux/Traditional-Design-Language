# OQ 47 — the ontology has no slot for an exposed structural member on a wall face

*Status: CLOSED 25 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the real solver (WP-2.3, 25 Aug 2026)*

**CLOSED 25 Aug 2026 — `expressed_frame` added at ontology 0.7.0, with the field the slot was actually for.** Ruled after the evidence was corrected downward (see below). The slot holds `member_status`, `face_width`, `spacing`, `projection` from the cladding plane, and `brace_geometry` — and `member_status` is the reason it exists, taking `structural`, `structural-and-expressed`, `applied` or `none`.
**What the field buys.** Two records state it as a hard rule in nearly the same words: `arts-and-crafts-british` c01, "every visible material must be doing the job it appears to do. Applied half-timbering, veneer stone, false beams and imitation finishes are forbidden", and `arts-and-crafts-american` c01, "exposed rafter tails must correspond to actual rafters at 16 to 24 in on centre… decorative tails applied" are not permitted. Before this the corpus could not tell a frame from a picture of one, which is the single distinction `stick-style` and `tudor-revival` both turn on — and `applied` is now recordable as a legitimate position rather than an accusation, which is what those two styles need it to be.
**Bound on 14 kits, and the three-way split is the finding.** `structural` on `english-medieval-timber-frame`, `german-fachwerk` and `tudor`; `structural-and-expressed` on `arts-and-crafts-british`, `arts-and-crafts-american` and `mission-revival`; `applied` on `stick-style`, `tudor-revival` and `french-normandy-revival`; and **`forbidden`/`none` on five** — `jacobethan-revival` ("applied half-timbering is forbidden. The variant is defined by its absence"), `prairie-school`, `shingle-style`, `mediterranean-revival` and `spanish-colonial-revival`. That last group is the same argument that got `arch` built at 0.6.0: a rule *about* the member which states that there is none had nowhere to live. Three sibling revival nodes now take three different positions on one member, and the Spanish revival family is told apart at the eave by this field and little else.
**Migration: 7 rules in 3 packs** off `corner_board` — `timber-panel` (stud spacing, face width, projection, brace ratio), `jetty-overhang` (corner return, dragon beam), `facade-medieval-english` (buttress). The kits gained an EMPTY slot rather than a moved one, and that is worth stating: a keyword sweep proposed 102 kit bindings across 32 slots carrying such content, and reading a sample found essentially all of them correctly placed — `primary_cladding` describing the infill panel, `construction_type` naming the frame. **The dimensional content was not misfiled in the kits; it did not exist there**, because there was nowhere to put it. That is a stronger argument for the slot than a migration would have been.
*Original entry follows, with its own over-count corrected in place.*<br><br>**OPEN — the ontology has no slot for an exposed structural member on a wall face.** Found while authoring `timber-panel` for WP-4.6, and it is the same shape as OQ 46 one layer out. A half-timbered wall is made of nothing but exposed structural members: the stud, its face width, how far it stands proud of the infill, and the diagonal brace are the four things that distinguish an English close-studded wall from a German Fachwerk from an applied Normandy Revival elevation, and the corpus has nowhere to put any of them. The envelope group's nearest slot is `corner_board` — a board at a corner — so all four rules in that pack are routed through it and all four read slightly wrong. `construction_type` is a categorical and holds no dimension; `primary_cladding` is the panel BETWEEN the members, which the pack does use and which is the other half of the same wall.
**It is not only a half-timbering problem, which is what makes it worth a ruling rather than a workaround.** `styles/stick-style.json`'s applied stick bands, the Swiss chalet's exposed purlin ends and carved bargeboards, `opening-craftsman`'s exposed rafter tail — already noted in that pack as the member whose structure-or-trim status has to be decided on the drawings — and the Tudor Revival's applied timber are all the same class: a member that is (or represents) structure, shown on the outside of a wall, with a face width and a projection that are the whole of its effect. **CORRECTED 25 Aug 2026, and the correction is the point.** This entry originally said "four packs
in this work package have now routed such a member through a slot written for something else", and
`facade-medieval-english`'s notes later raised that to five. Both are wrong. Measured by READING the
rules rather than by counting keyword hits, the true set is **7 rules in 3 packs**: `timber-panel`
(stud spacing, stud face width, projection proud of the panel, brace ratio), `jetty-overhang` (the
corner return and the dragon beam) and `facade-medieval-english` (the buttress). `trim-sawn`'s
brackets sit on `modillion_dentil`, which is the RIGHT slot -- a modillion is a bracket -- and
`facade-portada`'s estipite is a genuine pilaster, so neither was ever a compromise.
That is the **sixth** time in this work package that a keyword measurement over-counted its own
work list, and it is the worst of the six, because it happened inside the sentence arguing FOR a
ruling. Inflating the evidence for a change one wants is a different and more dangerous error than
the other five, all of which merely made a job look bigger than it was. The ruling was taken on the
corrected figure.
The concrete proposal, so the next person does not re-derive it: add `expressed_frame` to the envelope group, holding face width, spacing, projection from the cladding plane, brace geometry, and — the field that would do the most work — whether the member is structural, structural-and-expressed, or applied. That last is the distinction `styles/stick-style.json` and `styles/tudor-revival.json` both turn on and that no slot currently records, so the corpus cannot presently tell a frame from a picture of one. Cost is an ontology bump and a migration of four packs' bindings; the alternative is that every future timber tradition on WP-4.1's list — the Alpine bracket work, the jetty module, the Fachwerk ornament — routes through `corner_board` too and the compromise becomes the convention.
