# OQ 52 — the elevation generator invents measurements it never took, and the fault corpus convicts houses on them. Raised 25 Aug 2026 by an adversarial audit of WP-4.6

*Status: CLOSED 26 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the real solver (WP-2.3, 25 Aug 2026)*

**CLOSED 26 Aug 2026 — the twelve invented measurements are gone, the generator now declares what it does not model, and the seven faults decided on them come back could-not-judge.** The fix took the shape this entry wrote out, and the numbers moved exactly as it predicted. `build/elevation.py` supplied twelve measurements as constants; all twelve are removed, and the names live on in a `NOT_MODELLED` dict at the head of the file which carries a stated reason apiece and is FILTERED at the point the measurements are returned — so reintroducing one by a careless `m.update()` cannot put it back in front of the critic, which makes the guarantee structural rather than remembered. To take a name off that list you must model the thing and delete the entry in the same commit; that is the only way out, and it is the point of the list.
**What moved, on both shipped reference plans identically.** Seven faults left the judged buckets for could-not-judge — `dormer-off-the-bay`, `dormer-wall`, `overscaled-dormer`, `vestigial-chimney-chase`, `capless-stack`, `raking-cornice-that-does-not-match`, `cornice-gutter-without-a-liner`. Five of the seven had been reported PRESENT and two CLEAR, which is this entry's "a fabricated pass sitting beside a fabricated failure" showing up in the arithmetic. Pinned counts: Tidewater serious 40 → 36, minor 62 → 61; spec Colonial serious 70 → 66, minor 59 → 58. **Those numbers going down is the fix working** — four fewer convictions on evidence that never existed, not four fewer defects in the house — and the re-pinning in `tests/test_plan_validator.py` says so in as many words, because a future reader finding a count that fell will otherwise assume a regression.
**The other three members are done too.** `mcp_server/core.py`'s `_eval_test` now reads a present-but-`null` value as MISSING rather than letting it reach `eval` and become a `status: error` — both are honest states but they say different things, and null is the natural JSON encoding of "I could not judge this", not of "the corpus asked something incoherent". `schema/room.schema.json` no longer permits a null `clearance_in`: the twenty records that carried one all genuinely meant zero and now say zero, so `build/plan_check.py` reads the value instead of coercing it with `or 0`, and an author who forgets one now fails `check_rooms.py` instead of being checked as though the item needed no gap. And `build/roof.py`'s four hardcoded band fallbacks each record themselves in `bands_read_from_fallback` on their own record — the fallbacks are kept, because a check that refuses to run is worse than one that says where it read from, but a run that used one now says so, and on both shipped plans that list is empty.

Raised 25 Aug 2026 by an adversarial audit of WP-4.6.** This is CLAUDE.md's standing "unjudged is not passed" trap, found in six more places, and two of them are firing on both shipped reference plans right now.
**What is invented.** `build/roof.py:449` explicitly REFUSES to judge dormers — *"No dormers declared on this plan (no schema field authors dormer position yet)"* — and `build/elevation.py:563` then overwrites that refusal with `dormer_count: 0`. The secondary test on `faults/dormer-off-the-bay.json` is `dormer_count % 2 == 1`, so **"Dormers Off the Rhythm" fires as PRESENT (serious) on a house with no dormers modelled**. `elevation.py:558-562` goes further and states five chimney dimensions as constants — `chimney_width_in: 36.0`, `chimney_depth_in: 20.0`, a cap projection, a shadow-line count — on a plan where the neighbouring `visible_chimney_count` is correctly ABSENT because roof.py could not judge it. `vestigial-chimney-chase` is then reported present on evidence `20/36 = 0.5556 against at-least 0.6`, **adjudicated entirely from two numbers nobody measured**, and its primary test PASSES on the same invented figures — a fabricated pass sitting beside a fabricated failure. `raking_cornice_member_count: 0` convicts every gable-roofed plan of a pediment fault; `gutter_outlets: 2, overflow_scuppers: 0` convicts both plans of a gutter fault, and nothing in the corpus models a gutter.
**And a third state disappears entirely.** `mcp_server/core.py:514` builds its buckets from `evaluated` and `need_measurements` only, so a test returning `{"status": "error"}` lands in NONE of `faults_present`, `faults_clear`, `could_not_judge` or `summary`. It is reachable today: `dormer-off-the-bay`'s primary test divides by `dormer_count`, which is the invented 0, so the primary vanishes without trace and the fault is reported present on its secondary alone. That is unjudged-as-ABSENT, which is worse than unjudged-as-passed because nothing in the output hints that anything was missing. Separately `core.py:440` crashes with a `TypeError` on a measurement supplied as `null` — the natural JSON encoding of "I could not judge this" — because `missing` is built by key presence and the comparison sits outside the try block.
**Why this is not fixed in the audit that found it.** Every one of these is PRE-EXISTING, none was introduced by WP-4.6, and the fix is not a patch: a measurement that must be ABSENT rather than zero changes what the fault corpus reports on every plan, which moves the reference-corpus expectations, `plan_check` counts and a large number of pinned tests. That is a work package with its own re-pinning, and doing it inside an audit would mean changing the critic's output while nobody was looking at whether the new output is right. It is recorded here in full, with file and line, rather than half-fixed.
**The shape of the fix, so the next person does not re-derive it.** (a) `elevation.py` must emit no key at all where the generator did not model the thing — the corpus already has the right pattern, since `visible_chimney_count` is correctly absent two lines away. (b) `core.py`'s `_eval_test` must treat a present-but-`null` value as missing, and `check_measurements` must give `status: "error"` a bucket of its own. (c) A test should assert, for every fault the corpus ships, that each measurement it names is either produced by a generator that claims to model it or reported as could-not-judge — the check that would have caught all six at once.
**Two smaller members of the same family, recorded so they are not lost.** `build/plan_check.py:426` reads `cl = it.get("clearance_in") or 0`, turning an unstated furniture clearance into a measured zero — 20 of 278 catalogue entries omit the key and 15 of those are actually checked; all 15 are pseudo-items where zero is right, so there is no live false pass, but nothing distinguishes "0 by intent" from "author forgot". And `build/roof.py:313, 419, 422, 443` fall back to hardcoded bands when a corpus lookup misses, then report `"computed": True` with an evidence string naming the corpus file — so a reworded rule would produce a confident answer from a stale copy of today's numbers. All four lookups match today; the fallbacks are byte-identical to the corpus values, which is exactly what makes the rot undetectable.
