# OQ 43 — The EQUIVALENT groups are asymmetric in practice and symmetric in the code

*Status: CLOSED 24 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the real solver (WP-2.3, 25 Aug 2026)*

**CLOSED 24 Aug 2026 — directional, and the 115 went to 0 by being worked through rather than by being suppressed.** `EQUIVALENT` is now `SUBSTITUTES`, a directed map, and the one function that served both questions is two: `satisfied_by(want)` for "the rule wants this; would anything the plan HAS do?" and `serves(have)` for "this room is next to me; which rules does it satisfy?". Before OQ 43 those were one function, which is precisely the bug.
**What the promotion actually found.** Of 542 findings where the plan modelled something the rule would take, 294 were the table running backwards and returned to honest absence; 214 became real adjacency findings at their own severity. The predicted 115 fatal across 67 styles appeared exactly as measured — and working through them, **none of the 115 was a defect in a diagram**:
· **90 were two gaps in the table I had just written.** `hall` — the Anglo-American vernacular hall, the undivided room the front door opens into, whose own record gives its `entered_from` as the exterior and whose aka list carries "living hall" — was missing, so every hall-and-parlor and living-hall diagram was told its parlor could not reach an entrance hall while opening off exactly the room that does the job. And `garret-chamber`, a bedroom inside the roof, so a Cape's closets failed `closet must_adjoin bedroom` while doored to the room they serve. **The third time in three packages that a measurement of mine over-counted its own work list**, and the reason to verify a sample before accepting a number.
· **Two were rules a correct plan structurally cannot satisfy**, the same shape OQ 37 found twice: an UPPER centre passage required to adjoin an entrance hall on its own storey (now `vertical_ok`, as `landing` already is), and a gallery required to share a door with the stair hall when its own record gives four ways in and the rule accepted one (now `via`). The mirror of that second one failed `good-05-lobby-gallery-mansion`, a plan the reference corpus holds up as GOOD, so `entrance-hall` gained the same `via`.
· **Six were traditions the universal rules were never written for**, and those are the first suppressions ever declared in this corpus. OQ 15 built the mechanism and its own entry recorded that nothing used it, because inventing one to exercise the code is what this project forbids. A Charleston single's street door opens onto the PIAZZA; a bungalow's opens into the LIVING ROOM, which is the hall the type abolished and whose kit already said so in prose; a dogtrot's opens into the trot, which is open to the weather at both ends; a Creole cabinet is entered from the loggia and never from a chamber. Declared on seven nodes — four of them siblings carrying the same Creole fact, because a suppression is inherited DOWN a chain and does not travel sideways, and hoisting it to a shared ancestor would claim the cabinet-and-loggia range for traditions that do not have it.
**Result: fatal adjacency findings across all 129 composable styles, 115 → 0.** Total fatal 34 → 32, and every one that remains is a fault-layer finding. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**The EQUIVALENT groups are asymmetric in practice and symmetric in the code.** Raised by OQ 42, which could not be finished without a ruling on it. `build/plan_check.py`'s `EQUIVALENT` list holds nine groups of interchangeable room types, and `_alias()` treats membership as a two-way relation: if a primary bathroom counts as a bathroom then a bathroom counts as a primary bathroom. That is right for some pairings and wrong for others, and the wrong ones are the load-bearing cases. A primary bedroom's `must_adjoin primary-bathroom` is not satisfied by the hall bath being somewhere in the house; a parlor's `must_adjoin entrance-hall` IS satisfied by a centre passage; `{bedroom, bedchamber, primary-bedroom}` is symmetric for a daylight rule and asymmetric for a privacy one. **Measured cost of leaving it:** 542 completeness findings across the catalogue can state that an equivalent room exists and cannot say whether reaching it would satisfy the rule, and OQ 42 had to hold them at `minor` for that reason — 170 of them would otherwise be fatal, across 87 styles. **Measured cost of getting it wrong:** the same 170. The concrete proposal, so the next person does not re-derive it: replace each flat set with a directed statement — `{"is": "primary-bathroom", "satisfies": ["bathroom"]}` — so a group says which way each substitution runs, defaulting to symmetric where an author does not distinguish. Nine groups, roughly twenty pairings, one judgment each; the code change is small and the judgments are the work. It also wants a second axis nobody has thought about yet: an equivalence can be right for one KIND of rule and wrong for another, and a single directed graph cannot say that.
