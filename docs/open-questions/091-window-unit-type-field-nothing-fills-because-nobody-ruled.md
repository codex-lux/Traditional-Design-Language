# OQ 91 — a window has a `unit_type` field and nothing fills it, because nobody has ruled where the authority lives

*Status: HALF CLOSED 27 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the plan-semantics program (WP-6.1 / WP-6.2, 26 August 2026)*

**HALF CLOSED 27 Aug 2026 (WP-7.3) — RULED, AND TWO LAYERS DECIDE TWO DIFFERENT THINGS AND NEITHER MAY ANSWER FOR THE OTHER.** This entry's own framing was the answer: the question was never what the types are but which layer decides. The ruling: **`openings/window-grammar.json` decides the window's ROLE** — whether an opening is an ordinary lit window, a high transom band, a borrowed light off another room, a fixed or obscured light in a wet enclosure, or the one deep bay a library takes. That is a PLAN fact: it follows from what the room is for, which wall it is on and which storey it sits in, and it is the same in a Georgian house and a Tudor one. **The KIT decides the SASH KIND** — double-hung, casement, leaded casement — which is what `window_type` has always held. A room may not state a sash kind and a kit may not state a role. Rooms stay silent, so the fact is not in three places.
**THE MEASUREMENT THAT CORRECTS THIS ENTRY AND EVERYTHING BUILT ON IT.** The flat kit files carry `window_type` as `status: empty` on **120 of 159**, which is where "the corpus knows them" was doubted — and reading those files is reading the wrong layer. Resolved through the lineage by `build/resolve_kit.py`, **119 of 159 styles answer** and 40 do not. `tidewater-georgian`, this corpus's own worked example, states no `window_type` and inherits `double-hung` from `georgian-colonial-american` with six variants forbidden. A first version of the resolver read the flat file and would have reported COULD NOT EVALUATE for three quarters of the styles the corpus can answer for — the "unjudged is not passed" rule run backwards, which is its own kind of lie. The answer now travels with its provenance (`unit_type_from`), because the cascade delivers what nobody bound (OQ 51).
**BUILT.** `openings/window-grammar.json` — 7 room rules and 4 class defaults, editorial, every rule quoting the room-record prose it reads; `build/check_windows.py`, which reuses `check_openings.check_basis` rather than growing a second copy and **caught three loose transcriptions while the file was being written**; totality over all 120 (room type x wall exposure) resolutions with 112 (93.3%) landing on a rule that read something and the 8 fall-throughs published. `compose.py` stamps `role`, `role_rule`, `unit_type`, `unit_type_from` and `unit_type_unresolved`; the kit's variant-level `forbidden` bindings refuse through `resolve_kit`'s existing machinery. On a composed Tidewater Georgian: 27 lit, 2 bay, 2 obscured, all 31 given `double-hung` by the cascade.
**THE VOCABULARY HALF MOVED 27 Aug 2026 (WP-7.4), AND WHAT REMAINS IS AN AXIS PROBLEM RATHER THAN A SPELLING ONE.** Ruled: merge the exact duplicates and turn the existing checker on. `double-hung-sash` -> `double-hung` (the spelling `elements/slots.json` already gave as an example), `round-arched` -> `round-arch`, `round-headed-window` -> `round-headed`: the flat vocabulary went 44 -> 41 distinct ids, the consumed one 22 -> 21, and `double-hung` now carries the **54 of 119** resolving styles that were described two ways. **The hazard was list ORDER, not spelling** -- `compose.kit_window_type` takes the first non-forbidden variant, so a merge inside one kit can silently re-pick that kit's window type; the consumed pick and the full resolved variants list were snapshotted through the lineage for all 164 styles before and after and **0 differed beyond the rename**. `check_kits.py::check_slot_fields` has read `fields[].examples` as a permitted vocabulary since OQ 47 and `expressed_frame` was the only slot declaring one, so a window_type variant id was validated by nothing; `window_type` now declares its 41 and a `dubble-hung` typo errors. **The list is every id the corpus uses, not an idealised one, and the slot says so.** **TWENTY OF THE 41 ARE ON THE WRONG AXIS**: they describe an opening's head SHAPE or its SURROUND -- `clean-rectangle-flat-architrave` (8 styles), `horseshoe-arch` (7), `round-arched-paired` (5) -- and this corpus already has the slots that own them (`arch`, added at 0.7.0 for exactly this, plus `window_head_*` and `window_surround_*`); two more name a glazing treatment or a relative date. All 22 are listed one by one under the slot's `off_axis` key with the slot each belongs to, rather than left as a count in a report, because **24 of the 119 resolving styles are handed one AS THEIR WINDOW TYPE** and migrating them changes what those styles resolve as. That migration is this entry's remaining half.
**NO CLOSED ENUM, and refusing to invent one is part of the ruling.** `unit_type` carries the kit's own resolved string verbatim. A first draft published an idealised enum (double-hung / casement / fixed); the kits actually use ~40 free strings of which several are not sash kinds at all — `clean-rectangle-flat-architrave` (8), `horseshoe-arch` (7), `round-arched-paired` (5) describe an opening's SHAPE, and `double-hung` (22) and `double-hung-sash` (32) are the same thing spelled twice. **That vocabulary problem is now the open half of this question**: normalising it is a change to the kit corpus and needs its own ruling. `check_windows.py` fails the build if this file ever publishes a closed enum again.
**BAYS, BOWS AND ORIELS REMAIN UNMODELLED AND ARE NOW RECORDED AS SUCH.** `wg-library-bay` and `wg-hall-dais-window` write the role because the rooms state it outright — *"The elegant historic answer is the BAY OR ORIEL: one deep window in a projection"* — and nothing draws a projection: `$defs.geometry` is four numbers and every consumer (the slicer, the CP model, both renderers, both exporters) is rectangle-only. The decision is in the record and the geometry is refused. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — a window has a `unit_type` field and nothing fills it, because nobody has ruled where the authority lives.** Plan schema 0.3.0 gives each window unit a `unit_type` (double-hung, casement, fixed, bay), because the reported symptom included *"whether there would have been a single window, a double window, a triple window, a bay window, a double-hung window, a casement window, etc. at each location"* and the corpus could answer none of it per opening. It still cannot. The ontology's `window_type` and `special_window` are **whole-house slots** resolved from the kit — a Tudor Revival has leaded casements, a Georgian has double-hung sash — and that is the right level for the STYLE question. It is the wrong level for the PLAN question, which is that a stair landing wants a tall single, a bath a small high one, a library a bay, and a Georgian chamber a 9/6 where its ground floor takes 12/12 (`opening-proportion` already derives that last one: `window_height_diminution_by_storey = 5/6`). Only 6 of 60 room records cite `special_window` in their `slots` list and 1 cites `window_type`, so the room layer is not carrying it either. **The question is not what the types are — the corpus knows them — but which layer decides, and whether a room record may state a per-opening preference that a kit's forbidden-variant list can then refuse.** Filling the field before that is settled would put the same fact in three places. **Bay, bow and oriel windows are the sharpest case and are not modelled at all**: `special_window` is a categorical with no geometry anywhere — no projection depth, no cant angle, no roof, no floor structure — so a plan cannot draw one even if a rule chose it.
