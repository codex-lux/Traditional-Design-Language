# OQ 50 — ornament works by being bounded, and it looks like a property of this corpus rather than a regional habit. Should it be a fault?

*Status: RULED 25 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the real solver (WP-2.3, 25 Aug 2026)*

**RULED 25 Aug 2026 — stop at the principle; a fault would need the elevation layer to see ornament zones first.** Of the three answers this entry proposed, (c) is taken and (a) is deliberately not: the principle is stated once in `docs/model.md`, with its control case and its limits, so the next author meets it before writing a twelfth pack that rediscovers it. A fault was costed and refused for now — `build/elevation.py` contains **zero** occurrences of an ornament zone or anything like one, so `evenly-distributed-ornament` would mean first teaching the elevation layer where ornament IS, which is a modelling package in its own right and larger than the finding it would police. The entry stays OPEN on that one point: if the elevation layer ever gains ornament zones, the fault should be written, with the eight condemning records as its authority and `italian-renaissance` explicitly exempted. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — ornament works by being bounded, and it looks like a property of this corpus rather than a regional habit. Should it be a fault?** Noticed one pack at a time across WP-4.6 and measured on 25 Aug 2026. `facade-portada` found nine Spanish records saying it and read it as an Iberian characteristic; `corbel-course` found Mudejar, Anglo-American and Scottish records saying it and called it possibly general; `facade-medieval-english` found the Elizabethan frontispiece saying it too. This entry is the measurement that should have followed the second sighting.
**The keyword sweep proposed 40 of 132 buildable nodes and reading disposed of 14 of them** — ornament that must be *sawn wood*, buttresses only where thrust exists, rafter tails that must be real (that is OQ 47's `member_status`), an arch's geometry. **26 nodes state a genuine placement-rationing rule**: ornament concentrated in named places with a plain field around it, across at least nine unrelated traditions — Iberian and colonial Spanish, English Georgian and Palladian, Victorian American, Scottish, Swiss, Norman, French, Mexican.
**The strong form of the claim is the inverse, and it holds.** Ten statements across eight nodes explicitly condemn the opposite, in their own words: even distribution "destroys the hierarchy that carries the composition" (`beaux-arts-french`), "converts the complex into" another style (`california-mission-colonial`), is "a revival misreading" (`churrigueresque`), "misreads the tradition" (`mexican-colonial`), "destroys the composition" (`scottish-baronial`), "converts the building into a revival" (`spanish-colonial-american`), "means a later imitation" (`spanish-colonial-revival`), "destroys the style outright" (`spanish-plateresque`). **No node in the corpus requires ornament to be evenly distributed.**
**And there is exactly one control case, which is what makes it a finding rather than an artefact of how the records were written.** `spanish-plateresque`'s `distinguished_from` names it: "Italian work regulates the whole facade by an order and distributes ornament across it." And `styles/italian-renaissance.json`'s own bay rhythm agrees — "even, additive and non-hierarchical: five to eleven identical bays across a front, with emphasis given only by centring the portal". So the corpus holds one tradition that genuinely distributes, it is named as the contrast by the tradition that most opposes it, and it is not a counter-example to the pattern but the pattern's boundary.
**What is NOT claimed.** 26 of 132 is not "most of the corpus", and the other 106 are mostly silent rather than disagreeing — a node that says nothing about where ornament goes has not voted. The honest statement is conditional: *wherever a node in this corpus states a rule about where ornament goes, it states concentration, and eight of them say that distributing it destroys the style.*
The question is what to do with it, and there are three answers. (a) **Author a fault** — `evenly-distributed-ornament`, firing where a composed elevation carries worked ornament in more than N zones with no plain field between, with the eight condemning records as its authority and `italian-renaissance` as its explicit exemption. That is the useful answer and it is the largest: the fault layer would need a way to see ornament ZONES, which `elevation.py` does not currently model. (b) **Leave it in the three packs' notes**, where it is true and inert. (c) **Promote it to a corpus-wide principle in `docs/model.md`**, stated once with its control case, so the next author meets it before writing a twelfth pack that rediscovers it. (c) is nearly free and should probably happen whatever is decided about (a).
