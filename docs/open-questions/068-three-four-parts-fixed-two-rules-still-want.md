# OQ 68 — three of its four parts are fixed, two rules still want a ruling, and the entry it replaces was wrong about almost every part of it

*Status: HALF CLOSED 26 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the second audit round (26 Aug 2026)*

**HALF CLOSED 26 Aug 2026 — three of its four parts are fixed, two rules still want a ruling, and the entry it replaces was wrong about almost every part of it.** Recorded as "32 derived proportion rules evaluate outside their own band, ten of them comparing inches to a ratio... each needs a decision about what the band SHOULD say, and that belongs to the authority the pack cites". Re-measured from scratch on Lucas's instruction: it is **22 rules, not 32** — the 32 came from evaluating at bindings other than the pack's own defaults — and **not one of them was a wrong number**. Every band in the corpus is as its authority states it. What was wrong was the model around them, in three distinct ways, and all three are now fixed. `docs/reports/candidate-score-composite.md` carries the working; `tests/test_proportion_bands.py` is the guard.
**TEN were a band on a RATIO compared against a value in inches.** `greek-doric`'s entablature evaluates `column_height * 0.318` to 41.976 in and was checked against `[0.3, 0.34]` — whose own authority note reads *"The Parthenon's measured ratio, about 0.315"*. The same shape in `chambers-composite`, `chambers-corinthian` (entablature and crown), `chambers-tuscan`, `palladio-composite`, `palladio-corinthian`, `palladio-ionic`, `palladio-tuscan` and `moorish-arch`. **Every one of the ten implied ratios lands inside its own band** — 0.3180 in [0.3, 0.34], 0.2500 in [0.2222, 0.25], 0.0694 in [0.055, 0.08] — so the data was right and `range` was one field doing two jobs. **Ruling: split into two rules**, and done: a `*_ratio` rule carrying the proportion the authority states and the band, and the dimension rule beside it multiplying it out into inches with no band at all. The dimension is NAMED (`height_ratio`, `return_ratio`) and not the generic `ratio`, because `facade-arcade` already writes `(arch, ratio)` for `springing_as_fraction_of_height` — a generic name collided with it, `check_addresses.py` caught it in the same hour, and the corpus's own habit (`column_ratio`, `storey_ratio`, `base_ratio`) is the specific name.
**SIX were the engine's default bindings, and four of them SHOUT the fix in their own authority note**: *BIND storey_height TO THE FULL WALL HEIGHT*, in capitals, on all four Benjamin eave cornices. `DEFAULT_BINDINGS` gives `storey_height` 120 in — one 10 ft storey — and Benjamin's own worked example is *"suppose a house to be thirty five feet high, divide thirty five feet into thirty parts, and one thirtieth will be fourteen inches."* At 420 in all four land squarely inside their bands. The two Gibbs pediments are the same thing at a doorcase: the construction is on the span of the cornice of a principal door, and the default 36 in opening gives a 4.8 in rise. **Ruling: use `calibrated_for`, and refuse to judge outside it** — and the field had been in the schema for exactly this since the chair-rail correction, described in as many words as *"the context in which this rule was calibrated. Outside it the rule still evaluates and should not be trusted"*, on 2 of 751 rules, **read by nothing**. `proportion_engine.evaluate()` now withholds `in_range` outside a stated context and publishes `out_of_calibration` in its place. Judging a rule against a binding its own note tells you not to use is unjudged-reported-as-failed, which is the direction this corpus must never round in. Turning the mechanism on immediately picked up `trim-classical`'s chair rail, which had carried a `calibrated_for` since 0.2.0 that nothing had ever honoured.
**FOUR missed by 0.00025.** 7/32 = 0.21875 against a floor of `0.219` — the three-decimal rounding of the very expression the band exists to contain — while the Proportions surface displayed the value rounded to 0.2188 and so printed a number that looked inside the band it said was violated. **Ruling: one tolerance, the same idea `check_invariants` already carries** (*"these are ratios written down by hand in the sixteenth century and stored as decimals"*). Implemented as the author's own precision rather than a chosen constant: half a unit in the last decimal place the edge was WRITTEN to, capped at half a thousandth. A floor written `0.219` gets 0.0005 and admits 7/32; one written `0.2222` gets a tenth of that; one written `0.3` gets 0.0005 and still refuses 0.26.
**The checker gap was the durable half, and it is closed.** `check_orders.py` validated that a range was not inverted and never evaluated a rule against one, so 22 violations sat unreported for as long as the file has existed — while `check_modules.py --eval` and `check_systems.py` have both done exactly this check for years and neither covers `proportions/orders/` or `proportions/overlays/`, where all 22 lived. It evaluates through `proportion_engine.evaluate()` rather than a second implementation, so what it checks is exactly what the MCP tool and the workbench publish, tolerance and calibration included. A violation is a WARNING: two survive deliberately, and failing the build on a finding the corpus is correctly making would be the wrong answer.
**TWO REMAIN OUT OF BAND ON PURPOSE, and they are the only part of this entry still open.** `vignola-doric`'s cornice derives 9.0 in against its own `[10, 36]`, and `chambers-ionic`'s balustrade derives 28.8 in against `[30, 42]` on a rule whose `quantity` is `guard_height`. Both are the mechanism working rather than failing: a dimension derived from a small order, and a band saying the result is too small to build. The balustrade is the sharper case — a guard at 28.8 in is genuinely too low, and the corpus reporting the conflict between an order-derived dimension and a safety floor is the corpus doing its job. **What is wanted is a ruling on which of the two they are**: a calibration context these rules should declare (as the six above now do), or a finding that should stay red. They are pinned by name in `tests/test_proportion_bands.py` so the count can go down with a ruling and cannot go up silently.
