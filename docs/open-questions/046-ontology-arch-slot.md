# OQ 46 — The ontology has no arch slot

*Status: CLOSED 24 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the real solver (WP-2.3, 25 Aug 2026)*

**CLOSED 24 Aug 2026 — `arch` added at ontology 0.6.0, and the migration is eight bindings and not the twenty-two I counted.** The slot holds the arch as a SPANNING MEMBER: its form, its rise against its span, what it springs from, whether it is structural or applied, and whether an arch is permitted at all — Egyptian Revival's c03 is a rule *about* arches which states that there are none, and it had nowhere to live.
**The 22 was a keyword count and reading them changed it.** Most of the twenty-two `window_head_masonry` bindings that mention an arch are genuinely window-head statements: a stone lintel with a hood mould, a flat gauged arch used as a head, a plain unmoulded band, 'no masonry head at all'. Migrating on the keyword would have been the keyword deciding rather than the content — the same mistake OQ 41's sweep had to avoid, and the second time in two packages that a measurement of mine over-counted its own work list. **Six moved whole** (`italian-renaissance`, `norman-romanesque-english`, `roman-classical`, `gothic-revival-british`, `egyptian-revival`, `italianate-townhouse`), where the content was arch-as-form end to end and the head slot is left empty rather than carrying a pointer, because the same fact in two places is what this migration exists to prevent. **Two were split** — `georgian-colonial-american` and `tidewater-georgian` carried both claims at once, so the arch geometry (rise, camber, depth) moved and the head keeps how it is BUILT (gauging, one datum a storey, keystone, architrave) and names `arch` in `determined_by`, which is the mechanism OQ 19 built for exactly this.
**And OQ 19's check immediately earned its place on the new arrangement**: it flagged the architrave and keystone widths as editorial numbers sitting on a determined slot with nothing saying whether they restate the determiner or are independent of it. They are independent — an architrave is joinery around the opening and the arch is masonry spanning it, and neither sets the other's dimension — and both now say so. A rule added for one reason tightening another is the ratchet working.
`proportions/orders/moorish-arch.json`, the pack that found the gap, now targets `arch` instead of `window_head_masonry`. Its impost block honestly stays on `porch_support`: an arcade carrying a loggia is what that slot is for and the corpus has no `arcade` id, which was the third option on this question and is not built. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**The ontology has no arch slot.** Found while authoring `moorish-arch` for WP-4.6. `elements/slots.json` has 95 slots and none of them is an arch, an arcade or an opening head as a *structural* member: `window_head_masonry` is the nearest and is written for a lintel or a jack arch over a window, `porch_support` for what holds a porch up. So a system whose entire content is the geometry of an arch and its springing had to target the return of a horseshoe at `window_head_masonry` and the impost block at `porch_support`, and both bindings are recorded in that pack's own notes as the compromises they are. It is not only a Moorish problem: the Gothic Revival facade-and-opening pack WP-4.1 named, the Romanesque foliate order, Rome's arcuated pier-arch-pier bay rhythm and the Italianate round-arched window all want to say something about an arch that the corpus currently has no place to put. The question is whether to add an `arch` slot to the openings group (and possibly an `arcade` to the classical apparatus), which is a small ontology change with a large surface — every pack, kit and constraint that currently routes arch content through a window head would want revisiting, and doing it badly would leave the same fact in two places. Raised now because WP-4.6 will hit it again with every arched tradition it reaches.
