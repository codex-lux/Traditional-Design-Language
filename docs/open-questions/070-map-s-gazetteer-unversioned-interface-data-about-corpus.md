# OQ 70 — the map's gazetteer is unversioned interface data about a corpus that grows

*Status: CLOSED 26 AUG 2026; THE READING WAS CORRECTED THE SAME DAY BY AN ADVERSARIAL AUDIT · Raised in: Reissued 26 August 2026 — ids 64 and 65 collided a second time*

**CLOSED 26 Aug 2026; the reading was CORRECTED the same day by an adversarial audit.** `placeByHearth` scanned the gazetteer longest-name-first and took the first hit at the finest tier, choosing by an accident of key length rather than by what the sentence says: **25 of 93 hearth placements landed on a place named second or later, `craftsman` among them — placed in Los Angeles while its record reads "Pasadena and Los Angeles", Pasadena being the exact word this whole reading was justified by.** Earliest-mentioned now wins, applied AFTER the anchor filter (sorting first would hand `dutch-colonial-american` to Amsterdam, the bug the anchor exists to catch). A bare `Washington` key also matched a hearth meaning D.C. and drew `minimal-traditional` 3,700 km out; ambiguous names are now flagged regions-only, resolvable from a regions list but never from prose. And `check_gazetteer.py` no longer re-implements the algorithm in Python — it runs `gazetteer.js` through node, because the audit found the 45° rule named as a constant on one side and hard-coded on the other, with a third copy of the no-hearth classifier already disagreeing on one style. *Original closure follows.*<br><br>**CLOSED 26 Aug 2026 — and the check was the smaller half. The corpus already knew where these styles came from.** Ruled: build the check, and chase the hearths too. Chasing them turned out to need no authoring at all, which is the finding. The map placed styles from `geography.regions` alone, and **59 of the 81 country-level placements had a `geography.hearth` sentence naming somewhere finer that nothing was reading** — `craftsman` sat in the middle of Kansas while its own record said "Pasadena and Los Angeles, California"; `prairie-school` likewise, against "Oak Park and Chicago". Placement now takes the finest of what either field names, and the numbers moved **15 → 87 locality, 81 → 24 country**. The remaining 24 are correct: 22 are families and traditions, which are abstractions over styles and have no birthplace, and two are styles whose hearth says so in its own words (`neo-eclectic`: "No design hearth"; `craftsman-bungalow`: "Streetcar suburbs nationwide"). So the gap this question suspected in `geography.hearth` coverage does not exist — the gap was in the reading. **The rule that makes it safe: a hearth may SHARPEN a region, never contradict one.** A name found in the prose is refused if it is more than 45° from EVERY region the style names. Against only the finest region — the first version — it threw away `churrigueresque`'s Madrid because that style's finest region is in Mexico, while Spain sat in the same list. Against all of them it still does its job: `dutch-colonial-american`'s hearth reads "from Nieuw Amsterdam to Beverwijck (Albany)", all six of its regions are American, so Amsterdam is refused and the sentence resolves to Albany, which is what it meant. **`build/check_gazetteer.py --strict`** joins `check_all.py` and ratchets three numbers, all now at zero: unplaced, coarse-for-any-reason-but-the-record's, and hearth sentences it cannot read. It parses the JS gazetteer rather than keeping a Python copy, because a second copy is precisely how the two halves of the citation grammar came to disagree about dots. Proved by adding a style naming a place nobody has heard of: the check fails, names it, and says what to do. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — the map's gazetteer is unversioned interface data about a corpus that grows.** `workbench/app/src/data/gazetteer.js` places all 164 styles today from 180 region-name entries. It is deliberately NOT corpus data — the corpus holds no coordinates and none of these numbers may migrate into `styles/*.json` — and a style whose regions it does not know is reported as unplaced rather than guessed, which is the correct failure. But nothing **fails the build** to tell an author that their new style is invisible on the map: the map simply lists one more name under "not placed", where it can sit unnoticed indefinitely. A `build/check_gazetteer.py` ratcheting placement coverage the way `check_inheritance.py` ratchets its three numbers would close it in an afternoon. Not built, because it is unclear whether the map reading is load-bearing enough to earn a check in `check_all.py`, and that is a judgment about how the tool is actually used rather than a fact about the data. Related: the placement tiers are themselves a finding worth a ruling — **81 of 164 styles name only a country**, so half the corpus has no hearth recorded at all, and that is a gap in `geography.hearth` coverage that the map made visible rather than a gap in the map.
