# OQ 72 — the fine coastline tier is one megabyte of geometry, a fifth of it in a single un-cullable ring, and the download is the smaller half of the problem

*Status: OPEN · Raised in: From the atlas and the shell (WP-5.7, 26 August 2026)*

**OPEN — the fine coastline tier is one megabyte of geometry, a fifth of it in a single un-cullable ring, and the download is the smaller half of the problem.** WP-5.7 gave the atlas three levels of detail so that zooming in sharpens the drawing instead of magnifying its facets (`workbench/scripts/make_coastlines.py`, `app/src/surfaces/phylo/coastTiers.js`). The finest is Natural Earth 10m simplified at 0.012 degrees: 2,262 rings, 116,623 points, 1.17 MB raw and 363 KB gzipped, fetched as one dynamic-import chunk the first time a reader crosses sixteen degrees of longitude.
**The question was first written as a question about bytes, and an adversarial audit showed that framing would have steered the fix wrong**, so it is restated here. Two costs, and they want different remedies:
**(a) The download.** 363 KB gzipped, once a session, lazily, against a 132 KB application. On a self-hosted instrument usually served from localhost this may simply be fine, and if it is, the right answer is to say so and stop measuring it. A per-region *fetch* split would cut it to a fiftieth.
**(b) The render, which the fetch split does not touch.** Culling is by bounding box, and measured by RING COUNT it is superb — 2,262 down to three at maximum zoom. Measured by GEOMETRY it plateaus at about 78%, because the tier's largest ring is Afro-Eurasia: **one `<path>` with a 244 KB `d` attribute and 25,067 points**, whose bounding box spans some 190 degrees of longitude. Zooming anywhere in the Old World mounts it whole, and zooming further in never removes it. A reader at three degrees over London is holding ~25,000 path segments that are re-stroked in device space on every pointermove — and the WP-5.7 fix that put `vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke"` on each path, which is correct and necessary, is also what denies the rasteriser the cheap cached-raster path. Sectoring the *download* would still hand the Europe sector that same ring.
**What would actually fix (b) is splitting the mega-rings' GEOMETRY**, which is real cartographic machinery rather than a filter: a ring clipped at a sector boundary is an open polyline, and drawn with a stroke it puts a hairline seam through open water along the cut unless the clip distinguishes the coast from the cut. That is the harder half and nobody has costed it.
**A cheaper half-measure exists and was deliberately not taken**: ship the fine tier for the North Atlantic only, where five of five traditions are, and let the rest of the world stop at medium — which would be a drawing that is sharper in the places this corpus happens to care about and quietly blunter everywhere else, and a map that is better where you expect to look is exactly the kind of map that lies when you look somewhere else.
**Measured, so a ruling has numbers to stand on:** `visibleRings` itself costs 0.004–0.033 ms a call and is NOT the problem — reporting it as the cost would point the fix at the wrong place. A 60-move pan at the fine tier ran at the frame budget on this machine rather than under it.
