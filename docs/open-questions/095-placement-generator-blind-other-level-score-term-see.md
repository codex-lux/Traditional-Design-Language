# OQ 95 — the placement generator is blind to the other level, and no score term can see past that

*Status: CLOSED 27 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From geometry truth (WP-6.3, 27 August 2026)*

**CLOSED 27 Aug 2026 (WP-7.4) — BOTH ENGINES CHARGE DECLARED STACKING NOW, AND THIS ENTRY'S OWN STATED BLOCKER APPLIED TO A MECHANISM NOBODY HAD TO USE.** Ruled by Lucas: a SOFT term in both engines, no hard pin. Built: a charge in `geometry.vertical_score` and a penalty in `geometry_cp.py`'s objective block beside the wet-over-wet term it is shaped after. **THE HEURISTIC HALF ACTS AND THE CP HALF, SO FAR, DOES NOT — measured, not assumed.** Six CP placements were run with both weights at zero and at the shipped values and came out IDENTICAL: both shipped plans and two composed partis. On `tidewater-georgian-careful` the reason is structural — the winner is `hard-only phase A`, the feasibility placement the objective never touches, so no CP objective term can reach that drawing; on the others the polish phase wins and the outcome still does not move. The CP half is kept because two engines that disagree about what matters is the worse failure, and because `geometry_cp._score` (which decides CP's acceptance among hard-valid placements, and whose docstring claims it is the heuristic's scoring "term for term") now carries the span charge too. Whether it ever acts is an unmade measurement, recorded as inert on the way in rather than discovered two packages later. **Measured over the 14 partis that declare the field, composed against their own first native style: broken claims 27/49 -> 15/49 (-44%), over-capacity spans 29 -> 23 with the worst falling 80 -> 60 ft, and total `serious` findings 703 -> 685.** The cost is stated: fatal findings 89 -> 90 (6 new, 5 removed, every one a reachability finding on plans that already carried 89 of them, and the neighbouring weights run 93-94 so read it as +1 to +5 in this region), and **rooms below their catalogue band 17 -> 19**, which is the real price and is counted.
**THE BLOCKER THIS ENTRY RECORDS WAS TRUE OF A PIN AND NEVER OF A PENALTY.** "A stacking constraint would be non-downgradable by construction and would outrank every declared exterior wall" -- true, because the downgrade loop reads `k == "wall"`. A penalty creates no assumption literal, never enters a conflict core, and cannot displace anything; `geometry_cp.py` already carried the template. The question was sidestepped rather than solved.
**AND THE INERTNESS TWO PACKAGES MEASURED WAS A SAMPLING ARTEFACT.** WP-6.3's byte-identical output at 100x and 10,000x was real, of a charge keyed on landing-over-stair -- a pair neither shipped plan declares. Over 2,000 candidates rather than the shipped 250 at one seed, 12 tidewater candidates beat the winner's 3 broken claims WHILE KEEPING THE PORCH ON THE ENTRANCE FRONT, at +41.3 points, and 6 spec-builder candidates at +2.1. In the thin pool the only better-stacking candidates move the porch off the front, which is why the break-even there read 378 and 52. **The residual 15 of 49 is a quality number, not an open question**: the weight is set where it stops buying stacks with fatals, and `docs/reports/wp-7.4-*.md` carries the sweep. *Previous status and original entry follow.*<br><br>**HALF CLOSED 27 Aug 2026 (WP-7.1) — the generator is level-aware now, and it fixed BEARING, not STACKING. The two were conflated in this entry and they are different problems.** Ruled by Lucas: build the level-aware generator, not a score term. Built: `slice_rect` is called for level 1 with the ground layout in hand, and `snap()` prefers a wall line below over a bare bay line. The relaxation DEFINITION was corrected in the same commit -- a relaxation is *"a joist run that does not land on a bearing wall"* in `geometry.py`'s own prose, and the code had approximated that as "misses the bay module" while 18 of 30 ground wall lines are themselves off the bay grid, so a cut landing squarely on a wall below had been counted a compromise. **Measured corpus-wide over 14 composed plans: transfer beams 166 -> 109 (-34%), relaxations 96 -> 76 (-21%), total score improved. Over 23 plans including both shipped ones, transfer beams 205 -> 128 (-38%) for 2 additional over-capacity spans, worst span unchanged at 47.12 ft** -- not a shell game.
**WHAT IT DID NOT FIX, which is the finding.** `stacks_over` claims broken went **26/47 -> 27/47: flat.** Moving a cut line moves a wall; it does not move a room over another room. A second half of the change -- biasing a room's partition toward the room it stacks over -- was built and exactly cancelled the harm the cut-line half did to stacking, netting zero. So the stacking half of this question is OPEN and is a different mechanism from the one this entry proposed.
**TWO CORRECTIONS THIS ENTRY MUST CARRY.** (1) Its own stated reason for WP-6.3's refusal -- *"the search can only re-rank blind candidates and can never generate a stacking one"* -- is FALSE. Measured over 24 seeds the winning candidate satisfies 1 to 3 of tidewater's 3 cross-level claims and 0 to 2 of spec-builder's 2. The charge was inert for a narrower reason: it keyed on landing-over-stair and neither shipped plan declares that pair. (2) "one on each is drawn clear" was stale: the field is declared by **14 of 21 partis, 50 claims**, and roughly half are drawn broken on BOTH engines (26/49 heuristic, 23/49 CP). *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — the placement generator is blind to the other level, and no score term can see past that.** Raised by refuting WP-6.3's own first proposal. `build/geometry.py` generates each level with `slice_rect(copy.deepcopy(prep[lvl]), 0, 0, W, H, bay, tol, rng, …)` and **passes it nothing about the level below**. `vertical_score` then scores the pair — bearing lines, wet stacks — but it is scoring candidates that were produced without any knowledge of what they must sit on. That is why the stair-stacking charge this package planned turns out to be inert: implemented and swept, **raising its weight 100x and 10,000x produces byte-identical output**, because the search can only re-rank blind candidates and can never generate a stacking one. It is a tax the search pays forever and cannot act on, and it costs relaxations 11 → 16 to pay it.
**The consequence is live and counted.** Across the two shipped plans six rooms declare `stacks_over` and, on the CP placements, one on each is drawn clear of the room it names — `primarybath` over `butlers`, `hallbath` over `laundry`. Each is a waste stack with nothing under it. The `drawn` layer reports every one of them (WP-6.3) and neither engine can prevent them.
**The CP engine cannot simply be given a hard constraint instead**, and that is the second half of the finding. `geometry_cp.py`'s downgrade loop reads `wall_keys = [key for _t, k, key in core if k == "wall" and key]` and breaks when that list is empty, so **only wall pins are downgradable**: a stacking constraint would be non-downgradable by construction and would outrank every declared exterior wall in the corpus. Measured on a conflicting case it did not refuse — it downgraded an authored kitchen wall to satisfy an inferred stack, with a refinement that blames the wall and never names the constraint that displaced it. An authored fact losing silently to an inferred one is the OQ 52 family.
**What would actually work, so the next person does not re-derive it:** make the upper-level generator level-aware — seed its cut lines from the ground layout's own wall lines (they are already computed for the bearing-line term), or slice the two levels jointly against one tree. That is a change to how candidates are PRODUCED rather than how they are scored, and it is the only place the fix can live. Alternatively give the CP model a *downgradable* stacking pin with its own `kind`, ranked explicitly below declared exterior walls so an authored wall can never lose to an inferred stack — cheaper, and it makes the corpus's existing `stacks_over` claims enforceable rather than merely reported.
