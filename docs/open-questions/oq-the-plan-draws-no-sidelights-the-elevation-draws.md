# oq/the-plan-draws-no-sidelights-the-elevation-draws — one entrance, two widths

*Status: OPEN · Raised in: WP-14.6, the adversarial audit of Phase 14 (27 Sep 2026)*

**OPEN — where the elevation draws a sidelight pair beside the entrance door, the plan sheet of the
same house draws the door alone.** The elevation composes the entrance from
`elevation.entrance_composition`: the leaf, its casings and, where facade-classical's bay cap lets
them in and the plan leaves room (`elevation._clearances`, WP-14.6), two sidelights. On
`spec-builder-colonial` it draws them: a doorcase and a sidelight either side of the plan's
42 in door, where the record composes 68.9 in round a 34.4 in leaf. The plan sheet is drawn from the
placement's openings (`openings.place`), which seat the door at its leaf width and reserve nothing
beside it, so the plan's entrance is a 3.5 ft opening in a wall the elevation shows glazed for
several feet more. Neither record is wrong on its own terms; no layer holds the two to one width.

On the Tidewater plan the question does not arise today only because WP-14.6 refuses the pair --
the plan places the passage window 12.0 in from the leaf, where the casing and a sidelight need 21.1.
That refusal is the same fact read from the other side: the placement never knew the entrance was
wider than its door.

**What must be ruled:** whether the entrance composition is a PLAN fact (the placer reserves the
doorcase's and sidelights' wall, and the plan draws their glass) or an ELEVATION fact the plan does
not show (and the plan says so beside the door), and in the first case which record owns the
width. Until then the plan and the elevation of one house disagree about how wide its front door is.
