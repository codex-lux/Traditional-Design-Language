# oq/an-exterior-stacks-breast-and-shoulders-are-stated-nowhere — the stack stands on the ground at its own width

*Status: OPEN · Raised in: Phase 15, WP-15.5, the stack drawn from grade (27 September 2026)*

**Every elevation surface now draws an exterior end stack from the ground to its cap (WP-15.5), and
draws it the whole way down at the stack's own square.** On the Tidewater plan that square is
22 in, which `brick-course` states as a judgment: "the mason will build 18 or 27". The corpus has
said since WP-5.13 what is missing below it. The WP-5.13 report: "A chimney breast at the base of an
exterior end stack is several feet across." And `elevation.stack_outline`, since WP-15.5: the breast
"at an exterior stack's foot, several feet across in any built example, has no figure anywhere".
*(Corrected 28 Sep 2026, WP-15.8's audit: this sentence first gave one quotation spliced from those
two sources, and dated it to WP-5.11.)* The shoulders, the sloped
weatherings that step a breast in to its stack, are not stated either. The sheet's legend says
both on every face that draws such a stack. The DXF carries the same sentence and puts the breast
in each stack's XDATA as unstated.

**What is wanted is a figure or a rule, per style, for three things, each from a source this
corpus may cite:**

- the breast's width and its projection from the gable;
- the height at which it steps in;
- the number and slope of the weatherings.

What has been checked, and what it gives:

- **The precedent bench's surveys** name a breast or weatherings in words on English vernacular
  houses. The Higher Uppacott list entry has "a granite ashlar chimneystack with stone
  weatherings". They state no figure for a Tidewater stack.
- **The plates** that might dimension one are behind the same refused hosts as the orders' plates
  (Phase 15, WP-15.1).

**Until one is found the stack is drawn at the least it can be, and a breast is not drawn at a
width no record gives.** That is the WP-5.11 argument, which still decides the width. Two things
must not be read into the drawing:

- The stack's even width from grade is a drawing of what the record states. It is not a claim that
  the chimney is that narrow at its foot.
- A shouldered outline drawn from a picture of Gunston Hall or of any other exemplar would be a
  figure laundered as measured.
