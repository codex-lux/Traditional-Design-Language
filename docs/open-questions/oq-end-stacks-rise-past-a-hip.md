# oq/end-stacks-rise-past-a-hip — a hipped Georgian house with gable-end stacks has no gable end to run them through

*Status: OPEN · Raised in: WP-16.9, the roof, the rake and the deeper cornice (1 October 2026), on Lucas's answer B14 (R-4) of 30 September 2026*

**Under B1, the composer's candidates on the Tidewater Georgian brief are hipped by
tidewater-georgian's resolved kit, and their stacks are refused.** That kit makes the hip alone
canonical (the row is georgian-colonial-american's); a candidate declares no roof, so B1 draws it
hipped. The hearth record calls for gable-end stacks, and
`roof.chimney_positions` refuses them on a hip: *"the roof form here is 'hip', which has no full
gable-end wall to run a stack through -- a real design would need an interior or off-ridge chimney
solution this file does not model. Flagged rather than silently placed at a wall that is not
actually a gable end."* Lucas answered (B14): land B1, refuse the stacks and print the reason
(`disclosures.stacks_the_roof_refuses`), and raise this question.

**What a hipped house does with end stacks is not stated anywhere this corpus reads.** On a hip the
end walls rise to the eave and the roof slopes back from them, so an end stack rises past the hip,
through the roof plane or against it, and its height above the roof is taken off the slope it passes
rather than off a ridge at a gable end. No record states where it passes, how tall it stands there,
or whether the stacks move inboard to the ridge's ends.

**Reach.** Every composed candidate whose style's kit makes the hip canonical and whose hearth calls
for gable-end stacks; the shipped tidewater-georgian plan declares a side gable and is not reached.
