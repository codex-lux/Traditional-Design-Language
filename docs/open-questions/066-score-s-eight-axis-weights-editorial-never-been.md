# OQ 66 — the score's eight axis weights are editorial and have never been tested against a plan anyone ranked by hand

*Status: OPEN · Raised in: From the candidate score (26 Aug 2026) — reissued 66-68 at the 26 Aug merge*

**OPEN — the score's eight axis weights are editorial and have never been tested against a plan anyone ranked by hand.** The candidate score is now a weighted composite out of 100 (`compose.py`'s `SCORE_AXES`): solecisms 22, rooms 20, connections 17, fidelity 18, area 8, bedrooms 4, canon 6, buildability 5. Each axis's SHARE is measured — it is a real fraction over a real denominator, and a test pins that the published arithmetic is the arithmetic. The WEIGHTS are not measured. They are one judgement about what matters in a house, argued for in the comment above them and stated in `docs/compose.md`, and nothing in the corpus supports or contradicts them. The honest test is cheap and has not been run: take a dozen composed candidates across several briefs, have someone fluent rank them by hand, and see whether the composite agrees. Where it does not, the disagreement is either a wrong weight or a missing axis, and both are worth knowing. Until then the number is a stated opinion with its working shown, which is better than the demerit total it replaced but is not a measurement. Two specific weights to look at first: **fidelity at 18**, which is deliberately not enough to carry a plan that fails everything else and has never been checked against a case where it should be, and **buildability at 5** over a denominator of only two footprint tests, so a single note moves it half its range. See `docs/reports/candidate-score-composite.md`.
