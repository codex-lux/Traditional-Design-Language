# OQ 53 — `check_addresses.py` compares `quantity` but not `units`, and two rules can agree on what they measure while differing 36-fold in what they say. Raised 25 Aug 2026 by the same audit

*Status: CLOSED 26 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the real solver (WP-2.3, 25 Aug 2026)*

**CLOSED 26 Aug 2026 — the two live wrong dimensions are fixed, a bare ratio can no longer be delivered as a measurement, and the 68 are counted rather than invisible. The referent question survives as named work, stated at the foot of this entry.** All 68 were read, as the ruling required, and reading changed the answer this entry expected. It proposed that the fix was to add `units` to the address key and then decide, one by one, which of the 68 are one quantity in two clothings (convert) and which are two quantities (separate). The first half of that is done. **The second half turned out to be the wrong question**, and the corpus said so in its own records.
**What reading the 68 showed.** They are one phenomenon: the order packs state interior-trim quantities as a bare ratio, and the trim and module packs state the same quantities in inches. At `casing/width/casing_face_width` on `craftsman`, four rules are in play and **all four say the same thing** — `chambers-ionic` and `palladio-ionic` write `1 / 6`, `palladio-tuscan` and `vignola-tuscan` write `opening_width / 6`. There is nothing to adjudicate between them; the ratio form simply leaves out what it is a ratio OF, and puts it in the rule's prose note ("Of opening_width."), where no evaluator can read it. That is "prose stays beside the test" running backwards: the test was incomplete and the prose was carrying the missing half.
**But converting them would have been wrong, and this is the part the entry did not foresee.** A ratio rule's `range` is scale-free — 0.1429 to 0.1667 holds at every opening width, where an inch band only holds at one — so rewriting the expression in inches would have forced a band to be re-derived at a single assumed opening and made it worse. And some of the ratio rules are **not** the same account reworded: `palladio-ionic` says in its own note that Palladio does NOT proportion the Ionic pedestal to the column, that "the datum is the OPENING, not the order"; `chambers-ionic` measures its pedestal against the whole order's height and says the classical datum moves down under Chambers. Those are rival accounts of one quantity, which is exactly what precedence is for. Flattening them into inches would have invented a referent the source never gave — the failure this corpus exists to avoid.
**So the fix is at the resolver, not in the data.** `resolve_kit.choose_pack` now prefers a rule that yields a MEASUREMENT over one that yields a bare ratio at the same (dimension, quantity), and records the demotion in the chosen record (`ratio_demoted`, with its reason) rather than preferring silently — a silent preference would leave the corpus unable to show why one of two agreeing rules was passed over. **135 slot resolutions across 40 nodes now deliver a measurement where a bare ratio used to win** — far more than the 2 this entry predicted, because it counted only where the delivered value was visibly wrong: `chair_rail` 38, `baseboard` 36, `wainscot` 31, `casing` 26, `window_surround_wood` 4. The two named cases are among them: `craftsman` and `craftsman-bungalow` resolve `casing` to `opening_width / 6` — 6 in at a 36 in door — instead of 0.1667.
**`check_addresses.py` now compares units** and reports a third state beside collision and could-not-judge: two packs that agree on the quantity and differ on the units they say it in. It reports **68** at own scope, which is this entry's own number arrived at independently, and the count is pinned in `tests/test_wp46_packs.py` so the class cannot grow while the referents stay in prose.
**One thing was deliberately NOT built, having been built and withdrawn.** A first version also flagged every address where every rule is a ratio. It fired **1,463 times across all 132 nodes**, because most such quantities genuinely ARE ratios and are right in that form — `main_roof_pitch`, `opening_height_over_width`, `arch_rise_over_span`, `wall_thickness_over_span`. A checker that cries wolf on the corpus's own correct records is worse than no checker, so only the provable case is flagged: a measurement of this very quantity was on the table and a ratio was delivered instead.
**The named work this leaves.** The ratio rules still carry their referent in prose. The fix is a machine-readable one — a `ratio_of` field beside `units`, naming the bound variable the ratio is taken of — which would let the engine emit both the scale-free ratio (range-checked as now) and the derived dimension, and would let the address comparison see that a ratio-of-`opening_width` and an expression over `opening_width` are the same account. Raised 25 Aug 2026 by the same audit.** OQ 48 made `(slot, dimension, quantity)` the real address on the reasoning that two rules meaning the same thing are alternative accounts precedence can choose between. That holds only if they are accounts in the same unit. **68 own-binding addresses agree on all three and disagree on `units`** — `casing/width/casing_face_width` is `6.0 in` on `benjamin-doric` and `0.1667 ratio` on `vignola-ionic`, four nodes binding both, thirty-six times apart. `resolve_kit.choose_pack` groups on `(dimension, quantity)` and never looks at units, so those land in one group and precedence picks between them as though they were rival measurements of one thing. Across the 132 buildable nodes **659** (node, slot) resolutions choose a rule whose units differ from a peer at the same address, and in **2** of them — `craftsman` and `craftsman-bungalow`, slot `casing` — a `ratio` rule actually wins over inch peers, yielding `0.1667` where `3.5 in` was meant. Those two are live wrong dimensions, not a latent hazard.
**Why it is raised rather than fixed.** Adding `units` to the address is a one-line change to the grouping key and a much larger change to what it implies: some of the 68 are genuinely the same quantity expressed two ways and SHOULD be converted rather than separated, and deciding which needs reading all 68. Doing that inside an audit would either separate rules that belong together or convert ones that do not. The two live cases are named above so they can be fixed on their own if the larger question waits.
**A related latent one, same file.** `check_addresses.py` passes a pair on any overlap: `if not (qx & qy)` treats a partial intersection as agreement, so `opening-pointed` {`lights_across`} against `sash-light` {`lights_across`, `lights_high`, `total_lights`} passes while two of the three mean something else. And it skips intra-pack collisions entirely (`if len(sides) < 2`), which is correct for a deliberate menu and wrong for `sash-light`, which writes three different quantities to `window_lite_pattern/count` on its own.
