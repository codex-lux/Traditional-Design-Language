# OQ 34 — composer decisions are prose lines, not records

*Status: CLOSED 26 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the workbench (WP-5.2, 25 Aug 2026)*

**CLOSED 26 Aug 2026 — the log carries its structure and keeps its prose, and the judgment inside it is one line of code.** Ruled: structure it and mark the judgment. `build/compose.py` emits `decisions_structured` beside `decisions`; the prose list is byte-identical to what it always was, so every consumer that read it before reads it unchanged.
**The judgment this entry flagged, and the rule taken.** Which lines are DECISIONS and which are narration — and the log already answers it. A line the composer meant as a decision carries a prefix it wrote itself: `JUDGMENT`, `REFUSED`, `AUTHORED`, `NOT SOLVED`, `KNOWN FINDING`. Those are the corpus's own vocabulary, not a taxonomy imposed from outside, so the classifier is: prefix or no prefix. Everything without one is an assumption taken where the brief was silent — which the composer's own `how_to_read_this` already calls "the assumptions, not facts", so it is a decision too, of a quieter kind. Live counts across both shipped briefs: 45 assumption, 9 judgment, 2 authored, 1 unsolved, 1 disclosure.
**`field` and `chose` are DERIVED, and every record says so.** They are read off the sentence against a fourteen-shape table built by collecting what the composer actually emits — 58 distinct lines across every brief in `briefs/` — not re-authored at the twenty-five call sites, because rewriting those sentences would change prose that tests and reports quote. 56 of 58 lines get a field and 46 a `because`; the two that get neither are the `NOT SOLVED` and `KNOWN FINDING` disclosures, which settle no brief field, and null is the right answer there. A sentence outside the table keeps null rather than a forced guess: a derived value that is wrong would be OQ 52's defect one layer up, and the statement is right beside it carrying the whole thought.
The Candidate Set renders it — a judgment now reads as a judgment at a glance, with the field and the choice above the sentence — and falls back to the plain list for a record composed before this shipped. Five tests in `tests/test_measurement_honesty.py`, one of which runs the real composer and fails if its wording drifts away from the table. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — composer decisions are prose lines, not records.** `compose()`'s `decisions` is a list of sentences (`log + _summarise(rlog)`). They read well, but a decision-log *entry* — what was assumed, because which brief field was silent, overridable by whom — wants `{field, chose, because}` structure, and the workbench's decision-log component renders plain prose in the meantime. Structuring the log is a composer change with a judgment in it (which log lines are decisions vs narration?), so it is recorded here rather than done unilaterally.
