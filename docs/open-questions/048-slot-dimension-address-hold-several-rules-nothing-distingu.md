# OQ 48 — a `(slot, dimension)` address can hold several rules, and nothing distinguishes a deliberate menu from a silent corruption

*Status: CLOSED 25 AUG 2026 · Raised in: From the real solver (WP-2.3, 25 Aug 2026)*

**CLOSED 25 Aug 2026 — 139 to 0, by a targeted rename of 74 rules.** The migration this entry said was outstanding is done. At each of the **27 genuinely conflicted addresses** the quantity used by the most packs keeps the original `dimension` and the divergent ones take their own `quantity` as their dimension — so BOTH meanings survive at their own address instead of one being set aside by precedence. 74 rules moved, not the 246 a rename-everything-at-a-conflicted-address pass would have touched, and not the 483 a make-dimension-equal-quantity-everywhere pass would have.
**`build/elevation.py` needed four lookups updated** and they are the evidence the rename was worth doing, because each was disambiguating by note substring what a dimension should have been saying: the entrance-composition cap was asking `door_surround`/`width` and meaning the whole composition's maximum width rather than an architrave's face; both belt-course lookups were asking `belt_course`/`height` with `note_substr="ordinary case"` and meaning the band's own depth rather than its height above the floor; the bay count was asking `window_grouping_rule`/`count` with `note_substr="bay count"` among five quantities. All four now name the quantity and no longer depend on prose matching.
**`check_addresses.py` reports 0 collisions and 14 could-not-judge**, and `--strict` has nothing left to fail on. The pinned test now protects zero rather than 139.
*Original entry, and the measurement that produced it, follow.*<br><br>**PARTLY CLOSED — the mechanism is built, the corruption is visible and counted, and the fix is a migration that is NOT done.** Ruled as proposal (a): an optional `quantity` string on each rule naming what it MEASURES, so `(target_slot, dimension, quantity)` is the real address and a checker compares meanings instead of guessing.
**All 74 colliding addresses were read, one at a time, and 483 of the 490 rules on them annotated.** Seven were left without a `quantity` on purpose, because the note did not say plainly enough what the rule measured and inventing one would be the laundering this corpus forbids. `build/check_addresses.py` reports those as **could-not-evaluate**, separately from agreement.
**The real number is 139, not the ~20 the earlier 5% rate predicted.** That estimate came from adjudicating only the 63 pairs involving a WP-4.6 pack; reading all 442 co-binding pairs found **139 addresses where two packs that bind the same node measure different quantities**. A seven-fold under-estimate, and the seventh time in this work package that a measurement was wrong once it was read.

**Two defects in the resolver were found on the way and both are fixed.** `quantity` did not survive `proportion_engine.evaluate`, so the first version of the grouping silently did nothing — the same class of failure the field exists to catch, occurring inside the fix for it. And `resolve_kit`'s `by_slot` is keyed on the SLOT alone, so `choose_pack` was comparing a `count` rule against a `width` rule as though they were alternatives; grouping is now by `(dimension, quantity)`.
**What the resolver does now.** Where two packs measure different things at one address, precedence still picks a winner — it has nothing better to go on — but the loser is **named** in `other_quantities` and printed by `resolve_kit --verbose` instead of being discarded in silence. `prairie-school` reports 58 quantities set aside, including its own plate-rail and sill-band heights losing to a chair-rail height from an inherited classical pack.
**The migration is NOT done and should be its own package.** Fixing 139 means either renaming dimensions across ~50 packs — touching every binding note and test that references them — or teaching every consumer to key on the quantity. `check_addresses.py` runs in `check_all` and REPORTS rather than failing, deliberately: a known, counted, documented backlog must not block all work, and what actually protects the corpus is the **pinned count of 139 in `tests/test_wp46_packs.py`**, which fails the moment a new pack adds a 140th.
*Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — a `(slot, dimension)` address can hold several rules, and nothing distinguishes a deliberate menu from a silent corruption.** Found by `facade-arcade` and then measured properly by `facade-gable`, both in WP-4.6. **1,922 collision instances across the corpus**, on 75 distinct addresses, in 622 distinct pack-pairs.
**The first measurement was misleading and reading it changed the question.** The largest entries are packs colliding with THEMSELVES — `room-harmonic` writes `room_adjacency_overrides`/`width` **ten times** and `ceiling_height_rule`/`height` seven times, `storey-graduation` writes `height_proportion`/`ratio` four times, `sash-light` writes `window_lite_pattern`/`count` four times. Those are not defects. They are a **menu** at one address, authored deliberately and labelled as such in their own notes: "SHAPE 1 OF 7 — THE ROUND ROOM", "METHOD 1 OF 3 — THE ARITHMETIC MEAN", "THIRD STOREY OVER SECOND". A pack offering seven harmonic room shapes or three ways to derive a vaulted height is doing exactly what a proportioning system should. **A naive uniqueness check would flag 1,710 correct rules**, which is why this entry exists instead of a checker.
**So there are three cases and only one is wrong.** (i) Several rules at one address WITHIN a pack: a menu, correct, and the notes say so. (ii) Rules at one address from two packs meaning the SAME quantity: alternatives, and precedence is exactly the mechanism for choosing — `facade-arcade` and `moorish-arch` both give an arch's rise and that is right. (iii) Two packs meaning **different** quantities at one address: a corruption, because whichever resolves last wins and nothing reports it, and precedence cannot help because precedence decides which of two accounts of the same quantity to believe. Four instances of (iii) were found and fixed in WP-4.6 — `porch_support`/`height` meaning both an impost block's height and a springing line; `gable_treatment`/`height` meaning a finial, a parapet and a gable-end silhouette in three different packs; `ornament_vocabulary`/`count` meaning a cusped motif set and a shaped gable's curve count; `rake_condition`/`height` meaning a bargeboard's depth and a crow-step's rise.
**UPDATED 25 Aug 2026, and the proportion matters.** All 144 cross-pack pairs involving a pack written in WP-4.6 were adjudicated by hand, and **eight** were case (iii) — about **five per cent**. So the mechanism is mostly working, and the alarm the first measurement raised was mostly the menus. The eight: `porch_support`/`height` (an impost block's height against a springing line 84 inches higher); `gable_treatment`/`height` (a finial, a parapet and a gable-end silhouette, in three packs); `ornament_vocabulary`/`count` (a cusped motif set against a shaped gable's curve count); `rake_condition`/`height` (a bargeboard's depth against a crow-step's rise); `rake_condition`/`width` (a crow-step's tread against a bargeboard's stock thickness); `frieze`/`height` (a classical entablature frieze against a suspended spindle valance); `cornice`/`projection` (a cornice moulding against a bracketed eave's overhang); and `eave_condition`/`height` (an eave's height above ground against a verge's overhang depth) — the last on **ten nodes**, and the one that would most easily have shipped silently.
**A checker was considered and deliberately not built; an authoring aid was.** `build/pack_addresses.py` reports the addresses a named pack shares with packs it co-binds with, and prints both notes side by side so the author can adjudicate. It is not in `check_all` because only a human can tell (ii) from (iii) — the difference is what the rule MEANS — and a build check carrying a 144-entry allowlist that had to be re-adjudicated on every new pack would be a great deal of ceremony for a five per cent hit rate. Run it when you write or bind a pack. It caught six of the eight above.
**What is not known is how many of (iii) are in the 453 pre-existing pairs**, and finding out means reading them and deciding what each means — which is the work, and it is a package rather than a check. The concrete proposals, so the next person does not re-derive them: (a) give a rule an optional `quantity` string naming what it measures, so `(slot, dimension, quantity)` is the real address and a checker can compare quantities rather than guessing; (b) or accept named dimensions as the convention, which is what WP-4.6 fell into — `upper_ratio`, `springing`, `end_ratio`, `facade_ratio`, `parapet_height`, `curve_count`, `step_rise` — and write a checker that requires any address shared by two packs bound to one node to be declared in an allowlist with a reason. (a) is cleaner and costs a schema field; (b) is free and pushes the problem into naming discipline, which is where it has been failing.
