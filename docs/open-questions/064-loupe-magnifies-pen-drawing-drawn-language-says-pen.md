# OQ 64 — the loupe magnifies the pen with the drawing, and the Drawn Language says a pen is a pen

*Status: OPEN · Raised in: From the workbench legibility pass (26 August 2026)*

**OPEN — the loupe magnifies the pen with the drawing, and the Drawn Language says a pen is a pen.** Raised 26 Aug 2026 by the work in `docs/reports/workbench-legibility-pass.md`, and by the fix rather than by the bug. Every sheet in this instrument declares `vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke"` and `sheet/Sheet.jsx`'s own header states the reason: *"strokes are px and non-scaling — a pen is a pen."* A cut line is 2.6 px at any scale because a cut line is a weight, not a width, and the weights are the grammar — cut, profile, medium, fine, hairline. `components/PlateViewer.jsx` magnifies by a CSS transform on the whole plate, which scales the strokes with everything else: at 400% the cut line is a 10 px band and the hairline is as heavy as the medium line was. Nothing is *wrong* on the plate — the ratios between the five weights are preserved exactly — but the sheet at 400% is no longer set in the standard's line weights, and a reader judging poché against partition against grid is reading a ladder that has been stretched. The alternative was built and rejected for this pass, and it is the fork: **magnify by the `viewBox` instead**, which holds every non-scaling stroke at its declared pixel weight while the drawing grows around it. That is the drafting-correct answer and it has two costs, both real — a hairline at 400% is a wisp with a great deal of paper around it, which reads as a fainter drawing rather than a nearer one; and it works only for the SVG, so the plate's title block and caption would stay at their original size while the drawing inside them grew, which is the thing this frame was built to avoid (a plate is a sheet, and a sheet moves under a loupe whole). A third option nobody has costed: transform-scale the plate as now, and counter-scale the stroke widths by the same factor so the weights hold while the type and the geometry grow together — correct in both registers, and it means every stroke width in the sheet becoming a computed value, which is a change to how the drawing is written rather than to how it is shown. **What is not in question:** the magnification itself. Before it, the dimension figures on both the order plate and the plan sheet could not be read at any size the layout offered, which is what was reported. This entry is about what happens to the LINE while they are being read.
