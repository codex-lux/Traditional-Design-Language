# The image layer

Half the value of a good/bad pair is that both look like real houses somebody actually built. So the image format is deliberately open — a record can be satisfied by a sketch, a HABS photograph, a commissioned render, an owner's snapshot, or a drawing generated by the proportion engine. The **record** carries the meaning; the file is swappable.

## Records are authored before the images exist

A `wanted` asset has no file. It has a caption, a `shot_spec`, and `alt_text` written so a model with no access to the file can reason from it. This does three things:

- the gap is **visible** rather than silent
- the manifest **is** the shot list — hand it to a photographer or a renderer
- an agent can **use the record now**, because the alt text is the machine-readable form of the picture

`assets/manifest.json` currently holds **322 records — 311 wanted and 11 sourced, 150 good/bad pairs, 23 critical.** Every one was generated from the data rather than dreamed up:

| Source | Becomes |
|---|---|
| a `forbidden` variant in a kit | an incorrect/correct **pair**, framed identically so the two images differ only in the thing being taught |
| an `invented: true` slot | a **diagram**, because there is no precedent to photograph |
| a `code_conflict` | a **comparison** of the period dimension against the code minimum, drawn at one scale and overlaid |
| a proportion pack assembly | a **measured detail**, generated by the engine with its inputs recorded in `generated_from` |

The Georgian kit alone produced 117 forbidden variants. That is where most of the pairs come from, and it is why the fault layer and the image layer are the same piece of work approached from two directions.

## Provenance is mandatory

`license` may never ship as `unknown`. The best free source for correct examples is HABS/HAER — Historic American Buildings Survey material is public domain, professionally measured, and already tied to specific buildings and survey numbers, so `provenance.habs_number` is a first-class field.

For incorrect examples the supply problem is inverted: they are in every subdivision, and the constraint is permission rather than availability.

## Generated diagrams

Where an image is a render of a rule rather than a picture of a building, `generated_from` records the pack, the module, and the engine version. The drawing can be regenerated exactly, and the drawing and the data cannot silently disagree.

That is why the engine was built before the image layer, and not the other way round.
