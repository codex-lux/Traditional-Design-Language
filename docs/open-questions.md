# Open questions

Decisions deferred rather than made quietly. Two of these came from authoring the Georgian kit end to end, which was the point of doing it.

Status legend: **OPEN** — awaiting a ruling. **RESOLVED** — ruled, with the ruling recorded inline and a pointer to where it took effect.

## From the taxonomy

1. **RESOLVED — Should the 1877 sketching-tour styles cascade?** Colonial Revival is typed `references` toward Georgian because its transmission is photography and measured drawings. Shingle Style and Queen Anne Free Classic reach early New England building by exactly the same mechanism — the McKim, Mead, White and Bigelow tour of Marblehead, Salem, Newburyport and Portsmouth — and are typed `descends_from`, so they inherit seventeenth-century assembly. Those offices also employed carpenters who still knew the work, which is the counter-argument. **Ruling: apply the `references` reading to all three** — transmission by photography and measured drawing, however skilled the office, is not the same mechanism as a trade lineage, and the model should not blur that distinction for three styles just because the drawings were good. Reflected in `styles/colonial-revival.json`, `styles/shingle-style.json`, `styles/queen-anne-free-classic.json`.

2. **RESOLVED — Is `charleston-single-house` a style or a plan type?** It is a variant of Federal, its origin precedes Federal by sixty years, its floruit runs forty years past Federal's decline, and there is already a `charleston-single` massing. **Ruling: style, under `american-folk-vernacular`** (folk-vernacular family) rather than a Federal variant or a massing-only concept — the piazza orientation constraint, the single-room-deep plan, and the party-wall condition are style facts, not merely volumetric ones, and a massing alone cannot carry `piazza_bearing_deg` as an enforceable constraint. Reflected in `styles/charleston-single-house.json` (`member_of`/`rank`).

## From the proportion layer

7. **OPEN — Palladio's Tuscan entablature is not dimensioned in the text** and the plate numerals are illegible in every reachable scan. Carried as a judgment rule with a range rather than invented members.

8. **OPEN — Chambers's individual mouldings for Ionic, Corinthian and Composite** are engraved minute figures that every OCR renders as noise. They inherit from Vignola rather than being guessed. First thing to correct against a good facsimile.

Encoded as two fascias on that authority, with a three-fascia reading recorded as defensible.

10. **OPEN — Benjamin's 1806 first edition** could not be reached in a legible scan. The packs encode the 1816 third edition and the 1830 *Practical House Carpenter*, and whether the 1806 column heights match is marked unverified rather than assumed.

22. **RESOLVED — No date-conditional resolution.** `applies_when.date_range` existed and was populated (49 dated variant records corpus-wide as of WP-1.3), but nothing selected on it. ## From migrating the remaining families (WP-1.1, 23 Aug 2026)

28. **FIXED 24 Aug 2026 — `build/modcache.py`, one process-wide cache keyed by real path; every local `_mod()`/`_load()` now delegates to it. ## From the garage (WP-4.3, 24 Aug 2026)

31. **RULED 24 Aug 2026 — a room-level opt-out.** Option (b): a `daylight.depth_governs: false` flag on the room record. It keeps the decision in the data where this corpus keeps such things, is explicit per room rather than sweeping a whole function_class, and gives the corpus its first vocabulary for *judged, where the judgment does not apply* — which is what this question turned out to be about. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — the daylight-depth rule is unsatisfiable for a garage, and probably for every non-habitable room.** The validator checks a room's depth against the reach of its own window head (`depth_multiplier` from the room record, relaxed by half when cross-lit), which is exactly right for a room people sit in and wrong for one cars sit in. A two-car garage is 20 ft × 22 ft at the *shallow* end of `rooms/garage.json`'s own dimension band — a 16 ft car plus clearance — and no arrangement of windows in a 0-10% glazing band reaches its back wall. So every correctly-sized, correctly-glazed garage the composer produces reports one `serious` daylight finding that cannot be designed away, only ignored. The composer states this in its decision log rather than suppressing it, and the garage is deliberately *not* distorted (made shallower than a car, or over-glazed past the room's own band) to silence it. The question is where the fix belongs, and it is a judgment rather than an engineering call: (a) exempt `function_class: "work"` and `"service"` rooms from the depth rule, which is simple but would also stop flagging a genuinely unlit workshop or laundry, both of which people do occupy; (b) let a room record opt out with an explicit `daylight.depth_governs: false`, which keeps the decision in the data where the rest of this corpus keeps such things, at the cost of a schema field; or (c) leave it, on the argument that a finding a human dismisses in one second is cheaper than a rule that quietly stops looking. Related but distinct from the "unjudged is not passed" principle — this is *judged, and the judgment does not apply*, which the corpus has no vocabulary for yet. Surfaced by WP-4.3; nothing was changed on the strength of it.

## From the real solver (WP-2.3, 24 Aug 2026)

32. **RULED 24 Aug 2026 — report it in the geometry report.** `geometry_report` gains an explicit under-band list, the way `build/solver.py`'s `unmet_requirements` already works. The search keeps its freedom to trade a room's size against everything else, which is what a heuristic is for, and stops doing it silently. Deliberately NOT the deeper fix of teaching `plan_check.py` to read `room.geometry`: that would have the critic score the drawn house rather than the declared one, which is a larger and separate change every plan layer would feel. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — the heuristic places rooms below their band and nothing says so.** `build/geometry.py` puts the spec Colonial's dining room at 91 sf against the 122 sf floor of its own catalog band — 26% short — and it does so on every one of the six seeds WP-2.3 tried. It can, because `level_score` charges a flat 12 points for a room under its band and a candidate can win the search while paying it. What makes this hard to see is that the plan *record* still declares 12 × 12: only the placement shrinks the room, and `plan_check.py` never reads `room.geometry` at all, so no layer of the critic is looking at the number that changed. `build/solver.py` refuses to do it — the room minimum is the one requirement it will not trade away, and the refusal is what turns an over-stuffed brief into a named conflict instead of a bad plan — but the heuristic remains the default engine everywhere, so the corpus's usual placement still does this silently. Three fixes suggest themselves and the choice is a judgment: (a) make the heuristic refuse too, rejecting any candidate with a room under its band, which is the most consistent but may leave some plans with no candidate at all and no explanation of why; (b) have `geometry_report` carry an explicit under-band list the way the solver's `unmet_requirements` does, which keeps the search's freedom while ending the silence; or (c) teach `plan_check.py` to read `room.geometry` when it is present, which is the deepest fix — the critic would then score the house that was drawn rather than the house that was declared — and much the largest, since every plan layer currently assumes the record's declared dimensions are the truth. Worth noting that (c) is the only one that would also catch this class of defect from any *future* engine. Surfaced by WP-2.3; nothing was changed on the strength of it.

## From partis to full coverage (WP-4.5, 24 Aug 2026)

33. **CLOSED 24 Aug 2026 — reserved voids are built, in both engines, and building them found three more things.** Option (a) as ruled. An outdoor room whose own record says it sits within the block now takes a rectangle like any other room; the block is sized to hold it; `footprint.area_sf` keeps its meaning as the gross block and `heated_area_sf` states what it heats; the wall between a room and a court is an EXTERIOR wall in the structure pass; the roof pass records the hole rather than spanning it silently; and the drawing hatches it open. Nothing may sit over an unroofed void — charged 40 in `geometry.py`, stated as a hard constraint in `solver.py`, which is the difference between the two engines in one line. A roofed void is deliberately exempt, because a Charleston single's upper piazza sits on its lower one.
    **Three findings from building it, all of which changed the implementation:**
    (i) *Placing a void needed one fact per room that nothing carried* — whether it sits inside the block (a court, a piazza) or is appended at grade (a terrace) — and independently, whether it is roofed. Added as a `void` block on the four outdoor rooms. This is NOT option (b): `function_class` is untouched and nothing was re-typed. A room with no `void` block keeps the pre-ruling behaviour and stays out of placement, because a room nobody has judged must not be silently promoted into the footprint.
    (ii) *On a courtyard massing, `depth_rooms` describes the RANGE, not the block.* `courtyard-full` and `courtyard-u` both read `single-pile`, correctly — the ranges around a court ARE one room deep. Feeding the court's area into a 22 ft depth target produced a **121 x 24.6 ft strip with the patio inside it**: the right area, a shape that is not the diagram, a house nobody could build. The massing's own `footprint` field ('courtyard' has ranges both sides of the void, 'U' one) now sets the block's depth target, and the bay count is re-chosen against `rooms/courtyard.json`'s own 1.0–2.2 proportion band — left to the ordinary growth loops the court came out 16 x 40, in band for area and out of band for the one ratio the room's own record calls the number the diagram turns on.
    (iii) *The heuristic cannot find a ring.* Four thousand candidates put the court in the block's SW corner against two exterior walls every time: isolating one room into the innermost cell of a four-deep nesting is not a thing random guillotine partitions do. A scoring penalty alone would have charged for a defect no candidate could avoid. So `courtyard_slice()` states the ring as a guillotine tree — cut at the court's south and north edges, then cut the middle band at its west and east — and the ordinary search still slices the ranges, which is what it is good at. `solver.py` needed nothing for this: it reads its topology off the heuristic's layout, so it inherited the ring. When the block cannot hold the declared court with ranges deep enough to be rooms, `ring_depth()` returns None, the ordinary slicer runs, and the report says the court is a notch and to read the drawing before believing the plan.
    *Original ruling:* Option (a). Both placement engines carry outdoor rooms as placed and dimensioned *voids* — excluded from the area budget and from the heated envelope, drawn as open. It is the honest answer, and it fixes every porch, piazza and terrace in the corpus rather than only the 19 courtyard nodes that made it visible. It touches both engines, the renderer, and the structure and roof passes that read the footprint, and that cost is accepted. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — the courtyard is a void the geometry engine cannot draw.** `build/geometry.py`'s `prepare_rooms()` and `build/solver.py` both drop every room whose `function_class` is `outdoor` before placement, because an outdoor room is not part of the heated envelope and does not belong in the area budget. That is right for a terrace and wrong for a courtyard: in the 19 nodes whose canonical massing is `courtyard-full` or `courtyard-u`, the court is the thing the house is built around, and a plan that omits it is not a simplification of the diagram but a contradiction of it. The composed courtyard plans therefore score, place and render as a solid block with rooms packed against each other where the void should be, and the drawing — which this corpus insists is a render of the data — shows a house the record does not describe. The problem is pre-existing rather than introduced here (`charleston-single-piazza`'s piazza has the same status today, as does every porch in the catalogue) but WP-4.5 makes it the dominant case rather than an edge one. Three ways out, and the choice is a judgment: (a) let the placement engines carry outdoor rooms as *reserved voids* — placed and dimensioned, excluded from the area budget and from the envelope, drawn as open — which is the honest answer and touches both engines plus the renderer and the structure/roof passes that read the footprint; (b) introduce a `void` function_class distinct from `outdoor`, so a court is handled differently from a terrace, at the cost of a room-schema change and a re-typing pass; or (c) accept that this corpus draws courtyard houses wrong and say so on the drawing. Nothing was changed on the strength of it.

34. **RULED 24 Aug 2026 — add `level_offset_ft`.** `level` stays an integer and keeps its current meaning, driving stacking, vertical alignment and storey heights; an optional `level_offset_ft` sits beside it, defaults to 0, and is read only by the section and the stair check. Every existing parti and every consumer works unchanged. The split-level parti becomes writable once it lands. *Original entry follows.*<br><br>**OPEN — there is no half-storey, so the split-level cannot be expressed.** `schema/parti.schema.json` types `rooms[].level` as an integer and every consumer reads it as a level index — `compose.py` maps `{0: "ground", 1: "upper", -1: "cellar"}`, `geometry.py`'s `prepare_rooms()` keys its working dict by it, `solver.py` inherits that. A split-level Ranch, which `PLAN-OF-ACTION.md`'s WP-4.5 text names among the partis to add, is a house whose defining property is that two of its floors are half a storey apart, and there is nowhere in the record to say so. 35. **RULED 24 Aug 2026 — let adjacency rules declare a vertical relation.** Add `relation: "above"` / `"below"` to adjacency rules and teach `plan_check.py` to evaluate them across levels. `schema/parti.schema.json`'s own `adjacencies` array already anticipates exactly this and `five-part-palladian` already uses it for its stair. First: `rooms/overlook.json`'s defining relationship is that it is open to the hall on the storey *below* it, and `plan_check.py` evaluates adjacency within a level, so a `should_adjoin` written for that relationship can never pass on a correct plan — it reported `serious` on the one arrangement the room exists for, and was held at `preferred` rather than `strong` to stop a right answer being called a defect. Second: all thirteen public and living rooms in the catalogue carry a ground-floor adjacency rule — `drawing-room` must reach a dining room, `parlor`/`best-parlor`/`morning-room` an entrance-hall, `sitting-room` a parlor, `hall` a cross-passage — so the great chamber of a Tudor H-plan, which is the best room in the house and sits over the parlour, can satisfy none of them; typed `drawing-room`, which is what it most nearly is, the plan trips a **fatal** for not reaching a dining room a storey beneath it. ## Ruled with the rest, 24 August 2026

36. **RULED 24 Aug 2026 — spend the schema change: a slot-scope allowlist on `hybridizes_with`.** The problem, found in WP-4.2 and patched twenty-six times at the node rather than once at the mechanism: an edge drawn to carry one narrow aspect of a donor's practice transmits that donor's *entire* kit, because the cascade cannot partition a donor's bindings by which aspect the edge was drawn for. Greek Revival's hybrid edges bring Greek Revival's whole cornice apparatus with them whether or not the edge was about cornices. The ruling is to add an optional slot-scope allowlist to the edge, so `hybridizes_with` carries only the slots it was drawn for and carries everything only when no allowlist is given. That fixes the class rather than the instances, makes the twenty-six node-level patches removable one by one as each edge gains its scope, and — the reason it is worth a schema version — stops the next kit author rediscovering the same thing from scratch. The alternative considered and rejected was narrowing `hybridizes_with` to carry no kit content at all, like `references`: that would be cheaper but would lose the genuine partial inheritance the edge exists to express, and the corpus does have edges that legitimately transmit several slots. Costs a schema bump and a re-resolve of every kit with a hybrid edge in its chain.

37. **OPEN — five of twenty-one partis carry fatal findings against their own native style, so the composer cannot recommend them.** Measured 24 Aug 2026, each parti instantiated against the first style in its own `styles` array and run through `repair` and `reclaim`: `cape-central-chimney` 4 fatal, `five-part-palladian` 4, `charleston-single-piazza` 2, `creole-gallery` 2, `ranch-tripartite` 1. The other sixteen are clean. A fatal is 100 points and nativity is worth at most 140, so a Charleston-single-house brief gets `centre-passage-single-pile` and a Creole brief gets `bungalow-open-linear` — the composer working exactly as designed on data that is wrong. The failures are door-graph facts inside the parti files, not scoring: `charleston-single-piazza` has no door from its drawing room to its dining room and none from its upper hall to its stair hall. **This is not the borrowed-diagram problem WP-4.5 fixed** — those styles now HAVE a native parti and it is the parti itself that fails. Pre-existing and unrelated to OQ 33 (verified against the pre-OQ-33 tree). `build/check_partis.py` checks the door graph resolves; it does not check that a diagram satisfies the room-adjacency rules `plan_check.py` will hold it to, and that check is what would have caught all five at authoring time. The question is whether fixing the five and adding that check is its own package or belongs to WP-4.6.

38. **OPEN — the roof over a court is stated but not derived.** `build/roof.py` computes one ridge over one rectangle. Over an open court it does not: the real roof is ranges around the void, each with its own eave, meeting in valleys, and in the Spanish and Mediterranean cases falling inward to drain to the court. OQ 33 put the hole in the record with its own dimensions and labelled every height figure as computed for a roof that spans the whole block — which over the court it does not — so nothing is silently wrong. But `grade_to_ridge_ft` for a courtyard house is now a number that is explicitly not describing the building. The same is true, less visibly, of `elevation.py`'s silhouette. Deriving ranged roof geometry is a real package (valley lines, four eaves, the inward-pitch case) and it should be ruled whether it belongs to Phase 5 or is left standing as a disclosure the way the wing/hyphen simplification already is.

39. **OPEN — a portal range is one rectangle, and a portal range is not one rectangle.** `partis/courtyard-and-portal.json` gives the covered walk a single room record (`walk`, a `loggia`), and the parti's own note says that walk IS the house's entire circulation: "every room is entered from it and from nothing else." A continuous roofed edge around four sides of a court cannot be one rectangle, so the placement engines put it along one side and the other three ranges are entered from nothing. The plan validator does not catch it because the door graph is satisfied by the record, not by the geometry — `plan_check.py` never reads `room.geometry` (the same blind spot OQ 32 named). Three ways out and none of them is obviously right: give the parti four loggia records and lose the fact that it is one room; let a room carry more than one rectangle, which is a schema change with reach into every consumer; or accept it and say so on the drawing. Raised rather than decided, because the first option changes what a room IS in this corpus.
