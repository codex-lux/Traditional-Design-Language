## From the taxonomy

1. **RESOLVED — Should the 1877 sketching-tour styles cascade?** Colonial Revival is typed `references` toward Georgian because its transmission is photography and measured drawings. Shingle Style and Queen Anne Free Classic reach early New England building by exactly the same mechanism — the McKim, Mead, White and Bigelow tour of Marblehead, Salem, Newburyport and Portsmouth — and are typed `descends_from`, so they inherit seventeenth-century assembly. Those offices also employed carpenters who still knew the work, which is the counter-argument. **Ruling: apply the `references` reading to all three** — transmission by photography and measured drawing, however skilled the office, is not the same mechanism as a trade lineage, and the model should not blur that distinction for three styles just because the drawings were good. Reflected in `styles/colonial-revival.json`, `styles/shingle-style.json`, `styles/queen-anne-free-classic.json`.

2. **RESOLVED — Is `charleston-single-house` a style or a plan type?** It is a variant of Federal, its origin precedes Federal by sixty years, its floruit runs forty years past Federal's decline, and there is already a `charleston-single` massing. **Ruling: style, under `american-folk-vernacular`** (folk-vernacular family) rather than a Federal variant or a massing-only concept — the piazza orientation constraint, the single-room-deep plan, and the party-wall condition are style facts, not merely volumetric ones, and a massing alone cannot carry `piazza_bearing_deg` as an enforceable constraint. Reflected in `styles/charleston-single-house.json` (`member_of`/`rank`).

## From the proportion layer

**7 through 11 are ENVIRONMENT-BLOCKED, not unstarted (recorded 25 Aug 2026).** Every one of them is
the same shape: the text does not give the figure, or the plate numerals are illegible, and closing
it needs a better facsimile rather than more code or more thought. They are therefore blocked on
exactly what WP-4.4 is blocked on. Probed on 25 Aug 2026 from this environment:

```
https://www.loc.gov            -> connection failed (000)
https://archive.org            -> connection failed (000)
https://babel.hathitrust.org   -> connection failed (000)
```

Not a 403 from the proxy as WP-4.4 gets, but a failure to connect at all. Until outbound HTTPS to a
facsimile host is available these five cannot move, and the correct handling of each is what has
already been done: the figure is carried as a judgment rule with a range, or inherited from a source
that does state it, and marked unverified rather than assumed. **Do not close any of them from a
secondary source or a modern redrawing** -- that is precisely how a guess gets laundered as
`measured`, which this corpus forbids. Each entry below says what specifically to look for, so a
person with library access can close them in an afternoon.

7. **OPEN — Palladio's Tuscan entablature is not dimensioned in the text** and the plate numerals are illegible in every reachable scan. Carried as a judgment rule with a range rather than invented members.

Encoded as two fascias on that authority, with a three-fascia reading recorded as defensible.

    The concrete proposal, so the next person does not re-derive it: add `expressed_frame` to the envelope group, holding face width, spacing, projection from the cladding plane, brace geometry, and — the field that would do the most work — whether the member is structural, structural-and-expressed, or applied. That last is the distinction `styles/stick-style.json` and `styles/tudor-revival.json` both turn on and that no slot currently records, so the corpus cannot presently tell a frame from a picture of one. Cost is an ontology bump and a migration of four packs' bindings; the alternative is that every future timber tradition on WP-4.1's list — the Alpine bracket work, the jetty module, the Fachwerk ornament — routes through `corner_board` too and the compromise becomes the convention.

48. **OPEN — a `(slot, dimension)` address can hold several rules, and nothing distinguishes a deliberate menu from a silent corruption.** Found by `facade-arcade` and then measured properly by `facade-gable`, both in WP-4.6. **1,922 collision instances across the corpus**, on 75 distinct addresses, in 622 distinct pack-pairs.
    **The first measurement was misleading and reading it changed the question.** The largest entries are packs colliding with THEMSELVES — `room-harmonic` writes `room_adjacency_overrides`/`width` **ten times** and `ceiling_height_rule`/`height` seven times, `storey-graduation` writes `height_proportion`/`ratio` four times, `sash-light` writes `window_lite_pattern`/`count` four times. Those are not defects. They are a **menu** at one address, authored deliberately and labelled as such in their own notes: "SHAPE 1 OF 7 — THE ROUND ROOM", "METHOD 1 OF 3 — THE ARITHMETIC MEAN", "THIRD STOREY OVER SECOND". A pack offering seven harmonic room shapes or three ways to derive a vaulted height is doing exactly what a proportioning system should. **A naive uniqueness check would flag 1,710 correct rules**, which is why this entry exists instead of a checker.
    **So there are three cases and only one is wrong.** (i) Several rules at one address WITHIN a pack: a menu, correct, and the notes say so. (ii) Rules at one address from two packs meaning the SAME quantity: alternatives, and precedence is exactly the mechanism for choosing — `facade-arcade` and `moorish-arch` both give an arch's rise and that is right. (iii) Two packs meaning **different** quantities at one address: a corruption, because whichever resolves last wins and nothing reports it, and precedence cannot help because precedence decides which of two accounts of the same quantity to believe. Four instances of (iii) were found and fixed in WP-4.6 — `porch_support`/`height` meaning both an impost block's height and a springing line; `gable_treatment`/`height` meaning a finial, a parapet and a gable-end silhouette in three different packs; `ornament_vocabulary`/`count` meaning a cusped motif set and a shaped gable's curve count; `rake_condition`/`height` meaning a bargeboard's depth and a crow-step's rise.
    **UPDATED 25 Aug 2026, and the proportion matters.** All 144 cross-pack pairs involving a pack written in WP-4.6 were adjudicated by hand, and **eight** were case (iii) — about **five per cent**. So the mechanism is mostly working, and the alarm the first measurement raised was mostly the menus. The eight: `porch_support`/`height` (an impost block's height against a springing line 84 inches higher); `gable_treatment`/`height` (a finial, a parapet and a gable-end silhouette, in three packs); `ornament_vocabulary`/`count` (a cusped motif set against a shaped gable's curve count); `rake_condition`/`height` (a bargeboard's depth against a crow-step's rise); `rake_condition`/`width` (a crow-step's tread against a bargeboard's stock thickness); `frieze`/`height` (a classical entablature frieze against a suspended spindle valance); `cornice`/`projection` (a cornice moulding against a bracketed eave's overhang); and `eave_condition`/`height` (an eave's height above ground against a verge's overhang depth) — the last on **ten nodes**, and the one that would most easily have shipped silently.
    **A checker was considered and deliberately not built; an authoring aid was.** `build/pack_addresses.py` reports the addresses a named pack shares with packs it co-binds with, and prints both notes side by side so the author can adjudicate. It is not in `check_all` because only a human can tell (ii) from (iii) — the difference is what the rule MEANS — and a build check carrying a 144-entry allowlist that had to be re-adjudicated on every new pack would be a great deal of ceremony for a five per cent hit rate. Run it when you write or bind a pack. It caught six of the eight above.
    **What is not known is how many of (iii) are in the 453 pre-existing pairs**, and finding out means reading them and deciding what each means — which is the work, and it is a package rather than a check. The concrete proposals, so the next person does not re-derive them: (a) give a rule an optional `quantity` string naming what it measures, so `(slot, dimension, quantity)` is the real address and a checker can compare quantities rather than guessing; (b) or accept named dimensions as the convention, which is what WP-4.6 fell into — `upper_ratio`, `springing`, `end_ratio`, `facade_ratio`, `parapet_height`, `curve_count`, `step_rise` — and write a checker that requires any address shared by two packs bound to one node to be declared in an allowlist with a reason. (a) is cleaner and costs a schema field; (b) is free and pushes the problem into naming discipline, which is where it has been failing.

49. **OPEN — a node can need one rule of a pack without being an instance of the pack's type, and the binding model cannot say so.** Found while binding `jetty-overhang` for WP-4.6. `styles/french-normandy-revival.json` states one of that pack's rules almost verbatim — a vertical change of wall material must occur "at a floor line, a water table, or a jetty, with a minimum 4 in. horizontal offset" — and `styles/tudor-revival.json` states the same rule in nearly the same words. Tudor Revival also jetties, so it is bound and gets the rule. French Normandy Revival does **not** jetty: it names a jetty as one permitted datum for something else. It is a CONSUMER of one rule and not an instance of the type, and binding a jetty module to it would make the corpus assert a framed overhang on a style that does not have one — a false claim about the building, made in order to deliver a true rule about a detail.
    **The workaround available today is worse than the gap.** `role: optional` still says the pack applies; a note saying "bound only for `material_change_rule`" is prose that no resolver reads, and `build/resolve_kit.py` would hand the node the whole pack's fifteen rules including a jetty projection. That is the same class of problem as `hybridizes_with` transmitting a donor's entire kit (the trap CLAUDE.md already records and OQ 36 partly closed for hybrids), arriving now through bindings instead of through lineage.
    **It is not a one-off.** The same shape is visible wherever a pack owns a rule about a device rather than the device itself: `storey-graduation`'s dormer rules are wanted by styles with no graduated storeys, `facade-arcade`'s springing rule by styles with one arch and no arcade, `opening-mullioned`'s light-count quantisation by styles with a single fixed light. Each is currently either bound whole, or left unserved and the rule restated in the node — and restating it in the node is exactly the duplication this library exists to remove.
    The concrete proposal, so the next person does not re-derive it: give a binding an optional `slots` array scoping which target slots it may contribute — `{"pack": "jetty-overhang", "role": "optional", "slots": ["material_change_rule"]}` — which is the same mechanism OQ 36 built for `hybridizes_with`, applied one layer out, and would reuse its checker shape. Cost is a schema field on `proportion_packs[]` and a filter in the resolver. The alternative, and it is defensible, is to say that a rule wanted by nodes outside its pack's type does not belong in that pack at all and should be lifted into a small shared one — which is cleaner in principle and would mean deciding, for each such rule, whether the device or the rule is the real subject.
