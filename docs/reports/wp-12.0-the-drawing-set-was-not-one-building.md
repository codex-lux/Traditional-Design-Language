# WP-12.0 — The drawing set was not one building, and the face that would have shown it could not be asked for

*8 September 2026. Not a package of the Phase 12 PRD: it was added by the review that adopted
that PRD (`docs/prd/phase-12-the-sheet-in-the-round.md` §0.1, finding 3), and it is first
because the PRD's §5.4 holds the coming 3D scene to the 2D plates by asserting they agree, and
on one of the two shipped plans they did not.*

---

## The finding

**WP-6.4 wrote the rule down — *"one drawing set is one building or it is nothing"* — moved
three of the five sheets onto one placement, and left two behind. Nobody noticed for twelve
days.**

`corpus.drawing` routes `plan`, `section` and `bearing` through `_placed`: one placement per
set, on the proving engine, with the input's own digest taken before the solve. The
`elevation` and `roof` branches called `build_elevation(plan, pt)` and `build_roof(plan, pt)`
**with no section**, and both then built one for themselves through
`structure.build_section(plan, parti)` — whose engine default is the hill-climb and whose own
comment says, in as many words, that the default is *"for INTERNAL callers ONLY"*
(`plan_check`'s elevation layer, the composer's scoring loop, where a 25-second CP solve per
candidate is not affordable). A user-facing sheet is not one of those. The DXF export path
repeated it in two more branches.

The elevation reads placement. So wherever the set's own solve reached a proof, the elevation
plate was a drawing of a different house from the plan plate beside it.

## What it actually drew

Measured before anything was changed, on both shipped plans, `engine="auto"`:

| | `spec-builder-colonial` | `tidewater-georgian-careful` |
|---|---|---|
| the set's placement | **cp-sat**, 50.0 × 31.0 ft | heuristic (budget fallback), 63 × 38.17 ft |
| footprint the elevation was built on | 51.33 × 32.08 | 65.58 × 40.75 |
| footprint it should have been built on | 51.33 × **32.33** | 65.58 × 40.75 — the same |
| elevation measurements that differ | **2** of 172 | 0 of 184 |
| `porch_clear_depth_ft` | **4.75 → 4.00** | unmoved |
| faces S, N (drawn on the WIDTH) | identical | identical |
| faces E, W (drawn on the DEPTH) | **DIFFER** | identical |

**Two things in that table are the report.**

**First: the measurement that moved is the one the Four-Foot Porch reads.**
`porch_clear_depth_ft` is what `porch-too-shallow-to-inhabit` is tested on — this project's own
emblematic fault, and the type specimen `VISION.md` names in as many words: *"The four-foot
porch is the type specimen."* The elevation record was publishing **4.75 ft for a porch the
plan beside it drew at 4.00** — three quarters of a foot in the
flattering direction, on the fault the whole corpus exists to be able to name. That is the OQ 52
family exactly: not a fabricated measurement, but a real one taken of the wrong building.
*(It reaches the fault corpus by a different road — `plan_check`'s elevation layer builds its
own section, deliberately and correctly, on the heuristic — so no published fault verdict is
wrong today. What was wrong was the drawing set's own record of the house it was drawing.)*

**Second: the one face that would have shown it is the one face nobody could ask for.** The
front is drawn on the width and the width did not move; the gable ends are drawn on the depth
and they did. And `DrawingSet.jsx` had never sent a `face` — so of the four elevations this
system has been able to draw since WP-3.2, a reader could only ever see one, and it was the one
the defect could not reach. Two defects wearing one symptom, which is
`docs/reports/wp-5.7-the-atlas-and-the-shell.md`'s shape in a new place.

**And on the Tidewater plan nothing moves at all**, because `auto` spends its 25-second budget
there and falls back to the same hill-climb the elevation was already using, so the two
placements coincide. A sweep of one shipped plan would have found nothing. That is WP-8.6's
rule — *verifying a corpus-wide change on the plans that happen to ship is verifying it on 2 of
164 styles* — met at a scale of two rather than one, and it is why the guard below skips with a
named reason rather than passing when the proof is unavailable.

## What was built

- **`workbench/server/corpus.py`** — the `elevation` and `roof` branches of `drawing()` take
  `_placed`'s placement, build the section from it, and hand that section to `build_roof` and
  `build_elevation`. The `roof` and `elevation` branches of `export_cad()` do the same — its
  `section` branch already did, which is what made the omission legible — so a downloaded DXF is
  a sheet of the house on screen, WP-6.4's own reason for existing.
- **Both plates now carry `solver`** — the engine and `drawn_by.input_digest` the plan sheet
  has carried since WP-11.8. Two plates of "the same house" that disagree can now be told apart
  by their input, which is finding J6 of the 4 September diagnosis.
- **`api.drawing(kind, plan, opts)`** in `workbench/app/src/api/client.js`. The Drawing Set was
  the last surface calling `fetch` directly and therefore the last one outside
  `noteUnauthorized`: a session that expired while a reader was on it threw instead of showing
  the password screen.
- **Face chips on the Drawing Set**, `role="radio"` like the sheet chips, in compass order
  (S · N · E · W, `elevation.py`'s own `FACES` tuple), shown only for the elevation. Which face
  is the *front* is a fact the record states, so the chip says the compass point and the label
  says the role — WP-11.9's rule for the plan's north, one field over. The sheet chip is
  `elevation` now rather than `front elevation`, because it names the kind and would otherwise
  contradict a reader who has just asked for the north.
- **No client-visible default changed.** Sending no face still draws the entrance front, which
  is the server's own `face or elev["entrance_face"]`, and a test pins it.

## The cost, measured rather than assumed

The obvious objection is that two more `_placed` calls per drawing set means two more
25-second CP solves. **Measured: they are free.** `geometry._SOLVE_CACHE` is keyed on
`(plan, parti, candidates, seed, engine, time_limit)`, so within one warm server process the
second and third placements of one record return in **0.00 s** against the first call's 25.70 s.
The elevation and roof plates join the solve the plan sheet already paid for. Only the first
plate of a new record pays, exactly as before.

## What was found on the way, and it is about the tests rather than the code

**The first version of this file's guards passed with the fix reverted, and the harness's own
landed-assertion is what exposed it.** They asserted that every plate reports the same
`solver.drawn_by.input_digest` and the same engine — and the metadata is read from `_placed`
*whatever the plate was built on*, so reverting the `section=` argument left every assertion
green. **A test of what a plate REPORTS is not a test of what it was DRAWN from.** The guards
now compare the route's own SVG against one built the pre-WP-12.0 way, which is a comparison of
two drawings.

Three further things came out of mutating this package rather than reading it:

1. **A 12-space anchor matches inside 20 spaces of indentation.** The first mutation refused
   itself, because `"            elev = EL.build_elevation(…)"` appears twice — once as the
   drawing route's line and once *inside* the DXF path's deeper indent. It refused for a good
   reason and the fix was to anchor on the newline and the following line.
2. **A mutation that does not land reads exactly like a guard that works.** The attempt above
   wrote its edit, failed its own verification, and the suite then ran against an unmutated
   tree and reported seven passes — which would have been recorded as "the guard holds" by
   anyone not asserting the landing. This repository already records the class; it cost two
   runs to meet it again.
3. **The roof needed its own drawn guard.** With only the metadata test, reverting the roof fix
   stayed green for the same reason as (1) above.

**Mutation results, from a green baseline of seven, each verified to have landed by reading the
file back:**

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| elevation built with no section (the pre-WP-12.0 call) | **1 failed** — the east elevation is byte-identical to the stale one |
| roof built with no section | **1 failed** — the roof plan is byte-identical to the stale one |
| `face=face` dropped at `render_elevation` | **1 failed** — four faces gave fewer than four drawings |

## What was deliberately not done

- **`plan_check`'s elevation layer keeps its heuristic section**, which is the documented and
  correct choice: it is a derivation step inside a scoring loop, not a user-facing sheet.
  Changing it would put a CP solve inside every candidate the composer scores.
- **No renderer was touched.** `data-frame` — the attribute the PRD's §5.4 overlay needs — is
  WP-12.4's, and adding it here would be a change with no reader.
- **The face is not in the URL yet.** Phase 12 puts the whole view state in the query string
  (`view=`, `face=`, `ov=`); adding a lone `face=` param here would be a second mechanism to
  retire four packages later.
- **No new open question.** This was a defect with a fix, not a judgment anybody owes.

## Two states of the tree this package did not cause, recorded so nobody attributes them to it

**1. The browser walk is RED on `main` today, and on this branch's base, with the same two
failures — and they are not this package's.** Reproduced here by stashing every workbench
change and rebuilding: byte-identical failures on a clean tree.

```
FAIL no room label leaves the room it names (13 rooms) — Centre Passage —
     8′-7″ × 38′-2″ — the record declares 12′-0″ × 34′-0″: ∗
FAIL upper level: no room label leaves its room (13 rooms) — (the same room)
```

CI agrees: on `main` at `5869012`, all six corpus shards, the aggregate gate, the server tests,
the smoke and the docker build are **green**, and the single failing job is *"Walk every surface
and measure the plates."* The webfont the label fitter measures against is reachable from this
container (`fonts.googleapis.com` answers 200), so this is not the environment. It is the label
fitter meeting a passage drawn 8′-7″ wide carrying a name, a dimension string and a divergence
mark — the `∗` in the message is the third of those. **It is a Sheet concern and a package of its
own**; naming it here is the whole of what WP-12.0 owes it.

**2. `main` has moved past the commit the PRD was written against**, and WP-12.1 must know.
The PRD says `f54c5af`; `main` is now `5869012`, six commits ahead, carrying the merge of the
OTHER Phase 11 — the sheet line — into the precedent-bench line. That merge touches
`structure.py`, `roof.py`, `openings.py`, `plan_check.py` and `export_ifc.py`: **exactly the
modules `build/scene.py` is specified to import.** It also moved both shipped plans' footprints
by its own account. Its change to `corpus.drawing` is additive (a `register` argument for the
plan sheet) and does **not** fix the defect above, so this package stands as written and merges
cleanly with it — but **every figure in the table above was measured on `f54c5af` and is a
statement about that tree.** WP-12.1's first act should be the merge, and its second should be
to re-derive these numbers rather than quote them, which is this project's own rule about what
happens to a figure at exactly a merge.

## ADDENDUM — re-derived on the merged tree, 9 September 2026, and it is bigger than published

*The section above was measured on `f54c5af`. This package's own closing note said `main` had
moved past it and that the figures were a statement about that tree; the merge is done and the
figures are re-derived rather than quoted. **Every number above is left exactly as written**, per
this project's convention, so what was claimed stays legible beside what was true.*

| | `spec-builder-colonial` | `tidewater-georgian-careful` |
|---|---|---|
| the set's placement | cp-sat (unchanged) | **cp-sat — it reached a proof, where on `f54c5af` it fell back on budget** |
| `porch_clear_depth_ft` | **6.00 → 4.00**, was 4.75 → 4.00 | **5.40 → 7.00** |
| elevation measurements that differ | 2 of 172 | **10 of 184**, was 0 |
| faces S, N | identical | **DIFFER**, were identical |
| faces E, W | DIFFER | DIFFER |

**Three claims above are now false of the tree, and the direction is the uncomfortable one —
the defect was WORSE than this package published, not better.**

1. **The error on the Four-Foot Porch is two feet, not three quarters of one.** The elevation
   record published `porch_clear_depth_ft` of **6.00** for a porch the plan drew at **4.00** —
   fifty per cent over, still in the flattering direction, still on the fault the corpus is
   named for. The smaller figure was true of `f54c5af` and is true of nothing now.
2. **"Nothing moves on the Tidewater plan" is false.** It now reaches a CP proof inside its
   budget, so the two placements no longer coincide, and **ten** measurements move — including
   the ridge height (468.36 → 467.64 in), the eave-to-ridge height and the roof plane area
   (1,606.1 → 1,599.4 sf). The elevation was drawing a roof of a different height from the
   section beside it.
3. **"The front is drawn on the width and the width does not move" is false there too.** On the
   Tidewater plan **all four faces differ**. It held on `f54c5af` because that plan fell back to
   the same engine; it holds on `spec-builder-colonial` still, where the width genuinely does
   not move. So the sentence was never a rule — it was a property of one plan on one tree, and
   it read as an explanation.

**The lesson is the one the package was already carrying and had not yet paid.** *A figure is a
statement about the tree it was measured on*, and the tree moved between the measurement and the
merge. Nothing here changed the code: the fix is the same fix and every test passes unchanged
(15 of 15 across this file and `test_m3_drawings.py`). What changed is how much the fix was
worth, and it was worth more.

*(`test_the_front_elevation_is_unmoved_and_that_is_why_nobody_saw_it` still holds, because it is
driven on `spec-builder-colonial`, where the width still does not move. Its NAME now over-claims
— on the merged tree that is a fact about one plan and not about fronts — and the docstring says
so rather than the name being quietly widened.)*
