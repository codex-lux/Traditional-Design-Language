# WP-14.4 — the loop was not short of permission, it was short of time

*Phase 14, package 4. 20 September 2026. Cite this report by filename.*

## The plan's premise was false, and measuring it is what found the real defect

The approved plan said:

> `critique._lever` returns `candidates` unconditionally on a heuristic run, so `prove-it` is
> never offered — on the engine where this corpus measured the refusal rate falling from
> 84-in-182 to 1-in-12. Offer `prove-it` where ortools is importable.

**Measured first, deterministically, on `plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json`:**

    ctx engine=heuristic   ran=heuristic   levers offered: {'search-harder': 38}
    ctx engine=auto        ran=heuristic   levers offered: {'prove-it':      38}

**`prove-it` IS offered on the product path.** `compose()` defaults to `revise_engine="auto"`,
`revise()` puts that straight into `ctx["engine"]`, and `_lever` offers the proof on all 38
placement findings. The premise describes only the heuristic-**by-name** path.

And that path's withholding is not an oversight. It is a ruled refusal spelled in three places,
each with its reason written down:

- `critique._lever` — *"an EXPLICIT request for the search is a request not to spend a proof"*,
  and it honours it at the lever rather than one step later, because the first version let the
  loop ask and `_prove_it` refuse, so every heuristic-by-name run spent its first round on that
  refusal;
- `moves._prove_it` — the same rule at the move, so a caller reaching it another way is refused
  by name;
- `workbench/server/evaluate.py:254` — **THE ENGINE THAT PLACED, NEVER `auto`**, because `auto`
  *"would re-attempt the proof at 25 s inside EVERY round on a record where it has just been
  measured to fall back, which is the reader's whole wait spent re-learning what the solve above
  already reported."*

**Undoing that would put a 25-second proof inside the bench's wall-drag path**, which the
infrastructure audit measured as ~85% of the whole server's evaluate capacity for one person.
The lever stays exactly as it was.

## What the measurement found instead

Chasing why `spec-builder-colonial`'s loop was being offered `prove-it` at all — its first
placement should have proved — turned up the real defect, and it is deterministic:

    build/critique.py    def critique(..., time_limit_s=25.0, ...)
    build/revise.py      def revise(...,   time_limit_s=25.0, ...)
    build/geometry.py    def solve(...,    time_limit_s=BUDGET_BATCH_S)   # 40.0

    workbench/server/evaluate.py:78    time_limit_s=geo.BUDGET_INTERACTIVE_S
    workbench/server/evaluate.py:274   time_limit_s=geo.BUDGET_INTERACTIVE_S
    mcp_server/core.py:1635            time_limit_s=geo.BUDGET_INTERACTIVE_S
    build/compose.py                   — passes nothing —

**WP-11.8 ruled two budgets and gave each its callers.** `BUDGET_BATCH_S` (40 s) is
`geometry.solve`'s own default, for `check_all`, `corpus.drawing()`, the CLI and the reference
plans — *where a proof is worth waiting for*. `BUDGET_INTERACTIVE_S` (25 s) is passed **by name**
by the routes with a person waiting behind a 400 ms debounce.

**A compose is a job. It was getting the interactive number**, because it passed nothing and
`revise()` spelled its own default as the bare literal `25.0` — the ruling written a third and
fourth time, where changing the constant would have moved neither. The composer was on the wrong
side of a ruling by accident of a default.

## What the budget buys, where it buys anything

Through `revise()` on the two shipped plans, `engine="auto"`, two rounds, **one run each**:

| record | budget | first placement | key | applied | **refused** | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `spec-builder-colonial` | 25 s | **heuristic** | `[15,100,97,29] → [6,100,104,29]` | 6 | **1** | 37.0 s |
| `spec-builder-colonial` | 40 s | **cp-sat** | `[4,95,95,27] → [2,95,95,25]` | 2 | **0** | 118.4 s |
| `tidewater-georgian-careful` | 25 s | cp-sat | `[1,60,111,23] → [1,50,105,22]` | 2 | **4** | 190.5 s |
| `tidewater-georgian-careful` | 40 s | cp-sat | `[2,50,100,21] → [2,44,101,23]` | 10 | **0** | 92.5 s |

**Refused rounds 1 → 0 and 4 → 0.** That is the claim WP-14.4 was sent to test — *re-placement
noise, not the move, is refusing these rounds* — answered yes, with the lever never being the
lever. And on the Tidewater plan the larger budget is **faster end to end, 92.5 s against
190.5**, because a proof that CLOSES is cheaper than one that spends its budget and is then
re-attempted by the `prove-it` lever in every later round.

`spec-builder-colonial` is the clean case: at 25 s its first placement falls back to the search
and at 40 s it reaches CP-SAT, so the candidate the loop starts from carries **4 fatals instead
of 15**.

## AND ON THE PRODUCT COMPOSE IT BUYS NOTHING, WHICH IS PUBLISHED RATHER THAN OMITTED

The same call before and after, against a `git archive` checkout of this branch's HEAD,
`briefs/family-georgian.json`, `candidates=2`, `revise_engine="auto"`, one run each:

| | control (25 s) | tree (40 s) |
|---|---|---|
| wall | **153.9 s** | **177.9 s** |
| `side-hall-townhouse` | `[8,42,80,21] → [1,44,84,21]`, cp-sat, applied 7, refused 0 | `[8,43,78,21] → [1,42,85,21]`, cp-sat, applied 5, refused 1 |
| `courtyard-and-portal` | `[3,62,91,22] → [3,61,94,22]`, heuristic | **identical in every field** |
| fatal on the cards | 1 and 3 | 1 and 3 |

**No improvement, and 24 s slower on one run each.** Neither returned candidate's placement sits
at the 25–40 s boundary: the leader reaches CP-SAT at either budget and `courtyard-and-portal`
falls back at either. The `side-hall-townhouse` differences are within CP's own run-to-run
variation, and so, on one run each, is the 24 s.

**That is this repository's own trap — verifying a corpus-wide change on the records that happen
to be in front of you — caught in the other direction.** The `spec-builder-colonial` benefit is
real and reproducible; this brief simply does not exercise it. Publishing the table above without
this one would have been a figure three times its true reach.

**So the change is made on the RULING, not on a benefit**, and the benefit is stated
conditionally: it appears exactly where a record's placement sits between the two budgets, and
nobody has swept how many records that is.

## The costs, named

- **A candidate's placement may take 40 s rather than 25**, and `revise_budget_s` is the SET's,
  shared in rank order — so a candidate further down may not be reached. It is returned unrevised
  and says so, which is existing behaviour and not a new silence.
- **A budget-SKIPPED candidate pays it too**, and deliberately: WP-14.2 places such a candidate so
  its card is of the same kind of house as its siblings', and a set whose revised cards were
  placed at 40 s and whose skipped ones were placed at 25 would be two instruments again — the
  defect WP-14.2 removed, one field over. That placement is outside `revise_budget_s`, which the
  candidate has already been measured not to fit inside.
- **`check_all` pays nothing.** The build runs the composer on the fast engine, where
  `time_limit_s` bounds a CP solve that never happens — measured 0.53 s at 25 and 0.26 s at 40,
  and asserted as a relation rather than as a duration.

## Verification

Five guards in `tests/test_budget_by_name.py`, **all five mutation-checked and all five red**:
the main loop dropping the batch budget, the main loop passing the interactive one instead, the
budget-skip branch dropping it, `revise()`'s default going back to a bare literal, and an
interactive route ceasing to name its own budget.

**The guard reads the AST and every PLACING call site**, because `compose` calls `RV.revise`
three times and a source-text grep would have passed on one of two. **Two mutations were SKIPPED
on the first sweep** — their target string appears twice, once per call site — which is the same
selector fault the guard itself exists to prevent, met in the harness; they were re-cut with
enough context to be unique and both then bit.

**The premise is asserted first**: if the two budgets were ever set equal, every other assertion
in the file would pass while distinguishing nothing.

`tests/test_one_verdict.py` and `tests/test_convicted_instrument.py` 16 of 16.

## What is deliberately not done

- **`critique._lever` and `moves._prove_it` are UNCHANGED.** The refusal they implement is ruled,
  reasoned in three places, and protects the bench's drag path.
- **Neither budget is raised.** The plan said *"do not raise either to make this work; report what
  fits"*, and neither was; what changed is which of the two an existing caller is given.
- **The `25.0` defaults keep their VALUE.** They name `GEO.BUDGET_INTERACTIVE_S` now, so a caller
  passing nothing gets exactly what it got before, from the one place the number lives.
