# WP-14.4 — the plan sheet and the bench held to what they state

*Phase 14, the ink held to its plates. Built 27 September 2026. The code is one commit; this report
and its verification are the commit after it.*

The plan is the one sheet this system draws twice: once in Python (`build/render_plan.py`, the
printed plate and the Drawing Set) and once in the browser (`workbench/app/src/sheet/Sheet.jsx`,
the bench). WP-14.3 held every other building sheet to its record. This package does the plan, on
both surfaces, and holds each to the other. It moved the census **from 36 known disagreements to
21**. The 21 left are V2, which waits on Lucas's ruling on decision 4.

The rule is the phase's: **draw what the record states, say what it does not, and invent
nothing.** On the plan it has a second half, because there are two drawings: **the two sheets draw
one record the same way, or one of them says why not.**

## I. What was fixed

### 1. Where two massing elements abut, the wall between them is drawn once (PL3, new: 21 overlapping pairs on one sheet → 0)

`render_plan.wall_bands` drew each massing element's envelope outward from its own rooms. On a
one-rectangle house that is right: the rooms tile the block, so the block's edge is the wall's
inside face. At a hyphen it is not. The corpus rules that a hyphen is an element that abuts the two
elements it joins (WP-11.9, ruling 3), so there is no ground between them and no "outward":

- the main block's west wall was drawn over the hyphen's back hall;
- the hyphen's east wall was drawn over the main block's rooms;
- and `wall_lines`, which sees the rooms on both sides of the line, drew a third body centred
  between them.

That is three walls where the record has one join, each 15.5 in of brick, over floor the record
says is room. On `tidewater-georgian-careful`, the one shipped plan with a dependency, **21 pairs
of wall bodies were drawn over one another**. There were none on the fifteen one-rectangle plans.
It was found by rendering the sheet and looking at it; a new census check, PL3 ("no wall body is
drawn over another"), measured it.

The join is drawn **once, centred on the line both elements share, at the envelope's thickness**.
That is not a new convention. It is the court wall's, a few lines below in the same function ("an
interior LINE of the block … drawn centred, at the envelope's own thickness, which is what being on
the envelope means"), and the schedule's standing statement that a wall on a line two rooms share
takes half its thickness from each. `render_plan.element_joins` finds the joins. Each element's
ring keeps its free spans, and a south or north run stops at the wall it meets rather than running
through it: short by a thickness where a neighbour's face runs on past the corner, exact where the
two faces are flush. A room wall on a join line is the join and is not drawn again. On a
one-rectangle house there is no join, so all fifteen of those sheets are byte-identical.

### 2. An exterior opening is cut out of its own element's wall (PL2, new)

WP-11.14 moved the window and door *frames* onto the room's own element face (`edge_ft`). It did
not reach the *holes*. `opening_gaps` still read `_wall_axis(wall, W, H)`, the footprint's face. So
on the tagged Tidewater plan:

- the dependency's kitchen and breakfast-room windows were framed on the dependency's wall and
  drawn over solid brick;
- a hyphen door opened a break in the main block's west wall, where the record places nothing.

Measured: **7 of 110 exterior openings corpus-wide had their hole on the wrong wall, all 7 on that
plan.** PL2 reads the ink in both directions:

- every opening the record places is cut from the wall body at its own face;
- every break in an exterior wall body is an opening the record places, or a join the wall runs on
  in.

It was 7 through a solid wall on the parent tree and is 0 now.

### 3. The bay grid says whose module it is (PL1: 15 → 0)

`derive_footprint` lays the footprint on the parti's `bay_module_ft`, or on its own 10 ft where no
parti states one. Fifteen of the sixteen shipped plans name no parti. The working sheet drew that
grid and labelled its lines, both registers drew the walls on it as bearing bodies, and nothing on
either plate said the module was the placer's own.

The placed record now carries who stated it: `geometry_report.bay_module`, `{ft, stated_by, note}`,
written by `_disclose` for both engines. `disclosures.bay_module` is the line the printed plate
draws: *BAY GRID AT THE PLACER'S DEFAULT 10 FT — NO PARTI STATES A MODULE, AND THE BEARING WALLS ARE
READ OFF IT*. The bench prints it from the served list. As committed, the bench's sheet note ALSO
composed its own copy, so the bench said it twice in two spellings (see §VI).

A record carrying no module at all no longer gets a silent 10 ft grid, on either sheet. The Python
plate draws no bay lines and says so. The bench's `derive.bayLines` returns none and its caption
says so. The Round's `bayGrid` already refused.

### 4. The bench draws the plate's own walls, and the whole house

The bench drew one ring round the footprint from its own derivation. On the tagged Tidewater plan
that is a house whose west dependency and hyphen **had no walls at all**, and whose plate was
framed on the main block, so the dependency ran off the left edge of the sheet.

`render_plan.wall_bodies` is now the one spelling of a sheet's walls. The printed plate draws it,
and `core.placement_summary` serves it as `placement.walls`, trimmed to what a drawing needs and
rounded to 1e-4 ft for the wire. The bench draws it:

- each element's envelope, the joins, the bearing lines in masonry and the partitions;
- a hole for every opening;
- a floor field per element;
- a plate framed on every element.

A placement served without the walls (an older server) keeps the old ring, and the plate note says
that a wing or a join is not drawn.

### 5. The bench's marks stand where the record puts them

The marks' geometry was inline in the JSX, so none of it could be tested without a browser. It is
lifted into `sheet/marks.js`. **The lift moved no ink**: the bench sheet was server-rendered (react-dom,
bundled by the app's own esbuild) for all 22 plates of the sixteen shipped plans before and after
it, and compared byte for byte. Then these were corrected, each held by a `node --test` except
where the entry says otherwise:

- **Windows on the west and south walls stood on the footprint's face.** `windows()` has computed
  the room's own element face since WP-11.14, and the mark read `x = -t` and `y = 0` regardless. It
  reads `w.x` and `w.y` now.
- **Every window and exterior door was 0.75 ft deep, or a 0.7 ft break centred on the face**,
  against a Tidewater envelope of 15.5 in. Both take the wall's own thickness now, outward from the
  room's face, which is where the plate cuts the hole.
- **An exterior door hung from the low jamb whatever the record said.** `derive.js` dropped the
  record's `hinge` on the exterior branch; the interior branch has carried it since WP-13.2.
- **The stair had no arrow.** The bench printed "UP 20R" over parallel lines that did not say which
  way was up. `render_plan.py` has drawn the arrow since WP-9.6. `marks.stairArrow` draws the same
  one: along the first flight, in its own stated direction. (Its test asserted the direction and
  not the position, though its title said "over its middle"; see §VI.)
- **A bay line was labelled `Math.round(x)`,** so a 12.17 ft module read 12, 24, 37. The plate
  prints the record's figure, and so does the bench now.
- **The scale bar's zero stood on the sheet's margin,** which is nothing drawn. It stands on the
  clear face, x = 0, as the Python plate's has since WP-13.2. (No test held this when it was
  committed; see §VI.)
- **Partitions were filled with `--poche-partition`,** the pale field WP-11.1 measured as a hollow
  tube at plan scale and took the printed plate off. They are `--sepia` now, and a test holds the
  bench's partition fill to `sheet_style.POCHE`. (The same test's masonry half compared two
  literals and never related the bench's ink to the plate's; see §VI.)

### 6. The sheet contract holds positions, not only counts

`tests/fixtures/sheet_symbols/` has held the two renderers to one answer since WP-6.1, on counts,
pairs, widths and types, and **not one position**. Two renderers could agree on how many doors they
drew, and between which rooms, while hanging them from different jambs, swinging them into
different rooms, or standing them on different faces. The dropped exterior hinge was exactly that.

Both halves now compare where things are:

- **The JS half:** every door's along and across position, hinge and swing, and every window's
  face. (Over fixtures that all hang low, so it could not see a leaf hung from the wrong jamb;
  see §VI.)
- **The Python half, which read only the model before:** every single leaf's hinge and swing and
  every window's glazing line, read off the rendered plate through its own frame. It also runs on
  a copy of each fixture with every door re-hung from its high jamb, because `openings.place`
  writes `low` on every door it seats and no frozen fixture reaches the other jamb. A mutation
  hanging a high leaf from the low jamb was green until that copy existed.

### 7. The Drawing Set, the Round and the Transcription

- **The Drawing Set** captioned its plan "the workbench sheet redrawn … from the same coordinates".
  It posts the record, and the drawing route places it afresh at the batch budget with no corrective
  rounds. The bench solved it at the interactive budget and ran its rounds. The caption says what
  is true now: the set is one placement, not necessarily the bench's.
- **The section and bearing plates** came back without `solver`, so the Drawing Set printed no
  "placed by" line under either. They carry it now, and the set's digest test covers all five
  kinds.
- **The Round's `frameOf`** fell back to the first plate listed. An elevation states two plates
  since WP-14.1, the face and the eave inset, and the face was first only by the order
  `render_elevation` happens to write. It chooses by what a plate is now, is moved into the
  testable `round/frame.js`, and is tested with the plates in both orders.
- **The Transcription canvas** draws a person's own tracing, so its one fidelity question is that
  it draws exactly what the draft holds. That was true and nothing held it. Its geometry is lifted
  into `traceCanvas.js`, proved byte-identical by rendering the canvas from both trees, and held by
  census row TR1.

## III. What the instrument got wrong

- **PL2's first version convicted two correct sheets.** It listed only exterior entries as
  openings, and a door onto a terrace is an interior entry that stands on an exterior wall. Then it
  convicted the fixed Tidewater sheet: a join is broken by the doors through it, so no single span
  covers the break it fills. It reads the union of the openings and joins on a line now.
- **B2's first version failed every plan with equal counts.** It rounded the served bands (already
  rounded to 1e-4 ft for the wire) a second time. It compares within the wire's rounding now.
- **A driven census test could have poisoned the session's cache.** Re-rendering with a planted
  renderer before the clean sheets were built would have cached sixteen planted sheets for every
  test after it. The clean table is built first now.
- **The walk's wall check measured a different house, and it was the first walk run after the
  package's commit that said so.** It counted the page's wall bodies against the walls the walk's
  own evaluate served.
  - An explicit solve on the bench runs the corrective rounds before it surfaces the sheet
    (WP-13.9). The walk's bare evaluate runs none. So the two are different placements of one
    record: 34 bodies drawn against 28 "served", and every one of the 34 was drawn from what the
    page was served.
  - That is WP-6.4's one-building rule broken inside the instrument that checks it. The comment
    above the walk's own probe already records the same thing for three other checks.
  - The walk now captures the page's own evaluate response and holds the sheet to it. It prints
    both counts: 34 of 34 served to the page, and 28 from its own evaluate.
- **V9 was WP-14.3's and was found here.** WP-14.3 refuses a keystone or a stack no record sizes,
  and V9 counted only a sheet that drew one, so its population went empty exactly where the new
  behaviour lives. It is re-cut to judge the record and is folded into WP-14.3's commit.

## IV. What was deliberately not done

- **The bench still posts the declared record to the Drawing Set.** Drawing the bench's own
  placement needs a shared store for it. The caption now says what is true instead.
- **A per-element roof**, and the other half of the abutment question
  (`oq/a-massing-element-is-placed-and-nothing-below-the-placer-knows-it`), which this package
  does not reopen. The join is drawn on the court wall's convention, and the convention is stated.
- **The census reads the bench's modules, not its rendered JSX.** CI's corpus shards run without
  the app's `node_modules`, so the census runs the pure leaves under node, as it already does for
  the Proportions plate. The JSX that copies their numbers was proved byte-identical at the lift.
  The walk checks the rendered bench.

## VI. What the phase's adversarial audit found in this package

WP-14.6 audited this package with read-only auditors and a mutation sweep, before this report was
committed. Its findings against this package are stated here rather than folded silently into the
text above. Their fixes land in WP-14.6.

- **B2 compared the served bands' walls and dimensions and not their `kind`.**
  - The bench chooses its ink by `kind`.
  - A server dropping it drew all 88 Tidewater bands as partitions, the plate's 61 masonry bands
    among them, and B2 agreed.
  - "The bench draws the plate's own walls" (§I.4) is therefore true of the code as committed, and
    was not held by the row that claimed to hold it.
- **The bench stated the bay module twice.** It printed the served line in its list of what the
  placement gave up, and its sheet note composed a second spelling of it. That is a second copy
  of the one line §I.3 says both surfaces print.
- **Three corrections in §I.5 were not held by a test:**
  - the stair arrow's POSITION (a mutation moving it to the flight's corner was green);
  - the scale bar's zero;
  - the masonry half of the poché test, which compared two literals.
- **The JS half of the sheet contract could not see a wrong jamb.** It compared hinges over
  fixtures that all hang from the low jamb, the same blindness §I.6 found and closed on the Python
  half. A re-hung JS case is added.
