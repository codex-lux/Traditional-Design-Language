# WP-11.12 — the span nobody was told about

*7 September 2026. Branch `claude/georgian-tidewater-plan-analysis-qbwmqs`. Every corpus figure
is `engine="heuristic"`, which is deterministic, and says so; the one `auto` reading in this
report is labelled twice. Numbers re-derived on the working tree and, where they are BEFOREs, on
`git archive` checkouts of the commits named.*

---

## I. What was wrong

`build/structure.py::span_check` has measured the clear span between bearing lines since WP-3.1.
`build/geometry.py` has CHARGED it since WP-7.4 — `SPAN_W = 20`, traded against everything else
the search scores. `build/geometry_cp.py` carries a soft mirror of the same charge.

**And `build/plan_check.py` had no span or structural-capacity finding of any kind.** So the
Tidewater upper floor — drawn with a **60 ft clear run and no bearing line in it**, against the
20 ft its framing tradition states — got a clean verdict from the validator the workbench shows,
the fidelity score the composer ranks on, the critique, and every `revision_report`. That is
OQ 98's reporting half, raised by the adversarial audit of WP-7.4 on 27 August and open since.

The cost is not hypothetical. WP-11.8 made a room's own proportion band the first key of the
search's acceptance; a squarer room puts fewer cuts on the bay module, and **the only way this
slicer creates a bearing line is to cut on one**. The corpus figure roughly doubled. The only
reason anyone saw it was two guards in `tests/test_structure.py` that happened to pin
`spec-builder-colonial` flagging one span — and that plan is now the single one in the corpus
that flags none. Written against any other plan, the whole movement would have been invisible.

## II. What was built

**The record names them.** `geometry._disclose_spans` writes `geometry_report.span_capacity.marks`
— `{axis, from_ft, to_ft, span_ft, member, max_span_ft, note, level}` per over-capacity run —
beside the count and the charge that were already there. `marks` is the key `relaxations` uses
one block above and for the reason stated there: *counted AND locatable*. It lives in
`_disclose`, so **both** record writers carry it; OQ 40's own disclosure shipped in
`write_record` alone for two phases, which is the defect that convention exists to stop.

**One arithmetic, two adapters.** `over_capacity_spans` (rectangles + a prep list, what the
search holds) converts into `spans_over_capacity` (placed room records, what a finished plan
holds), and `_span_charge` is now two lines summing the first. A second loop over the same
`span_check` results would have been the third-spelling defect `_span_charge`'s own docstring
exists to refuse. `span_check` is called from exactly one place in the tree and a test holds it
there — reading the call sites with `ast`, because two of the three "calls" the first version of
that test counted were prose in `render_section.py`'s docstrings.

**The critic says it.** `plan_check.drawn` emits one `serious` finding per span, with
`need_ft`/`have_ft`/`axis`/`level` beside the prose (WP-9.1's structured evidence), and an `info`
`span-unjudged` where the construction catalogue could not be read — a third state, not a pass.
It **reads the record rather than recomputing**: these are the spans the search scored and
`SPAN_W` charged, so a second computation could convict a placement on numbers it was not chosen
by.

*Serious rather than fatal*, on the corpus's own words: `span_check`'s note says such a run
"needs an intermediate bearing support or an engineered member outside this catalog". That is a
floor framed differently, not a plan that cannot be walked — and `fatal` in this layer means
unreachable, a broken must-adjoin, a duplicate id.

**Both plates print it**, in `render_plan`'s margin schedule and the workbench caption, **and
they print the zero too** — because *"no span exceeds capacity"* is exactly the claim §IV can
make falsely, and `relaxations` one line above already prints its own zero.

**`critique` classes it `placement`, with a lever** — and that stretches `_is_placement`'s own
definition, which is said in the code rather than left to be noticed. Every other kind there is
decided against something the record DECLARES: a door, a stack, a size, a passage width. **A plan
record states no wall positions at all**, so there is nothing declared for a clear span to
disagree with. It is classed on the other half of the definition — an engine setting really does
change it, demonstrably (§I) — and under CP-SAT `_lever` then says there is no setting left,
which is the honest answer. `oq/a-placement-finding-is-classed-by-what-the-engine-is-for-five-kinds`
has a sixth kind now.

The +23 is exactly the 23 over-capacity spans, on 13 of the 16 plans, worst **60.0 ft** — and

The refactor costs nothing in the hot path: the split was measured on `git archive HEAD` before

## IV. Every count says it is a floor

OQ 98 has two halves and only the reporting one is built. The measurement half is that
`span_check` reads each wall's `position_ft` and never the `lo_ft`/`hi_ft` extent `wall_lines`
computes beside it, so a bearing line is credited across the whole plate however short the wall
actually runs — `tidewater-georgian-careful`'s upper y-wall at 30.0 runs 20 of the 60 ft it is
credited across. **It under-reports in the direction that looks safe**, which is the OQ 52 family.

Publishing a count with a known understatement and not saying so would be that same family in a
new place, so the note is on the record (`span_capacity.understated`), inside every finding's own
statement, and on both plates: *"at least that many: a bearing line is credited across the whole
plate however short the wall runs."* The measurement half still wants the ruling OQ 98 asks for —
what a partial bearing wall supports — before it wants code.

## V. WP-11.8's published pair was an unlabelled `auto` reading

Its report and CLAUDE.md both carry *"over-capacity clear spans 13 → 25"*. Re-derived here on
`git archive` checkouts of `1596ed2` (the commit before WP-11.8) and of WP-11.8 itself:

| `engine="heuristic"` | before WP-11.8 | after |
|---|---|---|
| over-capacity spans | **11** | **23** |
| worst | **40.0 ft** | **60.0 ft** |

`auto` gave **28** on one run of the current tree, and `auto` is not reproducible. So the count
pair was wrong and the worst-span pair was right. CLAUDE.md's own standing rule is *RATCHET THE
DETERMINISTIC FIGURES ONLY*; this is what an unlabelled figure costs — two numbers nobody can
reproduce sitting beside a third that anyone can. The correction is in CLAUDE.md beside the
original, with the engines named, rather than in WP-11.8's report, which is left as written.

## VI. The audit of this package's own guards

Ten mutations against the ten new tests in `tests/test_span_findings.py`, each **asserted to have
LANDED** before the suite ran; every one bites now. **One did not on the first pass** and it was
the familiar shape: `span_check` returns `member: None` where NO member in the catalogue covers
the run, no plan in this corpus takes that branch, and the first version of the finding
interpolated the None straight into its own prose — *"…states for a None."* It has a fixture
that proves the branch rather than a deletion.

**And one assertion stated a false reason and was caught by its own first run.** It said
`plan_check` cannot load `structure.py`; it can and does, lazily and inside a try, in the
elevation block at the foot of the file, and has since WP-3.2. The comment in `plan_check` said
the same thing. Both now give the reasons that are actually true — one spelling, and the spans
the search was chosen by — which are better reasons than the false one.

## VII. What this package does NOT do

- **OQ 98's measurement half.** It wants a ruling on what a partial bearing wall supports, it
  changes the structural verdict on both reference plans again, and it would re-open the weight
  sweep that set `SPAN_W`. The question carries the numbers; nothing here pre-empts it.
- **No move answers a span.** `moves/registry.json` gains nothing: the placer creates a bearing
  line only by cutting on it, and a move that edits the record cannot ask for a cut. The lever is
  the engine, which is what `placement` class means.
- **`check_all`'s two `structure.py` invocations still return 0 whatever they find** — `structure`
  has no `sys.exit`, and neither do `plan_check`, `roof` or `elevation`. OQ 98 calls that a
  convention question rather than a bug in one file, and it is left as one.
- **No new checker**; `TOTAL_CHECKS` does not move.
