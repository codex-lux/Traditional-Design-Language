# WP-11.6 — the record says what it means

*5 September 2026. The first package of the B line: the placement, not the drawing. Ruled by
Lucas on three questions put to him with the measurement already in hand — ship both claims and
publish the cost; split the field in two; report, and fix the level blindness too.*

`PLAN-OF-ACTION.md` opens the placement line with one sentence: *"`stacks_over` on the upper
passage and the landing, which the parti declares and the plan does not; no engine change."*
Two lines of JSON. Reading the code before writing them turned it into four findings, and the
largest of them is not about stacking at all.

---

## I. What was actually wrong

**`stacks_over` had seven readers and four different definitions of "below", and every one of
them declined in silence.**

| reader | what it did with a claim it could not answer |
|---|---|
| `geometry.py::vertical_score` | iterates the UPPER rects, looks the target up in the GROUND rects — level 0 hard-wired as "below", unanswerable claims are a bare `continue` |
| `geometry.py::bias` | the same, and it steers CANDIDATE GENERATION rather than only the ranking |
| `geometry_cp.py` | keys `(1, r["id"])` / `(0, so)` — the same hard-wiring |
| `plan_check.py` drawn layer | `if level_of[rid] == level_of[so]: continue`, with no note beside it |
| `plan_check.py` reachability | minted an edge to the stair room with **no level test at all** |
| `plan_check.py` servicing | read the field level-blind, for the PLUMBING meaning |
| `check_partis.py` | the target id must exist; the level relation unchecked |

The schema explains how one field came to mean two things. Its own description read *"room id on
the level below, **for plumbing and structure**."* Two duties, one field.

And there was an instance. `plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json:336`: the ground-level Powder
Room (`wc`, `lavatory`) declared `stacks_over: "cellarstair"` — the Cellar Stair, a `back-hall`
**on the same level**. Three readers dropped it. **The plan carried four claims of which three
were judged, and no surface anywhere said which three.** An unjudged claim that reads exactly
like a satisfied one is the OQ 52 family.

Swept: it is the only instance in the corpus. 0 of 50 parti claims, 0 on the other fifteen plans.

## II. The two claims, and what they cost

`partis/centre-passage-double-pile.json` declares five stacks; the plan declared three. The two
missing were the two the diagram is *named* for — a centre passage that runs through both
storeys and a stair that lands where it left.

Authored, `engine="heuristic"` (deterministic, identical on cold runs):

| | fatal | serious | minor | relax | transfer beams | claims | kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 3 | 62 | 85 | 7 | 9 | 3 | 1 |
| after | **4** | **55** | 88 | **9** | 14 | **5** | **3** |

**Both new claims are KEPT** — the landing lands over its stair and the upper passage over the
passage, so the search does reach a stacking placement when it is told to aim at one. Nine
`serious` findings clear: the stair hall's width-below-floor, three
proportion-above-band (stair hall, powder room, chamber 3), two windows off their placed wall,
two furniture fits, one drawn-vs-declared. Three appear, one of them a new fatal.

The visible half is not in the numbers. The upper floor stops being slivers:

| room | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Stair Landing | 6.0 × 15.4 ft (2.6:1) | **12.1 × 13.5 ft** |
| Chamber 3 | 23.3 × 11.3 | 19.4 × 12.9 |
| Chamber Bath | 6.6 × 15.4 | 13.3 × 7.5 |

`geometry.bias()` reads `stacks_over` to pull a room toward its target while the level is being
sliced, so two more claims change **which layouts are produced**, not only which is picked. That
is why a two-line record edit moved the placement as much as it did, and it is the answer to
"no engine change": there was none, and the placement moved anyway.

### The new fatal is named, not absorbed

`fatal unreachable: chamber3`. `cl2` comes to sit between Chamber 3 and the Upper Passage, so
the declared door has no shared wall and the opening goes unplaced. Clearing it inside this
package would mean authoring a door from a bedroom into a closet or another bedroom — **a
placement failure written into the record**, which is exactly what the plan of action forbade
when it said these were *"reachability findings, which must be examined one by one and cleared
with placed doors, not absorbed."* It is WP-11.7's, and it is stated in the margin schedule and
here rather than smoothed away.

### On the proof, the claims buy nothing, and that is the finding

`engine="auto"` returns `OPTIMAL (hard-only) — kept hard-only phase A`. **The CP objective never
runs on this plan**, so the soft stacking term is dead:

| | serious | minor | relax | claims | broken |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 73 | 80 | 16 | 3 | 2 |
| after | 79 | 81 | 11 | 5 | **5** |

Five of five broken, including `primary → drawing`, which the search keeps. This is `Part IV.B`
fact (iii) — *on Tidewater the CP objective cannot reach the drawing* — **measured on this plan
for the first time**, and it is the evidence WP-11.7 is built on: only a hard constraint moves
this plan under the engine that draws the sheet.

## III. The split

Plan schema **0.8.0**. Two fields, two duties, each description naming the reader it is for:

| field | means | shape | judged by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stacks_over` | this room's plan sits over that room's — STRUCTURAL | id on the level **one below** | strict positive rectangle intersection, both engines and `plan_check.drawn` |
| `wet_stack_with` | this room's wet stack shares a chase with that room — SERVICING | id on **any** level, including its own | `plan_check`'s servicing layer only; never charged by a placer |

The powder room's claim moved to `wet_stack_with`. **A re-classification of an authored claim,
not a new fact** — and that is a testable statement, made in
`tests/test_stacking.py::test_moving_the_powder_rooms_claim_changed_no_verdict`. Measured on the
tree as it stood before this package, putting the claim back on `stacks_over` left every finding
byte-identical. It is no longer byte-identical, and the difference is the package itself: the
reverted record now draws one `info` of kind `stack-unjudged`. So the test asserts the sharper
thing — **the only difference is the disclosure, and no weighted verdict moves either way.**
(The first draft asserted byte-identity, failed on its own package's new finding, and would have
been "fixed" by deleting the comparison.)

The field's target need not itself be wet. `cellarstair` carries no fixtures, and that is the
point: a stack needs a vertical chase and a stair shaft is one. The schema says so, and a test
says so, because the obvious "correction" is to re-point the record at a bathroom.

**One reader moved, and only one:** `plan_check`'s servicing layer, which had always read the
field level-blind for the plumbing meaning. It reads both fields now — a bath over a bath is
genuinely both — so no verdict on the shipped corpus moves.

## IV. `build/stacking.py`, a leaf

`geometry.py` loads `plan_check.py` at import. So `plan_check.py` may never load `geometry.py`,
and those are exactly the two files that must agree about what a stack is. `build/storeys.py`
(WP-9.6) and `build/assemblies.py` (WP-11.2) answered the identical problem the identical way.

It holds `STACK_UNJUDGED_REASONS` (a closed set of six, on
`build/construction_vocabulary.py`'s precedent), `rooms_by_level`, `overlaps`, `judge`, `report`,
`multi_level_disclosure`, and `takes_a_rectangle` — the last moved out of `geometry.is_placed`,
which now delegates to it.

**The verdict is read off the placed record, not re-derived.** It reads the coordinates the
engines wrote back, so the tally cannot disagree with the placement it describes.

`geometry_report.stacking` carries `claims`, `kept`, `broken`, `unjudged` and the weight, and
**`claims == kept + broken + unjudged` is asserted over every plan in the tree** — totality is
the guard, because the defect this package is about is a claim that fell out of every bucket.
`plan_check.drawn` gains `stacks_kept` and `stacks_unjudged` beside the existing `stacks_broken`,
and an `info` finding of kind `stack-unjudged` carrying the reason and, where one exists, the
remedy.

Both engines' record writers now call one `_disclose(plan)`. **Writing it turned up a fifth
finding**: `multi_element_disclosure` (OQ 40) was wired into `write_record` and **not** into
`_finish`, so every CP-produced multi-element placement has shipped with no disclosure since the
day OQ 40 was built. One line, and the reason it is one function now.

## V. A dormant check fired on the first run

`plan_check.py`'s `landing-off-well` branch runs only where a room's `stacks_over` names the
stair's own room. **No plan in the corpus said so**, so in the life of that check it had never
executed. The Tidewater record says it now and it fires immediately — `minor`, the landing
overlaps the well by less than the stair's own width squared.

CLAUDE.md's rule, in a new place: *the moment a record can finally state a thing, every rule that
presupposed it runs on it.* It hangs off the KEPT list on purpose — a landing drawn clear of the
stair hall entirely is already a `stack-broken`, and two findings for one defect is worse than
one.

## VI. The placer places two levels and said nothing about the third

The largest finding, and it is not about stacking.

`plans/reference/bad-03-narrow-lot-townhome.json` declares three levels. Solved on either engine:

```
level 0  rooms 4  placed 4
level 1  rooms 9  placed 9
level 2  rooms 1  placed 0
```

Its one level-2 room, the Gameroom, came back with **no geometry, no finding and no note**. All
eight findings naming it were declared-layer findings — its adjacencies, its ceiling, its
daylight, its furniture. The sheet drew the house **without its top floor**, in silence.

The ceiling is written down twice in `geometry.py`: `_finish` does
`best["ground"] if idx == 0 else (best["upper"] if idx == 1 else {})`, and `solve_heuristic` is
written throughout against `prep[0]` / `prep[1]`.

Three surfaces say so now, on `multi_element_disclosure`'s exact precedent — the layer cannot do
the thing, so it states that on the record rather than reporting numbers from an instrument
pointed at half the building:

- `geometry_report.multi_level` — levels placed, levels not placed, rooms not placed, and a
  COULD NOT EVALUATE note;
- `plan_check.drawn`'s `rooms_unplaced`, with a `serious` finding per room;
- `build/check_stacking.py`, which reports any record declaring a level outside the ceiling.

**The discriminator already existed and is exact.** `geometry.is_placed` tells a `terrace` whose
own record puts it outside the footprint (no rectangle, correct) from a `great-room` on level 2
(no rectangle, a defect). Collapsing those two is how a missing storey comes to read as a design
decision, and there is a test in each direction.

Placing a third level is not built and is not claimed to be:
`oq/the-placer-places-two-levels-and-says-nothing-about-the-third` carries the four things that
must be ruled first, among them what `index: -1` means — the schema documents a cellar and no
record uses one, so the placed window is not simply "the first N levels".

## VII. `build/check_stacking.py`, the 50th check

Five rules over 37 documents and 57 claims: the target exists; a `stacks_over` names a room
**exactly one level below**; a `wet_stack_with` target exists on any level; no room states both
fields naming the same room; and any record declaring a level outside `PLACED_LEVEL_INDICES` is
reported. Every branch was mutation-checked and every legal shape verified silent.

It never solves anything. `kept` / `broken` / `not placed` are properties of a placement and live
in `geometry_report.stacking`; a record error is the same on every engine.

### What it deliberately cannot check

**That a plan states the stacking its own parti declares** — which is how this package's two
claims were found, by hand, reading two files side by side. Measured: **no plan record in the
corpus names a parti.** Not one of the sixteen has the key. The parti reaches the placement as an
argument and is never written down on the record that results, so the join does not exist, and
building it on matching room ids would be inventing a fact from a coincidence. The checker says
so in its own docstring; `oq/a-plan-does-not-name-the-parti-it-was-built-from` carries the
ruling it needs.

## VIII. The pins, and one that earned itself

| pin | before → after |
|---|---|
| relaxation count (equality, three files) | 7 → **9** |
| drawn furniture shortfalls (equality on the pair) | 88 / 69 → **86 / 70** |
| roof sha256 over 180 records | **unchanged** — see below |
| published check total / test total | 49 / 1,628 → **50 / 1,644** |

**The furniture pair is the entry worth reading.** WP-11.3 moved the short ceiling 86 → 88 and
isolated the cause by proving the placement byte-identical: the CATALOGUE had moved. This package
moves it 88 → 86/70 and isolated the cause the other way, by proving the catalogue untouched and
diffing the placed rectangles: the PLACEMENT moved. The two look identical in that file and have
nothing in common. **When a ratchet moves, re-derive which layer moved it** — a reader who assumes
the last cause was the cause will be wrong half the time, and both notes are kept side by side so
that is visible.

**WP-11.4's roof pruning earned itself here.** That package found its byte-identity pin was
hashing the PLACEMENT (`build_roof`'s return embeds `section`, which embeds the placed plan) and
pruned it to six roof keys. Measured across WP-11.6, which moves the placement:

```
PRUNED  0d94e0cd...  →  0d94e0cd...   (unchanged)
RAW     4335d9e1...  →  d8107962...   (changed)
```

The pin said *"the roof did not change"* about a commit that changed the placement, which is
exactly the question it was rewritten to answer. Four lines were added to the test saying so, so
nobody un-prunes it.

## IX. Found in ordinary work, and left alone

**`tests/fixtures/sheet_symbols/` is regenerated by solving, and its own README says why that
cannot work.** The README: *"a fixture generated by solving would pin the machine as much as the
code."* `generate.py:40`: `solved = geometry.solve(plan)` — no engine argument, so `auto`, so
CP-SAT under a wall-clock budget.

Measured **on the pristine tree, with no code change at all**, regenerating both fixtures gives
`2 files changed, 825 insertions(+), 804 deletions(-)`, and `spec-builder-colonial.json` — a plan
this package does not touch — moves as much as the Tidewater one. So the README's instruction to
*"read the diff before committing it"* hands the reader eight hundred lines of solver noise with
the renderer's change buried inside.

WP-11.6 **neither regenerated nor edited** them. The fixture is contract INPUT, never re-solved
by the suites that read it, and this package's record change does not require it to move — the
suite is green untouched. The one-word repair rewrites both fixtures on a package about stacking,
and which placement the contract should freeze is a real question with three defensible answers:
`oq/the-frozen-fixture-is-regenerated-by-solving`.

## XI. What this package did NOT do

No engine weight or constraint changed. `STACK_W` is still 40, the CP term is still soft, the
`wall_keys` downgrade ladder is untouched, and the placement moved entirely through the record.
The `chamber3` fatal is named. CP's dead objective is measured and left. A third level is
disclosed and not placed. No parti was edited. `wet_stack_with` is authored on exactly the one
record that already made the claim.

**And the level generalisation was scoped honestly.** The arbiter is fully level-aware now —
`plan_check` has `level_of` in hand and uses it, in the drawn layer and in the reachability
reader that had no level test at all. The ENGINES were not generalised past level 0, because
they place two levels: generalising "below" in a solver that never produces a level 2 would be
writing a branch nothing can reach, which is the guard-that-cannot-fire this repository keeps
catching. It is disclosed instead, and the question names what must be ruled before it is built.
