# WP-14.6 — the adversarial audit of Phase 14

**Status: COMPLETE (27 Sep 2026).** Phase 14 asked whether every drawn trim, casing, column and
entablature profile, and every other drawn figure, reflects the plates and dimensions researched to
source it, on every surface that draws SVG. WP-14.1 built the instrument that reads the ink back,
and WP-14.2 to WP-14.5 fixed what it found. This package audited those five and re-derived every
figure they published.

**The census went from 60 checks and 41 known disagreements to 68 and 46.** The 46 are:

- **V2 15**: decision 4, which waits on Lucas;
- **N1 14 and N3 6**: the record's own notes disputing its figures, listed in
  `Plan Examples/Plates/WANTED.md` for the plates;
- **V19 11**: the elevation's roof, drawn 2.5 to 3.4 ft above where every other surface puts it.
  Every sheet now says so, and the question is put to a ruling.

**The audit's central finding is about the fifth link, surface against surface.** WP-14.1's census
held each drawing to its record. Nothing held one drawing of an object to another drawing of the
same object:

- one chimney stood in four places on four surfaces;
- one roof was drawn at two heights on one drawing set;
- a sidelight and fourteen sheets' shutter leaves were drawn over the neighbouring window's glass.

Each record was right on its own terms. These were found by rendering the sheets and looking at
them, then measured. Sixteen of the phase's own guards and readers could not see what they
claimed to hold (§IV). Every published figure that did not reproduce is corrected in a dated
section of the report that published it (§VII).

## I. How the audit was run

Read-only auditors worked from three angles:

- the drawings against their records;
- the guards that could not fail;
- the census rows that certified the defect they were written to catch.

The mutations ran in an isolated copy of the working tree, refreshed before each batch, and
nothing else read that copy while a batch ran. This is WP-13.9's lesson: a mutation run sharing a
checkout with a reader makes every figure the reader takes meaningless.

**No finding was acted on as reported.** Each was re-derived by the lead first. Two of the
audit's own figures did not reproduce and are published at the re-derived value: the 356-of-773
and 140-of-270 furniture counts (§III). Three of this package's own first drafts were caught the
same way before they were committed:

- the refused shutter pairs, first written as 29, are 37;
- the sidelight widths, first written as 84 and 16 in, are 68.9 and 21.1;
- "eight reference plans" is eight plans.

Where a finding named a cause, the cause was reproduced, not argued.

## II. What the drawings disagreed with, and what was done

### One chimney, one square, every surface

- **Four surfaces drew one stack in four places.**
  - The plan sheet drew `threshold._stack_rect`'s square, seated outboard of the gable wall for an
    exterior stack.
  - The roof plan centred its own square on the wall's outside face, so half the stack stood
    inside the wall it stands against.
  - The DXF drew a 36 in round chimney, a circle of 18 in radius, where the record states a 22 in
    square.
  - The scene centred a box on the flue's point.

  `roof.py` now carries the placement's square as `plan_rect_ft`, in its own frame and with the
  side of the wall it stands on, and every surface draws that. Where the placement seats no
  square, none is invented: the roof plan and the DXF mark the stack's point, and the elevation
  and the model say they have no seat for it.
- **The elevation drew two chimneys for one.** On the Tidewater plan the front drew 8.0 ft of stack
  from the ridge up, and the gable end drew 16.2 ft from a level cut at the rake's centre. The
  level cut floated the stack clear of the rake on its low side. `render_elevation._stack_outline`
  draws each face's view of one prism:
  - on the gable face, the part above the rake, following the rake across the stack's width;
  - on the long face, an exterior stack from the lowest point of its own rake;
  - on the long face, an interior stack from the highest roof in front of it.

  A roof whose ridge runs front to back is refused by name, where before it drew nothing and said
  nothing. Census V17 holds the ink of every face to one square and one height.
- **The Drawing Set stood the stacks 12.3 ft from the fires, under a false note.** Every user-facing
  caller builds the section on the placed record and then hands `build_roof` the record it was
  given. The client posts the declared record, which carries no hearths. So the roof plan and the
  elevation stood the Tidewater stacks on the gable's centre line, under a note that the plan
  "carries no placement", while the section on the same sheet carried one. It is WP-6.4's rule, one
  drawing set is one building, met at the chimney. The census never saw it because it hands
  `build_roof` the placed record, and the product does not. The roof now reads the section's own
  placement. The roof digest moved in `chimneys` alone, 49 of 180 entries, re-derived on a harness
  first proved against the old value.
- **A stack of no stated size blinded windows on an invented width.** `or 22.0` meant brick-course's
  figure for one coursing, itself a judgment. It was used to blind bays and refuse windows 11 in
  either side of an axis, while the same sheet declined to draw that stack for want of the same
  figure. The stack is now its axis and nothing wider. No sheet reaches it: swept over the eleven
  plans that draw an elevation and every style's front, no stack lacks a stated plan size.

### The window and the entrance

- **Every drawn transom was divided unevenly under a legend saying "evenly".** The SVG and the DXF
  each spelled the division as the bar centred on each division point of the whole width. That
  leaves the end lights half a bar wider than the middle ones: 10.06 in against 9.62. The sash's

  `elevation.even_bars`. Census V18 holds the drawn lights to sash-light's count at the drawn width;
  X2 holds the DXF's sash members and transom lights to the SVG's, member for member, and the
  transom's height as a judgment in XDATA.
- **12 of the 41 drawn styles took a keyed arch and drew no keystone, unsaid.** The flag the
  refusal reads was set by a substring test for "keystoned", which never matches
  `segmental-arch-keyed`. It is read off the head the kit chose, as a whole word, now, and the 12
  sheets say KEYSTONE NOT DRAWN, since no record states its width. This falsifies WP-14.3's "no
  drawn style reaches either fallback" (§VII).
- **A surround variant's own condition was not read.** `none-masonry-reveal` is canonical on
  `georgian-colonial-american` only where the wall is solid masonry, and the cascade carries it
  into the wood slot with that condition. Unread, the frame `good-03` and `good-05` were refused as
  naming two surrounds. `window_surround` now reads each variant's date range, through the

  curve by name as well as by kind, so a name that only mentions a neighbour's curve ("Fillet under
  the scotia") is not read as one.
