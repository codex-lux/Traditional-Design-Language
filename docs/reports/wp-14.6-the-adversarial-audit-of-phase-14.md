# WP-14.6 — the adversarial audit of Phase 14

**Status: COMPLETE (27 Sep 2026).** Phase 14 asked whether every drawn trim, casing, column and
entablature profile, and every other drawn figure, reflects the plates and dimensions researched to
source it, on every surface that draws SVG. WP-14.1 built the instrument that reads the ink back,
and WP-14.2 to WP-14.5 fixed what it found. This package audited those five and re-derived every
figure they published.

**The census went from 60 checks and 41 known disagreements to 68 and 46.** The 46 are:

- **V2 15**: decision 4, which waits on Lucas;
- **N1 14 and N3 6**: the record's own notes disputing its figures, listed in
  `Plan Examples/Plates/WANTED.md` for the plates;
- **V19 11**: the elevation's roof, drawn 2.5 to 3.4 ft above where every other surface puts it.
  Every sheet now says so, and the question is put to a ruling.

**The audit's central finding is about the fifth link, surface against surface.** WP-14.1's census
held each drawing to its record. Nothing held one drawing of an object to another drawing of the
same object:

- one chimney stood in four places on four surfaces;
- one roof was drawn at two heights on one drawing set;
- a sidelight and fourteen sheets' shutter leaves were drawn over the neighbouring window's glass.

Each record was right on its own terms. These were found by rendering the sheets and looking at
them, then measured. Sixteen of the phase's own guards and readers could not see what they
claimed to hold (§IV). Every published figure that did not reproduce is corrected in a dated
section of the report that published it (§VII).

## I. How the audit was run

Read-only auditors worked from three angles:

- the drawings against their records;
- the guards that could not fail;
- the census rows that certified the defect they were written to catch.

The mutations ran in an isolated copy of the working tree, refreshed before each batch, and
nothing else read that copy while a batch ran. This is WP-13.9's lesson: a mutation run sharing a
checkout with a reader makes every figure the reader takes meaningless.

**No finding was acted on as reported.** Each was re-derived by the lead first. Two of the
audit's own figures did not reproduce and are published at the re-derived value: the 356-of-773
and 140-of-270 furniture counts (§III). Three of this package's own first drafts were caught the
same way before they were committed:

- the refused shutter pairs, first written as 29, are 37;
- the sidelight widths, first written as 84 and 16 in, are 68.9 and 21.1;
- "eight reference plans" is eight plans.

Where a finding named a cause, the cause was reproduced, not argued.

## II. What the drawings disagreed with, and what was done

### One chimney, one square, every surface

- **Four surfaces drew one stack in four places.**
  - The plan sheet drew `threshold._stack_rect`'s square, seated outboard of the gable wall for an
    exterior stack.
  - The roof plan centred its own square on the wall's outside face, so half the stack stood
    inside the wall it stands against.
  - The DXF drew a 36 in round chimney, a circle of 18 in radius, where the record states a 22 in
    square.
  - The scene centred a box on the flue's point.

  `roof.py` now carries the placement's square as `plan_rect_ft`, in its own frame and with the
  side of the wall it stands on, and every surface draws that. A stack the placement seats no
  square for is drawn as its axis alone, and the sheet says why.
- **The elevation drew two chimneys for one.** On the Tidewater plan the front drew 8.0 ft of stack
  from the ridge up, and the gable end drew 16.2 ft from a level cut at the rake's centre. The
  level cut floated the stack clear of the rake on its low side. `render_elevation._stack_outline`
  draws each face's view of one prism:
  - on the gable face, the part above the rake, following the rake across the stack's width;
  - on the long face, an exterior stack from the lowest point of its own rake;
  - on the long face, an interior stack from the highest roof in front of it.

  A roof whose ridge runs front to back is refused by name, where before it drew nothing and said
  nothing. Census V17 holds the ink of every face to one square and one height.
- **The Drawing Set stood the stacks 12.3 ft from the fires, under a false note.** Every user-facing
  caller builds the section on the placed record and then hands `build_roof` the record it was
  given. The client posts the declared record, which carries no hearths. So the roof plan and the
  elevation stood the Tidewater stacks on the gable's centre line, under a note that the plan
  "carries no placement", while the section on the same sheet carried one. It is WP-6.4's rule, one
  drawing set is one building, met at the chimney. The census never saw it because it hands
  `build_roof` the placed record, and the product does not. The roof now reads the section's own
  placement. The roof digest moved in `chimneys` alone, 49 of 180 entries, re-derived on a harness
  first proved against the old value.
- **A stack of no stated size blinded windows on an invented width.** `or 22.0` meant brick-course's
  figure for one coursing, itself a judgment. It was used to blind bays and refuse windows 11 in
  either side of an axis, while the same sheet declined to draw that stack for want of the same
  figure. The stack is now its axis and nothing wider. No sheet reaches it: swept over the eleven
  plans that draw an elevation and every style's front, no stack lacks a stated plan size.

### What the placed openings leave room for

- **Since WP-13.3 the elevation draws the plan's placed openings, and two things were still
  composed without looking at them.**
  - The entrance's sidelights are chosen against facade-classical's bay cap. On the Tidewater
    front the left sidelight was drawn 9 in over the passage window, which the plan places 12 in
    from the leaf, where the casing and a sidelight need 21.1 in.
  - Each window's shutter pair is drawn at sash-light's leaf width. On 14 of 44 sheets a leaf lay
    over the next window's glass or over another leaf.

  `elevation._clearances` decides both once, for the SVG, the DXF and the scene. It REFUSES a pair
  that has no wall to stand on and says why; it never narrows one, because a narrower leaf is a
  figure no record states. Census V20 reads the ink for any opening or leaf drawn over another.
  **The same refusal moved seven V2 rows, and not for decision 4's reason**: six styles whose
  sidelight ban comes from `georgian-colonial-american`, and `minimal-traditional`, no longer draw
  sidelights their kits forbid, because the Tidewater placement leaves them no room, not because
  the ban was honoured (§V).
- **The scene dropped a refused shutter pair with no reason in its record.** It says so in
  `not_modelled` now, as the plate does in its legend.

### The window and the entrance

- **Every drawn transom was divided unevenly under a legend saying "evenly".** The SVG and the DXF
  each spelled the division as the bar centred on each division point of the whole width. That
  leaves the end lights half a bar wider than the middle ones: 10.06 in against 9.62. The sash's
  muntins, in both directions, and the transom's now divide the glass through one function,
  `elevation.even_bars`. Census V18 holds the drawn lights to sash-light's count at the drawn width;
  X2 holds the DXF's sash members and transom lights to the SVG's, member for member, and the
  transom's height as a judgment in XDATA.
- **12 of the 41 drawn styles took a keyed arch and drew no keystone, unsaid.** The flag the
  refusal reads was set by a substring test for "keystoned", which never matches
  `segmental-arch-keyed`. It is read off the head the kit chose, as a whole word, now, and the 12
  sheets say KEYSTONE NOT DRAWN, since no record states its width. This falsifies WP-14.3's "no
  drawn style reaches either fallback" (§VII).
- **A surround variant's own condition was not read.** `none-masonry-reveal` is canonical on
  `georgian-colonial-american` only where the wall is solid masonry, and the cascade carries it
  into the wood slot with that condition. Unread, the frame `good-03` and `good-05` were refused as
  naming two surrounds. `window_surround` now reads each variant's date range, through the
  resolver's own `in_period`, and its construction list. WP-14.3 wrote that no style reaches the
  "canonical alone" case. That was a measurement of the one masonry house the sweep draws on. Ten
  of the eleven shipped plans that draw an elevation are frame, and all ten reach it. It stays
  undrawn, because no drawn style's slot states a figure to draw: `colonial-revival` states only a
  3 in maximum, and `greek-revival-american` an editorial band of 5 to 6 in.
- **Three members named for a curve were drawn square.** "Ovolo of the abacus" on Vignola's
  Corinthian and Composite, and Gibbs's "Abacus, ovolo", carried `profile: abacus`. The Corinthian's
  own note reads "Quarter-round 2 parts high", and Gibbs's quotes the ovolo he gives three parts
  of. Each was corrected to `ovolo` on its own name and note, the OQ 81 precedent. P11 now reads a
  curve by name as well as by kind, so a name that only mentions a neighbour's curve ("Fillet under
  the scotia") is not read as one.

### The section, the bearing sheet and the plan sheet

- **The floor structure was drawn one storey out.** WP-14.3 hung each body under its storey's own
  floor. `storeys.py` derives the depth as the gap between a storey's ceiling and the floor above
  it, so every section drew a body in the crawl space and nothing under the eave. It was found by
  rendering the sections and looking. Census S2 holds it.
- **The bearing sheet drew a dependency's walls off the sheet.** The canvas was the main block's
  clear footprint, and the tagged Tidewater plan's west dependency drew its bearing lines up to
  196 px past the left edge. Its over-capacity spans were marked across the main block's middle,
  in the hyphen gap. Each plate is now the extent of the walls it draws, its `data-frame` moves
  with it, and a span is marked across its own element. Census S3 holds every mark inside the
  sheet.
- **The relaxation legend overprinted the record table on every working sheet.** The △'s
  definition is drawn a row below the margin schedule, and the band reserved no row for it. So the
  table's heading was printed 4.7 px above it on every working sheet with a relaxation and a
  diverged room. All sixteen shipped sheets carry both. The band reserves the row now.
- **The bench composed its own sentence about the bay module.** `disclosures.bay_module` is the one
  line both surfaces print. The bench showed it and also spelled it a third time in its sheet note.
  The note now prints only what it is served.
- **The bench hung an unseated exterior door from the low jamb whatever the record said.** WP-14.4
  restored the record's hinge to the bench's seated exterior branch. The branch for a door the
  record does not seat still dropped it, where no frozen fixture reaches, because every door there
  is seated. The Python plate carried the hinge on all four paths. Both suites now unseat every door
  and hang it high.

### The profile plates and the eave inset

- **150 labels on 58 of the 73 plates were drawn through the border**, by up to 27.8 px. The
  frame's right rule stood 36 px in from the canvas edge, inside the column the labels are laid out
  in. Cutting every label shorter pushed one past the fitter's last resort, so the frame moved out
  to hold the column. All 73 plates were re-rendered, and the manifest's review notes and hashes
  moved with them.
- **The eave inset was drawn 14 px over an end stack.** An exterior end stack stands 1.83 ft
  (44 px) outboard of the gable wall, and the inset stood 30 px past the face. It now clears the
  rightmost ink a stack puts past the face. Census V21 holds it.
- **A plate's review note read AGREES over a check that could not evaluate** on four plates. The
  headline now reads AGREES WHERE JUDGED and counts the unjudged checks.

### The datum and the words

- **The datum record wrote its face conversion out by hand, and it went stale.** After WP-13.3
  unmirrored the N and W faces, it still said they were mirrored, so a reader converting a plan
  coordinate by the record's own sentence put every N and W opening at the wrong end of its face.
  `elevation.face_u_words()` composes it from `FACE_MIRRORED` now.

## III. What was measured and not moved: four questions and an amendment

- **The elevation stands its roof on a band no other surface draws.**
  `elevation.grade_to_true_eave_in` adds the frieze and the cornice above roof.py's eave. So every
  elevation draws its roof and stacks 30.1 to 40.2 in higher than the section, the roof record and
  the model do: on the Tidewater plan, eave 28'-6" against 25'-5", ridge 41'-9" against 38'-9". The
  record has said so since WP-3.2 and no sheet did. Every elevation now says it beside the roof,
  census V19 pins all eleven plans, and facade-classical's own frieze rule is evidence for the
  other reading. Moving the roof chooses an answer, so it is put to a ruling:
  `oq/the-elevation-stands-its-roof-on-a-cornice-band-no-other-surface-draws`.
- **The shutter fault clears on a facade the sheet does not draw.**
  `shutter-on-an-unshutterable-opening` counts 2 of 2 leaves clearing on the rhythm's pier, while
  `_clearances`, reading the placed openings, refuses 37 of the 60 pairs on the ten plans that carry
  shutters. So eight plans show SHUTTERS NOT DRAWN beside a critique in which the fault came back
  clear. Moving the measurement moves a fault verdict on eight plans, and which facade the
  measurements describe is a package:
  `oq/the-shutter-fault-clears-on-the-rhythm-while-the-sheet-refuses-the-leaves`.
- **The plan draws no sidelights the elevation draws.** `spec-builder-colonial`'s elevation draws a
  doorcase and a sidelight either side of the plan's 42 in door, and its plan draws the door alone.
  Whether the composition is a plan fact or an elevation fact is a ruling:
  `oq/the-plan-draws-no-sidelights-the-elevation-draws`.
- **A room's name is drawn over its furniture.** 123 of the 267 room-name lines on the sixteen
  plans cross a piece of furniture. The audit published 140 of 270 by a different estimate; both say
  about half. `oq/a-room-name-is-drawn-over-its-furniture`.
- **Amended: `oq/the-placement-carries-no-wall-bands`.** 293 of the 650 furniture pieces drawn
  reach into a wall body, because a piece is seated against the room's rectangle, which is the wall's
  centre line. The audit published 356 of 773 and that did not reproduce; 650 is the furniture
  census's own placed count.

- **The census's own diff** was worked out separately by `--diff` and by the pin test, so the tool a

- **Four more, in the app and the notes:**

  - The JS hinge contract only ever saw doors hung from the low jamb.

  the bench's duty token through `tokens.css` now.
- **The census test's list of JavaScript-backed checks** named fourteen of seventeen, leaving out the
  bench's two and the tracing canvas. It is held to the call graph now, derived transitively, so a
  new check cannot be born outside it.
- **The walk's scale-bar check** matched the `translate(0,` literal on the bar's group. It reads
