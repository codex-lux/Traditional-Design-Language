## I. What was built

**1. Turned assemblies.** An assembly declaring `axis: across-from-the-jamb` is drawn turned, and
that is the whole of the change to how a band is drawn.
- `plate/assemblyLayout.js`: `extentOf` swaps the extent of a turned assembly. Across it is the
  assembly's height, which is the casing's width. Down it is the members' projection off the wall
  plane. The old body is now `wallExtentOf`.
- Frames are grouped by DRAWN height. A turned item needs
  `max(depth / (1 - strip - zone), (behind + depth) / (1 - zone))`, so the three casings stand in
  a frame of their own rather than being squeezed into a wall section's.
- A turned item with no depth is unplaced with a reason, and not drawn at zero size.
- `plate/assemblyPlan.js`: `bandTransform` puts ONE `rotate(90)` between the translate and the
  scale. So a model point (u, v) lands at (X + k·v, P + k·u). The jamb is at the left, the members
  read outward, the profile hangs below a horizontal wall plane, and the wall strip is above it.
- The served paths are untouched. No arc arithmetic reaches JavaScript, the banned-token scan is
  clean, and `OrderPlate` is unchanged.
- Every other mark is placed by orientation through one `at(along, out)` mapping: the strip, the
  chain line, the part ticks, the numerals and the leader knees.
- A turned item's numerals run along x in member order, from the jamb.

**2. Zones.** `zoneString(zones, partIn)` is the ONE reading of a served zone list.
- It returns the differences of the served cumulative `to_parts` joined as `4 + 12 + 3`, the same
  figures in feet and inches through `fmt.js`'s `feetInches16` (`2'-0" + 6'-0" + 1'-6"` on
  `trim-classical`'s Georgian wall at its 108 in module), and one run per zone.
- It returns null where the record gives no division, or where a list is malformed, which
  `check_orders.py` refuses upstream anyway.
- `planFrame` draws the result three ways: a zone dimension line beside the band with a tick at
  each boundary, each zone's figure at its middle (its `<title>` carries the zone's name, parts and
  inches), and the zone string and its inches line under the frame.
- An assembly with no zones gets no line, no figure and no string, and no zone reserve in its
  frame.
- The legend gains a zone entry and the key-terms line gains the `zone` record, both only where
  the frame draws a zone.

**3. Captions.** The foot line under each frame is per frame now, and it is gated.
- *Drawn upright* (`figure-drawn-upright`) appears only where the frame draws something with no
  axis declared.
- *Drawn turned* (`figure-drawn-turned`, written by WP-14.18) appears only where the frame draws
  something turned.
- `data-captions` states which, so the walk can hold the words to what is drawn.
- `glossary/figure-drawn-upright.json` is rewritten for a plate that turns: 45 words, the basis
  quoting the schema's assemblies and `axis` descriptions and the `geo_cas_reveal` note, and `see`
  gaining `figure-drawn-turned` and `assembly-axis`. Every word on the plate is a record.
- The page's `data-refused="zones"` note is now true per assembly. `zonesByAssembly` names the
  assemblies that carry the pack's division (with their string) and, separately, those the record
  gives none. The old sentence, *"nothing in the record says where a zone ends"*, had been false of
  the Georgian wall since WP-14.18.

**4. The class-A slider.**
- `proportions/page.js` carries `BUILDING_INPUTS`, the dimensions a module may be bound to that
  the page carries, each with its address key. `classASlider(module_bound_to)` returns a slider
  only for a dimension marked `bound`.
- Today that is `room_width`, bound by `room-harmonic` alone. `trim-classical`'s module is bound to
  `ceiling_height`, which has had a slider on every pack since WP-5.2, so it gets no second one.
- The slider rides in the address as `?room_width=`, exactly as `?ceiling=` does, and joins
  `FILTER_SPEC` as a reading that widens rather than narrows. Its range, 144 to 360 in, is quoted
  from `room-harmonic`'s own module note (*"12-14 ft for a chamber or a closet, 16-20 ft for a
  principal room, 20-30 ft for a hall or a salone"*).
- `requestFor` sends the input only to the pack whose module IS it. If a pack click carries
  `room_width` along, it would otherwise move another pack's rules with no slider on that page
  saying so.
- No other pack gets a slider it does not bind.

**5. Thumbnails in the pack index.**
- `GET /api/proportions` rows gain `drawing` (the pack page's own word, `corpus.pack_drawing`,
  which the detail route now also reads), `module_bound_to` and `thumb`.
- The thumbnail is the pack's first assembly at the wall datum, from `_wall_assemblies` at the
  module `core.module_binding` gives with no input. It is exactly what the plate draws first,
  trimmed to faces, height and axis.
- `AssemblyThumb` draws it in a 40 × 52 px box under the plate's own transform, via `planThumb`.
  One scale, centred, turned where the record turns it, strokes non-scaling per element.
- A row with no thumbnail keeps the column with a `data-thumb-none` spacer that says why
  (`stack`, or `none` for no assemblies).
- The list is built once per process. `corpus.invalidate()` drops it, and
  `test_invalidate_clears_every_cache_it_claims_to` names it.
- The MCP payload (`core.get_proportions`) is untouched, and `test_pack_plates.py`'s
  `STACKED_DIGEST` holds.

**6. The walk's `⑩c` block** holds all of the above in the browser, every expectation read off the
served payload (§IV).

## II. What was found

**1. The payload stays on the list route, measured.** The body is serialised as Starlette's
`JSONResponse` writes it and gzipped at level 4, the level `GZipExceptSSE` deploys. Before:
**65,799 raw / 20,709 gzipped**. After: **105,740 / 29,360**. That is **8,651 gzipped bytes** for
27 thumbnails. The live route reads 105,740 raw and 29,366 over the wire, the six bytes being the
gzip header. That is well inside the brief's ~60 KB, so there is no `GET /api/proportions/thumbs`,
and `test_pack_thumbs.py` asserts the bound by compressing the served list with and without its
thumbnails.

**2. The list now costs something to build, and is cached for that reason.** Median of 40
interleaved builds: **about 36 ms**. Resolving every pack for `drawing` is about 27 ms of that, and
the 27 thumbnails about 7 to 9 ms. The old list cost 0.02 ms. **A draft of the docstring said
"about 31 ms" for the thumbnails alone. That figure came from a prototype of the function and not
the shipped one, and it was wrong on both counts.** It was corrected before commit, with the
interleaved measurement.

**3. A stacked order gets no thumbnail, which is a stated reading of "each drawable pack".** The
wall datum is `build/profiles.py`'s datum for *"the 27 packs that carry assemblies and no column
stack"*. Measured over the 25 stacked packs:
- their first assembly at the wall datum has **no width at all on 10** (the five `chambers-*`,
  three `palladio-*`, `benjamin-doric` and `greek-doric`);
- **14** are `axis`-datum packs, whose projections are radii from the column's axis, so a wall
  reading of them is OQ 65's error one surface over.

So the index draws a thumbnail for the 27 wall-datum packs, a spacer saying `stack` for the 25
orders, and a spacer saying `none` for the 5 packs with no assemblies. **The draft of the refusal
said 15 `axis`-datum packs. Re-derived it is 14, over the whole corpus as well as over the stacked
packs**, and both docstrings carry 14. An order's thumbnail drawn at its own column datum would be
a second drawing path for orders in the index, and is not built.

**4. `room-harmonic` has no plate to move, so the walk holds what `?room_width=` does move.** The
brief asks that `?room_width=` on `room-harmonic` move the plate. That pack has no assemblies (it
is one of the five), so nothing is drawn to move. The walk holds what the address does move:
- the head's module figure, at 192 and at 288 in, each equal to the served `module_in`;
- the served rules whose value moves between the two widths, found from the payload and not named;
- the slider, resting at the address's width, and moving it writes `?room_width=240` and
  re-dimensions the pack.

**5. The ceiling and opening sliders move nothing on 22 of the 32 non-order packs.** This
package's first draft of the comment beside `BUILDING_INPUTS` said the two older sliders exist
*"(the rules read them)"*. Measured before commit:
- **by expression**, 10 of the 32 non-order packs name `ceiling_height` or `opening_width` in a
  rule, and 22 name neither;
- **by behaviour**, at a 132 in ceiling and a 60 in opening, the ceiling moved 4 packs and the
  opening 8, and 23 moved at neither.

The 23rd is `timber-bay`, whose opening rule is a `floor(...)` that happens not to step between
36 and 60 in. The behavioural figure is a property of the values tried. The expression figure is
the one that says a slider can never move a pack.

The ruling this package executed is about the class-A slider, so the two older sliders are not
gated here: `oq/the-ceiling-and-opening-sliders-show-on-packs-that-read-neither`. The comment
states the measurement.

**6. Closing the casings question would have orphaned its other finding.** That entry recorded
that four assemblies write a horizontal run, a face length or a pier width in `projection_parts`.
It offered a declaration as answer 2, and the ruling took answer 1 alone. The four notes were
re-read and hold (`dutch-gambrel.json:36`, `facade-pavilion.json:78`, `stone-course.json:78`,
`facade-arcade.json:46`).

Every surface draws each figure as a projection, **including from this package the index
thumbnail of `dutch-gambrel`'s first assembly**. It is raised as
`oq/four-assemblies-state-projection-parts-as-a-run-a-face-or-a-width` rather than left inside a
closed entry.

**7. A no-slider check can pass on a slider nobody opened, and the first draft of mine did.** The
building-input sliders live in the filter strip's folded `at` group, which renders its children
only when open. My first `⑩c` asserted `[data-class-a]` absent on `adobe-module` and on
`trim-classical`, and both passed. They passed because the group was closed, so there were no
sliders at all.

**It was caught only because the POSITIVE half failed**: the same selector found no slider on
`room-harmonic` either. The block now opens the group first, and reads the ceiling slider's
presence as the premise of every absence. This is this repository's *a negative assertion over a
locator that missed passes vacuously*, met in a guard of my own.

**8. A figure in a draft docstring had no provenance.** The list's before-size was written as
20,763 gzipped bytes. Nothing run in this package reproduces it: the harness gives 20,709 on an
archive of the base. It is replaced by the re-derived figures in §II.1, with the method stated.

**9. The index JS entry grows 9.4 kB.** The production entry goes from 621.45 kB to 630.86 kB
(gzip 198.09 kB to 201.21 kB), measured by building a `git archive` of `c8ed0c1` beside this tree.
`check_frontend.py` prints it as 617 KB, against its 700,000-byte ceiling.

## V. What was deliberately not done

- **No thumbnail for a stacked order** (§II.3), and **no `/api/proportions/thumbs` route**
  (§II.1: 8,651 gzipped bytes against a ~60 KB budget).
- **The ceiling and opening sliders are not gated** (§II.5). That is a question, not this
  package's ruling.
- **`projection_parts` is not declared on the four assemblies** (§II.6).
- **No zones on the order packs.** That is `oq/six-order-assemblies-state-a-division-and-carry-no-zones`,
  WP-14.18's, and the stacked plate is `OrderPlate`'s, which this package does not touch.
- **`OrderPlate`, `build/profiles.py`, the MCP payload and every order pack are unchanged.** The
  router, citations, `navModel`, `App.jsx`, the journey files and the rail are other lanes' files
  and are not touched. `api/client.js` is not touched: the list route already carried the
  thumbnails, so no new client function was needed.
