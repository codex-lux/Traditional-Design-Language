# WP-11.2 — The diagram reaches the record, and the record is checked against the diagram

*Phase 11, package 2 of 11, the second of the three that needed no ruling. 4 September 2026.
From `docs/reports/tidewater-layout-diagnosis-2026-09-04.md` findings A3, F2 and E3 — the sheet
that says* **6 BAYS OF 10.0 FT** *under a title that says five bays, on a massing that says five;
and a record that had dropped two of its parti's five stacking claims with nothing able to notice.*

## What this package is

Three things, and the first is the mechanism the other two need.

**1. A plan may name the parti it is an instance of.** Plan schema **0.6.0**: `parti`, an id and
never a record — WP-9.4's rule, because a caller-supplied parti RECORD once reached
`geometry.py`'s `bay_module_ft` unchecked and put 114 bays of half a foot on a house. It becomes a
path in exactly one place, `core.load_parti`, which `build/geometry.py::parti_for` calls rather
than copying. `plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json` names
`centre-passage-double-pile`.

**Until this the diagram's own bay module could not reach the placement at all.** The parti states
9 ft; `derive_footprint` used its own 10 ft default; the only route in was a caller passing the
parti separately, and the CLI, the bench's drawing route and both shipped reference plans all did
not.

**2. The massing's own bay count is read, and its parity survives growth.** `four-over-four` says
`bays: "5"` and *"Facade symmetry is a hard constraint, not a preference"*; no code had read
either. `massing_bays()` reads the two forms it can be sure of — a bare integer, a bare range — and
refuses the rest (`"variable"`, `"irregular"`, `"1 per face"`, `"3 (narrow end to street)"`,
`"5-7 main"`), because `main` is doing work in the last of those and a reader that dropped it would
state a fact about the wrong thing. `wants_a_centre_bay()` says whether the diagram needs an odd
count and **why**, preferring the massing's stated count over an inference from the circulation
type. Growth then steps by **two**, so a five-bay house that will not fit becomes seven and never
six, and `geometry_report.bay_count_forced_even` is a named refusal for the case where a lot cannot
hold the odd count.

The shipped Tidewater plan is now placed **seven bays** wide instead of six. It has a middle bay
for the first time.

**3. `build/check_plans.py` holds a hand-authored plan to the parti it names**, and is in
`check_all` (45 checks in the loop, 48 in the run — read the second number). It found what it was
built for on the first run.

## What was found

**1. THE RECORD HAD STOPPED DESCRIBING ITS OWN DIAGRAM AND NOTHING COULD SEE IT.** The parti states
five `stacks_over` claims; the shipped plan carried three. The two missing were **the upper passage
over the lower passage** and **the landing over the stair** — the two that organise the upper
floor. `plan_check`'s stacking layer reads that field faithfully and reports a broken stack as
`serious`; with the field absent it had nothing to read, so the sheet drew a landing over the
library and an upper passage across the entrance front and no layer of the critic said a word.

It could not have been noticed. `compose.py` copies every `stacks_over` a parti states onto the
candidates it emits, so a COMPOSED plan is right by construction — and nothing checked a plan the
composer did not write. **Both shipped reference plans are hand-authored.** The two claims are back
in the record.

**2. One kind of disagreement is REPORTED and not failed, and the reason is decision #3.** The
checker also found the plan's back hall on N/S where the parti says E/N, and its kitchen on N/S/W
where the parti says E/N. Those are not defects: the record's back hall is a **hyphen** (the two
long sides of a link) and its kitchen a **detached dependency**, and the parti has neither.
`exterior_walls` is not topology — `geometry_cp.py`'s own docstring says the field *"speaks
EXPOSURE in the fully-massed house"* — and decision #3 puts massing outside a parti's authority.
Erasing them to quieten the checker would delete the record's own account of the house. They are
counted against a ceiling of two, which goes to zero when WP-11.6 gives the diagram the elements
its exemplars have.

**3. THE PARTI'S BAY MODULE CONTRADICTS ITS OWN EXEMPLARS, AND NOTHING HAD EVER READ IT TO FIND
OUT.** 9 ft, against Gunston Hall's measured 12.17 (60'-10" over five bays) and the Wythe House's
10.8. The style's own `typical_ratios` says *"Facade 48–72 ft, five or seven bays"* — 9.6 to 14.4
ft a bay at five — and its `bay_rhythm` says *"Five or seven bays, unaccented, the centre marked
only by the doorway."* All prose, all unread. Raised as
`oq/the-partis-bay-module-contradicts-its-own-exemplars` rather than patched: a single module on a
diagram serving fifteen styles cannot be right for all of them, 9 ft is the folk end of a range
whose polite end is twelve, and that is `oq/register-is-not-style` arriving in the placer.

**4. THE COST, MEASURED AT EIGHT SEEDS, AND IT IS NOT ALL ONE WAY.** Naming the parti moves the
footprint from 60.0 x 40.1 (six bays of 10 ft) to 63.0 x 38.2 (seven of 9 ft).

| footprint | fatal findings, 8 seeds | mean | score mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 x 10 ft — before | 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 8, 8, 10 | **6.2** | 692 |
| 7 x 9 ft — after | 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10, 11 | **9.2** | 662 |
| 7 x 10 ft — odd count, old module | 5, 9, 9, 10, 10, 10, 11, 12 | **9.5** | 693 |

**The odd bay count costs about three fatal findings whichever module it takes; the 9 ft module
buys thirty points of score at the same count.** Every one of those fatals is an unreachable room —
a door the hill-climb could not realise — and the cost is that engine's: on CP-SAT at a budget
that reaches its second phase the same plan reports **zero** fatal findings before and after, with
serious 73 → 67, transfer beams 30 → 17 and undrawn window units 27 → 20.

**This package accepts that trade and states it rather than hiding it.** A house with a centre bay
and nine unreachable-room findings from a search that cannot realise doors is closer to the type
than a house with no centre bay and six: the findings are the critic telling the truth about a
search result, and the missing middle bay was the type's one non-negotiable move. The cause is the
container — twelve enclosed rooms in a four-room box — which is
`oq/the-parti-dissolved-its-own-dependencies`, ruled and unbuilt, and WP-11.6's.

**5. AND THE INSTRUMENT WAS WRONG BEFORE THE PACKAGE WAS.** The first before-and-after was taken at
one seed and read 3 → 8, which is decisive-looking and meaningless: at three seeds the same
footprints returned 3–8 and 8–10, and at eight they returned two overlapping distributions whose
means differ by three. **A single-seed comparison of a hill-climb number compares two draws.**
`build/diagnose_sheet.py --seeds` reports every column as a spread now, and the six-number baseline
rule in `PLAN-OF-ACTION.md` Phase 11 says so. This is WP-9.6's rule — ratchet the deterministic
figures, never the ones that drift — one layer up.

**6. Two of this package's own guards were blind, and both were caught by mutation.** A test
asserting that every bay count in the growth loop is odd **passed with the step forced back to
one**, because `grown` is EMPTY on the shipped plan — the starting count already satisfies the
depth test, and `all(... for b in [])` is True. And deleting the massing-count reader *entirely*
left every assertion green, because on this plan the area arithmetic already returns seven and the
floor never binds. Both are fixed with fixtures that enter the code under test: a program enlarged
until the loop runs, and one shrunk to 40% until the floor binds at five bays where the area asks
for three. **A reader that never binds is a reader nobody can tell is there.**

**7. NINE TESTS MOVED, AND TWO OF THEM WERE WRONG BEFORE THIS PACKAGE TOUCHED THEM.**

*The one that matters.* `tests/test_drawn_geometry.py` called `rf.build_roof(plan,
st.build_section(plan))` in three places — and `build_roof(plan, parti=None, section=None)` binds
a positional second argument to **parti**, so each of those handed a SECTION in as the parti and
build_roof then derived a second section of its own from it. Two buildings, one test: the roof was
built over one house and the assertions read the footprint of another. It was invisible for as long
as nothing in a parti reached the footprint — both sections came out identical — and WP-11.2 is
exactly the change that made a parti reach it. That is WP-6.4's *"a drawing set is ONE building"*,
inside a test written after WP-6.4.

*The one that was a coincidence.* `test_hip_ridge_is_shorter_than_the_gable_ridge_by_the_depth`
pinned the ridge length at `rel_tol=1e-6`. The ridge's two endpoints are each rounded to a
hundredth of a foot (`D/2` and `W - D/2`), so their difference equals `W - D` only when `D/2`
lands on a hundredth — true of the old 40.08 ft depth (20.04 exactly), false of 40.75 (20.375),
which loses half a hundredth at each end. The tolerance is the record's own rounding now.

*And one pin was replaced by a derivation rather than a number.* The blind-bay test read
`blind_bay_centres_ft == [21.33]`, a literal that moves with the footprint. It reads the roof's own
stack axis now, so what it actually holds — that the blind bay's centre and the stack's axis are
ONE number, which is what OQ 85 closed — cannot go stale with the next footprint.

The six that were ordinary pin moves: the relaxation count 7 → 5 in three files, the elevation
front's bay count 5 → 7, the dormer-rhythm bay count 4 → 5, and the spec plan's serious findings
56 → 57. **The elevation move is worth reading twice**: that layer derives its own odd bay count
from `facade-classical`'s window-grouping rule against the face's real width, and before this
package it said FIVE while the footprint said SIX. Nothing compares them. They agree at seven now
by coincidence of one plan, not by mechanism.

**8. AND A RATCHET WENT UP, WHICH IS RECORDED RATHER THAN ABSORBED.**
`tests/test_furniture_drawn.py`'s along-axis ceiling moves **69 → 74**: five more placed rooms fail
an essential item along their length in the new footprint. Across is unmoved at 86. That file's own
docstring says *"If it RAISES them, the placement got worse and the build should say so rather
than absorb it"* — so the ceiling comment carries the whole account, names 69 as the WP-9.6
measurement, and says this is the first time either number has gone up.

**9. One plan got better and it cost a test its subject.** `spec-builder-colonial` flagged an
over-capacity clear span until this package and flags **none** after: its massing states five
bays, the odd-count rule took it from six to seven, and more bay lines means more walls on the
grid, more bearing lines and shorter spans. Two tests asserted on that plan's flagged span and
would have gone quietly blind; they read the Tidewater plan (which still flags two) and assert the
spec plan's zero explicitly, so the improvement is pinned rather than lost.

## What was deliberately not done

**The bay module was not changed.** It is a data question with a register axis in it, and the entry
records the three shapes an answer could take. Moving 9 to 12 would fix one number against
exemplars whose program this diagram does not share.

**`spec-builder-colonial` names no parti.** Its rooms are a modern colonial's (`foyer`, `family`,
`study`) and match no diagram in the catalogue by id. Naming one would be asserting a claim to make
a checker busier; it is UNJUDGED, counted, and says so.

**The CP default budget was not raised**, though the measurement here says it should be looked at:
at 25 s the new footprint does not solve at all (UNKNOWN, and `auto` falls back to the hill-climb
saying why), at 60 s it reaches phase A, and at 120 s it reaches phase B and the compositional
objective finally runs. That is the open half of
`oq/a-proof-of-feasibility-is-not-a-proof-of-composition` and it belongs to WP-11.8, with the
infrastructure audit's finding that evaluate is the whole server's bound sitting against it.

**`check_plans.py` does not check dimensions**, in either direction. Decision #3: a parti specifies
topology and roles only.
