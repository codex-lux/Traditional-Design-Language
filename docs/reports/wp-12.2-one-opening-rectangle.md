# WP-12.2 — One opening rectangle, three callers, and the storey six houses do not have

*9 September 2026. The brief is `docs/prd/phase-12-the-sheet-in-the-round.md` §8. The subject is
the arithmetic that says where an opening is on a face — written out three times, and already the
cause of one shipped disagreement between two of the surfaces that read it. Lifting it into one
function then removed a row of windows from six houses that nobody had noticed being drawn.*

---

## What was built

- **`build/elevation.py::opening_rects(elev, face)`** — the one spelling of `(x0, x1, sill, head)`
  **and of the loop around it**. Inches throughout, x along the face from its own left edge, y
  above grade, deliberately unrounded. Returns `{"rects": [...], "refused": [...]}`; a bay that
  draws nothing is refused with a reason and the record path that could not answer it, never
  silently absent.
- **Three transcriptions removed, and one of them was not in the brief.**
  `render_elevation._window` for a sash, `render_elevation._entrance` for the door — the one the
  PRD did not know about — and `export_dxf._win`. Four consumers read the function now: those
  three, and `build/scene.py::_openings`, which turns each rectangle into an `opening-frame`
  solid.
- **`tests/test_opening_rects.py`** — 10 tests, 9 mutations, all 9 red from a green baseline.
- **A whole row of windows removed from six one-storey houses**, which both renderers had been
  drawing because each resolved the upper storey with a fallback to the ground one.
- Three further defects found and fixed, all in WP-12.1: an opening extruded out of its wall
  instead of into it, the same error in the gable ends with its own test ratifying it, and a
  `bounds` block that was the right size in the wrong place.

**THE FIRST VERSION OF THIS TABLE HAD ONLY THE SHIPPED-PLAN ROWS, AND THEY SAY THE LIFT CHANGED
ALMOST NOTHING.** Eleven of the sixteen plan records build an elevation, at four faces apiece, and
over all 44 plates the lift removes **120 window rectangles** — an entire row of openings from
each of six one-storey houses. Finding 1 below is what they were. `CLAUDE.md` states the rule this
broke in as many words: *verifying a corpus-wide change on the plans that happen to ship is
verifying it on 2 of 164 styles*, and both shipped plans are two-storey. **The DXF has no ties at
all** — it never leaves inches — so its 24 movers are exactly those six plans and nothing
else.

**Every remaining movement is one decimal tie, on four plates** — the spec Colonial's and
`bad-03`'s gable ends, 0.1 px on one coordinate apiece. The true value there is **exactly
203.35 px**, and `f"{x:.1f}"` resolves a tie by whichever side of it the double happens to land
on: `cx + w/24` in feet lands one ulp above and prints 203.4, `(cx·12 + w/2)/12` in inches lands
one ulp below and prints 203.3. Neither is more correct, and 0.1 px at 24 px/ft is 0.05 in of
building — a fifth of the finest pen the sheet draws.

**The unit was then chosen by measurement rather than by taste.** Over every one of the 144
edges the two shipped plans actually draw — 72 openings, two edges apiece:

| rectangle computed in | SVG printed coordinates that move | DXF coordinates that move |
|---|---|---|
| **inches (shipped)** | 2 | **0** |
| feet | **0** | 64 |

Inches is also the unit the record states every opening in, so it is one conversion rather than
two. A feet-first rectangle would have bought two printed pixels at the price of sixty-four CAD
coordinates.

**The first version of that table said 216, 2, 0 and 100, and the denominator was wrong.** It swept
every combination of bay centre and stated opening width, which counts a door's width at every bay
of every face — a superset of what is drawn. Re-derived over the rectangles `opening_rects` really
returns, it is 144 and the DXF cost of the feet-first spelling is 64. The direction and the
conclusion are unchanged and the number was not: *re-derive, do not re-read*, and quote one
denominator.

## What was found

### 1. Six of the eleven plans that draw an elevation were drawing a storey they do not have

Both renderers resolved the upper storey the same way —
`upper = next((s for s in section["storeys"] if s.get("index") == 1), ground)`, falling back to
the GROUND storey where there is no second one — and then drew a second row of windows at that
datum unconditionally, using the upper storey window's own sill and head above floor. On a
one-storey house that is **a whole row of openings the record does not hold**, standing above the
real row in the cornice zone.

**Measured across the plan corpus: eleven plans build an elevation and six of them state only
storey 0** — `bad-01`, `bad-02`, `bad-07`, `good-02`, `good-03` and `good-07`. `good-02`'s
south elevation drew **eleven** `op` rectangles where the record holds six, with eighteen shutters
where it holds eight and sixty panel rectangles where it holds thirty. Over the 24 affected
plates, **244 window rectangles become 124**.

`opening_rects` derives each storey's floor datum from the section's own storeys and refuses where
there is none, so the row cannot be drawn. **And the refusal carries the right reason of two**: a
storey the section does not state at all is a fact about the BUILDING — *"the section states 1
storey(s), so this building has no storey 1 for an opening to stand in"* — while a storey that
exists without a `grade_to_floor_ft` is a fact about the RECORD. They call for different actions,
so they do not share a message, which is WP-11.4's rule about a refusal with one message for
several causes.

**It cost nothing on either shipped plan, because both are two-storey.** That is why this
package's first measurement, taken over those two, reported that the lift changed nothing but four
pixels — and it is `CLAUDE.md`'s own standing warning arriving in ordinary work: *verifying a
corpus-wide change on the plans that happen to ship is verifying it on 2 of 164 styles.* The fix
was to widen the harness, not to reason about the code.

### 2. The blind bay is unreachable from the corpus, so the guard had to be driven

The defect this whole lift is about is OQ 85's: when the blind bay arrived, the SVG learned to
skip the bay a chimney stack stands on and **the DXF did not**, so the CAD file went on drawing a
window through a chimney. That is the case a parity test most wants to make.

**Swept over all sixteen plan records: zero blind bays.** The reason is dated and specific —
WP-11.4 moved this house's stacks off the gable centre line and onto the flues the plan states, to
11.77 and 14.21 ft, against bay centres of 6.79, 20.38 and 33.96 on a half-stack of 0.92 ft. The
centre bay a stack used to stand on is a plain window bay again.

So a parity test that rendered the shipped plans would have passed **with the skip deleted**, which
is WP-8.11's fixture rule arriving on the one branch that has already failed in production.
`test_a_blind_bay_is_skipped_by_all_three_callers` blinds a bay by hand and reads the delta from
three surfaces at once — the function, the SVG and the DXF — with `test_the_scene_drops_the_same_
bay` beside it for the fourth, and both assert a positive count first, because a selector that
matches nothing makes a delta of zero look like a delta of zero.
`test_no_shipped_plan_reaches_a_blind_bay` asserts the premise, so the day a plan grows one the
suite says so rather than the driven fixture quietly becoming redundant.

**And it makes a sentence in `CLAUDE.md` stale in the flattering direction.** That file says the
generator publishes `count_of_openings_on_the_axis_of_a_chimney_stack` as *"a MEASURED zero — it
resolved a collision, it did not fail to have one"*. On the shipped plan today it is a measured
zero because **there is no collision to resolve**: nothing is blinded, no `blind_bay_reason`
reaches any sheet, and the stacks simply miss the bay centres. The number is the same and what it
means is not, which is the shape this corpus calls a fake pass one level up. Corrected in place.

### 3. An opening stood proud of its wall, and my own record was ambiguous

`at` was written on the OUTSIDE face of each wall with a `thickness` that is always positive, so
the extrusion ran outward from there. On the south and west walls that is inward and correct by
accident; on the north and east it puts the frame **outside the building**. It rendered as a house
with blocks stuck to two of its faces.

The fix is a contract rather than a correction, and it is in the schema: **`at` is the LOW face and
the extrusion always runs along the plane's POSITIVE axis**, so a viewer never has to know which
side of the building a face is on.

### 4. The same error was in WP-12.1's gable ends, and WP-12.1's own test ratified it

Fixing the openings' contract moved the gable ends by one wall thickness, and
`test_a_gable_stands_on_the_face_it_names` went red — **not because the fix was wrong but because
the test had been written against the wrong contract.** It asserted the gable's `at` against the
OUTER face of the wall extent, which is exactly where the old, wrong `at` put it. So WP-12.1
shipped both gables standing 1.29 ft clear of the east and north walls — a ledge above the eave,
where a reader looks — with a green test saying each gable *"stands on the face it names"*.

It reads the interval from both sides now: a gable must occupy the same slab of space as the
exterior wall it stands on, so a shift in either direction fails.

**A test written against the wrong contract is worse than no test**, because it converts the
defect into a claim. The durable form: when a package introduces a contract, the test must assert
the contract's own statement, not a consequence that happens to hold under the version being
written.

### 5. `bounds` was the right size in the wrong place, and no agreement figure could see it

The frame's origin is the CLEAR SW corner — the plan frame with z added — so an exterior wall grows
outward to `-t` and the outside envelope starts negative. WP-12.1 stated `min: [0, 0, 0]` and a
`max` taken off the section's OUTSIDE footprint: declared `[0, 65.58]` against a drawn
`[-1.292, 64.292]`. The right size, offset by one exterior wall thickness.

**All three agreement figures accepted it**, because every one of them compares the walls, the
slabs and the datums to each other and **not one of them reads `bounds`**. Nothing in the record
disagreed with itself; a viewer framing the model from it would simply have drawn the house off
centre and nobody could have said why. It was found by printing the openings' own `at` beside the
bounds — the openings are the first solids whose position along the wall normal appears in the
record as a number of its own.

`test_every_solid_lies_inside_the_declared_bounds` is a **containment and not an equality**:
`section.footprint` is rounded to two places, so the stated frame and the drawn solids differ by
up to 0.004 ft, and rounding one to the other to make an equality hold would be OQ 48's error in a
new place. The tolerance is 0.01 ft — two and a half times the residue, three hundred times
smaller than the defect it caught. The extent helper it uses understands every primitive in the
schema and **raises on one it does not**, rather than skipping it: a containment test blind to
`plane` and `extrude` would have said nothing about the two things this phase has added and the
two that have gone wrong.

### 6. `docs/export.md` described two differences between the DXF and the SVG and neither exists

It said window openings are drawn at true recorded width in the DXF because *"the SVG shrinks to
0.9× for legibility"*, and door openings at the door's own width because *"the SVG uses a fixed
3 ft"*. Measured on `tidewater-georgian-careful`: the plan SVG draws its window glazing at 2.0,
2.5, 3.0, 3.5 and 4.0 ft against a record stating exactly those five figures, and the record
carries **eight** distinct door widths from 2.2 to 6 ft, each placed at its own size by
`build/openings.py`. WP-6.2 gave every opening a placed width and both surfaces began reading it;
nobody went back to the sentence describing the difference.

**The test the paragraph cited could never have caught it**:
`test_windows_drawn_at_true_width_not_the_svg_shrink` asserts that the DXF's drawn widths are a
SUBSET of the recorded ones, which is true whether or not the SVG shrinks. It pins one half of a
claim about two surfaces. That is WP-6.4's *"until X lands is a lie the moment X lands"* wearing a
citation.

### 7. Four dead locals, reading the very fields the lift removed

The first pass of the lift left `door_w_in, door_h_in = ent["door_leaf_width_in"],
ent["door_leaf_height_in"]` and a `cx`/`floor_ft` pair standing unused at the top of `_entrance`,
three lines above a rectangle already carrying all four numbers. Nothing used them and nothing
would have failed. **A dead local that reads the record field the lift was supposed to centralise
is how a second spelling grows back**, so the source guard is written against reads and not
against arithmetic.

**And the guard had to state a rule rather than name a file.** Its first version refused those
fields anywhere in the three callers and fired on the elevation sheet's own legend, which PRINTS
the stated opening width in its caption. Exempting `render_elevation.py` would have been fitting
the corpus that exists; the rule is that outside `build/elevation.py` one of these fields may be
**printed and never computed with**, so the guard walks up from each read to see whether it is
inside an f-string interpolation. It also asserts that the legend really does print two of them,
so the exemption is exercised rather than merely available.

**A second sweep after the guard was green found four more, and one of them was still a live
transcription.** The guard is written against the record's own field NAMES, so it cannot see a
number the caller derives by a different route: `export_dxf`'s door branch was drawing its leaf
from `floor1`, a `ground["grade_to_floor_ft"] * IN` computed at the top of the function, while
the rectangle beside it carried `sill_in` — the same number by derivation, read twice, which is
exactly how two readings stop being the same number. The other three were dead: `gw, uw` and
`floor2` in `export_dxf`, and `floor1_ft` in `render_elevation`. All four removed, and all 16
plates re-derived afterwards to prove nothing moved. **A source guard on field names is a guard
on one route in; read the diff for the others.**

### 8. A condition both renderers carried, and its false side cannot be reached

`opening_rects` draws a door only at the ground storey of the entrance face; on any other face a
bay the record calls a door draws a window. Both renderers already did this and the lift carried
it across rather than correcting it, because which faces carry a door is the elevation's judgment
and not the rectangle's.

**Measured, the false side of that condition is unreachable from this generator.** `_face_bays`
writes `kinds[mid] = "door"` only under `has_entrance`, and `has_entrance` is `f ==
entrance_face`, so a door bay can only ever be on the entrance front. Swept over all sixteen plan
records: **44 faces built, 11 door bays, 0 of them off the entrance front.**

It is **kept**, and the distinction is the one this corpus draws elsewhere: it is a fallback, not
a check. A check that cannot fire is a hazard, because its greenness is read as a verdict — the CI
id-collision gate that walked complements is this repository's own example. A fallback that cannot
fire is a decision about a malformed record, and deleting this one would draw a door on a wall
with no entrance composition to dress it, which is worse than drawing a window. So it is NAMED in
its own comment, with the sweep, and DRIVEN by a test, because nothing in the corpus can reach it.

**That is the second unreachable branch in this one function** — the blind bay is the other — and
both were found by asking what the corpus actually produces rather than by reading the condition.

### 9. The mutations

Nine against the lift, four against the scene, every one verified to have LANDED by reading the
file back before the suite ran:

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| delete the blind-bay skip | **2 failed** |
| round the rectangle to 3 places | 1 failed |
| a rect loses its source | 1 failed |
| the door branch applies on every face | 1 failed |
| an unstated storey window is silently skipped | 1 failed |
| the SVG derives its own rectangle again | 1 failed |
| the DXF walks the face's own bays again | 1 failed |
| the scene stops recording a refusal | 1 failed |
| the upper storey falls back to the ground one | 1 failed |
| the gable returns to the outer face | **2 failed** |
| `bounds` returns to WP-12.1's frame | **2 failed** |

The harness's own landed-assertion had a defect worth recording: it read `new in back and old not
in back`, which is false for an **insertion** mutation whose replacement contains the original. It
reported MUTATION DID NOT LAND on a mutation that had landed perfectly. `back != src and new in
back` is the form that holds for both.

## What was deliberately not done

- **No boolean subtraction.** An opening is a FRAME in the wall's own plane, not a hole cut in the
  wall. This layer does no CSG, and calling a frame a hole would be a claim about the geometry that
  the record does not support.
- **A dormer's window is not one of these.** It sits on a roof plane at a position `dormers()`
  computed — the same SHAPE, a different rectangle — so it is built where it is used and says so,
  rather than being smuggled through the face's own list.
- **The door-on-a-non-entrance-face behaviour is preserved, not corrected.** Both renderers already
  drew a window there; which faces carry a door is the elevation's judgment and not this
  function's. It is pinned as a test so that the preservation is a decision rather than an
  oversight.
- **The four moved SVG plates are not chased.** Each moves one coordinate by 0.1 px on a value
  that is exactly a decimal tie, and reproducing a float artefact to keep a byte count would make
  the function's unit choice a lie. The measurement above says which unit is right.
- **Sashes, muntins, sills, shutters and surrounds.** WP-12.6 dresses the envelope; what is here is
  the opening's extent, which is what the elevation determines.
- **No open question is raised, and that is a decision rather than an omission.** Every finding
  above was either fixed here or settled by a measurement in this report: the unit by the 144-edge
  sweep, the four moved plates by the decimal tie, the two unreachable branches by the
  corpus-wide sweeps that name them, the three WP-12.1 defects by their fixes, and the two prose
  claims by the figures that falsify them. Raising a question whose answer is already in the
  record would put a decision in the register that nobody has to make — which is the reasoning
  WP-12.1 used to decline a stair question, and it holds here for the same reason.
