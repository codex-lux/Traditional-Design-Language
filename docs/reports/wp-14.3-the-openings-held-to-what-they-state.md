# WP-14.3 — the openings, the entrance, the section and the roof held to what they state

*Phase 14, the ink held to its plates. Built 27 September 2026. The code is one commit; this report
and its verification are the commit after it.*

WP-14.1 built the instrument and WP-14.2 took the orders and mouldings to zero. This package is
everything else a building sheet draws except the plan: the elevation's weights, windows, heads,
entrance and small figures; the section's walls and floors; the roof plan's stacks; and the DXF
and the scene, which have to draw what the plate draws. It moved the census **from 203 known
disagreements to 36**, and the 36 left are two checks that are not this package's: V2 (21), which
waits on Lucas's ruling on decision 4, and PL1 (15), which is WP-14.4's.

The 203 is the census after the correction to WP-14.1's style sweep (WP-14.1's report, §VII). That
correction was found by this package, and is described in §III.

The rule is the phase's: **draw what the record states, say what it does not, and invent
nothing.**

## I. What was fixed, step by step

### Step 1 — the weight ladder (V1, 44 → 0)

In `render_elevation._style_block` the rung rules (`.w-hair` to `.w-ground`) came before the class
rules (`.sh`, `.mt`, `.rf`, `.wt`, `.dr`, `.ch`). Both are one class, so the cascade decides by
source order, and every class rule silently beat the rung written on the element. The meeting
rail, "the thickest bar in the window", drew at the muntin's weight under its own `w-med`. The
rungs come last now. V1 reads the COMPUTED width, which is the only reading that can see an
ordering defect: the attribute says what was asked for.

### Step 2 — one set of numbers per opening (V4 24 → 0, V5 27 → 0)

Since WP-13.3 each window rectangle has been the plan's placed width, while its lights, shutter
leaves and head were worked out once per storey at the width the storey was sized at. So a 42 in
opening was divided into the lights of a 38.6 in one and flanked by shutters cut for it, leaves
covering 56 to 122 per cent of the window they close over. `elevation.sash_at` is the one
spelling of sash-light's arithmetic at one width. `opening_rects` calls it at each opening's own
drawn width, and the SVG, the DXF and the scene all read the rectangle. The storey's pack width
travels beside it, and the plate says when the window drawn is not the one the storey was sized
at.

### Step 3 — the sash as the members that make it, and the heads as circles

**No surface had drawn a sash.** The plate divided the whole opening into equal rectangles with
lines of no stated width, and drew the meeting rail as a line; the DXF did the same; the scene
laid bars across the whole opening. sash-light states every member. `elevation.sash_layout` lays
them out once for all three surfaces:
- the jambs, "about 1 1/2 in" across both sides, so 0.75 in a side, marked approximate;
- 2 in stiles, a 2 in top rail and a 3 in bottom rail;
- each sash's own 1 1/4 in meeting rail, meeting at mid-height;
- 7/8 in muntins dividing the GLASS those members leave, so each light is exactly sash-light's
  own "resulting light width".

A new census check, V14, reads the members and the light widths off the ink: 27 sheets, all
agreeing.

**Checking V14 found a hairline of glass through the frame on 15 sheets.** Each member rectangle
rounded its origin and its width separately, so two members computed to meet exactly were
written up to 0.01 px apart. The opening, written to a tenth of a pixel beside members written to
a hundredth, missed the jambs by up to 0.05 px. Every abutting rectangle is written from its
rounded edges now.

**The heads (V7, 13 → 0).** A segmental head was a quadratic Bezier (a parabola). Its extrados was
the soffit moved up with vertical ends, and its joints radiated to the gauged flat arch's
60-degree strike point from the chord. It is the circle through both springings and the crown
now, with a concentric extrados, radial skewbacks, and joints on radii from the soffit to the
extrados. A flat arch's camber is a circular arc too. The rise is brick-course's own rule
evaluated at each opening's own width (`opening_width / 8` segmental, `/ 96` flat), where it had
been the storey's. `tests/test_arched_heads.py` evaluates the pack's expression rather than
transcribing it.

**The window surround (V16, new).** The plan said a casing is drawn wherever the resolved surround
variant states one. Measured over the 41 styles the elevation draws, **no style states one it can
draw**:
- 22 make a flat architrave and a bare masonry reveal both canonical, and the slot does not say
  which the house has;
- 19 make no surround canonical.

The first is refused, on the sheet and in the scene, naming the slot. The second is the ordinary
answer and is not said. All 22 are masonry walls reading `georgian-colonial-american`, whose own
rule sentence gives a masonry wall no surround at all, which is what is drawn. Its note says the
two slots were never split (`oq/the-window-surround-slots-were-never-split`).

### Step 4 — the entrance (V3 19 → 0, V6 4 → 0, V8 11 → 0, X1 5 → 0)

- **The transom.** The record had dimensioned a rectangular transom since WP-5 and fed it to two
  faults, and no surface drew it (`oq/the-record-dimensions-a-transom-and-no-drawing-draws-one`,
  closed). Where the kit makes the rectangular multi-light transom canonical, it is drawn at
  opening-proportion's height. That height is marked judgment, so the plate labels it one; its
  lights are sash-light's count. A canonical fanlight is refused by name, because no record states
  its rise, and so is a kit that makes more than one form canonical. Of the 41 drawn styles, 9 draw
  the transom and 10 refuse a fanlight and say why.
- **Judgments out of the measurements.** `transom_height_in` (and its width and head rise) and
  `pilaster_projection_in` are `judgment: true` rules. They were published as measurements on 11
  plans. They are `NOT_MODELLED` now, with the reason, so the two transom faults and the pilaster
  fault are unjudged rather than judged on a figure the corpus declines to settle.
- **The garage door.** Census V6 found a 192 in garage door drawn as six raised panels. The rect
  never carried the door's `type`, so the renderer's garage branch could not fire. **And on three
  reference plans (bad-02, bad-03, bad-07) the garage door is the widest door on the entrance
  front, so it was the rect carrying `entrance`.** The SVG, the DXF and the scene each composed a
  Gibbs doorcase, two sidelights and a transom around it. It is drawn as its opening on all three
  now, and the sheet says so.
- **The DXF draws the sidelights the SVG draws** (X1), and the transom with its judgment in XDATA.
- **The scene dresses the door carrying `entrance`**, the condition the SVG and the DXF test. It
  took the first door on the face, which is the entrance on every shipped plan only by the order
  `opening_rects` emits.

### Step 6 — the small figures (V11 3 → 0)

- A keystone the kit makes canonical with no stated width fell back to 0.6 x the arch's depth,
  under a comment promising a legend line that did not exist. A stack with no stated plan size
  fell back to 22 in. Both are refused now, and the legend says so. No shipped record or drawn
  style reaches either fallback, so both are driven with their premise asserted, and V9 was re-cut
  to judge the record rather than the ink (§III).
- The modillion anchors subtracted the plate's pixel origin divided by the scale from face feet.
  That put every tooth 23 in off its bay. It was latent: on all 41 drawn styles the eave's
  modillion states no width, so every band is drawn solid, and the fix is driven with one.
- Every Tidewater sill and head misses the 2.75 in brick courses drawn behind it: S 10 of 10 and
  N 10 of 10 by up to 1.00 in, W 2 of 2 by up to 0.60 in. The sheet says so, and names
  brick-course's rule of whole courses. Snapping them is
  `oq/the-openings-are-not-set-to-the-brick-courses`.

### Step 7 — the section and the roof (S1 16 → 0, F1 1 → 0)

- The section drew the exterior walls as lines of no thickness against a stated 15.5 in
  (Tidewater) or 8 in. They are bodies of the stated thickness now, inward from the outside face,
  grade to eave. The floor structure is drawn at the depth each storey states (15.35 and 13.95 in
  on the Tidewater plan), and the ridge note wraps instead of being cut at 70 characters.
- The roof plan drew the stacks as dots 11 in across against a stated 22 in. They are squares of
  the stated size now. That size is a judgment ("the mason will build 18 or 27"), and the plate
  says so through `disclosures.stack_plan_judgment`, the spelling the plan sheet uses.

## III. What the instrument got wrong

- **The census's style sweep drew one style 159 times.** It swapped each style in through
  `declared.style`, which nothing reads. Found here, because the transom drawn for `adam-style` was
  rectangular where its kit makes a radiating fanlight canonical. Corrected in its own commit
  (WP-14.1's report, §VII): the census was re-derived on the WP-14.2 tree, 255 → 203, and the
  question put to Lucas on decision 4 was re-derived with it (21 of 41 drawn styles, not 79 of
  159).
- **V6 counted a shutter's panels as the garage door's** (10 against 6) and counts inside the leaf
  now. Corrected in the same commit.
- **V14 is right about a defect nobody had seen**: the rounding slivers in §I, found the first time
  it ran. It reads glass as the gaps between members at the print's own precision, and the
  tolerance was not widened to make it pass.
- **V9 lost its population to this package's own fix.** It asked whether the keystone's and the
  stack's fallbacks were said, and it counted only a sheet that drew one. This package draws
  neither, so the population went empty exactly where the new behaviour lives, and a driven test
  planting an unsized stack found no row at all. Found by WP-14.4. V9 now judges every sheet whose
  record carries a keystone or a stack: it agrees only if nothing is drawn at a size no record
  gives and the sheet says what it did not draw. It reads 19 sheets, all agreeing.
- **The whole suite's first run found thirteen reds the targeted tests had not, and one was a
  regression.** To carry each stack's plan size onto the roof plan, `roof.py` had grouped the
  stacks by flue, which `tests/test_hearths_on_flue.py` forbids: a second grouping of the hearths
  by flue is what WP-13.2 removed from that file. The size is one figure for the house
  (`threshold.py` reads it once from the style's cascade and writes it on every stack), so it is
  read once now, and three mutations bite. The other twelve were guards this package moved on
  purpose, V9 among them. Each was attributed against the parent and re-cut against its property,
  never loosened:
  - the dormer and DXF doorcase tests selected on markup the sash-as-members replaced, and now
    read the two meeting-rail members either side of their join and the polyline that frames the
    door;
  - the flat arch's soffit is an arc, and its test accepts only the arc;
  - the refusal table grew by exactly the four withheld names (36 to 40), and
    `fanlight-before-its-date` left the list of passes on a generator constant (24 to 23 on the
    Tidewater plan) because it is unjudged now, naming `transom_head_rise_in` as refused. The test
    asserts that reading from the unjudged bucket rather than letting the count speak;
  - a forbidden-slot control went vacuous, since three of its six measurements are now withheld
    for every style; they are held absent on both styles and refused by name, and each forbidden
    slot keeps a measurement in the differential;
  - the elevation reads the two window-surround slots, so the research walk counts them:
    `measured_unsourced_read` 299 to 304 and `editorial_read` 78 to 80, exactly the seven
    parameters on those slots, counted by name before the ceilings moved, and CLAUDE.md's two
    prose counts followed;
  - the plate says two things are not drawn now, the openings and the undecided surround, so the
    test of the openings' line selects it by its own words rather than by "NOT DRAWN".
- **Four of this package's own drafts were wrong, each caught before it was committed:**
  - a code comment put a sentence inside quotation marks as brick-course's that is not in the
    pack;
  - the 13 heads were described as 13 segmental arches, when one is a keystoned flat arch at its
    kit's 4 to 6 in rise;
  - a comment cited `tests/test_elevation_small_fixes.py` as the driven guard for the modillion
    anchors, and the file did not exist;
  - a driven test of the scene's door choice caught the exception its thin fixture raised, so it
    passed either way.

  The file is written now, the test builds a fixture complete enough to reach the door, and every
  quoted sentence was re-read against the record it names.

## IV. What was deliberately not done

- **Decision 4 (step 5).** Refusing what a style's resolved kit forbids waits on Lucas. On the
  corrected sweep, 21 of the 41 drawn styles draw something their resolved kit forbids, 15 of them
  only by an ancestor's ban, among them the Tidewater sidelights
  (`oq/an-inherited-ban-decides-what-the-elevation-may-draw`).
- **The window surround is not drawn anywhere**, because no drawn style's slot states one it can
  draw (§I).
- **Fanlights.** No record states a fanlight's rise.
- **The brick-course snap** is a question with three answers, each moving a different record.
- **The measurement layer still describes a doorcase on the three garage-entrance plans.** The
  drawing refuses it on all three surfaces; `_derive_measurements` reads the entrance composition,
  not the rect, so the doorcase figures and the faults that read them are unchanged there. Moving
  them is the measurement layer's change, and it moves three reference plans' verdicts.
- **The DXF draws no window heads.** The SVG does and the DXF never has. No census check holds
  that pair, and adding one belongs with the parity work WP-14.6 audits.
- **sash-light's lights-high rule deducts 5 in a sash** ("less a 2 in top rail and a 3 in bottom
  rail"). Its own frame note gives each sash one of those and a 1 1/4 in meeting rail: 3.25 in
  above and 4.25 in below. The drawing takes the count from the rule and the members from the note,
  so the light height falls out of the members. The disagreement is the record's with itself, and
  it is recorded here rather than resolved by picking a number.
- **The section and the roof carry no line-weight rungs.** Only the elevation does, so V1 has no
  population there.
