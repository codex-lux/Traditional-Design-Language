# WP-11.4 — The fire: the thing the plan layer did not have

*Phase 11, package 4 of 11. 4 September 2026. From
`docs/reports/tidewater-layout-diagnosis-2026-09-04.md` findings D1, D2 and D3, and Lucas's ruling
of the same day: **a LIST on the room, the wall authored, the width derived and editorial.***

## What this package is

There was no fireplace anywhere in the plan layer. Not a field on a room, not a thing the placer
reserved, not a line on the plate. `grep -i "hearth|fireplace|chimney"` over `plan_check.py`,
`geometry.py`, `geometry_cp.py`, `compose.py`, `openings.py` and `render_plan.py` returns **two
hits, and both are comments** — one in `plan_check.py` about a Cape with two chimneys, one in
`compose.py` about editorial prose. The diagnosis called them *"one comment and one measurement
note"*; re-derived against the commit, they are two comments. `build/arrangement.py` declared four
chimney and firebox measurements NOT_DERIVABLE, one of them on the ground that *"the plan has no
chimney footprint."*

Meanwhile `massings/catalog.json` said `four-over-four` has `hearth: "gable-end-paired"` and
*"Paired end chimneys serve four fireplaces per floor"*, and `roof.py` and `elevation.py` drew the
stacks. **Two records of one house, and the plan under the stacks had no fire in any room** — which
is OQ 85's shape (the elevation drawing a window where a chimney stands) one layer down.

Now: `hearth` is an array on a plan room (schema **0.7.0**), `build/hearths.py` reads it against
what the massing and the room record state, `plan_check` publishes a census and two findings from the
declared record, `render_plan.py` draws the breast as poché with the opening in copper, and `roof.py`
stands its stacks over fires somebody actually drew.

## The rule the whole package turns on: a hearth is AUTHORED and never inferred

`rooms/bedchamber.json`: *"A corner or gable-end fireplace with a 30-36 in opening, **or none at
all: unheated chambers are historically normal and should be said out loud rather than quietly
given a register**."*

A generator that gave every room of a type a fireplace because that type usually has one would be
inventing exactly what that sentence forbids. So this layer derives **where a stated hearth's flue
can go** and **whether the records agree**; it never derives that a fire exists.

## Four verdicts, because the records make four statements

`wants_a_hearth` reads a room type's own `servicing.heat` and returns one of four. Collapsing any
of them loses something the corpus took trouble to say:

| verdict | what the record said | records |
|---|---|---|
| `stated` | a fire belongs here — *"One chimneypiece, and it is the room's compositional centre"* | 12 |
| `optional` | a fire is normal AND its absence is normal, say which out loud | 1 |
| `none` | this room positively has no fire — *"Historically none. The passage is the unheated buffer between two heated rooms"* | 5 |
| `unstated` | the record speaks about ductwork instead | 42 |

**`none` is the verdict a two-state reader would have lost, and it is the one that matters most
here.** *"Historically none"* carries no fireplace word at all, so a checker looking for one would
file the centre passage under `unstated` and go hunting for a fire the corpus has explicitly
refused it. On the Tidewater plan three rooms are `declined` on that basis: `passage`,
`upperpassage` and `stair`.

**The 42 are not a gap in the records and reading them as one would be wrong.** The room records
speak two vocabularies about heat and the split is not accidental: the rooms that predate central
heating state a fire, and the rooms that do not state ductwork (`bedroom`: *"over-heated and
under-cooled by the same duct run"*; `breakfast-room`: *"Perimeter"*). **A Georgian plan built from
modern room types therefore has no room-level statement about its fires at all** — which is a fact
about this corpus worth knowing before anybody reads a census of `unjudged` as a backlog.

## The width, and why it is judgment

**MORRIS'S OWN RULE IS EXECUTED, NOT TWO ROWS OF HIS TABLE INTERPOLATED — and the rule is the
thing he wrote.** The first draft carried two anchors off the table, `(12 ft cube, 36 in)` and
`(22 ft, 49 in)`, and drew a straight line between them. The table is not the rule, and Morris says
so himself, introducing it as *"a Table of all the foregoing Proportions calculated in the
[preceding] Manner"*. From the 1734 first edition:

> **RULE II.** To find the Breadth of a Chimney from any given Magnitude of a Room, add the Length,
> Breadth and Height of the Room together, and extract the Square Root of that Sum, and half that
> Root will be the Breadth of your Chimney.

In feet, `sqrt(L + B + H) / 2`. It is used **only** as a fallback and **always** marked
`judgment: true`.

**The rule reproduces both discarded anchors** — a 12 ft cube gives `sqrt(36)/2 = 3.000 ft = 36.00
in` and a 22 ft cube `sqrt(66)/2 = 4.062 ft = 48.74 in`. That is a consistency check and **not an
independent corroboration**: where the two anchors originally came from is not recorded, so a
common origin cannot be excluded, and the report says so rather than claiming the stronger thing.

**The difference is at the ends, and the honest version of it is smaller in the middle than the
headline suggests.** Between the anchors the chord tracked the square root to within 0.3 in. It was
**clamped**, though — `max(0, min(1, t))` — so every room smaller than a 12 ft cube got 36.0 in flat
and every room larger than a 22 ft cube got 49.0 flat:

| room cube | clamped interpolation | Rule II | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 ft | 36.0 | 29.4 | **−6.6** |
| 17 ft | 42.5 | 42.8 | 0.3 |
| 30 ft | 49.0 | 56.9 | **+7.9** |

A test that probed only the middle would have found 0.3 in and called the change cosmetic. The
guard asserts all three.

**AND THE LEVEL'S STATED CEILING WAS BEING IGNORED.** Rule II wants three dimensions and a plan
room states two. `plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json` carries `floor_to_ceiling_ft` of 11 on the
ground and 10 above — `build/storeys.py` has read that field for two packages — and
`opening_width_in` was reaching for an editorial constant on every room of both: substituting an
invented number for one the record states, one function away from the comment refusing to do that.
The three authored openings were re-derived at the stated ceiling and moved from
**46.3 / 43.7 / 41.8 in to 42.8 / 41.1 / 39.8**, and a test now holds each against the rule, which
is what makes an authored figure checkable at all. Where no ceiling is stated, `MORRIS_CEILING_FT`
is used and the return value says `ceiling_assumed`; it is worth **0.87 in** across the whole
plausible 9-to-11 ft range on an 18 x 20 room, because a square root flattens it.

**PROVENANCE, because it decides what may be built on this.** The rule text is OCR of a scan of the
1734 first edition, reached through the agent proxy. Its LOCATION is corroborated independently:
Dean Hawkes, *"The Origins of Building Science in the Architecture of Eighteenth Century England"*
(Cloud-Cuckoo-Land 33), reports that Lecture VI *"concludes with a discourse on the calculation of
sizes of Chimnies in relation to the dimensions of rooms"*, summarised in *"A Table of Harmonick and
Arithmetical Proportions for Magnitudes of Rooms and Chimnies by Universal Rules"*. **No facsimile
page has been read here** — the same standing caution as OQ 7-11 and OQ 18's source half — which is
why every figure derived from it still carries `judgment: true`.

**THE DEPTH KEEPS ITS TWO ANCHORS AND THE RULE BEHIND THEM IS UNRECOVERED, WHICH IS A THIRD STATE
AND NOT A GAP.** Morris's RULE III is legible — *"add the Breadth and Height of [the Chimney]
together, take one fourth of that Sum, and it is the Depth"* — but it needs the chimney's height,
and RULE I, which gives that, is corrupt in the only text reachable from here: it reads *"add the
Length 1 Bo Height of the Room together"*. It is a two-term sum (a three-term one would make Rules
I and II the same rule) and which two cannot be told. Both anchors are consistent with Rule III on
a square opening, which is a reading and not a reconstruction. Recorded in the constant.

Order of authority, and it is stated in the return value rather than left to a reader:

1. the plan record's own `width_in`;
2. the ROOM RECORD's figure where it states one — `bedchamber`'s *"30-36 in opening"* gives 33.0 in
   at `judgment: false`, and after the gate below it is the only one of the 60 that does;
3. **Rule II executed** on the room's own length, breadth and stated ceiling, `judgment: true`,
   with the reason in the record: *"a London figure in an English treatise, used because neither
   this record nor its room type states an opening; no facsimile has been read here and it may not
   be published as measured."*

**AND THE FIGURE IS ONLY READ WHERE THE RECORD ADMITS A FIRE, WHICH THE FIRST DRAFT DID NOT
CHECK.** `rooms/closet.json`'s `servicing.heat` opens *"None required and none wanted"* and then,
four clauses later, describes a closet ceiling *"drywalled from a stepladder through a 30 in
opening"* — an air-barrier failure, about a doorway. The bare inch-search took it and published
**30.0 in at `judgment: false`**: a room that wants no heat at all stating a MEASURED fireplace
opening, which is this corpus's worst sin committed by the package written to prevent a version of
it. The gate is `wants_a_hearth`'s verdict rather than a tighter pattern, because a regex cannot
tell a doorway from a chimneypiece and the verdict already can. **It was found by re-deriving a
claim in this report rather than re-reading it** — the report said `bedchamber` was the only
own-figure in the corpus, and running the function said there were two. WP-9.5's technique, and it
is still the only thing that catches these.

`docs/reports/wp-9.2-what-the-tradition-actually-does.md` §6 is explicit that Kerr's dimensions may
not be transplanted into an American vernacular record as though measured. The same caution binds
Morris and it is enforced in code rather than remembered: the plate's own tooltip says *"Morris
1734, judgment"*, and mutating that flag to `false` fails the suite. **Recovering the rule from the
source does not weaken that caution and must not be read as doing so** — a rule read correctly out
of an English treatise is still an English rule.

The breast's projection is the same table's depth column under the same caution. **It does NOT
retire `build/arrangement.py`'s NOT_DERIVABLE entries, and their REASONS had to be rewritten
instead.** They said *"no record carries a chimney PLAN dimension"* and *"the plan has no chimney
footprint"*, and both stopped being true the moment a hearth landed on a plan room. The refusals
stand — a judgment figure may be DRAWN and never published as a measurement, which is the standing
precedent for the chimney's own 22 in — but their ground has moved from *there is no such thing in
the plan* to *there is, and it is a judgment*. Leaving the old wording would have been WP-6.4's
finding exactly: a comment asserting a state of the world the package one directory over had just
falsified. **Found by re-deriving the report's own quotation and discovering it belonged to the
sibling key.**

## The massing is read conservatively, and six forms are all it acts on

`gable-end-paired`, `gable-end`, `end`, `central-stack`, `central`, `party-wall`. Everything else
in `massings/catalog.json` is **refused by name** rather than mapped to the nearest — the same
discipline `massing_bays` applies to `"5-7 main"` and for the same reason. *"gable-end-paired or
central-stack"* is a real statement about a type that admits both arrangements, and choosing
between them here would be authoring on the corpus's behalf.

## The stacks moved onto the fires

`roof.py` put a stack at the centre of each gable end, from the RECTANGLE and not from the rooms
inside it, so the elevation drew stacks over a passage and a kitchen. Closed the way OQ 85 closed
the window on the chimney axis: read the other record, and disclose.

On the Tidewater plan, three fires on two flues:

| | placement |
|---|---|
| the centre-line rule | `(0.0, 20.38)` and `(65.58, 20.38)` |
| over the stated flues | `(65.58, 11.77)` and `(0.0, 14.21)` |

Each moved 6.2 ft and 8.6 ft. **The note has FOUR states, and the version this package first wrote had two — one of which got
it wrong on its first run:** it said *"this record states no hearth"* about a record carrying three,
because an unplaced record cannot position a hearth (a fire's place along its wall comes from the
room's placed rectangle) and the note read that as having none. It now distinguishes *positioned
over N stated flues* / *states N hearths and none could be positioned* / *states none*, and the
third says *"not a house with no fires"* out loud.

**The fourth state was a bare `except Exception` and is WP-9.1's finding in a new place.** The
reconciliation was wrapped in `except Exception: axes = None`, which reverts to the centre-line
rule **silently** and then says *"this record states no hearth"* about a record carrying three —
the sheet drawing stacks over nothing while the plate asserts there is nothing to stand over. That
is `plan_check.check` deriving its elevation under an `except: pass` and judging two buildings as
one for a phase. The failure is caught, named in the note with the real exception text, and
published as `hearths_unreadable`. It is not re-raised: a roof is still drawable without the
reconciliation, and a renderer that dies on a malformed hearth is worse than one that says it could
not read them.

### The proving engine, and why there is no before-and-after for it

The phase's rule asks for both engines. **On CP-SAT this session can publish readings and not a
comparison, and the reason is worth more than the numbers would have been.** At a 120 s budget on
this four-core container:

| plan | key | diverged | windows | downgraded | transfers | score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tidewater | `0 / 62 / 85` | 17 | 18 of 35 | 12 | 31 | 719.4 |
| spec-builder | `4 / 87 / 96` | 17 | 2 of 14 | 5 | 26 | 577.8 |

A before-and-after needs the same instrument twice, and CP-SAT under a time budget **is not
deterministic under load** — this file's own standing caution, and the reason WP-9.6 ratchets the
heuristic's furniture counts and never `auto`'s. The baseline run had to share four cores with the
after run, so the two would have been solved under different contention and any difference between
them would have been unattributable. Running them serially is available and costs a further hour
per pass; it was not spent, because a sound argument was.

**The sound argument, and it is a guard rather than a claim.** Nothing `hearth_report` reads is a
placement: the room's authored `hearth`, the massing's `hearth`, the room type's `servicing.heat`.
So the two findings are engine-independent **by construction**, and
`tests/test_hearths.py::TestTheCriticAndThePlate::test_IT_IS_NOT_A_DRAWN_CHECK_AND_RUNS_ON_AN_UNPLACED_RECORD`
holds the census and the findings identical on a record with no placement at all — which is a
stronger statement than "two CP runs agreed", and it was written because finding 7 below proved
the layer choice had been wrong.

**What this does NOT establish**, said out loud because the temptation is to read it as more: the
roof's stack reconciliation is not engine-independent — it reads each hearth's room rectangle — and
its effect on a CP placement has not been measured on either plan.

## What was found

**1. FOURTEEN OF THE SIXTEEN PLAN RECORDS IN THIS TREE STATE NO MASSING AT ALL.** Only the two
shipped plans name one, and both name `gable-end-paired`. Every record in `plans/reference/` — the
seven `good-*` that are the corpus's own account of a well-made plan, and the seven `bad-*` — has
`massing: null`. So a check written on the massing does not evaluate on **87.5%** of the plan
records, and until this package nothing said so. Two more such checks were found by grepping every
reader: `plan_check.py`'s grouping `attaches_to` branch and its style `massing_affinities`
branch, neither of which reports the silence. `hearth_report` publishes `readable: false` with the
reason. `oq/fourteen-of-sixteen-plans-name-no-massing`.

Corpus-wide census over all 16 records: **3 stated, 1 absent, 15 declined, 219 unjudged of 238
rooms — 92.0% unjudged**, and the fourteen massing-less plans account for **181** of them, the two massed plans for 38.

**2. THE REFUSAL GAVE THE WRONG REASON TO FOURTEEN OF SIXTEEN PLANS.** `flue_walls` had one
refusal message — *"a compound is a statement about a type that admits both arrangements"* — and
handed it to every plan that stated no massing at all. A true sentence about a different situation.
There are three cases and they call for three different actions from the reader (author a
`massing`; author the `hearth` field in the catalogue; rule on the compound), so there are three
messages now, pinned against each other by a test asserting all three are distinct.

**3. THIS PACKAGE'S OWN DATA CARRIED A FALSE CLAIM ABOUT A CHECK THAT DOES NOT EXIST.** The
Tidewater dining hearth was authored on the W gable with a note saying its disagreement with
`rooms/dining-room.json`'s *"interior wall opposite the sideboard"* *"is reported by
build/hearths.py rather than resolved silently."* It is not. The only comparison made is against
the **massing's** stack walls, and W is exactly where `gable-end-paired` puts them, so it passes in
silence. The note is corrected to say so, and a test pins the actual scope so the absence of a
finding cannot be read as a comparison that came back clean.

**The obvious fix was measured and refused.** A prose reader over `servicing.heat` looking for a
named wall returns five hits of which **two** are the thing being looked for: `dining-room`'s
*"interior wall"* and `bedchamber`'s *"corner or gable-end"* are real; `drawing-room`'s
*"compositional centre"*, `library`'s *"clear of the chimney breast"* and `breezeway`'s *"each pen
has its own gable-end hearth"* are three different jobs sharing one syntax. That is
`oq/a-room-records-prose-states-a-floor-its-own-band-does-not` exactly, and shipping a 2-of-5
comparison inside the package whose subject is not inventing facts about fires would have been the
error in a new place. `oq/a-room-record-names-the-wall-its-fire-stands-on-and-nothing-compares-it`.

**4. A FIGURE WAS LIFTED OUT OF PROSE ABOUT SOMETHING ELSE AND PUBLISHED AS MEASURED.** See the
width section above: `rooms/closet.json` returned a 30.0 in fireplace opening at `judgment: false`
from a sentence about an air barrier, in a record whose first four words are *"None required and
none wanted."* Gated on `wants_a_hearth`'s verdict. **Found by re-deriving a claim in this report
rather than re-reading it**, which is WP-9.5's technique and remains the only one that has ever
caught this class here.

**5. THE RECONCILIATION SWALLOWED ITS OWN FAILURES.** See above: a bare `except Exception` around
`stack_axes` collapsed *"could not read the hearths"* into *"this record states no hearth"*. Found
by re-reading the diff adversarially rather than by any test, and it is the fourth state now.

**6. THE BRANCH ORDER IN `hearth_report` IS LOAD-BEARING AND IS MEASURED.** `none` is tested
BEFORE the unreadable-massing branch, so a room the corpus positively refuses a fire stays
`declined` whatever the massing says. Reversed, the corpus's own refusal is downgraded to *"nobody
could tell"* — the fake-unjudged direction, as dishonest as a fake pass. That is WP-8.11's
`kit.forbidden` ordering lesson in a new place, and it has its own guard because the test that
happened to catch the mutation was about the readable case.

**7. THE CHECK WAS PUT IN THE ONE LAYER THAT CANNOT RUN IT, AND IT IS THE SAME MISTAKE AS 5 AND
6.** The block went into `drawn_layer`, which early-returns on a record with no placement — and
nothing `hearth_report` reads is a placement. So the spec plan's dining room, which has no fire
under a massing that draws paired end stacks, produced **no finding, no census and no reason**
until somebody placed it: a check that could not fire reading exactly like a check that passed,
inside the package written to stop that. It runs beside the other declared-record layers now, and
`hearth_summary` is published on an unplaced record. `breast` and `stack_axes` DO read placement
and belong to the renderer and the roof; a placed hearth check — a breast overlapping a door or a
furniture run — would belong in `drawn_layer` and is not built. The finding kinds lost their
`drawn-` prefix accordingly (`room-without-a-hearth`, `hearth-off-the-stack-wall`).

**8. THREE OF TWELVE GUARDS WERE BLIND, AND SO WAS THE HARNESS THAT FOUND THEM.** Mutation-checked
twelve ways; three mutations left the suite green on the first pass and one did not apply at all:

| mutation | first pass | why |
|---|---|---|
| `breast` stands on the *opposite* wall | **BLIND** | the guard asserted only that the breast lies inside the room's x-extent, which is true of either wall |
| `drawn-hearth-off-the-stack-wall` never raised | **BLIND** | all three shipped hearths are on E/W, which is where `gable-end-paired` puts its stacks, so nothing drove the check |
| the schema's `wall` enum becomes a bare string | **BLIND** | nothing validated a plan against `plan.schema.json` at all |
| `render_plan` draws no breasts | **NO MATCH** | my own typo in the mutation string, `_h` for `h` |

The second is WP-8.11's finding in a new place: **a driven fixture must not accidentally be the
shipped corpus.** The fourth is this repo's own lesson wearing the tester's clothes — *a mutation
that silently does not apply looks exactly like a guard that works* — met while applying that
lesson. All four fixed. **Nineteen mutations in the end and nineteen caught** — the twelve above plus the opening-width gate, the two roof states, the branch order, the layer move, the interpolation and the stated ceiling, each added beside the fix it guards.

## What was deliberately not done

**No fireplace is inferred anywhere**, on the ruling and on `rooms/bedchamber.json`'s own sentence.
`absent` is a `minor` finding that quotes the record it rests on and asks for the choice to be made
out loud; it is not a repair, and no move in `moves/registry.json` writes a hearth.

**The placer reserves nothing for the breast.** A chimney breast is masonry standing in a room, and
`furniture_shortfalls` does not know it is there — a 12 ft dining room with a 22 in breast has 10.2
ft of clear width, and nothing has been told. That is real and it belongs with WP-11.6's container
work, not here, because reserving it in `geometry.py` alone would give the two engines two
different rooms.

**An `interior` hearth is not drawn.** The record names the wall it is opposite and this renderer
does not know which of four sides that is; putting it on a plausible one is the invention the layer
exists to stop. It is counted, and the count is on the plate.

**Exterior versus interior stacks are not modelled.** The breast projects INTO the room, which is
what an interior stack does; an exterior stack projects the other way. The massing vocabulary does
not state the difference — `end` says nothing about it — and guessing would be authoring.

## Addendum, 4 September 2026: a defect this package pushed

**`plan_check` gained a `hearth` finding layer and `compose.SCORE_LAYERS` did not gain a `hearth`
key**, so both findings landed in `score_unclassified_layers` and no scoring axis counted them.
`test_score.py::test_every_layer_the_validator_emitted_is_classified` caught it on the first full
build after the push, naming the layer and the remedy in its own assertion message. The guard was
built for exactly this and it worked.

**The reason it reached the remote is the process, not the guard.** A stop hook asked for a commit
while `check_all.py` was still running, and that build was the only thing that could have found it.
A build that has not finished is not a green build.

**Re-measured before re-pinning, because the pin's own message demands it** — a layer is worth a
different number of points on a different axis, and an independent audit once moved `grouping` from
canon to rooms and got a different returned set with the whole suite green. On family-georgian:

| hearth mapped to | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| unmapped | 78.7 | 68.7 | 62.2 | 60.5 |
| **rooms** | **78.4** | **68.4** | **62.2** | **60.5** |
| canon | 78.1 | 68.3 | 62.2 | 60.5 |

Same set, same order, all three. Only the two candidates that emit hearth findings move at all. So
the axis choice changes nothing today and the argument decides it: `rooms`, because a room's fire
is read from that room's own `servicing.heat` and `servicing` already maps there. The compromise is
recorded beside the mapping — `hearth-off-the-stack-wall` is a record-against-massing fact that
belongs on `canon`, and splitting one layer across two axes is the re-weighting the `drawn` note
declines for the same reason.
