# WP-11.4 — the parameter whose condition nobody read

*4 September 2026. The stoop at the entrance door and the stacks at the gable ends: two things
this corpus has stated in full since Phase 1 and no drawing had ever read. The package's own
title is about the third thing it was asked for and did not draw.*

Read `docs/reports/wp-11.3-the-record-said-against-the-wall-and-its-own-note-said-otherwise.md`
first if you are about to trust the shape of the record this writes; it is the package that
settled how a derived arrangement goes ONTO the placed record and is drawn by both renderers
from there.

---

## What was asked for, and what was built

`PLAN-OF-ACTION.md`, WP-11.4, in full: *"`threshold_pass` (steps at the entrance door; columns
and their answering pilasters where `portico_bays = 1`) and `chimney_pass` (the two gable-end
stacks at their 22 in plan). Which rooms take a hearth is not in any record: raise it, do not
read it."*

Three deliverables. Two are built and drawn on the shipped Tidewater sheet. **The third — the
columns — is refused, and the refusal is this report's subject, because reading the instruction
literally would have put a portico on the one reference plan whose own kit says a portico is
wrong there.**

| | built | drawn on the Tidewater sheet |
|---|---|---|
| the stoop's flight at the entrance door | yes | yes — two treads and a nosing, in the wall's own poché, outside the S wall |
| the two gable-end stacks | yes | yes — 22 in square, exterior, at the mid-depth of each end wall |
| the portico's columns and pilasters | **refused, with a measurement** | no, on any node in the corpus |
| which rooms take a hearth | **raised, not read** | no — `oq/which-rooms-take-the-hearth` |

New files: `build/threshold.py`, `threshold/grammar.json`, `build/check_threshold.py`,
`tests/test_threshold_pass.py`. Plan schema **0.7.0**. Checks 48 → **49**.

## I. The finding the package is named for

`porch_type.portico_bays` resolves to `1` on `tidewater-georgian`. Drawing two columns from that
is one line of code and it is what the work package asks for. It is also wrong, and the record
says so twice.

The FIRST clause of the slot's own rule carries the condition:

> *"The threshold is a raised stoop under a worked door surround; **where a portico occurs** it is
> one bay wide, centred, and carries the bound order."*

And the node's own `porch_type` answers whether one occurs: `stoop-only` is canonical and
`entry-portico` is **atypical**, with the reason —

> *"Ordinary in the parent and not ordinary here. A brick Tidewater house takes its entrance
> elaboration in the doorway itself; a timber portico that reads correctly on a clapboard New
> England front reads as an addition on a brick one."*

**Measured over all 159 nodes that carry a kit: 8 resolve a `portico_bays`, and 5 of those have no portico among
their canonical porch types** — `georgian-colonial-american`, `jeffersonian-classicism`,
`mid-atlantic-georgian`, `new-england-georgian`, `tidewater-georgian`, all `stoop-only`. Read
without its condition, `portico_bays` puts a portico on five nodes in eight, one of them a shipped
reference plan.

This is WP-8.4's finding in a new place. That package found 331 fault exceptions carrying a
`granted_when` that nothing read, because the field had been given a name already in use for
something else. Here the condition is not even a field: it is the first six words of the slot's
own prose, and nothing reads prose.

`th-a-portico-only-where-one-is-canonical` is the rule; the refusal is written onto every plan's
`threshold.unplaced` with the node's canonical porch types quoted in it.

## II. And where a portico IS canonical, the column has no width

Having refused the five, the honest question is what happens on the twenty-three nodes that DO
make a portico canonical. Three facts are needed to draw a column in plan: a canonical portico, a
bay count, and a diameter in inches. Measured:

| fact | nodes, of the 159 that carry a kit |
|---|---|
| a canonical portico among the resolved `porch_type` variants | **23** |
| a resolved `porch_type.portico_bays` | **8** |
| a resolved column diameter in inches (`porch_support`/`column` `base_diameter_in`) | **1** |
| **all three** | **0** |

The one node that states a diameter is `neoclassical-revival`, at `[20, 28]` in, `measured` — and
it states no bay count. **The intersection is empty across the whole corpus**, so this package
draws no column anywhere, and the reason is a measurement rather than a deferral.

The corpus states the column's height in DIAMETERS — nine of them, on five nodes — and almost
never a diameter. That is a coherent way to write a classical order and a useless one for a plan:
nine diameters of nothing is nothing. `build/check_threshold.py` re-derives all four numbers on
every run and WARNS the moment any node acquires all three, so the refusal stops being true the
moment the corpus moves rather than the next time somebody reads this file.

## III. The answer to the stack's hardest question was in a different slot

`tidewater-georgian`'s `chimney` slot makes THREE variants canonical:

- `gable-end-paired-interior`
- `gable-end-exterior`
- `paired-and-joined-by-arched-curtain`

**The first two put the same mass on opposite faces of the same wall.** A plan drawing must choose,
and the roof-expression slot declines to.

The answer is in `hearth_position`, and that slot's own `note` says why it is the one to ask:

> *"Kept separate from `chimney` because the ontology separates the roof expression from the plan
> fact. Here they are one decision that happens to agree, and the two slots therefore say the same
> thing twice — the one duplication in this file I could not remove."*

`hearth_position` is the PLAN slot, by its own words, and its canonical `exterior-end` settles it,
with the rule stating the reason: *"on the outside walls of the end rooms, so the mass of the stack
sits outside the heated volume in summer."* The Tidewater stacks are drawn outside.

`build/roof.py` reads `chimney`; this pass reads `hearth_position`; and the ontology's own note is
the authority for the split rather than a convenience.

## IV. The side table is closed, and the checker earned itself on its first run

Which face of the wall a mass stands on is decided by `build/threshold.py::SIDE_OF`, a CLOSED
TABLE on `build/construction_vocabulary.py`'s precedent — never a substring test, which is WP-8.4's
other lesson (*"a substring test over a surface cannot answer a question about an assembly"*).
Every `hearth_position` variant id in use is in it, mapped to a side or recorded as unmappable with
a reason. **Two of the fifteen name a side.** That is the measurement, not a shortcoming of the
table: the corpus's hearth vocabulary is about where in the PLAN the hearth is, and says which face
of the wall the stack stands on exactly twice.

`build/check_threshold.py` proves the table total over the corpus and **failed on its first run**,
on twelve tokens — because the massing's `hearth` field is a SECOND vocabulary, in free text, and
seven of the forty massings state a DISJUNCTION there in six distinct words: `gable-end or corner`,
`gable-end-paired or central-stack`, `party-wall or end`, `central or end`, `central or none`,
`interior or end`. Two of those put the stack at opposite ends of the plan.

`build/roof.py::chimney_positions` reads that field with `"gable-end" in hearth`, which answers
TRUE for two of the disjunctions — a massing that declines to choose read as having chosen — and
that fallback fires on **135 of 159 styles**. Neither shipped plan is affected (both are
`four-over-four`, whose `gable-end-paired` is unambiguous), which is why nothing has caught it.
`roof.py` is NOT changed here: it decides a HEIGHT, changing it moves real output on three
massings, and that measurement belongs to whoever makes it.
`oq/the-massing-states-its-hearth-in-prose-and-a-substring-test-reads-it`, with the two tables
(`SIDE_OF` and `MASSING_HEARTH`) kept separate and both proved total.

## V. Three statements of one depth, and the one figure all three admit

The stoop's depth is stated three times, in two slots, and the three do not agree:

| record | figure |
|---|---|
| `steps_and_stoop.platform_depth_in` | band `[36, 60]` in |
| `porch_depth.stoop_depth_ft` | band `[4, 6]` ft = `[48, 72]` in |
| `porch_depth.stoop_min_depth_in` | `48` in, a floor |

A 36 in stoop is inside the first band and below the third. **48 in is the only value all three
admit** — the low end of one band, the midpoint of the other, and the stated minimum exactly — and
that is what is drawn, as an INTERSECTION rather than an average. Where the three do not intersect
the depth is unjudged and nothing is drawn; `test_an_empty_intersection_is_unjudged_and_never_
averaged` is the guard, and it fails when the pass splits the difference.

None of the three is edited to agree with the others, which is
`oq/a-grouping-rule-and-a-room-record-can-disagree`'s standing rule applied one layer up, in the
kit.

## VI. The riser count, and a band standing over a derivation on 224 figures

`tidewater-georgian` states `riser_count_from_grade` as a band `[3, 6]`, `measured`. Its parent,
`georgian-colonial-american`, DERIVES the same parameter as `ceil(part * 2.4 / 6.75)` = **4**, from
`storey-graduation`. The child `extends` the slot, so the child's object replaces the parent's
whole object — correct, and what `extends` means — and the resolved record then holds a band where
one step up the corpus holds an expression with a source pack behind it.

`python3 build/resolve_kit.py federal-style --verbose` prints `riser_count_from_grade 4 ceil(part
* 2.4 / 6.75) [storey-graduation]`. The same command on `tidewater-georgian` prints nothing for
that parameter.

**Measured: 224 (node, slot, parameter) triples over 58 nodes and seven distinct slot+parameter
pairs** where the nearest statement is a band and an ancestor's is an expression —
`casing.width_in`, `height_proportion.second_over_first`, `pediment.rise_over_span`,
`pilaster.projection_in`, `pilaster.width_in`, `steps_and_stoop.riser_count_from_grade`,
`window_sill.projection_in`. `oq/a-child-band-replaces-an-ancestor-derivation`.

What is drawn is the band's LOW end — 3 risers — graded `editorial-from-prose`, for a reason about
what a plan asserts: the flight's tread count is the only thing on this drawing that claims ground
OUTSIDE the house, and the least the band permits is the least the drawing claims. Three facts are
recorded beside it rather than averaged away: the band, the parent's derived 4, and **the slot's
own basis sentence, which says "three to five risers" and so contradicts its own band's top end**.

The tread takes its band's MIDPOINT instead, deliberately, and the two rules are graded separately
so the difference is legible: a tread is bounded above and below by use, and the band's own note
says the width of the band IS the uncertainty.

## VII. Two of the fourteen reference plans have been failing their own schema since OQ 55

Found while bumping the plan schema for this package's two new objects. `build/geometry.py` writes

```
g["void"] = {"heated": False, "roofed": bool(vd.get("roofed"))}
```

and `schema/plan.schema.json`'s `void` object admitted `roofed` and `within_footprint` under
`additionalProperties: false`. So **every placed plan carrying a reserved void was invalid against
the contract it was placed from** — `good-01-veranda-gallery-estate` and
`good-03-parlor-drawing-room-house`, for three phases.

Nothing noticed because **nothing validates a PLACED plan** except `workbench/server/app.py`'s own
gate (WP-10.1), and a reference plan never goes through it. The `geometry` object's own 0.3.0
description records the identical shape one level up — *"before 0.3.0, forbidden by this schema's
own additionalProperties, so every placed plan was invalid against the contract it was placed
from"* — which is the same defect, in the same file, one nesting level out, fixed once and left
open in its child.

Admitted at 0.7.0; **16 of 16 shipped and reference plans now validate once placed**, and
`tests/test_threshold_pass.py::TestThePlacedRecordValidates` is the guard that would have caught
it. It reports COULD NOT EVALUATE without `jsonschema` rather than passing.

## VIII. What the guards caught that reading did not

- **A nosing on the bottom edge of the flight.** The first draft drew `treads` nosing lines and
  the flight rectangle's own two edges ARE the top and bottom nosings, so one line was drawn
  twice. It is `treads - 1` now, and a flight of one tread is a rectangle with no line at all.
- **A byte-identity pin that was measuring the wrong thing.** `build_roof`'s return embeds
  `section`, which embeds the PLACED plan. The pin on `roof.py`'s output across the move of
  `roof_form_for` / the ridge axis / the two gable-end points went green across the move and then
  RED two commits later, on a change that added two keys to the placed record and touched no roof
  code at all. **A pin that reads "the roof changed" when the roof did not is worse than no pin**,
  because the next reader believes it. It hashes the roof's own answer now — `main`, `chimneys`,
  `checks`, `outline`, `elevation_profiles`, `footprint` — measured on a `git archive HEAD`
  checkout of the pristine tree and again here: `0d94e0cd…`, over both shipped plans, all fourteen
  reference plans and the Tidewater block swept over all 164 styles.
- **A guard that could not fail, in the class this package exists to guard against.** The first
  version of the plate-extent test asserted every drawn stoop and stack rect lies inside the
  viewBox — and it PASSED with the widening deleted, because the sheet's own margin is deeper than
  the three feet a stack projects. It is a DIFFERENTIAL now: render the plan, render it again with
  the two records stripped, and the canvas must be wider and taller with them than without. Red on
  the revert both ways.
- **A widening that is inert on the browser sheet, said so rather than counted.** The plate's
  extent is widened for the stoop and the stacks in BOTH renderers. In `render_plan.py` that is
  load-bearing and its guard goes red on the revert. In `Sheet.jsx` it is a FLOOR the shipped plan
  does not reach — a stack projects 3.1 ft and the flight 3.5, against margins of 11 and 9 ft the
  sheet already carried for the street and the dimension line — so a mutation there leaves the
  walk green. The comment in the file says so, because a reader who mutates it and sees green
  deserves the reason rather than a mystery.
- **An import-graph walk that saw two modules.** `build/threshold.py` is deliberately NOT a leaf —
  it loads `resolve_kit` for the cascade-resolved kit, which `storeys.py`, `assemblies.py` and
  `furniture.py` are all forbidden to do, because `structure.py` loads `geometry.py` which calls
  `openings.py`. The test walks the import graph rather than trusting the docstring; its first
  version matched only `modcache.load(...)` and missed `import proportion_engine as pe`, reporting
  a graph of two modules. **The vacuity assertion at the foot of the test caught it** — the walk
  must find `proportion_engine` or its three negative assertions mean nothing.
- **Six mutations, six reds.** The gable-end verdict forced True; the side defaulted to exterior;
  the stack size defaulted to 22; the flight unclamped from the room behind it; the stack on the
  wrong face; the stack off the mid-depth. Each applied with a uniqueness assertion first, because
  WP-11.3 found a mutation that silently matched an earlier occurrence and never landed.

## IX. The engine decides whether a service door lands on the entrance front

`th-only-the-entrance-door` gives the stoop to the exterior door on the face the plan states as
its entrance. **The browser walk answered that check with TWO stoops where the test expected one**,
and the reason is a finding rather than a flaky count: on the SEARCH engine the Tidewater plan puts
one exterior door on the S front, the porch's; **on CP-SAT it puts the KITCHEN's there as well** —
legal by the record, which declares S among that room's exterior walls, and a service door on the
entrance front all the same.

A door carries no `rank` on any record in this corpus (the field exists at plan schema 0.3.0 and
nothing writes it), so the only structured fact available is the room's own `function_class`, and
`steps_and_stoop` describes the ENTRANCE composition. A service-class room's exterior door does not
get a 10 ft platform because the placer put it on the front: it is NAMED, with the placement's own
defect stated in the reason. That is the drawing telling the truth about the record rather than
dressing it.

The walk's own check moved with it, from a COUNT to a PROPERTY — every mark of the flight lies on
the plate, on whichever engine ran — because a count that is the engine's is not the record's.

**The placement is byte-identical.** Both passes run at the end of `openings.place()`, after the
level loop, and write only plan-level keys — nothing above them can see them, which is what keeps
`tests/test_furniture_drawn.py`'s equality ratchets and every placement pin in the suite green.

**Swept over all 159 kits with the Tidewater block, `engine="heuristic"`: 0 errors, 15 styles
draw a stoop, and exactly ONE draws stacks.** That last figure is the honest measure of how far
the record reaches, and it decomposes: **14 of 159 nodes make a hearth position canonical in
their OWN kit** (the reader `roof.py` argued for at length and this pass adopts), **3 of those
name a gable end**, and **1 of those names a side** — `tidewater-georgian`, `exterior-end`. Every
other node either says nothing about its hearth in its own file, or falls back to a massing whose
`hearth` names the end without naming the face. Fifteen stoops and one pair of stacks is not a
thin implementation; it is the corpus, measured.

`plans/spec-builder-colonial.json` draws neither, and says why for both: `colonial-revival`'s
cascade dimensions no `steps_and_stoop` at all, and its massing's `gable-end-paired` names the end
without naming the face. Two honest unjudged verdicts rather than two silences.

## XI. What was deliberately not done

- **The columns and their answering pilasters** — §I and §II. Not deferred: measured, and the
  facts do not exist on any node.
- **Which rooms take a hearth** — `oq/which-rooms-take-the-hearth`. The arithmetic is complete
  (two stacks, two to four fireplaces each, four per floor) and the ROOMS are not stated anywhere.
  The obvious reading — the two rooms adjoining each stack — is available in one line from the
  placement and is refused, because the parti names eleven ground rooms and the placement holds
  twelve, six of them service: the two rooms adjoining the stack are not the two the TYPE means.
- **The chimney breast, the firebox and the hearth.** Each is a dimension INTO a room, and which
  room is the question above. The exemplar plans draw all three.
- **`build/roof.py`'s substring test over the massing's prose** — §IV. Named, measured, not
  changed.
- **The water table.** `oq/the-raw-kit-read`: the slot is empty in the node's own kit and the
  figure arrives only through the cascade, which is the reader that question is about.
- **A second stack where `paired-and-joined-by-arched-curtain` is canonical.** The variant's note
  describes two shafts joined ABOVE THE ROOF by a curtain, which is an elevation fact; the plan
  count is `hearth_position.count` = 2, one at each end, and that is what is drawn.
