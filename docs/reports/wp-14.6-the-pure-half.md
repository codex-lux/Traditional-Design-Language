# WP-14.6 — the pure half

**Status: COMPLETE (24 Sep 2026).** Ten React-free modules under `workbench/app/src/`, eight spec
files at the top level of `src/`, one Python parity test, and one line in `refusal.test.mjs`. The
contracts are `docs/prd/phase-14-the-dossier-and-the-journey.md` §0.1, §F.3, §G, §I.2 and §I.6–§I.9,
and §J. Every deviation from them is listed in §V and was made because the tree said something the
contract did not. The UX reading this phase answers is `docs/reports/ux-first-principles-2026-09-24.md`.
The walk that will exercise these modules through a browser is
`docs/reports/wp-14.7-the-walk-navigates-by-address.md`.

**The rule the package was built to: the surfaces of WP-14.8 onward may decide nothing this half
decides.** Each module answers one question a surface would otherwise answer inline and differently:
how a figure is printed, what a verdict is called, what a citation is named, where a popover goes,
where a house stands on its journey, how the styles are filed, and where a trim plate's assemblies
stand. None imports React, a DOM or anything from `node_modules`, so each is tested by
`node --test` with no install, which is how `check_all.py` runs the app suite.

## III. Findings

**1. AN EVALUATION WHOSE CHECK ERRORED CANNOT COUNT FOR ANY PLAN, AND THE REFUSAL WP-13.4 SAVED FOR IT
IS THEN NOT READ.** §G counts `lastEval` only where `lastEval.check.plan === plan.id`. That rule is
right: a verdict about the house before is not a verdict about this one.

The problem is `core.check_plan`'s two error payloads, `plan does not match the plan schema` and
`could not validate`. Neither carries a plan id. `workbench/server/evaluate.py` still attaches
`placement_refused` on exactly that early return; its WP-13.4 comment explains why a refusal has to
survive a schema error. So on a malformed record whose placement was refused, the journey reads **the
plan as "not yet evaluated" and the drawings step as "ready"**, because §G's drawings rule is `ready`
wherever the plan is not refused.

The module follows the contract and does not guess that an unnamed evaluation is about the plan on the
bench. That guess is exactly the stale-verdict error the rule exists to stop. The fix is on the server:
stamp the plan id on the error payload, and the journey reads it with no change. It is not made here,
because it is not in this package's files. **The first surface to render the bar is where this becomes
visible**, so it is written down before WP-14.10 wires one.

**2. A SERVED FACE'S `x` AND `x_from` UNDERSTATE WHAT IT DRAWS, SO THE LAYOUT READS THE SEGMENTS.** §I.6
gives the layout a width and says nothing about where it comes from. The obvious source is each face's
`x` and `x_from`. That is wrong on **9 of 164 faces** over the 15 stackless packs with assemblies that
`pe.resolve` reaches.

This was measured under the default datum, because `datum: "wall"` is WP-14.4's and is not on this tree.
The deepest case is `facade-portada`'s `estipite` baluster, which turns **21.6 in** behind its own
`x_from`. `trim-classical`'s cornice cymas cross both ways by 0.087 and 0.159 in, and its beads and
astragals by up to 0.3 in. A layout built on those two figures would put one assembly's hollow into its
neighbour's leaders.

`extentOf` therefore reads every segment's end point too. For an arc it reads the two points where the
arc's own stated angles cross the horizontal (`cx ± rx`), and only where they are actually swept. That
bounds a curve the engine constructed. It constructs no curve, derives no sweep flag, and holds none of
`test_grammar_agreement.py`'s `_ARC_MATH` needles.

**The better home for it is Python.** If `pack_geometry` served an `extent` per face, even this reading
would leave the JavaScript. That belongs to WP-14.4, which owns the payload, and it is suggested rather
than done. An assembly served with no geometry falls back to its members' `projection_in` and says so
(`extentFrom: 'members'`). A width taken from a stated projection is not the width of a drawing, and the
plate should be able to tell which it has.

**3. THE ×2 RULE IS GREEDY BY THE FRAME'S TALLEST, AND THAT IS NOT THE SAME AS BY ITS LAST.** Heights
10, 6 and 4.5 give frames [10, 6] and [4.5]. The 6 joins the 10, and the 4.5 is measured against the
10, the frame's tallest, rather than against the 6 it would sit beside. The contract says "the frame's
tallest", and the test drives exactly this case.

`trim-classical` gives the two frames §I.6 names: 114/114/114, then the casings. The casings are
declared **6.0, 4.5, 6.9**, and they stand in that order rather than tallest first, because the
contract's "declaration order" is the order the rules table beside the plate lists them. What a
casing's "height" is at all remains `oq/casings-are-measured-across-and-drawn-upright`'s question. The
layout only places what it is handed.

**4. A GUTTER THAT IS A FRACTION OF THE FRAME'S HEIGHT IS A CONSTANT NUMBER OF PIXELS.** Every frame is
scaled to fill one plate height. So `GUTTER_FRAC × heightIn × pxPerIn` is the same on the 114 in frame
and on the 6.9 in frame: 210 px in a 600 px row. That is what a leader label needs. The test asserts the
equality on `trim-classical` rather than the constant's value. `WALL_STRIP_FRAC` reserves the nominal
hatched strip behind each wall plane the same way.

**5. THE HUES AND THE CONSTRAINT-TO-STYLE RULE HAVE TWO COPIES EACH UNTIL LATER PACKAGES, AND BOTH
PAIRS ARE HELD TOGETHER.** `TRADITION_HUES` lives in `styles/taxa.js` and still in `Phylogeny.jsx`,
which imports it at WP-14.11. `styleTree.test.mjs` parses the component's object literal and requires
the two to be equal.

`names.js`'s `constraint:` branch names the style from the text before the last `.`, which is §E.3's
rule. `routeCite` takes that rule at WP-14.5, and until then there are two spellings. `names.js` holds
**no regular expression at all**: it reads every citation through `parseCite`, and a source guard
refuses one. The citation grammar stays spelled in three places, not four.

## V. Deviations from the contract

- **`feetInches16` refuses NaN, the infinities and non-numbers**, where §I.8 names only `null` and
  `undefined` (→ `-`). This is Python's own behaviour, since `_fmt_in` raises `ValueError`. A plate
  handed garbage has a defect upstream, and printing the absent mark over it would make a broken figure
  look like an absent one. A boolean prints as a number, as it does in Python.
- **`journeyState`'s steps carry `originWords`, `originHref` and `stateTerm`** beside §G's fields.
  §G asks for "the `judgment-refused` term" in the refused words, but a pure function holds no glossary.
  The word is `JOURNEY_WORDS.plan.refused`, and `stateTerm` names the record id so the bar can render it
  as a `Term`. A test holds each such word to its record's `term`, and skips as COULD NOT EVALUATE until
  WP-14.2's records land.
- **"*k* candidates" is singular at one**, so it reads "1 candidate". The word table is the one
  spelling, and the test reads it rather than a literal.
- **A failed or expired compose's own reason is the candidates step's `reason`**, even where the
  canProceed rule would give "open a candidate on the bench". The job's words say more than the rule
  can. §G names both reasons for one field and does not say which wins.
- **`assemblyLayout(assemblies, { box })`** returns `unplaced` beside `frames`. Each frame carries
  `gutterIn`, `wallIn` and a `scale` (`barIn` off `SCALE_LADDER_IN`, `words` by `feetInches16`, plus
  `pxPerIn` and `barPx` when a box is given). Each item carries its extent and `extentFrom`. §I.6's
  `{ id, xIn }` is a subset of all of this.
- **`placeLabels` returns `{ labels, overflow }`**. The labels come back in input order, and `overflow`
  is set where the labels cannot fit their bounds; they are then spaced past the bottom rather than
  overlapped.
