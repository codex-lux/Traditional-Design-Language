## I. The refusal the brief asked for had been in the record since WP-11.4

The PRD asks this package to place porch columns *"where a bound pack states an
intercolumniation for the order `order_at_the_eave` names, else refuse and name the missing
rule"*. I derived that refusal from first principles — three unmet preconditions, each of a
different kind — and then found `build/threshold.py` already emitting it, on both shipped plans,
in the record's own words:

```
the portico's columns   (rule th-a-portico-only-where-one-is-canonical)
  'tidewater-georgian' states no canonical portico (canonical porch type: stoop-only), and its
  `portico_bays` of 1 is conditioned by its own slot rule -- 'where a portico occurs it is one
  bay wide' -- on a condition this node does not meet
```

So `_porch` **reads `plan.threshold.unplaced`** and republishes each reason verbatim, and a
source guard refuses `portico_bays`, `intercolumniation`, `porch_support` and `porch_type`
anywhere in that function. **Following the brief literally would have written a second reader of
one rule** — this repository's most-repeated defect, and the one WP-12.6 had just met twice.

Re-derived independently before that was found, and it confirms the shape: **23 nodes make a
portico canonical, 8 resolve a `portico_bays`, 1 states a column diameter in inches, and 0 have
all three.** The intersection really is empty.

## II. A pilaster is not a member of this doorcase, and that is measured

| | door | + 2 casings | + 2 sidelights | = | stated |

| Tidewater | 42.099 | 14.034 | 28.066 | **84.199** | 84.199 |
| spec Colonial | 34.447 | 11.482 | 22.964 | **68.893** | 68.894 |

To the thousandth of an inch. **Substituting the stated `pilaster_width_in` gives 88.875 against
84.199** — so the composition's own arithmetic excludes a pilaster, and drawing one would put a

`order_at_the_eave`'s own docstring warns in as many words against reading a neighbouring signal
as evidence that a member is present here.

The test asserts the reconciliation **and** the discriminator, because an assertion that no
pilaster is drawn would pass with the whole function deleted.

## IV. The stoop's treads reconcile and its risers do not

```
(riser_count − 1) × tread_depth_in  =  2 × 13 in  =  2.1667 ft
flight depth                                      =  2.167  ft     ✓ the top riser is the platform

riser_count × riser_height_in      =  3 × 6.875   =  1.719  ft
grade_to_floor_ft                                 =  2.0    ft     ✗ short by 3.4 in
```

The flight stops three and a half inches below the floor it serves. That is
`oq/a-child-band-replaces-an-ancestor-derivation` reaching its **second** consumer — WP-12.6's
sill was the first — because `tidewater-georgian` `extends` `riser_count_from_grade` with a

kit. **Reported, and a fourth riser is not invented to close it.**

## V. The transom is stated and refused

`elevation.py` dimensions the whole transom family together — height, width the door leaf's,
head rise 0, a rectangular transom and not a fanlight — and feeds it to
`fanlight-before-its-date` and `transom-bar-at-the-wrong-height`.
**`render_elevation._entrance` draws none.** Drawing one in the model would make the model and
the plate two different doorcases, which is the one thing WP-12.0 forbids, so it is refused and
the disagreement is the finding: `oq/the-record-dimensions-a-transom-and-no-drawing-draws-one`.

The guard reads `render_elevation.py`'s own source, so the refusal cannot outlive the omission it
is about.

**The surround's relief is refused for a different reason and it is worth telling them apart.**
`elev.entrance` states `casing_width_in` and no projection; the cascade DOES state one
(`casing.backband_projection_in`, 1 1/16 in from `trim-classical`), and reading the kit here
would make `build/scene.py` a second reader of a slot the elevation already resolves half of.

## VI. The frame is the union of what is drawn, and containment was half a guard

WP-12.1 stated `bounds` off the section's footprint; WP-12.2 found it the right SIZE in the wrong
PLACE; WP-12.6 had to grow it over the chimneys by hand. This package would have needed **two
more hand-written cases** — an entablature cornice projecting 6.7 in out of the wall it dresses,
and a stoop standing at y = −3.46, three and a half feet clear of the house. A frame maintained
as a list of exceptions is wrong the moment somebody draws a thing not on the list, and
invisibly: a viewer frames the model from `bounds` and simply clips what it does not know about.

So `bounds` is computed from the solids, and `_extent` **raises** on a primitive it does not
understand rather than skipping it.

**And a mutation then proved the containment test was half a guard.** Swapping the axes in
`_extent`'s `xz` branch left the **whole suite green**, because a wrong mapping makes the frame a
SUPERSET — and a frame too large is a real defect in the other direction that nothing could see.
`test_the_frame_is_tight_and_not_merely_large_enough` asserts the other half. The test file keeps
its own extent reader (per-axis pairs, a different shape), so the two are independent readers of
the same four primitives and neither can ratify the other's mistake.

## VII. The approach

The camera stands off the front the record names, at the ruled 5′-6″, and the distance is
**derived rather than chosen**: `framing` already answers what half-height must be held, and at a
stated field of view that is one distance. Measured on the Tidewater scene: the eye lands at
**(31.5, −74.23, 5.5)** — about 73 ft in front of the house — looking **up 8.54°**, because the
house's middle is nineteen feet in the air and the eye is not.

**Eye height is the ruling's; the field of view is EDITORIAL and says so.** A 50 mm lens on 35 mm
film subtends about 27° vertically and an architectural photographer works wider; 40 is a choice
between defensible numbers. It is admissible precisely because **nothing is measured off this
view** — and the caption says so:

```
APPROACH TO THE SOUTH ELEVATION · THE ENTRANCE FRONT · EYE AT 5′-6″ · PERSPECTIVE · DIMENSIONS WITHHELD
```

Four refusals come with it, and they are the part worth reviewing:

- **A point behind the eye returns `null`**, not a coordinate. An orthographic projection is
  defined everywhere; a perspective divides by depth, and a point behind the eye projects to a
  finite, plausible, **mirrored** number. That is WP-5.11's sweep-flag class exactly.
- **No flat plate is laid over it.** The overlay's whole argument is a uniform scale; a
  perspective has a different number of feet to the pixel at every depth and no affine exists.
- **A move between the two projections is a CUT and never a tween.** A camera cannot be half a
  projection.
- **The furniture table gives it a compass and nothing else** — no scale bar, no bay ticks, no
  overall width, no datums. Every one of those is a measurement read off the plate.

**And the surface needed no change at all.** `namedViews` offers the chip only where the record
names an entrance front, `plateKeyFor` returns null so no plate is fetched, and `plateTransform`
returns null so none is laid — the leaf design paid for itself.

## VII½. And then I looked at the approach view

This project rates *render it and look at it* as the technique that finds what nothing else
does. WP-12.5 added the caveat that it **finds** defects and does not **adjudicate** them. Both
halves earned themselves here.

The approach screenshot showed the house, its sash bars, its doorcase and its two chimney axes —
and **two pale boxes at the bottom of the front, well away from the door.** Those are the stoop's
two risers. Measured rather than concluded:

```
tidewater-georgian-careful   the elevation draws the door at 32.79 ft   the placement seats it at 38.21 ft
spec-builder-colonial        the elevation draws the door at 25.67 ft   the placement seats it at 47.00 ft
```

**5.42 ft apart on one plan and 21.33 ft on the other, and on the spec Colonial the drawn front
door stands over the GARAGE.** `elevation._face_bays` writes `kinds[mid] = "door"` — the entrance
goes in the middle bay of the front, always — while `openings.place` seats the real door where
the porch room's own wall allows. Each record is right on its own, and **no surface had ever
drawn both**: the scene takes the doorcase from `opening_rects` and the stoop from
`plan.threshold`, so the model is the first picture in this project with the two in it at once.
That is WP-12.1's own finding repeated — *"invisible for as long as no surface drew a roof and a
room in one picture"*.

**And the checker convicts the half that is not drawn.** `plan_check` already emits
`drawn-door-off-the-centre-bay`, `serious`, off `axis.door_bay`:

> The front door stands in bay 5 of 7, not the middle bay (4).

So the corpus criticises the **placement** for a displacement the **drawing** silently corrects,
and the plate a reader turns to for evidence is evidence against the finding beside it.

**Nothing is moved.** Correcting the elevation moves sixteen shipped plates and has a ruling in
front of it — WP-11.7 says the facade is a RESULT, which argues for the placement; WP-11.3 built,
swept and deleted a centre-bay score, which argues against moving the placement.
`build/scene.py::_entrance_agreement` draws both where their own records put them and states the
disagreement, naming **both** positions, because either alone reads as a defect in the other
record rather than as a disagreement between two. The placed figure is `axis.door_bay`'s — the
corpus's one reader of where the front door is, and the one `plan_check` convicts on — so this
cannot disagree with the finding it is about.
`oq/the-elevation-draws-the-front-door-where-the-composition-wants-it`.

## VIII. What was deliberately not done

- **The dormer solids, the swept cornice, the water table and the belt** stay refused by name.
  The cornice belongs with the columns, and this corpus can draw no column anywhere (§I).
- **A flight on any face but the south is refused by name.** The step geometry lays treads along
  −y from the flight's outer edge, which is a south face's arithmetic; no shipped record places a
  stoop elsewhere, so the branch is driven by hand and its premise is asserted.
- **The six door panels the sheet draws are not modelled.** `render_elevation` is explicit that
  their stile and rail widths are *"drawn, not asserted"* — an arrangement and not a dimension —
  and an arrangement drawn in three dimensions reads as a measurement.
- **`build/scene.py` does not read the kit.** Two members could be dimensioned by doing so (the
  casing's backband and the porch column's diameter); both are refused instead.
