# WP-11.3 — The axis: the centre line, the bay the door stands in, and a score term refused

*Phase 11, package 3 of 11. 4 September 2026. From
`docs/reports/tidewater-layout-diagnosis-2026-09-04.md` findings B1, B2, B4, B5, C3 and G2, and
Lucas's ruling of the same day: half a bay module for the passage's centreline, no tolerance for
the door's bay.*

## What this package is

`build/plan_check.py` said it in its own comment: *"There is no axis vocabulary anywhere in this
codebase."* `build/axis.py` is that vocabulary, in one place, and `plan_check`'s drawn layer is its
first reader.

What it can say, and what nothing could say before:

| question | answer on the shipped Tidewater plan (heuristic) |
|---|---|
| is the through-passage on the footprint's centre line? | **on-centre**, 2.7 ft off against a 4.5 ft tolerance |
| is the front door in the middle bay? | **no** — bay 5 of 7, middle is bay 4 |
| is the entrance front mirrored about the centre? | **could not be evaluated** — 7 declared window units are not drawn |
| does each upper opening stand over one below? | **could not be evaluated**, same reason |

**The tolerance is Lucas's ruling and it is editorial**: half a bay module for the passage, none
for the door. No source states either. It is chosen so the period's own *"slightly off-center"*
passages are admitted — Westover's and Wilton's, where the asymmetry moves the ROOMS either side
and not the passage — and a passage in an end bay is not. `axis.py` says so in the field that
carries the number (`tol_basis`), so a reader meeting it downstream cannot mistake it for a
measurement.

**The block binds on the DIAGRAM's own claim to a centre bay** — `geometry.wants_a_centre_bay`,
from the massing's stated bay count or the parti's circulation type — so a Charleston single house
entered sideways off a piazza is not judged by it. That exception is the corpus's own, in
`rooms/centre-passage.json`, and it is why this does not fire on every plan.

## The refusal, measured

**A score term for the door's bay was built, swept and REMOVED.** `entrance_score` is a pair of
boolean touch tests: it charges 100 points for a threshold room off the entrance front and nothing
at all for one at the wrong END of it. A `centre_bay_score` charging the distance in bays between
the threshold room's centre and the middle bay's centre is the obvious repair. Measured over five
seeds on the shipped plan:

| weight | door in the centre bay | fatal findings (mean) | score (mean) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 of 5 | 7.6 | 696 |
| 5 | 0 of 5 | 7.6 | 701 |
| 20 | 0 of 5 | 7.8 | 714 |
| 60 | 0 of 5 | 7.8 | 748 |

**The term is paid at every weight and changes the chosen candidate at none of them.** The score
mean rises because the winner is being charged; the winner does not move. At **1,500 candidates**
instead of 250 the term does bite — the door goes from bay 5 to bay 2 at weight 20 — and still
misses the middle bay, overshooting to the other side of it.

That is enough to refuse it. The porch's position is decided by the slicing tree, and a small room
lands where a cut allows; no weight on a term makes the search produce a candidate whose cut lines
put the threshold room over the middle bay. **A door in the centre bay is a property of a plan
organised about an axis, not of a placement scored for one** — which is WP-11.6's container work
and, on the proving engine, a hard constraint rather than a charge. Recorded here, with the
numbers, rather than shipped at a weight that buys nothing.

This is WP-9.4's precedent applied deliberately: build the charge, measure it, delete it, keep the
measurement. The function is not left in at weight zero — *"an inert wrong number is an instruction
to the next reader"*.

## What was found

**1. SYMMETRY MUST NOT BE JUDGED ON AN INCOMPLETE FRONT, AND THAT IS A REFUSAL RATHER THAN A GAP.**
Seven of the Tidewater plan's declared front window units are not drawn (WP-11.1's own
disclosure). A facade missing seven units is not the facade the record describes, and convicting
it of asymmetry would charge the house twice for one cause. The mirror and alignment checks report
COULD NOT EVALUATE with the count, as an `info` finding whose own text says *"this is a refusal to
judge it on an incomplete elevation, not a pass"* — and the census (`drawn_summary.axis`)
publishes which of the four questions were answered, so a check that cannot fire cannot read as a
check that passed. That is WP-8.6's lesson, honoured in the design rather than after the fact.

**2. THE PASSAGE IS ALREADY ON THE CENTRE LINE, AND WP-11.2 IS WHY.** When the diagnosis was
written the "centre passage" was the whole west bay of a six-bay house. With the diagram's own bay
module and an odd bay count it lands 2.7 ft off centre — inside the ruled tolerance, and in the
same relation to the centre that Westover's and Wilton's passages have to theirs. The finding this
package was built to raise does not fire on the plan that raised it, which is the right outcome
and is stated here so nobody reads the silence as a check that never ran.

**3. A THIRD BLIND GUARD IN ONE SESSION, CAUGHT BY MUTATION.** The test asserting that the mirror
check refuses a gable-end entrance front **passed with the gable guard deleted**: the reader then
looked for openings on the E wall, found none, and returned COULD NOT EVALUATE for a completely
different reason. Right answer, wrong reason, no way to fail. It asserts the reason now. Three of
this session's own guards have now been blind in this way — WP-11.1's canvas-height comparison,
WP-11.2's vacuous growth-loop assertion, and this one — and all three were found by mutating the
code they guarded, none by re-reading them.

## What was deliberately not done

**No hard constraint in either engine.** The refusal above is about a soft term; a hard one is
available in `geometry_cp.py` and belongs with the container, because on a plan whose programme
does not fit its diagram a hard centre-bay constraint would most likely prove infeasible and take
the proof away with it. WP-11.6.

**Nothing about the facade is derived**, only measured. `oq/the-facade-is-a-result-not-an-input` is
ruled and is WP-11.7's.

**`mirror()` refuses a gable-end entrance front** rather than guessing the other axis: that case
has not been shown to be right, and a plausible answer is worse than a refusal.
