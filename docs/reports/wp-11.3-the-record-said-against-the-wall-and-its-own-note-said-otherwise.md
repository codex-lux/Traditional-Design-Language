# WP-11.3 — the record said against the wall, and its own note said otherwise

*4 September 2026. Phase 11, package 3. The exemplar plans Lucas set beside the Tidewater sheet
furnish every room; this corpus has carried the furniture to do it since the room catalogue was
written and has drawn none of it outside the wet rooms.*

## The ruling this executes

OQ 92 was half closed on 27 August 2026 and Lucas's words are the whole brief: *"room sizing
should not be furniture driven — that's the tail wagging the dog — however furniture
arrangements that suit the program / room name and room size should be placed accordingly."*
`docs/model.md` carries the direction of authority. WP-7.2 built the half the corpus states with
a figure and named the rest as editorial; three questions put on 4 September settled how far to
go: **author a `kind` field** rather than guess with a regex, **draw one rectangle per item and
disclose** where the item names a count, and **execute the four placements plus any rule whose
own sentence carries the figure**, naming the rest.

## What was found before any code was written

**1. `rooms/library.json` authored its table `against-wall` and its own note says otherwise.**
The note reads *"the table is in the MIDDLE of a library and against a wall in a study, and that
difference is what distinguishes the two rooms"* — the study's answer, on the library's record,
and `rooms/study.json` has its desk correctly against a wall. Corrected here on the strength of
that sentence, which is a reading and not a judgment.

**A regex cannot find this, and proving that took one command.** A sweep for a note contradicting
its own `placement` returned exactly one hit and it was a FALSE POSITIVE — `nursery`'s glider,
whose note says it *cannot* be put against a wall, agreeing with its `freestanding` — while
missing the real one, because the library's note names both placements in order to contrast
them. That is `check_grouping_rules.py`'s lesson arriving in a new place: author the field, never
tighten the regex until the number looks better.

**2. `footprint_in` is sometimes one piece and sometimes the whole group, and nothing says which.**
38 items name a count. `drawing-room`'s *"sofas, pair, facing"* is `[84, 34]` — one sofa.
`bedroom`'s *"twin beds, pair"* is `[100, 80]` — both beds and the gap between them.
`parlor`'s *"six to eight side chairs"* is `[20, 20]` — one chair. Ruled: draw one rectangle at
the stated footprint and say so. Raised as
`oq/a-furniture-footprint-is-sometimes-one-and-sometimes-the-group`.

**3. `rooms/stair-hall.json` carries *"the stair itself, dog-leg with half landing"* as a
furniture item** at 120 × 78 in, and `openings.stair_pass` already places and draws the stair.
Unfiltered, the sheet draws two stairs in one room.

**4. `rooms/bedroom.json` states a queen bed, a full bed and twin beds** — three alternatives,
all `essential` by default. A naive pass draws three beds in one room. Same shape in `closet`
(single versus double-hung rod) and `walk-in-closet`.

**5. The packer places at footprint; the critic judges at footprint plus clearance.**
`fixture_pass` fetches `clear_in` and never uses it. A room can therefore pack full and still
fail its own furniture check. Recorded rather than reconciled: they answer different questions.

## What was built

**`build/furniture.py`, a leaf.** `free_runs` (was `openings._free`) and `pack_against_walls`
(was the body of `fixture_pass`) were MOVED there, not copied — `build/storeys.py` and
`build/assemblies.py` are the precedent and both say in their headers why a module in the
placement path may import no sibling. `openings.py` keeps `_free` as a delegation so every
caller is unchanged and there is one implementation. **The fixture output is byte-identical**
across the move and across the whole package: fixture layouts, room geometry and relaxation
counts compared over all sixteen plans, twice.

**`kind` on all 278 furniture items**, required by `check_rooms.py` on `placement`'s own
precedent — `object` 241, `reservation` 23, `variant` 6, `placed-elsewhere` 5, `covering` 3.
The two mechanical predicates (`min(footprint_in) < 8`, and `clearance_in == 0` with
`placement: freestanding`) drafted the list; every member of it was then read. Thinness is
deliberately NOT a kind: a television is an object that has no plan bulk, which is a scale rule,
and it lives in the grammar as `fg-too-thin-to-draw` citing `plan_check.py`'s own `fw < 8`.

**`furniture/grammar.json`, where every rule declares a GRADE.** This is the package's real
contribution and it is "unjudged is not passed" applied to a rule's provenance: `reading` where
the record gives both the rule and the figure, `editorial-from-prose` where it gives the rule in
words and we supply the number, `editorial` where it says nothing at all. Ten rules — four
editorial, three readings, three stated and NOT executed. `build/check_furniture.py` verifies
every quote against the record it names under the key path it names, by **calling**
`check_openings.check_basis` rather than copying it; that function already walks
`furniture[<item>].note`, so the reuse needed no change to either file.

**`furniture/symbols.json` and the marks on the record.** Ten symbols matched by keyword with
`block` — the plain outline — as the default; 163 of 219 drawn items get a real symbol. The
unit-square-to-model-feet map runs ONCE, in Python, and the result is written onto the record as
`marks`. Both renderers draw what is there and derive nothing. That is WP-6.2's own finding
applied a layer up: handing both renderers one position and letting each re-derive the geometry
put them 0.7 in apart on the first plan it was tried on.

**Both renderers**, plus two repairs found in passing: `Sheet.jsx`'s fixture rect still carried
`stroke` and a literal dash as ATTRIBUTES and used none of `PEN` — WP-11.2 moved nineteen stroke
widths onto the ladder and walked past that one — and `walk.mjs` counted fixtures as rects whose
`stroke-dasharray` attribute began `"1.4"`, a selector the pen ladder would have emptied and
that furniture drawn with any similar dash would have silently inflated, asserting only `> 0` so
either failure read as a pass. Both now key on `data-fixture` and `data-furniture`.

## Three things the guards caught that reading did not

**The piano swept through the wall, and the rectangle check could not see it.** The first symbol
drew a grand's bent side as an arc whose radius scaled off the item's SHORT side. Measured: **13
of 1,078 marks left their room** while every item rectangle was inside its room the whole time. I
saw it by looking at the sheet and then measured it; the check that existed could not. There are
two tests now and the second says in its own name why it is not the first. The curve is withdrawn
rather than clamped — the corpus states no grand-versus-upright fact — and `arc` left the
vocabulary with it, since a primitive no symbol uses is an unreachable branch in two renderers.

**A desk was drawn as a bed.** `symbol_for` matched bare substrings, on `_FIXTURE_ALIASES`'
precedent, and *"desk (any BEDroom occupied by anyone under twenty-five)"* contains `bed`. Caught
by the guard asserting a bedroom draws at most one bed. Matching is on whole words now, plurals
allowed, and the docstring says where it parts company with the table it was modelled on.

**A refusal did not name the rule that refused it.** Every placed entry carried `rule` and
`grade`; the unplaced branch carried neither, so an item the pass could not seat lost the
provenance every drawn item has — when the grades exist precisely so a reader can tell whose
judgment a mark is. Caught on the first run of the guard that asserts every entry names its rule.

## Two of my own guards could not fail, and mutation found both

Six mutations were run against the new tests. Four went red at once. Two did not:

- The kind filter's test asserted the skipped set was a **subset** of the two refusal rules,
  which a rule that stopped firing altogether satisfies. Deleting the thin-item rule left it
  green. It requires both to fire somewhere in the corpus now.
- The wall-run test used the real 5.5 ft sideboard against 4 ft segments, so the item was
  refused for being too WIDE and the test passed with the run rule deleted. The fixture now uses
  an item that fits the segments and fails only the run.

Both were re-mutated after the fix and both go red.

## The ratchet moved, and the placement did not

`tests/test_furniture_drawn.py`'s short ceiling goes **86 → 88**, and the cause is not the
drawing. Re-derived both ways over all sixteen plans: the two extra shortfalls are the library
table in `good-02-portico-library-house` and `good-05-lobby-gallery-mansion`, and they appear
because the CATALOGUE was corrected. A table in the middle of a room takes clearance on two
sides rather than one — 6.83 ft becomes 10.33 — and two libraries that had passed now fail. The
conviction is right, so the ceiling is raised rather than the correction reverted, and the
reason sits above the number. Fixture layouts, geometry and relaxation counts being
byte-identical is how the cause was isolated rather than guessed at.

## The browser walk caught what nothing else could, twice, and one was my own edit lying

The suite was green and the walk was not. Both failures were invisible to 1,796 unit tests and
a clean build.

**A schema edit reported success and changed nothing.** The step that was to add `marks` to
`schema/plan.schema.json` used a `replace` whose target string was not in the file, asserted
nothing, re-parsed the (unchanged) JSON successfully, and printed *"4. schema admits marks"*.
The print was not evidence. The consequence was three layers down and silent: the solved record
failed its own schema, `core.check_plan` returned `{"error": "plan does not match the plan
schema"}`, and `workbench/server/evaluate.py` has an early `if "error" in check: return out`
**above** the branch that attaches the placement — so the API answered **200 with neither a
placement nor a placement_error**, and the Plan Workbench sat on "placing…" forever. No
exception, no console error, nothing in any log. It took a probe on the evaluate response body
to see that both fields were absent, and only then did the early return explain it. **Assert
that an edit landed, then re-read the file** — CLAUDE.md records this for a corrected number and
it is just as true of a schema key.

**And `placement_summary` did not carry the furniture**, so the browser sheet would have drawn
none of it while the Python sheet drew all of it, from one record. Fixed in `mcp_server/core.py`
beside the fixtures, for the reason the comment there already gives: it is a placement fact and
there is nowhere else for a reader to get it.

**A third selector repaired, and this one my own markup exposed.** `measureLabels()` called any
`<g>` whose first child is a `<title>` and which contains a `<rect>` a ROOM. That was never a
description of a room; it was a description of the markup a room happened to have. Furniture
groups are the same shape, so the room count went **13 → 44** and "every room is lettered"
failed for counting chairs. It selects `[data-room]` now. With the fixture counter and the
drag-preview stroke from WP-11.2, that is three guards in this file repaired for selecting on
incidental shape rather than on identity.

## Three more the suite caught, and each guard was right

- **A glob whose sort was one line up.** `tests/test_determinism.py` scans source line by line
  for an unsorted directory read, and `sorted(glob(a) + glob(b))` reads as unsorted on the second
  line. Each call site sorts itself now, which is also the plainer thing to read.
- **A by-path loader outside `modcache`.** `check_furniture.py` carried a
  `spec_from_file_location` fallback; OQ 28's guard counts them and found it. Routed properly.
- **The plan schema pin, caught a fourth time.** `tests/test_ingest.py` names the current schema
  version so a bump cannot pass unnoticed, and 0.5.1 → 0.6.0 duly failed it. Moved, with the
  reason beside its three predecessors.

## What was deliberately not done

- **The three `editorial-from-prose` rules are stated and not executed** — the library table
  centred *because it is a library*, the parlor's peripheral arrangement, the hall's high table
  facing down the room. Each needs a fact no record carries (which end is the dais? how many
  chairs is "six to eight"?), and OQ 92 drew the line exactly there.
- **No cardinality.** An item naming a count is drawn once. The question is raised.
- **Coverings and reservations are not drawn**, only counted. A rug lies under the furniture and
  drawing it as an occupying rectangle blocks the room.
- **The kitchen keeps its fixtures and gets no furniture.** The two gates are exact complements
  so nothing is drawn twice; the four items `rooms/kitchen.json` carries that no fixture list
  names are counted as `furniture_not_reached` — 11 across the Tidewater plan — rather than left
  to be noticed.
- **`furniture_layout` was NOT added to `tests/fixtures/sheet_symbols/`**, and the generator now
  says why: that fixture holds two implementations of one derivation to one answer, and
  furniture has only one. Adding it also meant regenerating, and `freeze()` re-solves with the
  live solver — measured here at **4,104 lines of diff moving every door and window position**,
  because CP-SAT answers on this machine and did not on the machine that froze it. That is
  precisely what the README warns the file must never absorb.
- **Two stale claims corrected in the same commit**: `schema/room.schema.json` and
  `docs/model.md` both said one item carries `needs_uninterrupted_wall_ft`. WP-7.4 corrected the
  count to five and neither was updated. And CLAUDE.md's published test count was **1,568
  against a measured 1,796** before this package started; it is the measured figure now.
