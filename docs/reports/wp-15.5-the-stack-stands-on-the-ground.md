# WP-15.5 — the stack stands on the ground

*Phase 15, the plates drawn. Built 27–28 September 2026, as two commits:*
- *the stack (`d70343c`);*
- *the words the elevation says about what it leaves out (`5049b95`).*

*This report and the Phase 15 bookkeeping are the commit after them.*

On 27 September Lucas reviewed the drawn Tidewater front and named four defects:

- a ground-floor window hard against the doorcase;
- **the chimneys**: *"the chimney continuing all the way down to the ground rather than just
  stopping"*;
- a cornice drawn as a flat band with none of its members;
- a Gibbs Ionic capital that looks nothing like Gibbs's.

Three of the four need the plates. Every host that holds them still answers `CONNECT tunnel
failed, response 403` from this container (WP-15.1). This package fixes the one that needs no
plate.

**Every elevation surface now draws an exterior end stack from the ground to its cap**, wherever it
stands in front of the face and at the square the placement seats. A stack the house hides still
stops at its roof line. The breast at the foot is stated by no record, so it is not drawn and the
sheet says it is not.

## I. What was built

### 1. One spelling of the stacks, which the DXF reads as well

The stack geometry moved out of `build/render_elevation.py` into `build/elevation.py`. The
renderer keeps its old names as aliases, for the readers that reach them through it. The moved
code:

- `profile_top_at` and `near_end_last`;
- `stack_outline(face, c, roof, fp)`: what one face draws of one stack;
- `stack_marks(elev, face)`: every stack a face draws, in drawing order, and every one it does not
  draw with the reason;
- `stack_notes(elev, marks)`: the sentences the face prints about them;
- `STACKS_UNSIZED_NOTE`.

The move follows `opening_rects`, which has been the one spelling of an opening since WP-12.2. The
sheet and the DXF elevation both read `stack_marks`, and **the DXF had drawn no chimney on any face
since the day it was written.** The CAD file of the Tidewater front was a house with no stacks,
which the kit calls *"visible from a mile away and conclusive against New England"*.

### 2. The rule: what stands in front of the face stands on the ground

Two cases depend on where the stack stands:

- **An exterior end stack in front of the face** is drawn from grade to cap at its own square:
  on both long faces, and on its own gable face. An exterior end stack stands wholly outboard of
  its gable wall, so on a long face nothing of the house is between it and the eye except the
  front's own wall bands and cornice. Those project a few inches past the corner and are drawn over
  it. This case is decided **before the roof profile is read**: a stack standing on the ground needs
  no roof line to foot it, so a roof record with no end profile refuses only the stacks such a
  profile would foot. A test drives exactly that.
- **A stack the house hides** keeps the roof-line foot it had. These are the far stack on a gable
  face, which stands behind the whole house, and any interior stack, which comes up through the
  roof. Where the far stack lies wholly inside the outline of the grounded stack in front of it, it
  is counted HIDDEN rather than drawn under the near one.

The WP-5.11 evidence argument, recorded in OQ 80 and still true, decides the stack's **width** and
not its height. The only width this corpus states is the stack's: brick-course's 22 in, itself a
judgment, *"the mason will build 18 or 27"*. The breast at an exterior stack's foot is several feet
across in any built example and has no figure anywhere. So:

- the stack goes to the ground at the least the mass can be;
- a breast is not drawn at a width no record gives;
- the legend says so on every face that draws one.

### 3. The sheet

- **The margin.** A grounded stack at the face's left end stands 1.83 ft (44 px) outside the face
  on the Tidewater plan. The sheet takes a left margin for it in WHOLE pixels, rounded up, because
  a fractional origin would move the rounding of every coordinate on the sheet. The frame's origin
  stays an integer where there is no such stack, which is why the other 124 sheets keep their bytes.
- **The ground line** runs 1.5 ft past a grounded stack, as it runs 1.5 ft past the wall. A ground
  line stopping at the wall would stand the stack on nothing.
- **The order of drawing.** On a long face a grounded stack is drawn before the wall and its
  bands. It stands at the stack's own depth, 31 to 33 ft back on the Tidewater plan, and the
  water table, belt and cornice band project past the corner nearer the eye. On its own gable face
  it stands in front of the wall and is drawn with the rest, after the roof.
- **The lift.** The cornice band the elevation stacks on the wall lifts the roof, and everything
  above it, by `ridge_delta_ft` (V19,
  `oq/the-elevation-stands-its-roof-on-a-cornice-band-no-other-surface-draws`). A stack's top is
  lifted with the roof it stands above. **A foot on the ground is not lifted**: lifting it would
  float an exterior stack 3.07 ft clear of the ground line on the Tidewater plan.
- **The legend** has two sentences, each printed only for the stacks it is true of:
  - `EXTERIOR STACKS DRAWN FROM GRADE TO CAP … NO RULE IN THIS CORPUS STATES THE BREAST OR ITS
    SHOULDERS AT THE FOOT OF AN EXTERIOR STACK, SO EACH IS DRAWN TO THE GROUND AT THE STACK'S OWN
    WIDTH`
  - `STACKS DRAWN ABOVE THE ROOF LINE ONLY … THE HOUSE HIDES THE REST` (or `THE ROOF HIDES THE
    REST` for an interior stack).

### 4. The DXF

- The stacks go on a `TDL-ELEV-STACK` layer, as closed polylines. What stands above the wall top is
  lifted by the cornice band, exactly as on the sheet, and nothing on the ground is lifted.
- Each polyline carries XDATA:
  - `from_grade`;
  - the stack's `side`;
  - the placement's square (`plan_rect_ft`);
  - its size and its `plan_judgment`;
  - `breast`: *"no record states it; drawn at the stack's own square"*.
- The grade line runs 24 in past a grounded stack, as it has always run past the wall.
- The sheet's own stack sentences are written beneath the drawing, from `stack_notes`.

### 5. Census V23 and X3 (the census holds 71 checks now)

- **V23** reads a stack's FOOT, which no check read before. An exterior stack in front of the face
  must:
  - stand its foot on the ground line the sheet draws;
  - have that ground line run more than a foot past it;
  - overlap no opening, door leaf, shutter leaf or doorcase.

  A stack the house hides must stand on the drawn roof: exactly on the drawn rake on a gable face,
  and between the drawn eave and ridge on a long face. The legend must say each of the two things it
  drew and neither thing it did not. On a gable face two stacks can share one extent: the Tidewater
  plan's two end stacks stand at the same depth, so each gable face sees one behind the other. There
  the check matches the NEAREST stack. Its first version took the first match, read the E face's
  near stack as the far one, and convicted a correct drawing of standing on the ground.
- **X3** holds the DXF's stacks to the sheet's, read back through the plate's frame in the face's
  own inches, to 0.15 in. A grounded stack must stand on a grade line running a foot past it, and
  every stack's XDATA must carry its square and its judgment.

### 6. What the elevation says it leaves out (the second commit)

Standing the stacks on the ground made two sentences the sheet had always printed read differently.

**Why an opening is not drawn.** The sheet named every opening it could not draw under one reason,
`THE PLACER OR A STACK REFUSED THEM`. On the tagged Tidewater front, five of the eleven openings
it named stand on the WING's face, and neither the placer nor a stack refused them:
`placed_openings` refuses them because this elevation is of the main block. That is WP-11.4's
*"a refusal with one message for three causes has stopped being a refusal"*, met in the sentence
written to account for what the plate leaves out.

The fix:
- Every refusal `opening_rects` returns now carries a `cause` from the closed
  `elevation.REFUSAL_CAUSES` (`placer`, `element`, `storey`, `stack`, `record`) beside its
  `why`.
- The sheet says each cause for the openings it is true of, reading the cause and never the prose:
  - with one cause, one line;
  - with several, a total and a line per cause, in the shutter legend's shape.
- A test holds the sheet's words to the causes, so a cause added without words fails rather than
  printing nothing.

On the Tidewater front the sheet now reads:

```
11 OPENING(S) ON THIS FACE NOT DRAWN — BACKHALL, BREAKFAST, DRAWING, KITCHEN, LIBRARY, UPPERPASSAGE — THE ELEVATION RECORD NAMES EACH:
· 6 (BACKHALL, DRAWING, LIBRARY, UPPERPASSAGE): THE PLACER REFUSED THEM
· 5 (BACKHALL, BREAKFAST, KITCHEN): THEY STAND ON THE FACE OF ANOTHER MASSING ELEMENT, AND THIS ELEVATION IS OF THE MAIN BLOCK
```

**That the elevation is of the main block.** No sheet had ever said so, and on the tagged Tidewater
plan it now matters for the stack.

The measurements, in the clear frame:
- the west stack stands at x −3.12 to −1.29 and y 30.01 to 31.85;
- the hyphen occupies x −7 to 0 and y 9.62 to 27.62;
- the dependency occupies x −34 to −7 and y 5.28 to 31.96.

So the stack stands in the corner north of the hyphen, and nothing stands in front of it from the
north. **From the south the hyphen stands in front of it, and from the west the dependency does.**
Neither is drawn, so the south and west faces draw the stack to the ground in open air.

What the elements hide is not computed, because the record states no roof over any of them.
`elevation.main_block_note` is the sentence, written by both the sheet and the DXF wherever the
placement sets a massing element beside the main block:

```
THIS ELEVATION IS OF THE MAIN BLOCK — THE DEPENDENCY AND THE HYPHEN THE PLACEMENT SETS BESIDE IT ARE NOT DRAWN, AND WHAT THEY STAND IN FRONT OF IS DRAWN AS IF THEY WERE NOT THERE
```

## II. What was found

**1. The sheet's own comment had said "drawn from grade" for a month while the block beneath it
drew the other thing.** The sentence went in with `f768c02`, thirty lines above a block that drew
only the part above the roof line on the evidence argument. Comment and code sat side by side
disagreeing, and Lucas's review of the drawing found it. It is corrected in place with a dated note.

**2. The 3D model has stood every stack on the ground since WP-12.6** (a box from z = 0, or an axis
where the size is a judgment). So this was a disagreement between surfaces as well as between
drawing and building, and nothing compared the two.

**3. Before V23, the census could not see the change. The stack change moved four sheets and the
census by zero rows** (`NEW 0, FIXED 0, MOVED 0`). V17 has held each stack's SQUARE and its TOP
across the faces since WP-14.6. It never held the FOOT, so a stack stopped at the roof and a stack
standing on the ground read identically. The new checks were run against the parent's code
(`07178dc`, with this census file) and against this tree:

| Check | Parent's code | This tree |
|---|---|---|
| V23 | 19 of 19 rows disagree | 19 of 19 agree |
| X3 | 4 of 4 disagree | 4 of 4 agree |

V23's 19 rows are the Tidewater plan's four faces and the fronts of the 15 styles whose kit gives
the stack a size, drawn on the Tidewater placement. X3's rows are the four faces.

**4. On the parent, the DXF elevation drew no stack on any face.** No row asked: X1 and X2 held the
doorcase and the sash and nothing else.

**5. Two mutations were not what they said, and both are why the guards are shaped as they are.**
- **M10** (the DXF grade line stopping at the wall again) left X3 GREEN on its first run. This
  file's grade line has always run 24 in past the wall, which covers a 22 in stack by 2 in, so
  "the line runs past the stack" held with the defect in. X3 now asks for a line running a foot
  past a grounded stack. Re-run, M10 is red on exactly the N and S rows.
- **M3** was meant to draw the long-face stacks AFTER the front. It edited one site, and that site
  removed them instead, so its redness said nothing about draw order. **M3′** edits both sites, so
  the stacks are drawn after the front and not removed. It is red on the one assertion that holds
  the order: the first stack precedes every band it stands behind.

All 23 mutations are red. The 15 of the stack were re-run on a worktree of the committed head with
the failing test or the moved census row read off each, not just its colour:

| Mutation | Red on |

| M2, the far stack not hidden | three tests |

The eight mutations of the second commit's guards are each red on the test written for them:
- a wing's opening labelled as the placer's;
- the pass-through dropping the cause;
- one cause printed for all;
- no main-block sentence;
- the DXF omitting it;
- the sentence printed on a one-rectangle house;

Every tree was restored and checked by sha256.

**6. What moved.** The stack commit moved four sheets and kept 124 byte-identical. Over the 128
sheets `render_digest.py` hashes, exactly the four Tidewater elevations move. A model-space diff (every mark matched by tag, class
and text, with every coordinate within 0.0045 ft) accounts for each change:

| Face | What changed |
|---|---|
| S, N | The two stack outlines' feet go from 32.965 ft to the ground line, and their shading lines follow. The ground line goes from −1.50…49.08 ft to −3.33…50.92 ft. One legend sentence is replaced by the other. Six marks go and six come, and nothing else moves. |
| E, W | The one drawn stack's foot goes to the ground, and its shading line follows. The legend sentence is replaced. Three marks go and three come. |

The other 124 are byte-identical, which is why the left margin had to be a whole pixel and the
frame origin an integer.

**The words commit moved 23 sheets, every one an elevation, and every one in its words alone.** The
23 include the four Tidewater faces. Each was rendered on both trees:
- every non-text mark is identical in model space;
- the only text added or removed is the refused-openings line, its cause lines and the main-block
  sentence.

The census is unmoved by it (`NEW 0, FIXED 0, MOVED 0`).

**And one defect of the second commit's own, found on its first run.** Its first version named its
line `_head`. That is the lambda the renderer defines 360 lines earlier to write the sheet's root
element, and calls again 190 lines later: WP-9.6's `top` collision, one name over. It failed
loudly (`'str' object is not callable`), which is the good version of that defect.

## III. What was deliberately not done

- **The breast and its shoulders.** No record here states the breast's width and projection, the
  height at which it steps in, or its weatherings. It is raised as
  `oq/an-exterior-stacks-breast-and-shoulders-are-stated-nowhere`, which forbids reading a
  shouldered outline off a picture of an exemplar. OQ 80 is amended in a dated section rather than
  rewritten.
- **A rake overhang.** The roof record models none, so on a long face the roof stops at the wall's
  end and overlaps no outboard stack. A test asserts that premise. Were an overhang ever modelled,
  the stack's depth against the ridge would decide which of the two is in front.
- **What stands in front of a stack, beside the main block.** The elevation draws the main block's
  face (`oq/the-elevation-draws-the-main-blocks-face-and-not-the-buildings`). From the south the
  hyphen stands in front of the lower part of the west stack, and from the west the dependency
  does. The sheet now says that the elements are not drawn, and that what they stand in front of is
  drawn as if they were not there. It does not cut the stack at an element's roof, because the
  record states no roof over any element. What an elevation of a house of several masses should
  draw is still that entry's question.
