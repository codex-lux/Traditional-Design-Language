# WP-15.6 — the wall beside the doorcase

*Phase 15, the plates drawn. Built 28 September 2026.*

Lucas, of the drawn Tidewater front (27 September): **"there's still no concept of how close windows
can be to doors."** He was right, and the measurement said exactly how. The placer reserved the
entrance door's LEAF and `MIN_SOLID_FT`, one foot, either side, and nothing else. The elevation then
drew the doorcase round that leaf: a 7.017 in casing each side, and a 14.033 in sidelight each side
where the composition fits them. So the centre passage's south window stood 12 in from the leaf and
**4.98 in from the casing**. The sidelight that would have stood there was refused for standing over
the window (WP-14.6), so the front drew no sidelights, because of a window and not because of its
kit.

The corpus had stated the rule all along, in facade-classical's rule for the entrance composition's
width. Its note says the composition *"is not allowed to touch the flanking windows"*. Its authority
note says *"The residual wall each side of the entrance composition should not fall below about half
the ordinary pier; below that the flanking windows begin to read as part of the doorway."* Nothing
read either sentence. The composition was computed in `elevation.py` after the placement, and
`elevation.py` cannot be imported by the placer.

**Now the placer reserves the doorcase's whole run, and half the ordinary pier beside it, before it
seats a window.** The elevation measures the wall it draws and says what it could not judge. On the
Tidewater front the passage window stands **33.01 in** from the sidelights, against a floor of
**33.0 in**: half of one 9 ft bay less the window's own 42 in. The sidelights are drawn again.

## I. What was built

### 1. `build/doorcase.py`, a leaf for the arithmetic both callers need

`elevation.py` loads `geometry`, which loads `openings`, so the placer cannot import the elevation.
The doorcase's arithmetic is a LEAF, on the precedent of `storeys.py` and `assemblies.py`, and both
callers read it:

- `rule`, `pack_env` and `val`, one derived rule read out of a pack. These moved out of
  `elevation.py` unchanged, and the elevation keeps its old names as aliases.
- `applies`, the elevation's own gate: a style inside opening-proportion's and facade-classical's
  `applies_to`. A doorcase exists only where the elevation draws one. 118 of the 159 styles with a
  kit fall outside it, and reserving a doorcase for them would move a placement for a drawing
  nobody makes.
- `composition`, the width figures of `elevation.entrance_composition`: the storey-derived leaf, the
  casing, the sidelights where the resolved kit does not forbid them, facade-classical's width cap,
  and whether the sidelights fit under it.
- `entrance_index`: the widest door, ties to the lower coordinate.
- `stated_bay_ft`: the bay a parti states, or why there is none. It has three states: the record
  states no bay; the record does not say who stated it; or the bay is the placer's own 10 ft default.
- `residual_pier_ft`: half the ordinary pier, at the flanking window's own width. The ordinary pier
  is facade-classical's `window_grouping_rule` pier, `module - opening_width`, with one bay as the
  module.
- `RESIDUAL_FRACTION_OF_THE_ORDINARY_PIER = 0.5`, the pack's prose "about half", transcribed once
  and held to the sentence by a test.

**The refactor was proved before the pier was built on it.** With `elevation.py` calling the leaf
and nothing else changed, all 128 sheets `render_digest.py` hashes came back byte-identical.

### 2. The placer reserves the run: `openings._reserve_doorcase`

The new pass runs on the ground level, after the exterior doors are seated and before the windows.
It picks the entrance door by the ELEVATION's rules, each read rather than restated:

- the gate (`doorcase.applies`);
- the face `context.entrance_faces` names;
- the elevation's own test for "on the main block's face", which asks whether the element's face lies
  on the main block's within 0.01 ft;
- the widest door, ties to the lower coordinate;
- no doorcase round a garage door, which the elevation never frames.

Its run is the door as PLACED, the width the elevation draws the leaf at. To that it adds the casing
each side, and the sidelights each side where `doorcase.composition` fits them.
`_doorcase_keepout` then adds half the ordinary pier each side, at the window's own width, wherever a
parti states the bay. Elsewhere it adds the placer's own foot. `opening_report.doorcase` records the
verdict either way: the door, the figures, the rooms the run touches, and whether the residual was
judged. A window that no longer fits is refused by name: *"the wall has no clear run left beside
its doors and the entrance doorcase"*.

### 3. The elevation measures the wall it draws: `doorcase_piers` and `doorcase_pier_notes`

`elevation.drawn_extent_in` is the outline an opening is drawn over: the entrance with its casing
and its drawn sidelights, and any other opening its own rect. It was lifted out of `_clearances`, so
that the shutter leaf refused and the pier measured read one outline. `doorcase_piers(elev, face)`
measures each side of the doorcase and judges it by each sentence where the sentence can be applied:

- **`touches`**: the doorcase touches or stands over the flanking window. This needs no module.
- **`agrees`** or **`short`**: half the ordinary pier, where a parti states the bay.
- **`unjudged`**, with its reason, where no parti states the bay.
- **`not_applicable`** where the nearest opening is a door or there is none before the corner. Both
  sentences are about the flanking WINDOWS. The side is measured all the same.

`doorcase_pier_notes` is the one spelling of what the sheet and the DXF say. They say every side
that touches or falls short, and they say once that the floor is not judged where no parti states
the bay. A side that agrees says nothing, because a plate certifies nothing it did not prove.

### 4. Census V24, which reads the ink (the census holds 72 checks now)

V24 reads the doorcase off the drawing, as the casing rect with the glass hard against it. A flanking
window is the nearest glass on each side, at the doorcase's height, that is neither a sidelight nor
a transom, and a door leaf nearer than any window ends that side.

- **The floor is read out of the pack in the census itself, never through `doorcase.residual_pier_ft`**,
  because that is what the subject calls. That is R4's trap from WP-14.2.
- **The census transcribes "about half" for itself** and holds its transcription to the pack's
  sentence.
- **The tolerance is the print**: an edge is written to a tenth of a pixel, at 2 px an inch.
- **The legend is held to the verdicts in both directions.** A shortfall the legend does not say is a
  disagreement, and so is a NOT JUDGED line on a sheet whose floor was judged.

**On the parent's code V24 disagrees on 43 of 43 rows**: the Tidewater front, each of the 41 styles'
fronts in the sweep, and the spec Colonial, whose sheet did not say its floor was unjudged. **On this
tree 42 agree, and 1 could not be evaluated**: the spec Colonial, whose sheet now says so.

### 5. Two rules at one address, and the checker that could not see them

On 15 nodes `sash-light` and `timber-bay` both wrote `window_grouping_rule` /
`minimum_solid_between_openings`, under the same `quantity` label *(corrected 28 Sep 2026, §V: 15
nodes bind both packs; through the cascade the two rules met at that address on 19 resolved kits)*:

- sash-light's rule is the solid between two openings, `opening_width * 1.4`, 24 to 96 in;
- timber-bay's is *"The minimum clear wall between the face of a post and the jamb of a window:
  about 15 in"*, `part * 1.25`, 8 to 30 in.

These are two quantities at one address, which is OQ 48's silent corruption. By OQ 48's own
procedure the dominant meaning keeps the address, and timber-bay's minority rule moves to
`solid_from_post_face_to_window_jamb`, as both its dimension and its quantity. Nothing in the tree
reads either address, so no drawing moved. The only generated file that moved is `dist/orders.html`,
which carries the packs' rules. `check_addresses.py --strict`, `check_pack_bindings --strict`,
`check_kits`, `check_inheritance --strict`, `check_orders` and the engine's selftest are all
unchanged.

**4. What the ordinary pier is measured at is itself a choice, and my first instrument made the
other one.** The measure-first census read the floor as half the elevation's `pier_width_in`: one
bay less the storey's PACK window width of 38.65 in, which gives 34.67 in. The placer and V24 read it
at the flanking window's DRAWN width of 42 in, which gives 33.0 in. The rule is about the windows
beside the doorcase, and those are the windows drawn, so the drawn width is taken. But the 1.67 in
between the two readings is `pier_width_in` meaning something other than the drawn pier, seen from
the inside. That is one of the three items in the new question.

**5. The fault that should catch the other half clears on a tautology.**
`pier-narrower-than-the-opening` tests `pier_width_in / adjacent_opening_width_in` at-least 1.0.
Nothing produces `adjacent_opening_width_in`, and the elevation's `pier_width_in` is the rhythm's
69.35 in. So on both shipped plans the fault clears on its solid-to-void secondary, and `plan_check`
already lists it in `fault_clear_on_a_generator_constant`. Meanwhile 24 of the 35 drawn
window-to-window piers stand below its floor, and 12 of those are exactly the placer's 12 in.

**6. `check_addresses.py` judges a collision by its label.** The sash-light/timber-bay pair passed
OQ 48's checker because both rules' `quantity` said the same thing. The checker counts co-binding
PACK pairs, and compares what their rules' authored labels declare, so two rules labelled alike
that mean different things pass it unseen. How many more there are is not known, because nothing
counts them.

**7. One guard was blind, and one mutant was equivalent.** Fifteen mutations went red on the first
run with one exception, M6: reading every room as on the main block's face. It was blind because no
shipped wing stands within the placer's 0.6 ft of the main front, so the test decided nothing on the
corpus. Its first version also asked the wrong question: which element a room is in, where the
elevation asks whether the element's FACE lies on the main block's. The two part on a wing built
flush with the front. The test now reads the elevation's own criterion, and it is guarded by a
driven fixture: a wing set back 0.3 ft with a wider door, beside a control where the same wing is
flush. M6 is red. **M6b was equivalent and is named**: OR-ing membership into the new test changes no
answer, because a main-block room's face always lies on the main block's. Its honest form, M6c
(membership alone), goes red on the flush control.

**8. A guard's premise ran out, as its own docstring said it would.**
`tests/test_refused_sidelights_are_said.py` asserted that the Tidewater front refuses its sidelight
pair, *"so the day the placement moves that window the file says so rather than passing over

the placer used to seat it. The placed front is a new control: its pair is modelled and drawn, and

**9. `--pin` pins whatever is red, including the page the change itself left stale.** Renaming a

## III. What was deliberately not done

- **The pier between two windows is not ruled and not applied.** The corpus states it three ways:
  - the packs' 1.2 to 2.0, with a figure of 1.4;
  - the Georgian kit's 0.6 to 1.0, which says it is editorial;
  - the fault's floor of 1.0.

  The placer keeps its foot. At 1.4, 25 of 35 drawn piers would have to move or lose a window, which
  is the placement change with the largest reach in this corpus:
  `oq/the-placer-seats-windows-a-foot-apart-and-the-corpus-states-the-pier-three-ways`.
- **The fault's wiring is not moved.** Pointing `pier-narrower-than-the-opening` at the drawn piers
  would apply one of the three statements, and choosing one is the ruling. `pier_width_in` also means
  four other things in five style constraints, so the drawn pier wants a name of its own when it is
  wired.
- **A doorcase's corner side is not judged.** Both sentences are about the flanking windows. The spec
  Colonial's doorcase stands 2.74 in from the corner of the house, and its 76.4 in composition is
  wider than the 6 ft porch room it serves. That is recorded, and put to the same ruling.
- **Decision 4 is not applied.** The sidelights the Tidewater kit forbids are drawn, and V2 says so.
- **The plan sheet still draws no doorcase**
  (`oq/the-plan-draws-no-sidelights-the-elevation-draws`), so there the reservation shows only as the
  window's new position.
- **Only the heuristic engine was measured.** The reservation is in `openings.place`, which both
  engines go through, so it applies to a CP-SAT placement too. The CP figures, which are not
  deterministic under a wall-clock budget, were not taken. *(28 Sep 2026, §V.3: one CP run was
  taken by the audit, and on it the passage window is refused rather than moved.)*

## V. Corrections, 28 September 2026 (the audit of Phase 15, WP-15.8)

rewritten except one quotation, which was not verbatim.
