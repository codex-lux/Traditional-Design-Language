# WP-16.1 — The governing test decides

*Phase 16, 29 September 2026. The fault verdict, the incomplete front, and the composer's tie-break.*

## I. What was asked

Three of Lucas's rulings of 29 September 2026 (`PLAN-OF-ACTION.md`, Phase 16):

- **R4.** A fault is clear only if its governing test ran: the primary, or the bounds test of an
  exception the style earns. Otherwise it is could-not-evaluate, with the tests that ran carried as
  evidence. A clear names every secondary that did not run, and a failed test still makes the fault
  present.
- **R12.** An incomplete front is unjudged for symmetry and alignment everywhere, not only where
  the diagram wants a centre bay.
- **R13.** The composer ranks by judged fatals, then by unjudged fatals. An unjudged fatal is never
  a fatal and never a pass.

The plan added two data gates (`storeys-out-of-vertical-alignment` on `storey_count >= 2`, and every
`overscaled-dormer` test on `dormer_count >= 1`). It also named the consumers and pins to
re-derive.

## II. What was done

**The verdict (R4), `mcp_server/core.py`.**
- `_judge` takes the governing test before anything is filtered: the primary, or the bounds test
  of an exception the style earns. A fault is clear only where that test ran and passed.
- Where the governing test could not run and another test did, the fault is could-not-evaluate.
  The row carries the tests that ran as `ran`, and names what the governing test wanted in
  `governing_not_run`.
- A clear lists every applicable secondary that did not run as `secondaries_not_run`. A test that
  declined on its own precondition is not listed, because it was not applicable.
- A failed test of any kind still makes the fault present.
- The judge-both-ways branch (an exception whose precondition cannot be resolved) used to return
  `needs: []` and a note saying "the two rules disagree" even when one side had simply not been
  judged. It returns the union of the needed sides' measurements now, and says which side could
  not be judged.
- Every could-not-evaluate row carries the fault's severity for the style, which R13 reads.

**The gates, `faults/`.** Four faults, every test and every exception bounds test:
- `storeys-out-of-vertical-alignment`, `top-heavy-second-storey` and `ungraduated-storeys`, on
  `storey_count >= 2`;
- `overscaled-dormer`, on `dormer_count >= 1`.

The second and third were not in the plan. §III.2 says why they had to be.

**The incomplete front (R12).**
- `axis.front_complete` is the one reader: every storey of the entrance front, the declared window
  units not drawn, and a sentence saying why that matters.
- `plan_check`'s drawn layer reads it where it read the ground storey's own count.
- `elevation.py` reads it and withholds the five symmetry and alignment figures on every incomplete
  front, naming each in `front.withheld` with the reader's sentence. The mirror and the alignment
  are still measured and stay on the elevation record.
- `plan_check` attaches the elevation's per-house reasons to each could-not-evaluate row that
  needed one, as `withheld`, beside the standing refusals in `refused`. The two are separate
  fields because they are separate claims. A refusal says no house will be given the figure; a
  withheld figure says this house was not.

**The tie-break (R13), `build/compose.py`.**
- `rank_key` orders by judged fatals, then unjudged fatals, then the score, the demerits and the
  parti id.
- Each card carries `unjudged_fatal` by name. The bench's candidate column shows the count, with
  the names as its reason, and `candidateOrder.js` sorts by it.

**The loop (`build/revise.py`).**
- `_judged_key` counts a verdict made could-not-evaluate where it stood. The declared loop can no
  longer accept a round whose "improvement" is a fault nobody can judge any more (§III.5).
- `raises_fatal_or_serious` is the one spelling of the rule a step that runs after the loop is held
  to. Both reclaims read it (§III.6).

**The composer's reclaim.** `compose.compose` rolls back an area reclaim that raises fatal or
serious. It says so in the decision log and on the card as `reclaimed` (§III.6).

**The surfaces.**
- The bench's reason for a could-not-evaluate fault is `faults/unjudged.js`, lifted out of
  `PlanWorkbench.jsx` so it can be driven under `node --test`. It says when the governing test
  could not run, and gives the withheld reason when there is one.
- The `tdl_check_measurements` docstring describes the governing test, `ran` and not-applicable.

**The instruments.**
- `tests/fault_clears.py` counts could-not-evaluate rows carrying evidence and clears naming an
  unrun secondary. Its docstring now describes both trees.
- `tests/compose_sets.py` is new. It composes every brief with the declared loop's wall clock
  lifted, and records the returned set and every scored candidate with the figures the ranking
  read.

## III. What was found

**1. The verdicts, all of them accounted for.** The sixteen shipped plans were placed on
`engine="heuristic"` against a worktree of `a4abb85`. All 3,273 (plan, fault) verdicts are
conserved:

| Movement | Rows | Cause |
|---|---:|---|
| clear -> could-not-evaluate | 134 | R4: the governing test did not run (18 faults) |
| clear -> not applicable | 8 | the gates: `overscaled-dormer` on the two houses that state no dormers, `storeys-out-of-vertical-alignment` on six one-storey plans |
| present -> not applicable | 12 | the gates: `top-heavy-second-storey` and `ungraduated-storeys` on six one-storey plans (§III.2) |
| present -> could-not-evaluate | 15 | R12: `one-bay-symmetry-break` (6), `closet-on-the-exterior-wall` (5), `storeys-out-of-vertical-alignment` (4), over six plans |

Totals: clear 550 -> 408, present 266 -> 239, could-not-evaluate 2,454 -> 2,603, not applicable
3 -> 23.

- **The option put to Lucas said 142 verdicts would become could-not-evaluate; 134 did.** The other
  8 are the gates.
- **Two figures of 142 are easy to confuse.** On `a4abb85`, 284 clears had some applicable test
  unrun: 142 the governing test and 142 only secondaries. The first 142 left the clear list. The
  second 142 stay clear and now say what did not run. The equality is a coincidence.
- **The count of fatals nobody could judge rose.** 40 of the 138 could-not-evaluate rows carrying
  evidence are fatal. `veneer-reveal-collapse` is one of them on eleven plans: it cleared on
  `reveal_depth_in` while its governing test wanted a brick stretcher length nothing supplies.

**2. `storey_count` was a constant, and six one-storey plans were convicted of a second storey.**
- `elevation.py` set `storey_count = len(elev["storey_windows"])`, and `storey_windows` always holds
  two entries, so every house was two storeys to the fault corpus.
- Worse, `upper_w` falls back to the ground storey's window where the section states no upper
  storey. So `second_floor_sash_height_in`, `second_floor_sill_height_in` and
  `second_storey_floor_to_floor_in` were the ground storey's figures under a second storey's name.
- `top-heavy-second-storey` and `ungraduated-storeys` convicted all six one-storey reference plans at
  a ratio of exactly 1.0: a storey compared with itself. Twelve present verdicts, eleven of them
  serious.
- `storey_count` is read off the section now. The three figures are withheld on a one-storey house,
  and both faults are gated on `storey_count >= 2`.
- Found by the sweep that followed the planned gate on `storeys-out-of-vertical-alignment`, which
  read the same constant.

**3. The drawn layer counted the ground storey only.** Both shipped fronts were reported one unit
short: the tagged Tidewater record has five undrawn units (four ground, one upper) where the sheet
said four, and the spec Colonial three where it said two. The finding sentence on the spec Colonial
moved 2 -> 3, which is one of the four rows `tests/test_element_awareness.py`'s finding digest moved
by. The Tidewater fixture there has no upper unit undrawn, so its sentence did not move.

**4. A could-not-evaluate row did not say why a figure was missing, when the reason was one layer
down.**
- R12 withholds figures per house, and the reason lived only in `elevation.front.withheld`. A reader
  of the fault was handed "needs max_abs_offset_between_upper_and_lower_opening_centrelines_in", a
  work item for a figure withheld on purpose.
- The row carries the reason now. So does the bench, through `faults/unjudged.js`.
- Separately, a one-storey record had always left the alignment trio out of its measurements with
  nothing in `front.withheld` saying so. It names them now, with the storey count as the reason.

**5. The declared loop could have accepted a lost verdict as an improvement.**
- After R4 and R12, a move that leaves a front window undrawn turns two fatals from present to
  could-not-evaluate, and the key falls by two.
- `revise._improves` compared keys, so it would have accepted that round. `_judged_key` counts the
  lost verdict where it stood.
- It is inert on both shipped briefs, measured: over the 113 key comparisons the two briefs'
  declared loops make, the raw key and the judged key never disagree.
- It holds R13's own words: an unjudged fatal is never a pass.

- Both read `revise.raises_fatal_or_serious` now. A reclaim that raises fatal or serious (a verdict

## IV. What was deliberately not done

- **The `not-applicable` severity** (§III.8) is a question about whether a fault applies at all,
  which is not R4's question. Counted, named, not changed.
- **No measurement was supplied to lower a could-not-evaluate count.** `brick_stretcher_length_in`,
  the entablature depth and the others R4 now names are what a later package might model. Supplying
  one to make a number fall is the move this corpus refuses.
- **R13 does not reach serious.** The ruling names fatals.
- **`refused_all` is unchanged.** It says a fault can never be judged, and a figure withheld on one
  house does not make that true.
- **The plates** stay blocked; nothing here needed them.
