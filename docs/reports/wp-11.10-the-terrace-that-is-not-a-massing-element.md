# WP-11.10 — the terrace at grade: the package that was gated on a question it did not have to ask

*7 September 2026. Branch `claude/georgian-tidewater-plan-analysis-qbwmqs`. Every figure in this
report was re-derived on the working tree and, where it is a BEFORE, on a `git archive HEAD`
checkout of the pristine tree — WP-9.5's rule: re-derive the number, do not re-read the sentence.
All corpus figures are `engine="heuristic"`, which is deterministic; nothing here is an `auto`
reading.*

---

## I. The ruling, and the gate that turned out not to apply

`PLAN-OF-ACTION.md` opened this package gated on
`oq/the-proving-engine-cannot-place-a-second-massing-element`, and the plan file's own Part IV.B5
says why: *"an at-grade, unroofed element on the wall of the room it serves … is a third massing
role and lands in the same six-layer disclosure."*

Reading the code before writing any turned that from a fact into a choice. The CP refusal is keyed
on the room's `block` TAG and not on `footprint.blocks`:

```python
# build/geometry.py:2399
_blocked = any(is_block_tag(r.get("block"))
               for lv in plan.get("levels", []) for r in lv.get("rooms", []))
```

A pass that writes no tag never reaches it. **Under the massing-element reading WP-11.10 is gated;
under the appendage reading it is not gated at all.** Ruled 7 September 2026, before any code:

> **An at-grade unroofed appendage is NOT a massing element.** It is a plan-level record on
> `plan.threshold`'s precedent — derived after the solve, drawn outside the block by both
> renderers, read by no structural layer.

The argument for it is not tidiness. It is that three filters already in the tree keep every layer
below the placer blind to an appendage **by construction**, provided its room's `geometry` stays
absent:

| layer | the filter it already has | what a rectangle on the room would have done |
|---|---|---|
| `structure.wall_lines` | `placed = [r for r in level_rooms if r.get("geometry")]` | four exterior walls round a terrace; a clear span measured across it |
| `plan_check` `rooms_unplaced` | `takes_a_rectangle(type)` | its own WP-11.6 comment names the terrace as the CORRECT `is_placed == False` case |
| `geometry._record_prep` / `blocks_record` | `is_placed(r["type"])` | terrace area inside a massing element, and `footprint.blocks` describing a rectangle no room occupies |

So WP-11.9's six taught layers are not reopened, no plan trades its proof for a search, and the CP
question stays open and untouched. The cost is exact and it is not absorbed: the drawn rectangle is
held against no band, which is `oq/an-at-grade-appendage-is-drawn-and-not-judged`.

## II. What the corpus did with a terrace before this package

Six `terrace` rooms across five of the sixteen plans — `tidewater-georgian-careful` (14 × 22),
`bad-07` (two wood decks, 8 × 14 and 8 × 16), `good-02` (10 × 24), `good-04` (10 × 16), `good-07`
(12 × 40). `rooms/terrace.json` is the only record in the corpus with `void.within_footprint:
false`, and **none of the six was placed, on either engine, by design**.

**The record's own note is right, and it is right about the block:**

> *"a terrace keeps the pre-OQ-33 behaviour — left out of placement entirely — which for a terrace
> is the right answer rather than a limitation."*

It is. A terrace takes no rectangle in the footprint, is not in the heated envelope, and the
building is the same shape without it. What that sentence could not know is that the corpus has
since grown a second place to put an at-grade thing: `plan.threshold` (WP-11.4). The note is not
contradicted by this package; it is answered.

## III. The fatal has two causes and the terrace is one of them

This is the sentence the package is most likely to be misread against, so it is stated first. On
`tidewater-georgian-careful` the `breakfast` room declares exactly two doors and **both were
refused**:

```
door -> kitchen  : "the placement leaves these two rooms no shared wall"  (needs 5.7, have 0.0)
door -> terrace  : "one of the two rooms is not placed on this level"
```

The kitchen sits at x 0–20 and the breakfast room at x 47.5–60. **That refusal is the placer's and
this package does not touch it.** Placing the terrace removes the second reason only. The
`drawn:breakfast` fatal is cleared, but by §VI's reachability ruling and not by the door.

## IV. What was built

**`build/appendages.py`, a LEAF** on `build/stacking.py`'s precedent and for the same mechanical
reason: it runs inside `openings.place()`, `geometry.py` calls that, `structure.py` loads
`geometry.py`. What it needs from `build/elements.py` — `bounds_index` and `boundary_walls` — is
**passed in**, exactly as `stacking.multi_level_disclosure` takes `is_placed`.

**It runs BEFORE the level loop, and that is forced rather than tidy.** `_place_interior` is the
first pass inside it, so an appendage derived afterwards could never seat the door it exists for.
`entrance_pass` runs last for the opposite reason: it reads placed doors.

Plan schema **0.9.0** admits `appendages`; `openings.PLACEMENT_PLAN_KEYS` gains it so the DXF round
trip still returns the authored record. All 16 placed plans validate.

### Three rules, all `reading`, each quoting the record it read

`build/check_openings.py::check_basis` verifies every basis — the record exists, the quote is
really in it, and the quote lives under the key path cited. `build/check_furniture.py` calls the
same function for the same reason; a second copy of that machinery is the thing this corpus keeps
being bitten by, so there is no new checker and `TOTAL_CHECKS` does not move.

1. **`ap-hangs-on-the-room-it-opens-to`** — the door is what says which room an appendage is
   appended to. `rooms/terrace.json adjacency.should_adjoin[breakfast-room].why`.
2. **`ap-the-face-both-readings-admit`** — §V.
3. **`ap-the-record-says-which-is-depth`** — `width_ft` is the depth out from the wall and
   `length_ft` the run along it, because `dimensions.critical_dimension` names DEPTH as the
   dimension that matters (*"A terrace under 10 ft cannot hold a table and a passage behind the
   chairs"*) and the bands are `width_ft [10, 30]` against `length_ft [12, 40]`. The run is clamped
   to the element's own face and the clamp is disclosed with `need`/`have`.

## V. `exterior_walls` means two different things on an outdoor room, and the figure is the intersection

The finding this package's design turns on, measured over all six terrace records:

| plan | `exterior_walls` | reads as |
|---|---|---|
| `tidewater-georgian-careful` | E, N, S | **the free faces** — a rectangle cannot be attached on three sides |
| `bad-07` (east deck) | E, N | ambiguous |
| `bad-07` (west deck) | N | — (no door; refused earlier) |
| `good-02` | W | **the side of the house it is on** |
| `good-04` | N | the side of the house it is on |
| `good-07` | N | the side of the house it is on |

**Neither reading resolves the six on its own.** The free-face reading turns one declared wall into
three candidates; the side-of-the-house reading turns three declared walls into three. Read as free
faces the served face is `OPPOSITE(f)` for any `f` NOT declared; read as the side of the house it
is a wall that IS declared. Both admit `w` exactly when **`w` is declared and `OPPOSITE(w)` is
not** — one line, and it says something true either way: *a face whose opposite is also free cannot
be the one against the house, and a face nobody declared is not a face the record put outside.*

That is **WP-11.4's own rule one layer up** — three statements of one stoop depth, *"the figure is
the intersection and never an average"* — and it is this corpus's standing answer where two
readings disagree: take what both admit, or take nothing. The candidate is then intersected with
the faces the placement actually put on the outside of the served room's own massing element
(`elements.boundary_walls`, WP-11.9 ruling 4). Unique after all three, or **UNJUDGED**.

### What it places and what it refuses

| plan | appendage | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `tidewater-georgian-careful` | terrace | **placed**, E face of `breakfast`, 14 × 22 at (60.0, 18.08) |
| `good-02` | terrace | **placed**, W face of `family`, 10 × 24 at (−10.0, 11.96) |
| `good-04` | terrace | **placed**, N face of `enclosed-porch`, 16 × 10 at (34.0, 37.06) |
| `good-07` | terrace | **placed**, N face of `foyer`, 40 × 12 at (30.0, 33.88) |
| `bad-07` | `wood-deck-w` | **refused** `no-door` — the record declares none, and nothing says which wall it hangs on |
| `bad-07` | `wood-deck-e` | **refused** `no-face-both-readings-admit` — it serves two rooms, one of them landlocked, and E and N are both admissible |

**Neither refusal is patched into a placement.** Reading `should_adjoin` for the first, or taking
the first candidate for the second, would each be a guess wearing a figure. Refusal codes are a
CLOSED table (`REFUSALS`, on `build/construction_vocabulary.py`'s precedent) with a reason apiece.

## VI. A placed at-grade appendage is OUTSIDE — one fatal cleared, twenty-seven created

`plan_check`'s reachability walk seeded `outside` from placed `to: "exterior"` doors alone. A
person standing on a terrace is outside the house. The guard added is narrow and every clause is
read off the record `build/appendages.py` wrote: the appendage must be in `placed` (a refused one
mints no way in), `at_grade`, and NOT `roofed`.

**Measured over the sixteen plans:**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| fatal | 136 | **162** |
| serious | 681 | **685** |
| minor | 992 | 992 |
| info | 167 | **164** |
| advisory | 27 | 27 |
| refused doors | 243 | **236** |

It clears `drawn:breakfast` on `tidewater-georgian-careful` (9 `unreachable` fatals there → 8), and
it turns **three COULD-NOT-EVALUATE verdicts into 27 `unreachable` fatals** — `good-02` 7,
`good-04` 10, `good-07` 10 — which is *unjudged is not passed* working, because **those three plans
have no placed exterior door at all** and the walk had nowhere to start. Their only report was one
`info` line saying so. **27 created against 1 cleared is the net +26 in the table above**, and the
two numbers are stated separately because a reader given only the net cannot tell which way either
half went. Every one of the 27 is true of the drawing: `good-07`'s foyer is reached from the
terrace and its own onward doors are all refused, so ten rooms behind it really are unreachable.

**This is published as a package that made three plans judgeable and not as a package that added 27
fatals**, and both halves are in the same table so a reader can decide for themselves. The one
argument against it is legibility — ten fatals whose single cause is one missing front door is a
report a reader has to reconstruct — and that is answered rather than argued away: the cause gets
its own `serious` finding, `outside-is-only-an-appendage`, on exactly those three plans and not on
the Tidewater plan, which has a placed front door as well.

Two things NOT claimed here. It is not claimed that the terrace door clears the Tidewater fatal —
§III. And it is not claimed that the three plans got worse: they were as broken before, silently.

## VIII. The drawing, and the disclosure

Both renderers draw the appendage **from the record and derive nothing**: `render_plan.
appendage_rects()` feeds the plate's own extent list beside `threshold_rects` (a rectangle outside
the block that the plate was not sized for leaves the sheet with no error anywhere — WP-11.4's own
lesson), and a `<g data-appendage="…">` group draws it on the ground plate only. `Sheet.jsx` takes
the same two additions in the same two places.

It is drawn **OPEN** — an edge in the fine pen and a name, no poché and no wash. An at-grade
appendage is a FLOOR: the paper is the terrace, exactly as the paper is every room. Giving it the
wall's body would say the house is that shape, which is the one thing the terrace record's own note
is at pains to deny.

`geometry_report.lot_extent` gains an `at_grade` block. WP-11.9's ruling 2 put the lot cap on the
BUILT EXTENT, elements only; an at-grade appendage is not an element, so `fits_lot` is measured
WITHOUT it — and a terrace fourteen feet past the east wall while `fits_lot` reads green is the
OQ 52 family wearing the safe-looking sign. On `tidewater-georgian-careful`: built extent
**60.0 ft**, ground covered **74.0 ft**. Two questions, two numbers; the record says which is which
and no rule yet says which a setback is held against.

**`_disclose_at_grade` is a SECOND disclosure call and not a line inside `_disclose`**, because
`_disclose` runs inside each record writer BEFORE `openings.place()` and `plan["appendages"]` does
not exist yet. That ordering is stated in the function rather than left to be discovered.

## VIII.b The door was seated and the sheet said it could not be drawn — found by looking

The first version of this package placed the terrace, seated the door, and **both plates went on
printing that door under "N DECLARED DOOR(S) WITHOUT A DRAWABLE OPENING"**. `good-02`'s schedule
read *"6 … FOYER–STAIR-HALL, STAIR-HALL–KITCHEN, LIVING–DINING, DINING–KITCHEN, KITCHEN–MUDROOM,
FAMILY–TERRACE"* with `family`'s door carrying a wall, a position, a hinge and no `unplaced` mark
at all.

The cause is the mechanism of the ruling meeting one of its own consequences.
`render_plan.derive_openings` builds its lookup as `idx = {r["id"]: r for r in rooms if
r.get("geometry")}` — and an appendage's room deliberately carries none. The lookup missed, and the
branch it fell into says *"the other room is not placed on this level"*. **The record said seated
and the drawing said undrawable: two records of one door**, which is WP-6.1's own finding arriving
through the door the package had just opened.

`derive_openings` and `workbench/app/src/sheet/derive.js::doors` both take an `appendages` argument
now, in the same position, merged into the lookup and used for the shared face and the swing
direction and nothing else. The plates read **14 → 13** and **6 → 5**.

**It is guarded by a HAND-BUILT pair rather than through `tests/fixtures/sheet_symbols/`**, and the
reason is that fixture's own README: its generator calls the live solver on `auto`, so regenerating
it to reach this branch would be eight hundred lines of solver noise around two rectangles. The
same numbers are asserted on both sides —
`tests/test_appendages.py::test_derive_openings_draws_a_door_to_an_appendage_and_refuses_it_
without_one` and `derive.test.mjs`'s *"a door to an at-grade appendage is drawn once the appendage
is passed in"* — so if either renderer stops taking the argument the two disagree.

**And the first guard for it could not fail.** A test reading `derive_openings` directly stayed
green with `render()`'s call site reverted to `appendages=None`; it now reads the rendered plate's
own schedule, which is the number a person sees.

**Nothing in the suite could have caught this and looking at the sheet did** — six packages running.

## IX. The audit of this package's own guards

Nineteen mutations were run against the twenty-three new tests, each **asserted to have LANDED**
before the suite ran — a replacement that silently does not apply looks exactly like a guard that
works (WP-9.6). Every one now bites. **Three did not on the first pass, and all three were branches
nothing in the shipped corpus can reach:**

- the `roofed` refusal — no record says `within_footprint: false` and `roofed: true`;
- the `no-depth` / `no-run` refusals — every terrace record states both figures;
- the run clamp — none of the four placed appendages asks for more run than its face has.

Each is a real guard against a record this corpus does not yet have, so each was given a synthetic
fixture that proves it bites rather than being deleted or left green. A branch that cannot fire is
worse than no branch.

One assertion was **written wrong and caught by its own run**: a whole-file check that
`render_plan.py` does not mention `exterior_walls` — which it legitimately does, for the openings
it derives. Scoped to the appendage block in each file. A guard that can only ever be deleted is
not a guard.

`test_every_definition_this_package_added_landed_once` is WP-11.9's own lesson carried forward:
that package left two `def multi_element_disclosure`s in one file, new above old, and Python took
the last.

- **`tests/test_modcache.py::test_no_new_by_path_loader_outside_modcache` caught a local by-path

  `storeys`, `elements` — carries no CLI at all, and this pass's whole output is already on the

## X. What this package does NOT do

- **The terrace is not a massing element.** No walls from `structure`, no slab or storey in
  `export_ifc`, no roof plane, not in the built extent.
- **Its drawn rectangle is not judged** against area, proportion, width or furniture fit, because
  the drawn layer reads `room.geometry` and there is none.
  `oq/an-at-grade-appendage-is-drawn-and-not-judged` carries the three things that must be ruled.
  Nothing is lost: every one of those bands already fires on the DECLARED record, and `bad-07`'s
  two 8 ft decks are convicted today exactly as they were before.
- **No terrace furniture.** The catalogue's outdoor table is judged on the declared record and
  stays there.
- **The kitchen door on the Tidewater plan is still refused**, and it is the placer's failure.
- **`servicing.plumbing: "light"` on a terrace** goes on producing five `info` findings ("a plumbed
  room type with no fixtures listed"). Defensible as authored — a hose bibb is plumbing — and not
  touched; named here so the next reader does not read the silence as agreement.
- **`oq/the-proving-engine-cannot-place-a-second-massing-element` is not closed and was not
  needed.** It still gates the shipped-record dependency tags.
- **No new checker.** The three rules' bases are verified through `check_openings.check_basis`;
  `TOTAL_CHECKS` and the `N of M checks passed` illustration do not move.

## XI. What was looked at

The Tidewater sheet was rendered and opened — five packages running, that has found what nothing
else did. The terrace draws east of the block, at grade, in the fine pen, named, with the plate
sized to hold it. One thing seen there and left alone with its reason: the appendage's west edge
sits on the **block edge, which is the wall's INSIDE face** (WP-11.1), so its 14 ft depth is a
clear extent measured exactly as every other room figure on the sheet is — and the margin schedule
already says so in as many words (*"room figures are the record's clear extents"*). Pushing it out
by the 15.5 in of envelope would have made it the one figure on the plate measured differently, and
would have broken the 0.4 ft abutment `_shared` needs to seat the door.
