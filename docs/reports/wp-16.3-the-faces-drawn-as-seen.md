# WP-16.3 — The north and west faces drawn as seen

*Phase 16, 29 September 2026. R2: every elevation drawn as a person standing in front of it sees it.*

## I. What was asked

Lucas's ruling R2 of 29 September 2026, asked as a direct question
(`oq/an-elevation-does-not-state-which-end-of-the-face-it-starts-from`):

> The draughtsman's convention. One switch mirrors N and W for the sheet, the DXF, the 3D scene
> and the stack reader together. Every N and W sheet redraws with the same content reversed, the
> Round lays all four plates, and the pins are re-derived with attribution.

From WP-13.3 until now every face was drawn with the plan's own axis: west to east on S and N,
south to north on E and W. From the north and the west that axis runs right to left, so an N or W
elevation read as the house's mirror image. The Round refused both plates (WP-15.8's audit) rather
than lay them over the model backwards.

## II. What was done

**The switch.** `elevation.FACE_MIRRORED` is True on N and W. On those faces `u` runs from the
face's own left edge as seen from outside: east to west on N, north to south on W. On S and E it
still runs with the plan's axis, which on those faces is the same thing.

**The axis is the face's drawn width.** A mirrored face is the plan-direction face reversed about
its own drawn width: the outside figure `section.footprint` states, which the wall band spans and
roof.py lays the roof over. `elevation.face_span_outside_ft` is that figure, one spelling for
every mirrored reader. §III says why it is not the exact span.

**The readers that assumed the plan's direction, and what each reads now.**

| Reader | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `elevation.face_u_ft` (openings, bays) | `span + t - along` on a mirrored face, never live | the drawn width less `along + t`; takes `outside_ft` |
| `elevation._face_bays` | sorted `u` beside the clear centres in plan order | pairs each `u` with its clear centre, then sorts |
| `elevation.placed_openings` | entrance tie broken on the face's `u` | tie broken on the plan coordinate, as the placer does |
| `elevation.dormers` | a tie between two central windows went to the lower index | goes to the lower plan coordinate |
| `elevation.stack_axes_for_face` | the roof's own x or y | the face's `u` (`face_u_outside`) |
| `elevation.stack_outline` | outline in the roof's frame | left-to-right in the face's `u`; the gable branch reorders the rake's foot |
| `render_elevation`, `export_dxf` | the roof profile as roof.py states it | `elevation.face_profile`, the profile in the face's `u` |
| `scene._face_extrude` | laid `u` along +x or +y on every face | reflects a mirrored face's `u` back about the same drawn width |
| `scene._entrance_agreement` | the first door on the face; `u - t` written by hand | the door the elevation dresses; `elevation.face_along_ft`, the inverse |
| `scene` faces record | no direction | `bays.mirrored` beside the centres |
| `plan_check`'s off-centre door finding | "bay 2 of 7" | "bay 2 of 7 counting from the west end" (or south) |
| the Round (`frame.js`, `RoundPlate.jsx`) | refused N and W: the plate ran against the camera | unchanged code; the record says `mirrored: true` on N and W, so all four plates register |

**New conversions in `build/elevation.py`:**
- `face_along_ft`, the inverse of `face_u_ft`;
- `face_u_outside`, for the roof's and a stack square's outside frame;
- `face_span_outside_ft`, the axis;
- `face_profile`, a roof profile as the face draws it.

**roof.py is deliberately left in the plan frame.** The scene lays its roof planes and gables in
model space from the same profiles, and the roof's pinned digest (`tests/test_threshold_pass.py`)
is a statement about the roof, not about which way a drawing of it reads. The two drawing readers
reflect through `face_profile` instead.

**Two things stay plan-relative, and say so.**
- A placed door's `hinge` names the jamb at the plan's low or high coordinate
  (`openings.place`). On N and W the low jamb is on the right. No elevation surface draws a leaf's
  swing; the comment in `placed_openings` says a reader that ever does must convert.
- `axis.bay_of` counts bays from the plan's low end, and the finding now names that end.

**Opening ids renumber on N and W.** An opening's id carries its index along the face as drawn
(`{face}-{n}-{storey}-{room}-{kind}`), and on a mirrored face `n` counts from the other end. Over
the sixteen shipped plans, 356 scene solid ids changed their text on six plans (N on five, W on
`good-05-lobby-gallery-mansion`). Where two openings on a face share a room, the same ids now name
the other window: the spec Colonial's two W dining-room windows are the case.

## III. What was found

**The first version mirrored about the exact span, and a reflection about any axis but the drawn
width moves the openings against their own wall.** `face_u_ft` has carried a mirrored branch since
WP-13.3, never live, written as `clear span + t - along`: the exact outside span, clear plus two
walls. The wall band, the roof silhouette and the scene are drawn to the footprint's stated outside
width, which structure.py rounds to two places: 47.58 ft against 47.5833 on the Tidewater plan.
- **Measured on the first draft:** every mirrored sash stood 0.003 to 0.004 ft off its place in
  model space, on 23 to 214 solids per plan over eleven plans. The scene mirrored about the rounded
  width and the elevation about the exact one.
- **The second draft taught the scene the exact span.** The solids came back, and then the roof
  silhouette of every N and W face moved by the same 0.0033 ft (16 more sheets). The wall beside
  the spec Colonial's doorcase read 435.81 in against 435.86 in the plan's direction: a physical
  length changed by redrawing it from the other side.
- **The third draft is the one kept.** It reflects everything about the drawn width. An N or W
  face is then the plan-direction face reversed end for end and nothing else, which is R2's own
  wording. The footprint's rounding stays at the house's east (and north) end on every face, as it
  always was on S and E. `tests/test_faces_as_seen.py::test_a_mirrored_face_is_the_plan_direction_face_reversed_and_nothing_else`
  builds each house twice, as ruled and with the switch off, and requires every placed opening's
  two `u`s to sum to the drawn width. It also requires every stack mark and the roof profile to be
  each other's reflection, and the wall beside a doorcase to measure the same two walls on the
  other sides.

**The census could not see the flip, and that is structural rather than a gap in it.** Every check
holds a drawing to its record, and the record moved with the drawing.
- 0 verdict moves over 3,411 rows.
- `--diff` reports NEW 0, FIXED 0, MOVED 0.
- One detail changed. V24 on the spec Colonial's N front read "left 435.86 in" and reads
  "right 435.82 in": the same wall, now on the right, measured off the ink. V24 reads the SVG at
  0.1 px, which is 0.05 in at this scale. The record's own figure is unchanged, and the reversal
  test above asserts that.

The guard that can see a flip holds one named opening to one model point on every surface
(§V).

**Two readers the shipped corpus cannot reach, found by mutation.**
- **The stack axes.** Both Tidewater stacks stand at the north end of their gables and no window
  stands near either. A stack axis read in the plan's direction refused nothing and said nothing,
  so the first mutation sweep reverting it stayed green across four test files.
- **The far gable's stack outline.** The `stack_outline` gable branch (a stack the house hides)
  was likewise unreached by any assertion that could tell a reflected outline from an unreflected
  one.

Both are driven now: a stack placed on the W face's own window, which must be refused, and the E
gable's stack on the W face, whose span and whose rake-following foot must both reverse.

**A third was unreachable by the corpus's own door layout.** `scene._entrance_agreement` took the
first door on the face. On every shipped plan the entrance is the first door in the face's own
order, so reverting to the first door was green too. It is driven with a second door at the
face's left end.

**Nothing moved in model space.** The scene with ids and source strings stripped, coordinates to
the thousandth, is identical to the parent's on 9 of 16 plans. On the other 7 it differs only in 26
solids, each by 0.001 ft: a value on a half-thousandth, resolved by which side of it the double
lands on (WP-12.2's decimal tie).

## IV. What was deliberately not done

- **roof.py's profiles, a door's `hinge`, `axis.bay_of`** stay in the plan's frame (§II).
- **The N and E faces' extrusion planes.** `scene._face_extrude` stands an N or E face's `at` on
  the section's ROUNDED outside figure, so those openings sit up to 0.005 ft off the wall's own
  line. This is older than this package (it was so before the flip, on E as on N) and not R2's
  subject. Moving it would move every E-face solid on every plan.

## V. Verification

- **The guard.** `tests/test_faces_as_seen.py`, 34 tests:
  - One named, asymmetric opening on each of six (plan, face) pairs is held to one model point on
    the record, the sheet (read back through its own `data-frame`), the DXF, the scene and the
    Round (`frame.js::modelAt` under node, fed the direction the server serves).
  - A lopsided roof profile is drawn from each face's own left on the sheet and in the DXF.
  - Two equally wide front doors on N tie to the door the placer would choose.
  - A dormer between two equally central windows takes the one the plan would.
  - The two stack drives.
  - The first-door drive.
  - The mirror axis and its premise.
  - The reversal property.
- **Mutations**, each asserted to land and each file restored by sha256. The first sweep ran 18:
  - 15 were red;
  - 2 were blind (the stack axes and the far-gable outline) until their drives existed;
  - 1 did not land (a quoting slip in the harness), and was blind when it did (the first door)
    until its drive existed.

  Moving the axis to the drawn width made two of the 18 into the correct behaviour; they were
  re-cut as their opposites (the scene and the axis each taking the exact span). Three more were
  added for the new axis: the openings, the bays and the scene's inverse, each dropping the drawn
  width. All 21 are red on the final code.
- **Tests re-cut.** Each asserted the old convention:
  - `test_one_bay_system` (the switch's ruled value; the N face's bays as the S face's reflected);
  - `test_stack_side` (the rear stack on W; each face's span of the off-centre stack);
  - `test_doorcase` (the reserved run on the spec Colonial's N front);
  - the gate row in `test_sheet_coherence`, which wrote its own mirrored conversion for N and W and
    took the WIDTH on the W face;
  - the words of the datum sentence in `test_elevation` and `test_elevation_small_fixes`;
  - the conversion calls in `test_opening_rects` and `test_scene_entrance`;
  - the walk's N-plate check (`e2e/walk.mjs`), which asserted the plate was refused and now
    asserts it is laid with a frame and a transform.

  Stale comments stating "no face is mirrored" were corrected in:
  - `round.test.mjs`, `frame.js`, `RoundPlate.jsx`;
  - `corpus.py`, `facade.py`;
  - `test_scene_endpoint`, `test_opening_rects`.
- **The drawings, `tests/render_digest.py`.** Extended here to hash each elevation's DXF (by
  entity: type, layer, geometry to the thousandth, text) and each scene. It was run on a worktree
  of the parent `2ff37e0` and on this tree:
  - **39 of 208 sheets moved:** the N and W elevations of every drawn face with something
    asymmetric on it (N 9, W 5), their 14 DXFs, and 11 scenes (ids and `bays.mirrored`).
  - No S or E sheet, plan, section or roof moved.
  - The 18 N and W sheets that did not move are 10 refusal sheets (the five plans whose style the
    elevation refuses) and 8 faces with nothing asymmetric on them.
- **The test count** is 3,463 against the parent's 3,429, reconciled by name: 34 added, all in
  `tests/test_faces_as_seen.py`, and none removed. (The parent's 3,429 is CLAUDE.md's 3,414 plus
  WP-16.2 stage 1's 15, which its own report will account for.) The app suite is 764, unchanged.
- **The targeted suite** covers every test file that reads the elevation, the scene or the
  conversion, with the server's scene, drawing-set and m3 tests. On the final code it ran 22 failed
  / 1,243 passed / 26 skipped, in a worktree of this tree:
  - 21 of the 22 are on WP-16.1's known list, by id.
  - The 22nd is `test_sheet_coherence.py::test_every_upper_bearing_line_stands_on_a_ground_bearing_line[careful-cp]`,
    a CP row. It passes alone twice on the parent `2ff37e0` and twice on this tree (41.9 to 42.2 s
    each), and this package moves no placement: every plan sheet is byte-identical. It went red
    while another agent's two test runs held the machine. CP-SAT under a wall clock on a shared
    CPU is the CP band, not this package.
- **The app suite** is 764 of 764.
- **The browser walk**, against a fresh build: 702 green, 0 failed, and the same six could not
  evaluate as at WP-15.8's close, exit 3. Its N-plate check reads *"the N plate is laid over the
  model, reading as the camera does"*.
