# WP-13.1 — The gate: the sheet read as a fluent reader reads it, written red first

*15 September 2026. Phase 13, "the coherent sheet". Cite this report by filename (OQ 90).*

## What this package is

On 15 September Lucas read the two plates of a Tidewater sheet off the workbench's Drawing Set
and listed nine things wrong with it — the stair halls do not stack, the door swings are
mirrored, the furniture makes no sense and carries no label, the chimneys stand on windows and
bear no relation to a fireplace, the powder room is not enclosed, the bays are not coordinated
between floors, plans or elevations, the scale does not scale — and asked whether the fix was
*"a ridiculous number of additional changes"* or *"reconsider our approach from the ground up"*.
It was his second such list. The first, 4 September
(`docs/reports/tidewater-layout-diagnosis-2026-09-04.md`), produced sixty findings and an order
of work whose structural items were built as score terms on the SEARCH engine, measured inert
there and stood down, while the sheet he reads is drawn by the PROVER.

The answer, argued in the plan of record and ruled on the same day, is neither. What was
missing before anything else was a guard that reads the whole plate — 1,469 tests test parts,
and none of them asked any of the nine questions a reader asks at a glance. **This package is
that guard, `tests/test_sheet_coherence.py`, and it was written RED before any fix**, so that
every fix in the packages that follow is judged by whether a row of it moved.

Lucas's four rulings of 15 September, which every later package is drawn from: the system stays
a constraint system and the type's facts become HARD on the prover, in a stated precedence; a
placement that breaks a hard fact of the type is REFUSED, not drawn; gate first and no new
surface until it is green; and the service program moves into the dependency the plan already
carries.

## What was built

`tests/test_sheet_coherence.py` renders two records on two engines — the shipped reference plan
`plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json` and the composer's own top candidate for
`briefs/family-georgian.json`, each placed by the search and by CP-SAT — reads the presentation
SVG and the placed record back, and asserts as a number what a reader sees. Seventeen rows, each
parametrised over the four sheets:

| row | what it reads | how |
|---|---|---|

The rules the file is held to are in its own docstring: it reads emitted ink; the heuristic
figures are deterministic and may be ratcheted while the CP-SAT figures may not (the `auto` sweep
returned 130/132/133 on one tree); unjudged is not passed — a sheet with nothing to judge skips
with the words COULD NOT EVALUATE and never passes by absence; and **no re-baselining**: a red row
is a defect on the sheet, not a ceiling to raise.

## What the adversarial pass corrected before the gate was committed

The reproduction ran as a workflow — one explorer per complaint, one refuter per reproduction,
and a matrix of what is hard, soft and absent on each engine — and five of its refutations
changed what the gate asserts. Recorded here because each is a figure the plan of record had
already published the other way.

1. **The door arcs are mirrored on HORIZONTAL walls, not vertical ones** — 12 of 28 on cp-sat,
   every one with its centre exactly r√2 from the hinge, and the four-case sign proof of
   `render_plan.py:1142` confirms the `horiz` arm is the inverted one. `Sheet.jsx:59` is correct.
   The plan's first table row had the walls the other way round.
2. **The chimney breast swallows the window, and it is guaranteed to.** The refuter measured the
   breast rather than the 22 in exterior stack square: the breast's centre (`hearths.breast`), a
   lone window's position (`openings._place_windows`, `(k+1)/(n+1)` for n = 1) and the flue axis
   (`hearths.stack_axes`) all default to the same mid-wall point, so a room with one window and one
   position-less hearth on one wall puts the sash dead centre in the breast. The dining and library
   sashes lie 100% inside their breasts on the search sheet. The gate gained its breast row.
3. **The scale bar, not the figures.** The clear face IS drawn — the poché is a stroked band and
   its inner edge at x = 0 carries 360 px of ink; every printed figure strikes off it correctly.
   What is wrong is the bar: its zero is at `M + BP`, the plate's own origin, model x = −4.42 ft,
   ruled 26 px straight under the plan, so a straightedge from that 0 reads 13.4 ft against "9".
   The gate's datum row was rewritten to read the bar.
4. **The powder room is sealed on the search engine.** It is enclosed there (the search tiles
   exactly) and has no door: 14 of 31 declared doors are undrawable on that placement, the
   backhall–powder door among them. Both engines fail the reader; they fail differently. The gate
   gained its undrawable-door row.
5. **`STACK_HARD` is not inert on the prover and is a trap.** It is read inside `solve_heuristic`,
   which `geometry_cp._hint_heuristic` warm-starts from; at the shipped budget `STACK_HARD=True`
   makes the cp-sat sheet worse (broken 2 → 3, the kept placement falling to objective-free phase
   A). The operative defect on the sheet path is budget ALLOCATION: of 40 s, phase A takes 20 s,
   the wall-reinstatement ladder ~12 s and the compositional polish 3.95 s, after which "best of
   2" is ranked by the heuristic's score against a candidate with no objective. And at the Plan
   Workbench's 25 s budget phase B never runs at all — the bench draws a placement to which not one
   soft term applied.

Two of the plan's own claims were withdrawn on the same pass: the sheet Lucas read is
**not identifiable as the composer's candidate** (the candidate prints `63' x 33'-5" CLEAR · 2105 SF`
and `… FOR FAMILY HOUSE, TIDEWATER GEORGIAN`, against the screenshot's `63' x 34' · 2142 SF` and
`… FOR TIDEWATER`; a brief typed into the bench, on a tree that may not be main), and **main's CI
is red on three jobs, not one** — the walk's caption regex, shard 5's
`test_shape_pins.py::test_no_room_is_drawn_outside_its_own_band_when_the_pins_hold` (chamber3 at
1.4 against a 1.35 ceiling with its pin held), and the gate job that reads them.

## What was deliberately not done

- **No fix.** Not one line outside `tests/` moved in this package; the rows are red on purpose
  and stay red until the package that owns each one lands. `python3 build/check_all.py` is red at
  this commit, by design, and the commit says so.
- **No `xfail`.** A strict expected-failure would keep the suite green and record the defect, and
  it is the shape "until X lands" takes in a test file. The rows are plain failures with the
  measured figure in their message, so the tree cannot be read as clean while the sheet is not.
- **No ratchet on the prover's figures.** They are pinned only once step 3 makes the fact hard,
  because a proved fact is deterministic by construction and a soft one is not.
- **No new open question.** Every red row is a package in Phase 13, not a question; the two
  things the pass could not settle — which record and which build drew Lucas's screenshot — are
  recorded above as COULD NOT EVALUATE and answered by the `/api/health` sha in the next package.
