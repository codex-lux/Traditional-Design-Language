**The two provenance censuses differ for a reason worth reading rather than a rounding.**
`structure.wall_thickness` writes a `note` on exactly one occasion — when the plan declared no
`construction_type` and it assumed one. `tidewater-georgian-careful` declares solid masonry, so
its walls are `derived`; `spec-builder-colonial` declares nothing and gets *"assumed
platform-frame"*, so all 41 of its walls are honestly `editorial`. The weakest number in a wall
box is its thickness, and the census says so.

**The spec Colonial also draws no roof**, and that is the layer working: `colonial-revival` has
no migrated roof-pitch constraint, so `roof.py` leaves the ridge height unjudged and the scene
records two refusals — the planes and the ridge datum — rather than picking a pitch. That missing
ridge datum is why it has six datums against the Tidewater's seven, and the two numbers are the
same fact told twice.

*(Both of those figures were wrong in the first draft of this table — the provenance census and
the datum count — and both were caught by re-deriving them rather than re-reading the sentence.
That is WP-9.5's technique, and it is the third time in this session it has caught a number: the
other two were WP-12.0's porch figure after the merge, and its "eleven weeks" for what was twelve
days.)*

**The figures are on the heuristic deliberately.** `auto` reaches a CP proof on one machine and
spends its budget on another, so a suite built on it would assert different geometry on
different runners — which is precisely what WP-12.0 measured happening to the elevation between
two trees, hours earlier. The engine-dependent numbers belong in a report, not a pin.

## What was found

### 1. The roof record and the plan record do not share an origin

`roof_outline` and `elevation_profile` lay the roof out from (0, 0) over the **outside**
footprint; `wall_lines` lays the walls out from (0, 0) over the **clear** one. Both call their
corner the origin and the two are half an exterior wall apart — 1.29 ft on the Tidewater plan —
so read literally **the roof sits east and north of the house it covers**.

It has never mattered, and the reason is worth keeping: no surface has ever drawn a roof and a
room in one picture. `render_plan.py` draws rooms, `render_roof.py` draws a roof plan, and each
is a separate plate at a separate origin. The two records were only ever read one at a time.
The scene layer is the first thing that has to put them in one coordinate system.

It is resolved here by FOLLOWING `export_ifc.slab_boxes`, whose slabs are already centred on the
clear rectangle, rather than inventing a third convention — and the choice is recorded as a
choice: `oq/the-roof-record-and-the-plan-record-do-not-share-an-origin` asks which record should
move. **Underneath it is a smaller disagreement that is not the same question**:
`section.footprint` and `slab_boxes` differ by 0.17 ft about the outside depth of one house,
because one grows a rounded `clear_depth_ft` and the other grows the placed
`geometry.footprint.depth_ft`. Both are called the outside footprint. That residue is the
0.001–0.004 ft the agreement figure reports, and rounding one to the other would be OQ 48's
error in a new place.

### 2. The roof was a box with a lid, and only the picture said so

Each roof plane was written as a **prism** — one polygon extruded vertically between the eave
height and the ridge height. That is a box that spans the roof's rise: the right footprint, the
right two heights, and **no slope anywhere in it**.

**All three agreement figures accepted it and all fifteen tests passed**, because every one of
them measures the PLAN extent, and in plan a box and a slope are the same rectangle. The house
rendered as a two-storey block with a lid.

It was found by **drawing the scene with three.js in a headless browser and looking at it** —
WP-9.6's lesson arriving on its own terms (*"Lucas found it by looking at the sheet; nothing in
the suite could"*). The fix is a primitive rather than a correction: a sloping plane needs a
per-vertex height, so the schema gained **`plane`** — four vertices in 3D — instead of coercing
a slope into a `prism` that cannot hold one. `test_a_roof_plane_slopes_and_is_not_a_box` asserts
the shape rather than the extent: every plane must carry at least two distinct vertex heights,
and they must be **the eave and ridge the record states**, not any two. Mutation-checked by
flattening every vertex to the eave: red.

**The durable form of it**: three agreement figures, sixteen tests and a green build said this
scene was the same building as the record, and they were all measuring the plan. A layer that
adds a dimension needs at least one assertion in that dimension, and the cheapest one is a
picture.

### 3. Every exterior wall grew INWARD, and no per-wall assertion could have seen it

The first version of `_walls` had the growth direction exactly backwards: a wall on the low side
(S at y = 0, W at x = 0) grew into the house instead of out of it, and so did the high sides.
Every wall was the right thickness in the right place along its own axis, and the envelope came
out **63.0 × 38.17** — the CLEAR footprint — where an outside-to-outside envelope must be larger
than the rooms it wraps.

**It was caught by summing the extent, not by reading the expression**, and that is the durable
part: the defect is invisible in any single wall and obvious in the whole. The guard is the
agreement between two independent derivations of one rule — the wall boxes and `slab_boxes` —
rather than a pinned number, so it stays true as the corpus moves.

### 4. The east gable stood at a y coordinate

`_roof` took the DEPTH as the east gable's x position, which put the gable inside the house. A
gable on a constant-x face sits at an x; the code read `W if axis != "x" else D`, which is a
sentence that parses and is wrong. Also caught by measuring against the wall extent rather than
by re-reading, and now guarded by a test that asserts each gable stands on the face it names.

### 5. Two of this package's own guards could not fail, and its own harness said so

The literal detector — the test that refuses a dimension written into `build/scene.py` — flagged
**its own allowed constant** on the first run, because `ast.walk` does not prune: `continue` on
the assignment still yields its child `Constant`. It also flagged `round(x, 4)` and a
`candidates=250` default, neither of which is a dimension. A guard that fires on the thing it
exempts is a guard somebody widens until it fires on nothing, so the exemptions are now by node
**identity** and each is named rather than a blanket numeric allowance.

The second was better fixed in the code than in the test: rather than exempt an argparse
default, the pool size became `DEFAULT_CANDIDATES`, named because
`oq/a-placement-rule-is-free-at-a-pool-the-server-cannot-afford` records that four packages have
now measured a placement rule whose verdict is a property of that number. It was the last figure
in the file sitting unnamed.

### 6. The mutations

Seven, from a green baseline of 16, each verified to have LANDED by reading the file back
before the suite ran:

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| walls grow inward again | **4 failed** |
| the roof returns to its own frame | **2 failed** |
| the gable takes the depth as an x | **1 failed** |
| a hip is drawn as a gable | **1 failed** |
| a solid loses its source | **2 failed** |
| the note stops counting what is not modelled | **1 failed** |
| every roof vertex flattened to the eave height | **1 failed** |

A seventh attempt reported "15 passed" and meant nothing: its anchor did not match, the file was
never mutated, and only the landed-assertion made that visible. That is the third time in two
days this session has met *a mutation that does not land reads exactly like a guard that works*
— once in Python, once in a browser bundle, once here.

## What was deliberately not done

- **Openings.** WP-12.2's, and until it lands an exterior wall is a plain box **with a
  `not_modelled` entry saying so**, because a blank wall must not read as a wall with no
  windows. `test_the_walls_say_they_have_no_openings_yet` is written to FAIL when 12.2 arrives,
  and its docstring says the entry should be replaced by holes rather than deleted.
- **Hip, gambrel and cross-gable planes.** `roof.py` gives a hip its lines in plan only, with
  height "implied", and says in its own note that a cross-gable has no valley geometry because
  the plan layer never placed a second volume to cut one against. Each is named with its form
  rather than approximated by the gable this layer can build.
- **Chimney solids.** The stack's plan size is `judgment: true` in `brick-course` — *"the mason
  will build 18 or 27"* — so a solid here would be an invented dimension. WP-12.6 draws an axis
  line and names the judgment.
- **Stairs.** Named in `not_modelled`. **The PRD asks for an open question here and it is not
  raised, because the record turns out to determine the thing the question would ask about.**
  Measured on the Tidewater plan: `stair.riser_in` is 7.367 and each flight carries its own tread
  count — `{"treads": 9, "direction": "E"}` and `{"treads": 10, "direction": "W"}` against 20
  risers — so a flight's rise is its treads times the riser, and what is missing is the
  arithmetic rather than the data. That is WP-12.6's work, not a judgment for Lucas, and raising
  a question whose answer is already in the record would put a decision in the register that
  nobody has to make.
- **The route and the viewer.** WP-12.3 and WP-12.4.
