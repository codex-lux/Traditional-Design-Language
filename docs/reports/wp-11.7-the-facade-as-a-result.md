# WP-11.7 — The facade as a result

*5 September 2026. Executes `oq/the-facade-is-a-result-not-an-input`, ruled by Lucas 4 September.*

**The ruling in one line: the bay rhythm follows from the plan's organising move — for a
centre-door diagram, the centred passage — and not the other way round.** Wenger's chronology is
the historical form of the same dependency: Virginia houses with advanced or pedimented central
bays gained popularity *"only after the central passage had achieved status as an important social
space"*. The generator ran it backwards.

**What this package does NOT do is compose a facade, and that is the ruling's second half rather
than a shortfall.** *"The ordering of commitments is how findings are EXPLAINED, not how they are
resolved."* The system stays a constraint system; Glassie is explicit that his own rule sets are
order-independent (*"It may start at any point, take any route, and yet come to the same end"*),
and a pipeline cannot backtrack when a dependency will not fit the lot. So `build/facade.py`
derives the rhythm the plan's own bays imply, compares the drawn front against it, and **reports**.

## What shipped

**`build/facade.py`** — `rhythm()`, `blind_bays()`, `compare()`, `room_front_bays()`,
`facade_share()`, and a CLI. Wired into `plan_check`'s **drawn** layer, which is where it belongs
by WP-11.4's rule — *ask what a check READS, not what it is about*: every one of these walks the
placement, and `drawn` is the only layer permitted to (OQ 54) and is already mapped in
`compose.SCORE_LAYERS`, which is what WP-11.4 pushed without doing.

**The elevation reads the plan's bay count now.** `_face_bays` took its count from
`facade-classical.json`'s `window_grouping_rule` against the face's outside width and had never
read `footprint.bays`. See §"the coincidence" below.

**Two data records the ruling committed to**, both changed:

- `groupings/centre-passage-core.json`'s facade-share rule loses its `test` and drops from `hard`
  to `preferred`. Its band survives as `measures.advisory_band` and its prose says the share is a
  CONSEQUENCE. This is *"the one place the ruling makes a currently-executable rule less
  executable, and it is deliberate"* — a test that measures a consequence as though it were an
  input is the error the ruling names. The **8 ft floor is a different rule and stays hard.**
- `faults/passage-that-is-a-corridor.json`'s `correct_practice` opened *"Size the passage from the
  facade rather than from the code"*. It now says to take the width from the BAY, and says why the
  old instruction runs the dependency backwards. The observed one-third/one-fifth figures are kept
  — the ruling calls the observation real.

## The coincidence, which is the finding of this package

`elevation._face_bays` composed the front from a pack formula; `derive_footprint` computed
`footprint.bays`. **Two records built from different rules, and nothing compared them.** That is
OQ 85's shape exactly — a window drawn on a chimney axis — and the fix for OQ 85 sits four hundred
lines below the code carrying this one.

**They agree on both shipped plans: 7 against 7 on the Tidewater, 5 against 5 on the spec
Colonial.** So the elevation now takes the plan's count and **the corpus output is
byte-identical** — this is the removal of a second rule, not a new answer. Only the faces spanning
the block's WIDTH take it; a gable end keeps the formula, because `footprint.bays` counts bays
across the width and handing it to a face spanning the depth would be the OQ 48 error in a new
place.

**Because they agree, the corpus cannot test the join.** A mutation deleting it leaves every
natural assertion green — measured. `test_DRIVEN_the_elevation_FOLLOWS_the_plan_when_the_two_would
_disagree` drives the plan to nine bays, which the formula would never pick, and that is the only
assertion that sees it. WP-8.11's rule, met again.

## Two errors that cancelled, and how they were found

The first draft of `facade.compare()` emitted `front-door-off-the-centre-bay`. **`plan_check`
already emits `drawn-door-off-the-centre-bay` from WP-11.3's axis reading** — the same rule spelled
twice, in the package whose own ruling is about not saying one thing in two vocabularies. **And the
two disagreed about the number**: the existing finding reads *"bay 5 of 7, not the middle bay (4)"*
and the new one read *"bay 4 of 7 ... puts it in bay 3"*, because `axis.door_bay` returns a
ZERO-based index and the older finding adds one for the reader. A sheet carrying both would have
given one door two bay numbers.

**It was invisible in the totals.** Removing the facade-share hard test took one `serious` away and
the duplicate put one back, so 63 stayed 63 and both changes cancelled — the shape CLAUDE.md
records at corpus scale for the arc sweep flag. It was found by asking why a `serious` finding had
been added and the serious count had not moved, then reading the findings themselves rather than
the totals.

## Fifteen of sixteen plan records name no parti

`facade.rhythm` derives for **one** record in this tree. The other fifteen — every reference plan,
`good-` and `bad-`, **and `spec-builder-colonial`** — name no `parti`, so their circulation diagram
is unknown and the whole block is one `info` naming the reason.

**The refusal is correct and the count is the deliverable.** `oq/fifteen-of-sixteen-plans-name-no-parti`
is the sibling of WP-11.4's `oq/fourteen-of-sixteen-plans-name-no-massing`, one field over, and the
two together say the same thing about this corpus: the plan records were authored before the layers
that read these fields existed. The entry forbids the obvious fix in the same words the massing
entry uses — do not derive the parti from the massing, the style or the room list.

## What was deliberately not done

- **No facade is composed and no window is invented.** The ruling's trap: *"the window that gets
  invented to complete a rhythm is this ruling's version of the invented measurement OQ 52 swept
  out of the elevation."* An empty bay is stated empty; a declared window count is never
  overwritten (WP-6.2's rule stands, and a test holds every declared count across a `check()`).
- **The generalisation beyond `center-hall` is not made**, by name, in the refusal's own text —
  the ruling says each parti's facade rule is that parti's to state.
- **The sheet does not yet say the rhythm is underived.** On the fifteen it draws a front composed
  from the pack formula and the finding is `info`; a reader looking at the plate cannot tell a
  facade that follows the plan from one that does not. That is item 3 of the new question.

## The band was spelled twice, and `check_rooms.py` found the half I did not

The schema's `measures` object is `additionalProperties: false`, so adding `advisory_band` and
`reported_by` to the rule made `check_rooms.py` fail — **while `validate.py` passed**, which is
worth knowing: the two do not check the same things about a grouping.

**Chasing it found the code half.** `facade_share` had the pair written into it as a literal,
beside the grouping rule that states the same two numbers — one rule in two places, committed
inside the function whose whole subject is one rule in two places. The schema is widened (with the
field's own description saying a rule carrying `advisory_band` must not also carry a `test` on the
same quantity, because that would be requiring and reporting one number at once), and the reader
takes the band from the record.

**And the guard against it flagged this file's own comment**, which quoted the figures to explain
the defect — the note demonstrating the finding by committing it. The comment is reworded, which is
the move CLAUDE.md records for the citation guard: *"this entry deliberately does not repeat the
offending string, because writing it here would raise the unchecked count by one in the paragraph
reporting the unchecked count."* The first version of that guard was also a bad instrument — it
flagged any file containing both numbers anywhere and matched three unrelated files' constants —
and it searches for the literal pair's SHAPE now.
