# WP-18.0: two rooms, and the reader first

**Phase 18, 26 Nov 2026.** The first tranche of the Workbench's second redrawing: the shell, the
front door, the styles index, a style's dossier and the fault corpus. The other surfaces are on
the new shell and in their room, and are not yet redrawn; §VI lists them.

## I. What was asked, and what was found

Lucas, a month after Phase 17 shipped: *"Right now it feels stale and there's a lot of information
thrown at you all at once and there's very little visual journey throughout."* He asked for the UI
to be reviewed and overhauled until it is intuitive, stunning and a joy to use.

**All eighteen surfaces were built, served and screenshotted before anything was drawn**, at
1440 × 900 with the two webfaces loaded. Read off those pictures:

1. **A page's own content began about 400 px down a 900 px window.** On a link opened cold, a page
   stacked the top bar, the banner saying what the workbench is, and the page head with its lede
   and its list of how to read the page, open. §V gives the measured figures.
2. **Where the reader is was said three times**: the crumbs in the top bar, a second trail in the
   banner, and the sidebar's own highlight.
3. **Every name was followed by its machine id in mono**, and every defined word carried a dotted
   rule, so a page of labels was a page of underlines.
4. **Every page was the same page.** One dark ground, one size of type, hairlines, and a list.
   Nothing on a page was its head, and nothing separated reading a style from working on a house.
5. **Nothing answered the hand.** The interface is set in inline styles, which cannot carry a
   hover state, so a row, a card and a button looked the same under the pointer as away from it.
6. **The drawings were absent from the library.** The one plate on the front door arrives after a
   sweep, so a first visit met an empty frame.

## II. The three rulings

Asked as three direct questions on 26 Nov 2026, each with its alternatives, and answered the same
day. Each took the recommended answer.

| | Question | Ruled |
|---|---|---|

| R2 | How far may the head, the banner and the ids be folded away? | **Reader first.** Content leads. *How to read this page* opens on demand, the cold-link banner is one dismissable line, there is one trail of crumbs, and a machine id shows once in a record's head and on demand elsewhere. R1 was put with evidence: three pages were rendered on vellum by setting the ground attribute in
the running page, and the duties rebound cleanly, because Phase 17 bound every duty on both
grounds.

## III. What was built

**The two rooms** (`app/src/grounds.js`). `ROOM_OF` says which room a surface is in and
`SURFACE_GROUND` is derived from it, so the room and the ground cannot disagree. Twelve surfaces
are the library and six are the house. No token changed value.

**A second stylesheet** (`app/src/theme/rooms.css`): the layout of the two rooms and how a page
behaves under a hand. It declares no colour token and names no pigment, so one rule draws in
either room. It is where a hover state can live, which an inline style cannot give.

**The shell** (`App.jsx`, `Chrome.jsx`):
- the page head is one control in the top bar (`components/PageHead.jsx`), and its panel holds
  the record's term, its lede, its list and its try link, as before;
- the mono address is gone from the top bar;
- the cold-link banner is one line (`components/ColdLinkBanner.jsx`);
- the wordmark carries a drawn mark, a doorcase in elevation, in the gilt line;
- a sidebar row is taller, set a size larger, and answers the pointer;
- the `ids` control at the sidebar's foot shows every machine id, kept per browser;
- opening a surface fades it up, and the `<main>` is keyed by the surface so that plays once.

**The front door** (`surfaces/Overview.jsx`) is the threshold between the rooms: a dark band with
the thesis, the two acts, the lit plate and the five stations on one line, and below it the
library on paper. The thesis is `about-tdl`'s one sentence in two sizes (`thesisParts`).

**The styles index** (`surfaces/StylesIndex.jsx`, `styles/chapters.js`) is five chapters, each
tradition's families set in columns.

**A style's dossier** (`surfaces/StyleDossier.jsx`, `dossier/Identify.jsx`) is a page: a head at
display size that scrolls away, a section strip that stays in reach, the section beside the
record's relations. The kit keeps the window.

**The fault corpus** (`surfaces/FaultCorpus.jsx`, `components/FaultCard.jsx`) opens on its own
line and three severity figures, and a fault is set as an article.

**The drafting room is loaded when entered.** Nine surfaces are chunks of their own: the house's
six, the family tree, the proportion packs and a comparison.

**The dossier route serves the record's period** (`workbench/server/corpus.py`), which §IV.1 found
no route did.

## IV. What was found on the way

1. **The dossier's period bar has been drawn for no style since it was written.** WP-17.9 drew a
   period bar from `rec.period`. `GET /api/styles/{id}` gives the years as one string in its
   summary and serves no `period`, and the dossier route served none either, so the bar was handed
   `undefined` on all 164 styles and drew nothing, under a board row reading COMPLETE.
   - Nothing read the rendered page for it. Its arithmetic is tested (`periodBar.test.mjs`); its
     input was not.
   - It was found by looking: the redrawn page left a gap where the bar should stand.
   - The dossier route serves the node's own block now. The server suite holds the route to the
     record over every style, `rooms.test.mjs` holds the join, and the walk counts the bar on the
     page, reads its years against the record, and checks its ink against the paint behind it.
2. **And once it drew, its one heavy segment was in no ink at all.** The floruit was filled with
   `--paper`, which is full ink on the instrument and invisible on a page. `PeriodBar.jsx` is a
   drawing file, exempt from the duty rule, and the exemption let a ground-specific pigment
   through. It takes the body-text duty now. The decline's first year was also lettered at the
   same x as the floruit's last, so the bar read "17801780"; it is lettered once.
3. **A new attribute took the name of an existing hook.** The shell marked `#root` and `<main>`
   with which room the page is in, as `data-room`. That name is the plan sheet's hook for a placed
   room, so every reader of `[data-room]` met two page-sized "rooms" called house.
   - The walk's relaxation-mark check measures each mark against every `[data-room]`, and reported
     six marks adrift in a room.
   - It was attributed by reading the same bench on the base UI and the new UI against ONE server:
     the base read 10 rooms and no mark adrift, the new read 12 rooms and six.
   - The attribute is `data-in-room`, and `grounds.test.mjs` refuses the old name.
4. **A guard that excuses a token excuses every site the token is set at.** Mutating the new
   stylesheet to set a live row's name in `--text-disabled` left the suite green, because that
   token is excused by name as "an inactive control". In a stylesheet the selector says whether
   the control is inactive, so the excuse is held to it there (`disabledInkOnLiveRules`). The same
   hole in the `.jsx` reader is older than this package and is not closed.
5. **The section strip scrolled sideways at 1280 px.** The redrawn tabs are a size larger, and the
   walk's width block measured nine sections 28 px wider than their 890 px column on eleven
   dossier addresses. The strip wraps to a second line now and never scrolls.
6. **The entry chunk stood at 690 KB of a 700 KB ceiling**, so the shell could not have been
   redrawn inside it.
7. **Moving the library to paper makes eight sub-AA inks much more visible.** They are older than
   Phase 17 and are held by identity in `grounds.test.mjs`. On the dark ground they were rebound
   to lit pigments; on paper a severity word is still its pigment. §VII puts the question.

## VII. Questions for Lucas

Stated here and not acted on.

**Q1. Should a word on paper take a deeper ink, as a word on the dark ground took a lit one?**
Eight duties read below 4.5 : 1 as words on vellum. They are older than Phase 17 and held by
identity in `grounds.test.mjs` under `oq/two-inks-set-as-small-text-read-below-aa`. Phase 17's
audit met the same problem on the dark ground and answered it with three lit pigments, each its
pigment mixed toward paper by the least step that reads. On paper nothing was changed, because
the instrument was then the page's ground and paper only a card's.

With the library on vellum, a whole page is now set in these inks. The mirror of the lit pigments
is a paper ink for each: the pigment mixed toward `--coal` by the least step that reads 4.5 : 1 on
every paper surface (`--paper`, `--paper-deep`, `--paper-mat`, `--paper-lit`). Computed, not chosen:

| Duty on vellum | Pigment | Reads now, on its worst paper | Paper ink | Step toward coal | Reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--text-accent`, `--sev-minor` | gilt-deep `#8A6D33` | 3.64 | `#775F2F` | 0.18 | 4.54 |
| `--sev-fatal`, `--refusal-text` | brick `#AF6B50` | 3.11 | `#895641` | 0.27 | 4.52 |
| `--sev-serious` | salmon-deep `#C08066` | 2.41 | `#815948` | 0.40 | 4.54 |
| `--sev-cleared` | green-deep `#6B7C5C` | 3.37 | `#5A674D` | 0.23 | 4.51 |
| `--text-tertiary`, `--sev-info` | ink-3 `#8C8371` | 2.80 | `#696254` | 0.33 | 4.52 |
| `--text-secondary` | ink-2 `#6C6557` | 4.32 | `#696255` | 0.04 | 4.51 |

The pigments themselves would not move: a fill, a rule and a mark keep their colour, and only the
duty a WORD is set in is rebound on vellum. The cost is that fatal and serious, as words on paper,
come within a shade of each other (`#895641` and `#815948`), so the two would need to stay told
apart by their mark and their word, as the standard already requires. That is why it is a ruling.

**Q2. Is the direction right, and may the rest follow it?** WP-18.1 to 18.4 in
`PLAN-OF-ACTION.md`: the rest of the library, the drafting room's six pages, the filter strips and
pickers, and an audit on the rendered page.
