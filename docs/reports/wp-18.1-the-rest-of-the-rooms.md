# WP-18.1 to 18.4: the rest of the two rooms

*Status: BUILT (27 Nov 2026) · Phase 18 · follows `docs/reports/wp-18.0-two-rooms.md`*

The first tranche redrew the shell and five pages and stopped, as ruled, to be shown. This report
covers the other twelve surfaces, the small controls, and the paper inks.

## I. What was asked

Lucas saw the first tranche and its two open questions on 27 Nov 2026. The two questions, as put
to him on 26 Nov:

1. Is the direction right, and may the remaining twelve surfaces follow it?
2. Should words on paper take the deeper inks computed in `wp-18.0-two-rooms.md` §VII?
   Recommended: yes.

His reply, in full: *"Looks good! Keep going"*.

**How the reply is read, stated as a reading.** It answers the first question directly. It was sent
in answer to a message carrying both questions, the second with a recommendation, so it is read as
yes to the second as well. That is this report's reading and not a separate ruling: the paper inks
are one block in `tokens.css` and are reversed by rebinding nine duties to their pigments.

## II. What was built

**The rest of the library (WP-18.1), on vellum.**
- **Elements.** The index leads with its record's own line and a large filter. The four plan-type
  kinds are four cards, each with its count and its own surface record's line. Each slot group is a
  block set in columns: a slot's name and the count of styles that specify it.
- **A slot, a room, a massing, a grouping, a plan type.** One page shape for all five: the kind in
  the letter, the record's name at display size with its id printed once beside it, a double rule,
  the record's prose as a lede, then its sections. A list of records is set in columns. A record
  named with a reason is a two-column row, the name and then the reason. Figures are in a ruled
  table.
- **The glossary.** A dictionary. The index leads with its line and a filter; the twenty-four
  families are a contents line of words where they were twenty-four ruled boxes; each family is a
  chapter of entries. The key to the marks stands at the head of the Marks family, where it stood
  over every word before. A word's own page sets the word at display size and its definition as a
  lede.
- **Two styles compared.** A spread: both names at display size, the sections as tabs that stay in
  view, the tells as two numbered lists side by side, and the kit as a three-column table whose
  rows answer the pointer.
- **A style's kit.** The page this phase was most asked about. The table was five columns of
  Courier: a group id, a slot id, a binding, `↑1 georgian-colonial-ameri…` cut off, and a fault
  count. It leads with the slot's name now, then what the style answers (its canonical variants in
  the reading face), then where the answer comes from as a style's name. The slots stand under
  their groups, in the ontology's order, each group a heading. The cascade stands to the right of
  the table where it stood to its left, each level a style's name with its bar and count, and the
  bans each level writes fold under a count. An opened slot is brought into view.
- **Proportions.** The index is a book of plates: each kind a chapter, each pack a card that leads
  with its own drawing where the list route serves one. A pack's page sets its name at display
  size; the list beside it is in the reading face.
- **The family tree.** The name column is 260 px where it was 210 and its names are 14 px where
  they were 12, so a tradition's name no longer runs into its own lane rule. The taxon's record
  beside the chart is set at reading size and names its descendants by name.

**The drafting room (WP-18.2), on the dark ground.**
- **The plan.** The sheet leads. What the placement gave up, the critique, the revision and the
  solver's refinements stood above the drawing; they stand under it now, in the order they had and
  as open as they were. Nothing was removed and nothing was folded.
- **The brief.** The page leads with its record's line and its one act, struck in gilt. The act
  stood in a 34 px strip in eleven-pixel Courier. The brief, the site and the feasibility notes are
  three panels, and a field is set in the reading face at sixteen pixels.
- **The candidates, the plan, the drawings and the tracing, when empty.** Each says what it waits
  for at display size and offers the way to it, centred in the room, where each was three lines in
  the top-left corner of a dark page.
- **Type.** Fifty-four declarations of 11 to 13.5 px body type across the house's pages and their
  components were raised by two pixels.

**The small controls (WP-18.3).** A filter chip, an act chip, a fold, a filter field and the style
picker were Courier at eleven pixels in a ruled box, which is how a record's id is set. They are in
the reading face, and each answers the pointer through a class. The filter strip is 42 px where it
was 34. The binding, variant and source chips are a designed ladder from Phase 17 and are not
changed.

**The paper inks (WP-18.4).** Six derived inks in `tokens.css`, each its pigment mixed toward
`--coal` by the least step that reads 4.5 : 1 on all four paper surfaces. On paper the word duties
are bound to them: the accent, the working grey, the tertiary grey, the refusal's word, the six
severities and the fault card's three fix inks. The pigments are unmoved.

## III. What was found on the way

1. **The plan page's drawing began 880 px down the pane.** On a first visit at 1440 x 900 a reader
   met four panels of disclosure before the house. This is the page a person spends longest on and
   it was not in the first tranche. Measured figures are in §IV.
2. **The kit could not say a slot's name.** `GET /api/kit/<style>` serves `slot`, `group` and
   `source` as ids and no names. The names index the palette already holds has all three, so the
   table reads them from it and the route is unchanged. A slot the index does not name shows its id.
3. **A level of the cascade was its id, its three counts in words, and every ban it writes, run on.**
   Georgian Colonial American writes 108 bans, so one level took a screen of machine text.
4. **A link naming a slot opened a kit with the slot out of sight.** Under the old alphabetical
   table `cornice` happened to be the seventeenth row. Grouped, it stands in the second group, so
   the open slot is scrolled to on arrival.
5. **Rebinding a duty moved every guard that knew the pigment by its hex.** Seven guards went red
   on the paper inks and each was a reader holding a duty to a pigment's value:
   - the paper contrast table's thirty-four known rows, now none;
   - the clickable-ink remainder's eleven rows, now none;
   - the eyebrow's default ink, pinned to `--ink-2`;
   - `--sev-info`, listed as a faint ink, which it no longer is on paper;
   - the refusal-ink reader, which allowed the brick and the working grey by hex.

   None was loosened. Each was re-cut to the new truth, and the two lists that are empty are held
   empty.
6. **Three ink-ratchet rows were re-keyed and none was added.** A row is known by the first sixty
   characters of its tag. A class for a hover state moves those characters, so the Chip, the
   ActionChip and the slot row's *trace to origin* were the same three controls under new keys.
   That was before the paper inks emptied the list.
7. **The brief's one baseline sentence was re-keyed.** The copy ratchet knew it as the strip's text,
   which began with the strip's eyebrow. It is a paragraph of its own now, with the same words.

How to read these:
- **Courier characters** counts the text a page sets in the data face inside `<main>`, in view,
  outside any drawing, and not inside a closed disclosure. It is a measure of how much of a page
  is machine text; it is not a measure of quality.
- **The meter was wrong once.** Its first run read 4,998 for the new kit, because a closed
  `<details>` reports a box for its hidden content. `checkVisibility()` is the reader now, and
  both columns above are from that meter.
- **The elements index barely moved, and that is right:** its Courier is the count beside each
  slot, which is a figure.
- **The sheet's position depends on the placement.** The panels that stood above it are as tall as
  what one solve gave up, so 624 is one solve's figure. A screenshot of another solve showed the
  sheet beginning near 880. After the change the panels are under the sheet, so 377 does not move
  with the solve.
