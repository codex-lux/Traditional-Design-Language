# WP-11.8 — The bench chooses, and says which

*5 September 2026. Executes `oq/a-proof-of-feasibility-is-not-a-proof-of-composition`, ruled by
Lucas 4 September.*

**The ruling: WP-6.3's principle does not transfer.** A CP-SAT placement outranks a hill-climb
placement on FEASIBILITY and on nothing else. Where the compositional objective did not run, the
bench draws both and labels both, and the plate says which it drew and why.

## The measurement, and it moved to the other plan

The ruling was taken on `tidewater-georgian-careful`, where CP-SAT returned `OPTIMAL (hard-only)`
with `objective: null`. **On this machine that plan no longer reaches CP at all** — `auto` spends
its 25 s and falls back to the hill-climb, `fallback: budget`. The case the ruling is about is live
on **`spec-builder-colonial`** instead, which is the other shipped reference plan and was not named
in the diagnosis:

| | `spec-builder-colonial` |
|---|---|
| engine | `cp-sat` |
| status | `OPTIMAL (hard-only) — kept hard-only phase A (best of 1 hard-valid placements)` |
| **objective** | **`null`** |
| declared exterior walls set aside | 5 |

So the bench draws a proof whose compositional objective never ran, with nothing offered beside it —
the ruling's exact case, on a plan nobody had looked at for it. **Which plan demonstrates a
budget-dependent defect is itself budget-dependent**; the entry is corrected to say so.

## Both numbers, or the disclosure lies

The ruling says to offer the search *"with its demerit score"*. Measured, the demerit score alone
would mislead in the search's favour:

| placement | demerit score | declared facts broken |
|---|---|---|
| the proof (drawn) | **789.3** | **0** |
| the search (offered) | **592.3** | **16** |

The search is 197 points better and buys it by breaking sixteen declared facts the proof holds —
judged against the SAME downgrade list, because a heuristic record carries none of its own and
charging it for pins CP-SAT proved impossible would rig the comparison the other way.
`hard_fact_violations`' own docstring says exactly this: *"when the hill-climb 'outscores' the
constrained optimum, this is the number that says what the cheaper score actually bought."*

**So both travel together and no surface may print one without the other.** That is not a
departure from the ruling but its intent — *"a reader choosing the search over the proof is
choosing a better composition over a proved feasibility and should be told that is the choice"* —
and telling them requires the second number.

## What shipped

**`geometry._offer_the_alternative`** runs on the CP branch only where `objective is None`, solves
the hill-climb (~0.3 s measured), and writes `geometry_report.solver.alternative`: both scores,
both violation counts, the reason, and the alternative's own geometry so a surface can draw it. A
proof whose objective ran is offered nothing and no second solve happens. A failed solve or a
failed comparison writes `could-not-evaluate` **with its reason** rather than going quiet — `roof.py`
wrapped its own reconciliation in a bare `except: pass` and then asserted there was nothing to
stand over (WP-11.4).

**`disclosures.alternative_offered`** is the plate's line, emitted immediately after
`objective_not_run` because it is the second half of one disclosure — a reader told the composition
was not evaluated and not told what else is available has been told half of it. It is a separate
function rather than a longer version of the first because the alternative is not always computable
and must be able to say so. `build/disclosures.py` remains the ONE spelling (WP-11.1); no second
was added.

**`corpus._placed`** records `solver.drawn_by`: the surface, the engine asked for, the candidate
count, the parti, and an **`input_digest` taken BEFORE the solve**. That is finding J6: two CP-SAT
runs of one record agree to the foot, so when the bench's sheet and the CLI's disagree the INPUT
differed — a style, a parti, an edit — and the plate carried nothing that would let a reader tell
which. Digested before placement because the point is to identify what was handed in.

**`PlanWorkbench.jsx`** said *"This placement was **proved**, not searched"* over exactly the
placement the ruling says must not be preferred. It says **proved feasible** now, and where the
objective did not run it says so and prints the four numbers off the record. Every figure is read;
none is written into the JSX, which CLAUDE.md records as unpoliced by `check_counts.py` and is how
the Kit's header claimed 95 slots against an ontology holding 97.

## What was deliberately not done

- **The budget is not changed.** The ruling names it as unruled and the entry's own reasoning
  still holds: it is cheaper to fix the container, and the infrastructure audit measured this
  server's whole evaluate capacity as one core.
- **A placement that set aside sixteen declared facts is still drawn**, rather than refused with
  the conflict named. That is the deeper question the ruling records and does not take.
- **The bench does not yet DRAW the alternative**, only offer it in words and numbers. The
  geometry is on the record for a surface that wants it; a second plate is a UI package.

## Guards

`tests/test_proof_and_search.py`, 16 assertions in four classes. **Ten mutations, ten caught** —
the offer made on the wrong engine, made when the objective ran, judged without the proof's
downgrades, stripped of its violation counts, going quiet on either failure path, the banner
printing only the score, the banner line dropped from the assembler, the bench recording nothing,
and the digest taken after the solve instead of before.

**The fixture is DRIVEN and not the corpus**, and the reason is measured: whether a real solve
leaves `objective` null depends on this machine's budget — one shipped plan reaches phase A only
and the other times out into the heuristic entirely, and neither is stable enough to assert on. A
fixture that were the corpus would make these tests statements about a 25-second budget
(WP-8.11's rule).

The JSX is held by a source-reading test, because **vite compiles it in CI and there is no
`node_modules` here**, so nothing local parses that file — the same asymmetry that made WP-5.7's
first audit pass green locally and red in CI.
