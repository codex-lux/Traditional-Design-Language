# WP-16.6 — The wall between two windows, and the upper window on the axis below

*Phase 16, 1 October 2026. R5, R5a and R5b: a floor of 1.0 x the wider window between two windows,
an aim of 1.4 x where the wall allows, the centre holding, and the five licensed styles spared. R6
and R6a: the upper window on the axis of the opening below it, or refused by name, and left where
it is placed where nothing stands below it.*

## I. What was asked

Lucas's rulings of 29 September 2026, each asked as a direct question and recorded in
`oq/the-placer-seats-windows-a-foot-apart-and-the-corpus-states-the-pier-three-ways`:

- **R5, the governing figure.** The placer never seats two windows closer than the pier fault's
  1.0 x the wider window, and refuses by name a window it cannot seat. It aims for sash-light's
  1.4 x where the wall allows. The Georgian kit's unsourced 0.6 to 1.0 band is brought into line.
- **R5a, which window yields.** The centre holds: windows nearer the entrance keep their places,
  and on other faces those nearer the face's centre do. The outer window moves along its own wall,
  or is refused by name.
- **R5b, ganged windows.** The five styles the fault licenses are spared (craftsman,
  prairie-school, tudor-revival, richardsonian-romanesque, shingle-style). The placer reads the
  licence's style list, not its checks.
- **R6, alignment.** The upper window moves onto the axis of the ground opening below it, or is
  refused by name where its own room cannot take it there.
- **R6a, nothing below.** An upper window with no ground opening under its room's stretch of wall
  stays where it is placed, and the alignment fault judges the front.
- **Read together, not ruled separately:** an upper window that, once aligned, would break the
  upper pier floor cannot be taken there, so it is refused by name.

**Taken as recommended under Lucas's standing instruction of 1 Oct 2026, never put.** The
instruction was given at 00:42 UTC: *"Proceed with all recommended answers for all future questions
in this session"*. The code met four choices the ruling's words did not settle, and the
recommended option was taken for each:

- **U1.** An upper unit left over when its room's openings below are all taken goes to R6a.
- **U2.** The pairing takes axes the room can take before one it cannot. A unit left with an axis
  its room cannot take is refused by name.
- **U3.** The aim is taken pier by pier, wherever it costs no later window.
- **U4.** Where the widest door on the entrance front is a garage door, the face's centre holds.

Two more readings are stated rather than chosen:

- R6 applies to every style, because R5b spares the pier floor and nothing else. *(Corrected 2 Oct 2026, WP-16.8, auditor A: false of the code. A licensed style's line keeps the old foot in record order with no aim, so R5b spares R5a's centre-out order and the aim too (U10); and the aim reaches only the 53 nodes sash-light is delivered to, a reading of R5 (U11). Both taken as recommended under Lucas's standing instruction of 1 Oct 2026 and never put.)*
- The composer's window cap reads the floor for every style, because the composer composes no band.

## II. What was built

**1. One reader, `build/window_pier.py`.** It is a leaf beside `doorcase.py`, and reads three
things:

- **the floor:** this fault's primary test, an at-least ratio. Where the fault states none, the
  floor is unjudged and the placer keeps its old foot and says why;
- **the aim:** sash-light's `minimum_solid_between_openings` (`opening_width * 1.4`) at
  `window_grouping_rule`, where `resolve_kit.eval_packs` delivers it to the style. 53 of the
  graph's 164 nodes receive it. A pack the opt-in gate withholds, or a kit refuses, gives no aim
  and says why;
- **the spared styles:** the fault's `exceptions[].style`, by exact id, as every licence in this
  corpus is read.

The placer, the composer's cap and census V26 read it. None of them carries a 1.0 or a 1.4 of its
own, and V26 reads the fault file itself so that it does not agree with what it checks.

**2. The placer, `openings._place_windows`.** Windows are seated per face line, the wall a reader
sees, whichever room is behind it. Before this package the foot was kept inside one room, so two
windows either side of a partition could stand a few inches apart: 10 of the 35 drawn piers
crossed a partition.

- **The centre (R5a, U4).** The entrance door's axis on the entrance front is the centre. It is
  read by `_entrance_door`, the reading `_reserve_doorcase` already used, lifted out with its
  refusals' words unchanged. Elsewhere, the face's centre is.
- **Aligned units first (R6).** Each stands exactly on its axis, or is refused, `rule: alignment`,
  with the axis and the reason. *(Corrected 2 Oct 2026, WP-16.8, auditor A: no ruling put aligned units first, and the order ran R5a and the ruled 'read together' backwards -- an outer aligned window was seated before an inner one, and the inner one was refused or moved. The placer seats ONE queue by distance from the centre now, each aligned unit at its axis, so the window nearer the centre holds and an aligned window that would break the floor is refused by name, `rule: alignment`. Moved: the spec Colonial's upper S face seats `primary` at 9.0 and refuses the aligned unit at 6.745; Tidewater's chamber3 [32.099, 40.5] -> [33.0, 40.5].)*
- **The rest, centre out (R5).** Each takes the floor, and the aim where that costs no later
  window (U3). A unit that cannot keep the floor is refused, `rule: pier`.
- **The licensed styles** keep the pass from before this package, verbatim (`_seat_a_foot_apart`),
  including when each refusal's sentence is composed.
- **The pairing** (`_assign_axes`, `_order_assign`) is order-preserving and least-cost. An axis
  the room cannot take costs more than any wall (U2), and ties go to the lower index.
- **The record:** plan schema 0.13.0 admits `unplaced.rule`, `parts` (one window refused for
  several causes), `axes` and `needs.pier_over_the_wider_window`. `opening_report.window_seating`
  says per line which centre held, the mode, and how many units were seated and refused, by how.

**3. The words.**
- `disclosures._group` groups a ruled refusal by its `rule` and the floor the record states, never
  by its sentence. The plan sheet says *"too near the next window for a wall of 1 × the wider"*.
- `elevation.placed_openings` carries the rule as the refusal's cause. The sheet says *"SEATED
  BESIDE THE NEXT WINDOW, THE WALL BETWEEN THEM WOULD FALL BELOW 1 × THE WIDER WINDOW, THE PIER
  FLOOR"* and *"THEY COULD NOT STAND ON THE AXIS OF THE OPENING BELOW THEM"*.
- A licensed style's wall under the floor is said on its sheet.

**4. The measurement.**
- `elevation.window_piers` measures every wall between two windows side by side on one storey of
  a face. It reads glass edge to glass edge, the fault's own unit, and opening edge to opening
  edge, the placer's. A door or the doorcase between two windows breaks the pair. A window whose
  sash is refused draws no glass, and its piers are could-not-evaluate, never a number.
- The pier fault's primary test read `pier_width_in / adjacent_opening_width_in`, and nothing in
  this tree supplies `adjacent_opening_width_in`, so it never ran. It reads
  `narrowest_pier_over_wider_adjacent_window` now (the narrowest is withheld where a pair draws no
  glass), and it is gated on `count_of_window_piers_on_the_front` >= 1. The threshold is unmoved.

**5. The Georgian band.** `pier_measured_ratio` is [1.0, 2.0], still editorial, with a dated note
saying it was brought into line, and a test holds it to the floor.

**6. The composer's cap.** `compose.derive_openings` bounded a wall's window count by
`opening_width * 1.4`, the placer's aim, as a transcribed literal. It reads the floor now. On both
briefs it moves nothing: shimmed back to 1.4, the returned sets are identical (§III).

**7. Census V26** holds the ink both ways: every wall between two windows side by side is at least
the floor times the wider, unless the style is licensed and the sheet says so. The legend names the
floor where the placed record refused a window for it, and only there. The census is 75 checks.

**8. A committed instrument**, `tests/window_piers.py`, re-derives the pier census on any tree.
WP-15.6's *"24 of 35"* had lived only in a report.

## IV. What was found

**1. The pier floor holds the shutters.** A wall of 1.0 x the wider window holds two half-width
leaves exactly. So R5 removed almost every refusal WP-16.7 was planned to order: 193 refused
windows became 1. WP-16.7 is still R7 and still a package, but its figures (232 and 150, taken on
the old placement) must be re-derived on this one before it starts.

**2. The centre holds, and that costs windows.** R5a keeps the inner window at the placer's
default place, `(k + 1) / (n + 1)` of its room's wall, which nothing ever composed. 7 of the 16
units refused for the floor would stand at it if the inner window moved along its own wall. Of the
other nine, eight are the floor's at any spacing, and one is held by the axis below it. Executed
as ruled, and raised: `oq/the-centre-holds-and-refuses-a-window-the-floor-would-seat`.

**3. A fatal flips with the placer's refusals.** `even-bay-front` judges the upper storey's drawn
opening count, and R12's withholding does not reach that count. WP-16.6 moved it both ways on one
record: on the shipped Tidewater house 4 drawn became 3 and the fault cleared, and on the same
record untagged 5 became 4 and it fired. The record's own count is 5 on both readings. The fault's
note calls it a symmetry fault, and extending R12 to it would be a ruling by analogy:
`oq/the-even-bay-fault-judges-the-drawn-parity-of-an-incomplete-front`.

**4. The wall at the corner is ruled by nothing.**
- R5 rules the wall between two windows. The wall between the last opening and the corner of the
  house is unruled, and the ruling said so.
- 7 of the 68 windows ending a run stand closer to the corner than their own width, against 6
  before. The narrowest is 0.3 of the window: good-05's upper east face, 18 in beside a 60 in
  window. Seating from the centre out moves the outer window toward the corner, and nothing stops
  it there.
- The spec Colonial's doorcase stands 2.737 in from its corner.
- Raised: `oq/the-wall-at-the-corner-is-ruled-by-nothing`.

**5. A census check lost its population to the fix it guards, again.** V22 holds the legend to the
leaves the record refuses, and its specimen face, the spec Colonial's south front, stopped refusing
any. The test's own premise fired, as written to. The specimen is good-05's west face now, with
each room's two sashes driven back a foot apart, where the placer seated them before this package.
Before WP-16.6 that same face refused five windows over these two rooms.

The scene's statement of a refused leaf (`scene._dress_openings`) lost its population the same way.
Its one test held the refused windows to `not_modelled` one for one, which on the shipped corpus is
now `[] == []`, so it is driven too.

**6. The composer's cap read the aim as a floor.** It bounded a wall by
`(run + 1.4w) // 2.4w`: the pier the placer aims at, not the one below which it refuses a window. It
reads the floor now. On both briefs that moves nothing, which was measured, not assumed.

**7. Six plans put a garage door widest on their entrance front, and eight place no exterior door
on it.** So the entrance holds the centre on two of the sixteen plans, and the face's centre holds
on the other fourteen.

**8. The mutation pass found a defect of this package's own.**

- No shipped plan reaches it: 24 of 24 drawn piers have glass on both sides.
- It was found by writing the test the mutation pass asked for. It is keyed on the count now, and
  the test raises the KeyError with the old line restored.

**9. Eight of the package's 38 guards were blind on the first mutation sweep.** Each now has a

  claim is removed rather than built, because the facade is the bay grid's result
  (`oq/the-facade-is-a-result`), and no ruling asks the placer to centre on it.
