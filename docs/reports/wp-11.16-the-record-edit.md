# WP-11.16 — the record edit, and the three predictions that did not reproduce

*14 September 2026. The last package of the drawn-sheet Phase 11. Its span precondition shipped
separately as `93790ae` —
`docs/reports/wp-11.16-the-prover-that-charged-the-wrong-rectangle.md`.*

## I. What was asked for, and what it cost

Tag `plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json` so its service programme becomes a west dependency:
`kitchen`, `pantry`, `breakfast`, `powder`, `cellarstair` → `block: "service"` (617 sf);
`backhall` → `block: "service"`, `hyphen: true` (112 sf); `butlers` stays in the block; the
redundant direct `butlers↔kitchen` door dropped on both sides.

That is the whole of the edit and it is thirteen insertions. Everything else in this report is
what the edit revealed.

## III. The premise did not hold: the door costs nothing

WP-11.13's ladder said the redundant direct `butlers↔kitchen` door is what forces CP to refuse the
tagging, and that dropping it is what proves OPTIMAL. Measured on the merged tree:

| | status | objective | wall / shape pins | `[f, s, m]` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tagged, door **kept** | OPTIMAL 9.4 s | 308.1 | 4 / 1 | 0 / 47 / **94** |
| tagged, door **dropped** | OPTIMAL 9.5 s | 308.1 | 4 / 1 | 0 / 47 / **96** |

**Identical proof, and dropping it costs two minors.** The reason the ladder no longer binds is
WP-11.6's own design: `geometry_cp` refuses to MODEL a door between elements that do not abut and
says so in `refinements`, because *"a detached dependency is detached and proving a buildable
house impossible is the one thing a hard constraint here must never do."* The door survives as an
un-modelled declared door rather than as a conflict.

So the door is dropped on different grounds, and they are the corpus's own:

- `rooms/butlers-pantry.json` has said since OQ 59 (24 Aug) that in a Tidewater plantation house
  *"the pantry is in the block and the kitchen is in another building"*. A direct door between
  them is not a door this type has.
- With it kept, **both engines report it permanently unplaced** — 2 of the 4 unplaced doors on the
  proved placement, *"the placement leaves these two rooms no shared wall"*. A record that
  declares a door the drawing can never realise is a record that does not describe its own house.

## IV. What dropping it surfaced, in three layers

### 1. `check_plans` failed the build, and its own docstring held the answer

Doors are **topology** in `build/check_plans.py`, so a door the parti declares and the plan does
not is a hard failure — exit 1. But that file already carved out the neighbouring field for
exactly this reason, and had written the argument down:

> A parti's `exterior_walls` is not topology … a plan that recasts a room's exposure is making a
> MASSING statement, which decision #3 puts outside a parti's authority. … erasing it to make this
> checker quiet would delete the record's own account of the house. It is counted and ratcheted
> instead.

Whether two rooms **can** share a door is the same kind of statement. `centre-passage-double-pile`
has no dependency; the plan says the kitchen is in one. So a door finding **where the plan itself
puts the two rooms in different massing elements** is now `door-across-elements`, REPORTED and
ratcheted at 2, beside the two exposure findings that were already there.

**Narrow, and mutation-checked.** Same-element misses still FAIL: driven by dropping
`dining↛drawing` on the shipped record, `check_plans` returns exit 1 and names it `[door]`, while
the two cross-element ones stay reported. The file was restored byte-identically.

`geometry.is_block_tag` is borrowed rather than re-spelled — its own docstring records that two
readers answering this differently is how a room comes to be sized into one element and placed in
another.

**And a superseded prediction was corrected in the same file.** Its comment said the exposure
findings *"go to zero when WP-11.6 gives the diagram the elements its exemplars have"*. WP-11.6
tried precisely that and `check_partis.py` refused all three arrangements, each by a hard room
rule. The elements arrived on the PLAN instead. `oq/the-parti-dissolved-its-own-dependencies` is
what takes these four to zero, and it is a ruling nobody has given.

### 2. The servicing layer passes a wet pair across a 27 ft gap

Dropping the door adds two `servicing` minors — *"Butler's Pantry is a wet room with no other wet
room adjacent or below it"*, and the same for the kitchen. **Controlled**: tagged with the door
kept, they do not appear. So they are the door and not the tagging, and they are TRUE of this
house — the two rooms are in different buildings and cannot share a chase.

Which means that with the door kept, the servicing layer was giving a **false pass** on an
adjacency the massing makes impossible. The layer reads the declared door graph and is
element-blind; it was never in `not_element_aware`'s list because that list is of placement
readers. `oq/the-servicing-layer-does-not-know-about-massing-elements`.

### 3. The terrace becomes a named refusal — and CP had already refused it

`breakfast` now stands in the west dependency, so its east face looks back across the gap.
The terrace declares `E/N/S`, the both-readings rule admits `E` alone, and the intersection with
the faces the placement put outside is empty: `no-face-both-readings-admit`.

**Measured before publishing it as a cost**: on `engine="cp"`, *the terrace was already refused
with this same code before the tagging*. Only the hill-climb placed it. WP-11.10's published *"4
placed, 2 refused"* was one engine's figure, and the shipped sheet — drawn on `auto`, which takes
the proof — never had this terrace. The edit made the two engines agree.
`oq/the-terraces-declared-faces-were-written-for-a-house-its-room-has-left` asks what a terrace's
declared faces mean when its room moves between elements; re-authoring them to fit the new
placement was available and is the edit this corpus forbids.

## V. A duplicate JSON key, invisible to everything here

The round-trip that applied the tagging collapsed **two duplicate `stacks_over` keys** — `landing`
and `upperpassage` each carried the key twice, with identical values, since WP-11.6, whose
CLAUDE.md entry says it *"now declares the two `stacks_over` claims its own parti had always
made"*. It declared them twice.

`json.loads` keeps the last and drops the rest in silence, so no reader was ever wrong. **And
nothing in this tree could see it**: `jsonschema` validates the PARSED object, and every checker
and test parses first. It surfaced only because a git diff showed two lines removed that nobody
had edited — a tell you get once.

Swept over the tree as committed: **2 duplicates in 1,437 JSON files**, both here, both identical.
`tests/test_duplicate_json_keys.py` is the guard, mutation-checked by injecting a real duplicate
into a real corpus file (red, then restored byte-identically). It is a TEST and not a checker, on
WP-11.6's precedent, so `TOTAL_CHECKS` does not move for a hygiene invariant costing under a
second.

## VII. What this package did NOT do

- **It did not regenerate `tests/fixtures/sheet_symbols/`.** The package text said the README's
  825/804 full regeneration applies because the fixture's rooms change. It does not: both
  consumers — `tests/test_sheet_symbols.py` and `workbench/app/src/derive.test.mjs` — read only
  the frozen fixture and never the shipped record, and the README says in as many words *"Do not
  regenerate to keep the fixture current — it is contract INPUT … and a stale-looking fixture is
  not a stale contract."* A predicted cost, refused with the reason.
- **It did not edit the parti.** Dropping the door there would make `check_plans` quiet by
  changing the diagram 21 partis' worth of checking depends on, and WP-11.6 measured that
  re-authoring refused three ways.
- **It did not re-author the terrace's `exterior_walls`**, which would have placed it immediately.
- **It is not a record edit only, and that is a deviation worth stating.** `build/check_plans.py`
  changed. The package's scope was written before anyone knew the checker's door rule was
  element-blind, and a red build is not an option; the change is six lines and the argument for it
  was already in that file.

## IX. And the prover was stating a proportion band the corpus does not

Found while repairing `tests/test_shape_pins.py`, which went from failing about one run in four
to failing **three of three**. `geometry_cp._build` wrote a room's proportion ceiling as
`10 * mxs <= int(round(_ceil * 10)) * mns`, and `int(round(1.35 * 10))` is **14** — so the prover
asserted a ceiling of **1.4** on every room whose record states 1.35, and this plan's `chamber2`
was drawn 18 × 13 = **1.3846** with its shape pin **held**.

Swept over all 54 banded room types, one decimal place is wrong in both directions: **4 LOOSE**
(`bedroom`, `keeping-room`, `morning-room`, `nursery`) and **1 TIGHT** (`parlor`, 1.45 → 1.4).
The tight direction is the sharper one and had no instance to catch it — the prover can downgrade
an *authored* wall pin, or call a house infeasible, to escape a band the room's own record does
not state.

This answers `oq/a-held-shape-pin-is-not-held-on-the-hard-only-path`'s own Question 1 — *does
phase A assert the pin at all?* It does, at the wrong number, and that entry's own recorded
evidence confirms it: its two rooms are at 1.38 and **1.40**, and 1.40 is exactly the loosened
ceiling. Closed. `_absorb` is exonerated for the fifth time, by a probe rather than by reading.

Neither shipped plan became infeasible under the fix. Its own commit, with its own controls.
