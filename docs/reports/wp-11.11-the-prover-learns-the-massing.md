# WP-11.11 — the prover learns the massing, and then refuses the tags for a better reason

*7 September 2026. Branch `claude/georgian-tidewater-plan-analysis-qbwmqs`. Every figure
re-derived on the working tree and, where it is a BEFORE, on a `git archive HEAD` checkout.
Corpus figures are `engine="heuristic"`, which is deterministic; every CP figure names its
budget.*

---

## I. The question, and the three things it asked to be ruled

`oq/the-proving-engine-cannot-place-a-second-massing-element` recorded the state WP-11.9 left:
`geometry_cp.py` built every room as `x = NewIntVar(0, Wi)` with `x + w <= Wi` — one rectangle,
one non-negative coordinate space — and REFUSED a plan carrying a second massing element rather
than flatten two into one. **That refusal was right**, and the reason is worth carrying: handed a
tagged plan, the model had placed a garage at x = 50 of a 0–70 block while `footprint.blocks`
described an element at x = 84–114, and it FLATTERED the fatal count, because rooms crammed into
one rectangle are all trivially reachable. Refusing beat lying.

The consequence was that on any multi-element plan the engine that PROVES was unavailable and the
engine that SEARCHES carried every finding — which is why WP-11.9 refused to author the dependency
tags on `plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json`, one of the two plans this corpus proves.

The question named three things to rule. All three are taken here, and the third is a measurement
rather than a decision:

1. **One coordinate space per element, or one shifted space?** Ruled: **one space, each room
   bounded by its own element's box** — the question's own "direct reading". CP-SAT integer
   variables take negative lower bounds, so a west dependency at x = −34 needs no shift and no
   second origin.
2. **What does the relaxation ladder do with an element boundary?** Ruled: **nothing. It is not
   downgradable.** `_RANK` is `("wall", "axis", "shape")` and an element edge is none of those —
   it is the massing, and a room drawn outside the mass the record states is not a compromise, it
   is a different building. It is stated as a plain `m.Add`, never a `reqs.lit`, so it creates no
   assumption literal, cannot enter a conflict core and cannot be relaxed.
3. **Is a multi-element proof affordable?** **Measured: yes, and by a wide margin.** The
   three-element selftest fixture is proved OPTIMAL in **0.47 s**; the tagged Tidewater is decided
   in **1.5 s**; both shipped plans keep their status at the batch budget
   (`tidewater-georgian-careful` OPTIMAL hard-only, `spec-builder-colonial` FEASIBLE, both at
   40 s as before). One coordinate space rather than three is why: the model gains constraints,
   not variables.

## II. What was built

Everything the model says about "the block" is now said about the element the room stands in.
`geometry_cp._element_boxes` is the one reader, `geometry.blocks_for` is its source — computed
inside `_build` rather than threaded, because `_build` has seven call sites and a parameter would
have been seven chances to pass a different answer.

| what the model asserts | was | is |
|---|---|---|
| containment | `x + w <= Wi` | `x >= ex` and `x + w <= ex + eW` |
| the coverage floor | 0.97 of the block | 0.97 of **each element** |
| a declared exterior wall | `y == 0`, `x + w == Wi` … | the room's own element's four faces |
| a spanning room's through-axis | the block's `Wi`/`Hi` | its element's `_eW`/`_eH` |
| an exterior door reaching the envelope | the block's four edges | its element's four edges |
| the bay grid (soft) | edges modulo the bay over `[0, Wi]` | over the element, shifted whole bays |
| the span capacity (soft) | grid lines across the block | grid lines **within each element** |
| `_absorb`'s four growth limits | `0`, `W`, `0`, `H` | the room's element's four faces |

The refusal in `geometry.solve()` is gone; `multi_element_disclosure` drops `engine=cp` from
`not_element_aware`, leaving `["roof", "composer"]`.

**The integer box is a SUBSET of the stated element, never a superset.** This model is integer at
1 ft and a dependency's edges are not — `dependency_sizes` lands on 4.6 or −3.7. Rounding to the
nearest foot let CP place a room half a foot outside the mass the record states, which is the
record and the drawing disagreeing about where the house is: the exact defect the refusal was put
there to prevent, arriving through its replacement. The low edge is ceiled and the high edge
floored; `_absorb` afterwards works in float space against the element's true edges and grows the
room back out to them.

## III. The guarantee: the model for a one-rectangle house is byte-identical

Not the placement — the **model**. CP-SAT under a wall-clock budget is not reproducible, so
pinning what it finds would pin this machine; what is reproducible is what it is asked. The
serialized `CpModel` proto for both shipped plans in both phases, measured on a `git archive HEAD`
checkout before the package and on the working tree after it:

```
tidewater-georgian-careful   objective=False  b51561f2924f1720   unchanged
tidewater-georgian-careful   objective=True   6d5a6779e293f7a2   unchanged
spec-builder-colonial        objective=False  dc3527d9347b96e6   unchanged
spec-builder-colonial        objective=True   947589fc1c88444d   unchanged
```

Three guards exist only to keep them, and each is written as a condition rather than a branch:
`if gx0 < ex` (§V), `elif len(els.get(lvl) or []) > 1` for the per-element coverage, and `if base:`
for the modulo shift. The corpus's own placement and openings hashes are unchanged too
(`151126d0269bbc61`, `770a886c7387f3ab`), and the finding triple is unmoved at
fatal 162 · serious 685 · minor 992.

## IV. What it can now do, and what it says about the shipped record

**It places three massing elements.** `geometry_cp._multi_element_fixture` — a main block, a
hyphen and a dependency, whose door graph is consistent with being three masses because every
cross-element door goes THROUGH the hyphen — is proved OPTIMAL with every room inside its own
element, worst overhang **0.00 ft**. That is the third line of the CP selftest now, beside the
feasible fixture and the K5 refusal.

**And it refuses the tags on `tidewater-georgian-careful` — for a completely different reason
from the one WP-11.9 refused them for.** WP-11.9 withheld them because tagging would trade a
proved reference plan for a searched one. Measured now, with the prover available:

| `tidewater-georgian-careful`, six service rooms as a west dependency, back hall as its hyphen | |
|---|---|
| `engine="heuristic"` | fatal **8 → 6**, serious **61 → 53** — the tagging IMPROVES the plan |
| `engine="cp"` | **INFEASIBLE, proven, minimized core, in 1.5 s** |

**The search rewards a tagging the prover refuses**, and there are two independent reasons, both
of which must be stated because they bind at different settings:

- **At the shipped `COVERAGE = 0.97` the plan is infeasible with every declared requirement
  dropped** — *"the rooms cannot tile any footprint this parti and lot allow"*. Per-element
  coverage on boxes `dependency_sizes` sizes to exactly their rooms' areas is an exact cover,
  three times over. That is WP-2.3's own finding (*"the exact-tiling formulation does not work"*)
  arriving one element down. `oq/the-coverage-floor-is-an-exact-cover-per-element`.
- **Below 0.97 the coverage floor stops binding and the minimized core is ONE DOOR**:
  *"Dining Room and Butler's Pantry share a door — they must share enough wall for one."*

That second one is the architecturally meaningful refusal, and it is small enough to check by
hand. Of the **16 interior door pairs on the Tidewater ground floor, exactly two** cross from the
main block to the dependency without passing through the hyphen:

```
butlers  - dining     MAIN TO DEPENDENCY ACROSS THE GAP
breakfast- terrace    (the terrace is an at-grade appendage, WP-11.10; CP does not model it)
```

So **one declared door decides it**, and it is the door the butler's pantry exists for — that
room's own record calls the visual lock between kitchen and dining room "the rule the room exists
for". Putting the butler's pantry in a detached dependency severs it; leaving it in the main block
severs its door to the kitchen instead (measured: the five-service tagging's core is
*"Butler's Pantry and Kitchen (Dependency) share a door"*). **Every boundary `blocks_for` can draw
through this record cuts a declared door.**

That is a finding about the RECORD and its parti, not about the engine, and it is
`oq/the-parti-dissolved-its-own-dependencies` measured for the first time: the parti names eleven
enclosed ground rooms where its three exemplars have six, with service in a basement, an
outbuilding or a wing. The tags are still not authored — and now the reason is *the prover says
no*, with a one-door core, instead of *the prover cannot look*.

## V. The three defects this package found in itself

- **THE GUARD WRITTEN FOR BYTE-IDENTITY CREATED THE DEFECT IT WAS GUARDING AGAINST.** The first
  version of the containment lower bound read `if ex:` — "on a one-rectangle house `ex` is 0 and
  `x >= 0` is already the domain". True of the OLD domain and false of the new one, because `gx0`
  is the leftmost element's edge and a west dependency puts it at −34: every MAIN-BLOCK room then
  had a domain reaching 34 ft west of the house with nothing holding it back. Measured on a
  hand-tagged Tidewater, **five untagged rooms were placed or absorbed at a negative x, in no
  element at all**. `gx0 < ex` is the honest test — False on every single-element plan, True
  exactly when the domain really is wider than the box. **Caught by a probe that ran
  `elements.element_of` over every placed room**, not by a test and not by reading.
- **A MEASUREMENT TAKEN ON A MODEL WITH THAT BUG REPORTED A PROOF THAT DID NOT EXIST.** Before it
  was found, the affordability spike reported the six-service tagging without a hyphen as
  **"SOLVED, OPTIMAL in 13.5 s"**, and that number was one edit away from being published as the
  package's headline. It was an artefact of rooms being free to roam into negative x. With
  containment enforced the same plan is INFEASIBLE in 1.6 s. *An instrument that cannot fail is
  worse than a test that cannot fail, because its output is a number rather than a green tick* —
  and this one produced the flattering number, which is the direction that gets published.
- **`_absorb` UNDID A PROOF FOR THE FOURTH TIME**, and its own docstring already recorded the
  other three. CP proved the breakfast room at y ≥ 4.95, the west dependency's own south edge;
  the absorb pass grew it to y = 1.6 — **3.35 ft of drawn floor outside the mass it belongs to**,
  in a record that looks exactly like a solved plan. It takes `bounds` now, beside `caps`,
  `keepout` and `ratios`, which are the other three.

Two smaller ones, both found by running rather than reading: `AddModuloEquality` truncates toward
zero, so the SOFT bay term would have been infeasible on a west dependency (a scoring preference
silently deciding a plan cannot be built); and the span-capacity term drew grid lines across the
gap between two detached elements, which is WP-11.9's `structure.wall_lines` defect arriving in
the objective.

## VI. The audit of this package's own guards

Nine mutations against the eleven new tests in `tests/test_cp_elements.py`, each **asserted to
have LANDED** before the suite ran. Every one bites: reverting the `gx0 < ex` test, the
ceil/floor rounding, `_absorb`'s bounds, the per-element coverage, the containment itself, the
modulo shift, the per-element exterior wall, making the boundary conditional, and restoring the
dispatcher's refusal. Five of the nine fail two tests rather than one.

`tests/test_geometry.py::TestCPRefusesASecondMassingElement` is
`TestCPPlacesASecondMassingElement` now — the POSITIVE form of the same protection. What CP may
never do again is flatten; what it may do is place each room in its element, or prove the brief
cannot be housed and name the conflict. The class asserts both, and asserts that `auto`'s
fall-back reason is the proved conflict rather than *"the CP model places every room in a single
rectangle"*, which is the sentence the plate used to print.

**And `tests/test_modcache.py::test_no_new_by_path_loader_outside_modcache` caught this package
too, fifty minutes into the full suite** — a local `spec_from_file_location` inside the new
selftest fixture, loading `build/elements.py` by path. **That is the second package running that
this one guard has caught**: WP-11.10's `appendages.py` had a `main()` with the same line in it.
It is CLAUDE.md's standing trap (*"Do not reinstate a local loader"*) with a test behind it, and
in both cases nothing else in the tree noticed — not a checker, not the package's own test file,
not the app suite. The selftest goes through `_mod`, which is this file's own delegation to
`modcache`, and was three characters from doing so all along.

## VII. What this package does NOT do

- **The roof still spans the union bounding box**, by WP-11.9's ruling 1. `not_element_aware` is
  `["roof", "composer"]`.
- **The composer still writes no `block` tag**, so the only way to reach a multi-element plan is a
  caller-supplied or hand-tagged record.
- **The shipped record is still not tagged**, and §IV is the reason. It is a better reason than
  WP-11.9's and it is not this package's to fix: it is a door in the record.
- **The per-element coverage floor is an exact cover and is left at 0.97**, because lowering it or
  exempting a dependency would be inventing a number to make a refusal go away.
  `oq/the-coverage-floor-is-an-exact-cover-per-element` carries the measurement and what must be
  ruled.
- **No abutment constraint.** WP-11.9 shipped the hyphen-abutment term into the SEARCH and
  measured it inert on everything `blocks_for` produces; nothing here changes that, and CP gets no
  abutment constraint for the same reason — `blocks_for` clamps the hyphen's depth and centres
  all three on one axis, so a shared wall exists by construction.
- **No new checker.** `geometry_cp.py selftest` already runs in `check_all` and grew a third
  fixture; `TOTAL_CHECKS` does not move.
