## II. THE DEVIATION FROM THE RULING'S LIST, AND ITS MEASUREMENT

**The butler's pantry does not move.** `rooms/butlers-pantry.json` states two hard adjacencies and
they are not equivalent:

- `must_adjoin dining-room`, `strength: hard`, **no `via`**;
- `must_adjoin kitchen`, `strength: hard`, **`via: [back-hall, gallery-corridor]`** — added at
  OQ 59 on 24 August 2026, with the reason written into the record: *"in a plan whose kitchen is
  in a dependency — a five-part Palladian, a Tidewater plantation house, any arrangement with a
  detached or hyphenated service wing — the pantry is in the block and the kitchen is in another
  building, and the connecting gallery is the connection. Read without this, the rule asked the
  pantry to be in two buildings at once."*

A pantry in the dependency must cross an element boundary to reach the dining room and has no
`via` to cross it by. A pantry in the block crosses to reach the kitchen and has one, which this
record already holds in full: `butlers → backhall → kitchen`. WP-11.13 measured all three
taggings and only that one proves. **The corpus's own worked precedent agrees**:
`partis/five-part-palladian.json`'s butler's pantry doors `dining` and `westhyphen` and never the
kitchen — the ruling names that parti as the precedent, and this is what it does.

**So the redundant direct butler's-pantry-to-kitchen door is dropped**, from both sides of the
pair, in both records. It is the door CP-SAT cores on.

**The breakfast room is added to the list, and a hard rule adds it.**
`rooms/breakfast-room.json` states `must_adjoin kitchen`, `strength: hard`, **no `via`**. Left in
the main block with the kitchen in the wing it would be exactly the defect the butler's-pantry
clause exists to prevent, one room over — a hard adjacency with no route across the boundary.
That reading is the room record's, read here; CLAUDE.md records WP-11.11's own hand-tagging
putting the breakfast room in the dependency too, which is corroboration and not the reason.

**The ruling's sentence has a slot for this.** It reads *"the main block carries the type's six —
passage, stair hall, drawing, dining, library and one more the parti names"*. There is an unnamed
sixth room in the main block, and the corpus says which one it is. That reading and the literal
mover-list reading cannot both hold; this package takes the corpus's, states the deviation here
and in the commit message, and did not follow the list silently.

## IV. WHAT GOT WORSE, WITH THE NUMBER

Three costs, and none of them is netted off against the gains above.

**1. The shape pins. 0 → 20 downgraded on CP** (16 at the 60 s budget). The ladder now reaches
its seventh and last rank. A 45 ft main block holding the same seven rooms and the same eleven
upper rooms is a tighter packing problem than a 63 ft one, and the proportion bands are what
gives. Two rooms are convicted on the placed record: the pantry drawn 5.0 × 10.0 (2.0 : 1 against
a 1.0–1.8 band) and the principal bath drawn 8.0 × 16.0 (2.0 : 1 against the same band), both
`minor`, both with the record's own declared figures beside them.

**2. The hearth fact, on the heuristic, and it went from HELD to DOWNGRADED.** The search draws
the drawing room and the dining room 4.95 ft inboard of the main block's west face, so neither
gable fire stands on an exterior wall that carries its flue. On CP the drawing room's fire holds
and the dining room's is 6.00 ft inboard — 1 of 3 rather than 2 of 3. This is a placement cost of
the narrower block and not a record error: the record states the same three fires on the same
three walls it always did. The two W-face fires remain
`oq/a-shared-flue-cannot-stand-behind-two-centred-breasts`, which this package does not touch.

**3. Spans, on the heuristic. 3 → 4 over capacity and the worst 35.5 → 45.0 ft.** On CP it is the
other way — 2 → 0, and the worst falls 63.0 → 37.0 — so this is one engine's arithmetic and not
the container's. Both halves are published because the net alone hides which way either went.

**4. THE TERRACE IS NO LONGER DRAWN ON THE HEURISTIC, AND IT IS REFUSED BY NAME RATHER THAN
LOST.** It goes from placed at `x 63.00, y 16.17, 14.0 × 22.0` to
`code: no-face-both-readings-admit`, in the pass's own closed vocabulary:

    declared ["E","N","S"]   outside ["S","W"]

`rooms/terrace.json` is an at-grade appendage, not a massing element and not a moved room
(WP-11.10), so this package does not touch its record. WP-11.10's rule admits a face `w` exactly
when `w` is declared and `OPPOSITE(w)` is not — N and S cancel, so only E is admitted — and the
breakfast room's own element is outside on S and W. **E is now the face that looks back at the
house across the gap.** The declaration was authored for a breakfast room at the east end of one
rectangle and is no longer true of the house the record describes.

**It is not patched, on WP-11.10's own stated precedent** (*"4 placed, 2 refused by name and
neither patched"*). Re-declaring the terrace's exposures is a massing statement about a house
nobody has drawn yet, and inventing one to keep a rectangle on the sheet is the move this package
exists to avoid. **And the refusal is not new to the prover**: on CP the terrace was ALREADY
unplaced before the edit, with the same code and the same `outside ["S","W"]` — so what moved is
one engine's placement, not the record's answerability.

And two findings that are costs on the heuristic only: `fixture-unplaced` 0 → 1 (the principal
bath's shower with bench, all four walls tried), and `drawn-passage-off-centre` 0 → 1 (the passage
drawn 4.71 ft off the centre line of a 45 ft front against a 4.5 ft allowance).

## VI. THE DEFECT THIS PACKAGE FOUND BY ACCIDENT: A DUPLICATE JSON KEY

**`plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json` stated `"stacks_over"` TWICE on `landing` and twice on
`upperpassage`.** `json.load` keeps the last occurrence and says nothing; `jsonschema` validates
the parsed object, so the first copy is gone before any schema sees it. Both engines, both
renderers, `check_plans.py`, `check_stacking.py` and `plan_check` therefore agreed with one
another about a record that says one thing twice — and agreed *correctly*, because both copies
carried the same value. **Nothing in this corpus has ever behaved differently, which is exactly
why it survived**: the failure needs the two copies to disagree, and the first reader to notice
would then have been a person reading a sheet.

It was found by round-tripping the file — parse, edit, dump — and reading the diff, which
collapsed the pair and showed as the deletion of a line nobody had deleted.

Swept: **2 duplicates corpus-wide, both in that one file.** The instrument was proved on a
`git archive HEAD` checkout before it was believed — it returns 2 there and 0 on the working
tree, which is the difference between a detector and a silence. `build/validate.py` now carries
the sweep over every tracked and untracked-not-ignored `*.json` (1,439 records read), **inside an
existing checker so `TOTAL_CHECKS` does not move** — WP-12.1's scene selftest is the precedent —
and enumerating with `git ls-files` and never `os.walk`, which is WP-13.2's own rule after a
checker walked an agent worktree and went red on a copy of the repository inside itself.

## VIII. WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT DONE

**The plan does not name `dependency-and-hyphen`.** It now demonstrably has a hyphen, and the
grouping that governs one therefore goes unevaluated — a check that cannot fire reading exactly
like a check that passed. Naming it was measured and refused, because it makes the sheet carry
two numbers for one dimension: `roof.wing_step_down` publishes `hyphen_length_ft` as the band's
MIDPOINT, **16.0 ft**, while `geometry.flank_sizes` reads the hyphen room's own `width_ft`,
**7.0 ft**. And the band itself cannot be met by this room at all —
`groupings/dependency-and-hyphen.json` floors `hyphen_length_ft` at 12 and `rooms/back-hall.json`
ceilings `width_ft` at 7, two bands with no overlap and a stated reason on each side.
`oq/a-back-hall-cannot-be-a-hyphen-and-stay-inside-its-own-width-band`.

**No room's type was changed, no dimension invented, no `exterior_walls` edited.** The movers
keep their declarations exactly as authored — WP-11.6 item 4's finding is that a service room
declaring N/S/W is declaring the exposures of a WING, and the element box is what gives them real
faces.

**The parti's own `exterior_walls` were left alone**, which keeps `check_plans.py`'s two exposure
findings at their ceiling of 2. The parti states its service wing to the EAST (`kitchen` E/N) and
this plan builds it to the WEST (`kitchen` N/S/W); `geometry.flank_sizes` reads the side off those
very letters, so the two records describe mirror-image houses. Both are instances of the diagram.
Editing either to make the count fall would be deleting a record's own account of its house —
and the comment predicting that count would go to zero at WP-11.6 was wrong and is corrected in
place rather than quietly replaced.

**`STACK_HARD` was not flipped** and no bound, ratchet or gate row was loosened.

## XI. WHAT THE FULL SUITE FOUND, AND THE TWO READER DEFECTS IN IT

A whole `pytest tests/` on the committed tree came back **39 failed / 2459 passed / 21 skipped in
47 min 33 s**, and **32 / 2467 / 21 after the repairs below**. Twelve are the base's, reproduced on a pristine `git archive HEAD` checkout;
eighteen are the Phase 13 gate, not re-baselined; one is the shipped-sheet hash, already red on
the base; one is `tests/test_solver.py`'s half bound, left red on purpose. The remaining seven
were this package's and are worked below. **Two of them were not ratchets at all.**

### THE AGREEMENT TEST'S OWN CONTROL WAS A FOURTH SPELLING OF THE RULE

`tests/test_typefacts.py::test_the_bearing_lines_agree_with_the_gates_reader_on_every_shipped_plan`
holds `build/typefacts.py`'s transcription against the original, line for line. Its "original"
was a hand-rolled `ST.bearing_lines(ST.wall_lines(rooms, W, H), bay)` — **every room of the level
in one call, against the MAIN BLOCK's `W, H`**. That is precisely the reading WP-11.6's layer 2
removed from `structure.build_section`, where the arithmetic runs once per element over that
element's own rooms at that element's own origin.

It agreed with the leaf for as long as every shipped plan was one rectangle. The container parted
them at once:

    typefacts (per element)   L0  [('y', 26.56)]
    the test's own control    L0  [('x', 0), ('y', 26.56)]

`x = 0` is the main block's own WEST wall. It reads as an interior bearing line only to a caller
that believes the hyphen and the dependency are inside the block. **The leaf was right and the
control was wrong**, which is the direction that matters: a control that has not been taught what
the code under test knows convicts the code.

The control is `structure.build_section`'s own per-element walls now — still an independent
reader, a different module and a different call path, and the one every other consumer already
uses. The two agree exactly on both levels. The mutation that proves it bites is `typefacts`
reverted to the level-wide reading, and it fails with the mirror image of the original message.
The premise is asserted too: at least one shipped plan must state a container, or the comparison
runs only where the defect cannot occur.

### AND `as_one_element` IS NOT A CONTROL FOR THE CP MODEL HASH

`tests/test_cp_elements.py` pins the serialized `CpModel` proto for both shipped plans — the
thing the prover is ASKED, since what it FINDS is wall-clock bound and would pin the machine. The
Tidewater pair moved, and the obvious control was to strip the six tags and check the old value
came back. **It does not.** Stripping gives a THIRD hash, because WP-13.5 made three edits to that
record and the tags are one of them:

    shipped                                        ed29a965f427f03f
    tags stripped                                  b9ef896282bf7bbd
    + the butlers<->kitchen door restored          933320674985d6b5
    + `hallbath stacks_over powder` restored       f499ab415b2add2e   <- the old pin, exactly

Every step is a fact `_build` states as an assumption literal: a door is a hard abutment, and
WP-13.3 made each declared stack one through `stacking.lands`' own relation. So the movement is
accounted for edit by edit and **none of it is the model builder** — which is confirmed
independently by `spec-builder-colonial`, genuinely one rectangle, whose two hashes are unchanged
and re-derived on a pristine checkout.

**A control that reverts SOME of a package's edits and is quoted as though it reverted all of
them is a new number wearing a measurement's clothes.** The pin is split now: the one-rectangle
guarantee runs on `spec-builder-colonial` with its premise asserted, and the shipped container's
model has a pin of its own, stated as a separate fact rather than folded into a guarantee whose
docstring says any movement is a defect.
