*9 September 2026. Closes §V of `docs/reports/wp-12.8-the-adversarial-audit-of-phase-12.md`.*

WP-12.8 audited Phase 12 and deferred five items with reasons. This package finishes them. Two
needed a ruling and both were given on the day they were put; three did not.

**The headline is that the first item was not the question the audit asked.** WP-12.8 wrote:

> **The chimney's plan size is still read twice** — `elevation.py` from the proportion pack,
> `threshold.py` from the resolved kit cascade. They agree at 22 in today by construction.
> Reconciling them is a data question about which record owns the figure, not a drawing change.

Measured, the two readers agree on the **number** on all 14 styles that resolve one, with zero
disagreements. They do not agree on whether it is a **judgment**, and the plan sheet published a

## II. Item 1 — a judgment laundered as a derived figure, thirteen times

`proportions/modules/brick-course.json` states the stack's plan size as `part * 8` and flags it:

```json
"judgment": true,
"note": "... Twenty-two inches on the default coursing is between sizes; the mason will build
         18 or 27 and someone should decide which rather than discovering it on site."
```

The baked snapshot of that rule in `kits/georgian-colonial-american.kit.json` carried
`kind: derived`, a `source`, a `note` — and **no flag**.

### The census, which is what made it a package rather than a patch

Over the 143 snapshots `check_baked_snapshots` reaches — a `kind: derived` parameter carrying an
`expr`, a resolvable `source` and a `computed_at.value` — matched to their source rule by
`(target_slot, expression)`. **One denominator throughout**: a wider sweep that drops the
`computed_at.value` condition sees 145 and 52 unmatched, and the two extra are named in §II's
closing paragraph rather than quietly averaged in here.

| | |
|---|---|
| snapshots matched to a source rule | **93** |
| rule `judgment: true`, snapshot **carries** it | **0** |
| rule `judgment: true`, snapshot **drops** it | **13** |
| rule is not a judgment | 80 |
| could not be matched to any rule | 50 |

**The bake has never carried the flag.** Not one snapshot in the corpus carried a judgment, so
this was not a slip on one parameter — it was a systematic loss at a layer nothing inspected.

### Why nothing caught it

`check_kits.py::check_baked_snapshots` re-derives the **value** and compares that. It has reported
*135 re-derived at the context they record and AGREEING* for a year, and it was green over every
one of the thirteen, because the number was right. A snapshot can be perfectly faithful to its
expression and unfaithful to everything else its rule says.

### And the corpus could not have said it anyway

`schema/kit.schema.json` admitted `judgment` on a **slot** (`/properties/slots/additionalProperties`)
and not on a **parameter** — `$defs/parameter` is `additionalProperties: false`. So the corpus
could say *"this slot is a decision"* and had no way to say *"this number is"*. Adding the flag
to the data failed schema validation on the first run, which is how that was found.

### The third surface

`brick-course`'s 22 in is drawn by three surfaces, and until this package only two disclosed it:

| surface | what it draws | what it said |
|---|---|---|
| `render_elevation.py` | the stack, 22 in wide | `STACK DRAWN 22″ SQUARE — A JUDGMENT, NOT A MEASUREMENT …` in the legend |
| `build/scene.py` | a two-vertex **axis**, never a solid | a `judgment` entry naming the figure and the basis |
| `render_plan.py` / `Sheet.jsx` | a solid poché square | *"chimney stack, 22.0 in square"* — **nothing** |

The scene's own judgment note says *"the two layers disclose one judgment two ways"*. There are
three, and the third is the plan — the one surface where a reader is choosing a brick.

### What was built

1. `judgment: true` on all **13** snapshots whose source rule carries it. Derived from the rules
   rather than typed from a list, and the write round-trips `build.py`'s own
   `indent=2, ensure_ascii=False` exactly: **13 insertions, 0 deletions.**
2. `$defs/parameter` admits `judgment`.
3. `check_kits.py::check_baked_flags` — a **second question with its own two counters**, because
   a snapshot's value and its flags are different populations and quoting either for the other is
   the error this file's own baked/refused pair warns against. Reports
   **93 matched and agreeing, 50 could not be compared**, ratcheted at 50.
4. `threshold.py::stack_pass` carries `stack_plan_judgment` **and `stack_plan_basis`** onto each
   stack. The basis travels with the flag because *a judgment with no basis named* is what this
   corpus forbids one step further than a figure with no source — the shape the two-Phase-11
   merge met when a hearth tooltip's `Morris 1734, judgment` became a bare `judgment`.
5. `disclosures.py::stack_plan_judgment` — one spelling, read by both surfaces, and **both were
   measured rather than assumed**: the line appears in the rendered `tidewater-georgian-careful`
   SVG (diffed fragment by fragment against the previous commit) and in
   `core.placement_summary(out)["disclosures"]`, which is what the bench strip renders —
   `['relaxations', 'windows', 'transfers', 'stack-judgment', 'engine']`, the same text on each.
6. Both plan tooltips name it.

### The pack is resolved ONCE and the flag question is asked of every snapshot

`proportion_engine.resolve()` deep-copies and recurses over overlays and is not cached. Adding a
second walk would have doubled that cost, so the resolve is **hoisted** out of the value block and
the flag check runs before the value check's early returns. Net cost: zero extra resolves, and the
flag question reaches the eight snapshots whose value cannot be re-derived — a snapshot whose
number is unjudgeable can still be unfaithful to its source's flags.

### The match is strict on purpose

50 snapshots name a `source` pack that states no rule for their slot with that expression —
`english-georgian.door_surround.order_height_ratio` cites `gibbs-ionic`, which carries **zero**
rules targeting that slot. Those are COULD NOT COMPARE, counted and printed. Loosening the match
until they resolve would pair a snapshot with a rule that is not its source, which is OQ 48's
error one layer up.

**The ceiling is 50 and not 52**, and the two-unit gap is accounted rather than padded:
`english-georgian.door_surround`'s `order_height_ratio` and `entablature_depth` carry an `expr`
and **no** `computed_at.value`, so they are live expressions rather than snapshots and never
enter the loop. There is no snapshot there to be unfaithful to anything.

## IV. Item 3 — `sweep` and `lathe` removed

The schema declared both by **field name alone**:

```json
{ "properties": {"type": {"const": "sweep"}}, "required": ["type","profile","path","scale"] },
{ "properties": {"type": {"const": "lathe"}}, "required": ["type","radius_at","axis","z0"] }
```

No shape, no units, no frame. Is `path` a list of 3-points? Is `radius_at` a list of `(z, r)`
pairs? Is `scale` a scalar or per-station? **An extent rule for either would have had to invent
all of it**, which is this corpus's first rule met one layer over — and nothing emitted either.

Removed: **28 deletions, 1 insertion** (a description saying why, and what a package adding one
back owes). `scene_version` is **not** bumped and that is a decision: no record's shape changes,
because no record could hold either primitive.

`build/scene.py::_extent` is now **total over everything a valid record can hold**. Its
`UnknownPrimitive` raise stays, and the reason is worth stating because "unreachable" is usually
an argument for deletion here: what `_extent` is handed is not a validated record off disk, it is
geometry `build_scene` has just constructed and not yet validated. So the raise guards the case
that actually matters — **`build_scene` growing a primitive without an extent rule** — which is
the day the frame silently becomes too small. A fallback against a malformed input, not a check
whose greenness reads as a verdict.

The guard is new and reads the property rather than the source:
`test_the_frame_measures_every_primitive_the_schema_admits_and_no_others` builds a real instance
of each of the four kinds, **validates each against the schema** and requires `_extent` to measure
it, in both directions. Mutation-checked: putting `sweep` back goes red.

## V. Item 4 — the CLI printed nothing in the case it exists for

`build/geometry.py`'s CLI read

```python
for r in (out.get("conflict") or {}).get("requirements", []): print(f"    · {r}")
```

**Neither key exists anywhere in this tree.** `geometry_cp.solve_cp` states
`infeasible.{proven, conflicts, minimized, downgraded_wall_pins, downgraded_shape_pins, note,
attempts}` and always has. So on the one path that reaches that branch — CP-SAT proves the
declared facts cannot all hold **and** the hill-climb cannot place the house either — a person at
the CLI was shown the error and none of the reasons.

The reader now takes `infeasible.conflicts`, **and the `note` as well**: a sufficient core that
was not fully minimized says so in that sentence, and a reader given the list without it reads a
removable requirement as a necessary one.

It is a function (`geometry.conflict_lines`) rather than inline, for `export_ifc.slab_boxes`'
reason: **computed where a test can read it, printed where it cannot.** No shipped record reaches
that branch, so a guard written against `main()` would be a guard nothing runs.

And the test holds the reader against the **writer's own output** rather than a shape written by
hand — it drives the same real K5 refusal `test_k5_door_graph_is_a_named_proven_conflict` uses and
requires the printer to find that core inside it, so the two cannot drift without going red. The
defect itself is driven too: the old shape produces `[]`.

## VI. Item 5 — two schema-impossible keys

`moves.py::_split_per_grouping` read

```python
types = {x.get("type") or x.get("room") for x in (g.get("rooms") or [])} | set(g.get("required_rooms") or [])
```

Censused over all 17 grouping records: `rooms[]` items carry `room`/`role`/`count`/`note` and
**never** `type`; **no** record carries `required_rooms`.

They are not merely absent. `schema/grouping.schema.json` sets `additionalProperties: false` at
**both** levels and makes `room` **required**, so a record carrying either fails validation and
never reaches the reader.

**That distinction decided the fix.** This corpus keeps a fallback that cannot fire (WP-12.2 kept
one: a bay the record calls a door on a wall with no entrance composition) and removes a check
that cannot fire, because a check's greenness reads as a verdict. This is neither: the schema
makes both keys impossible, so the schema is the guard — and that is what the new test asserts,
in both directions, beside a corpus census and a behavioural check that the move still selects
what it is meant to.

## VIII. The movement, and how it was accounted

**One shipped sheet of sixteen moved**: `tidewater-georgian-careful`, the only plan that draws a
stack. **+296 bytes.**

| what | detail |
|---|---|
| four tooltips | gain `(a judgment, not a measurement)` — two stacks × two plates |
| one schedule row | the new banner line; canvas **962 → 976 px** |
| every row below it | shifts by exactly **14** px, the row height |
| the drawing field | **unchanged** |

**The accounting was proved, not reasoned.** Removing the single field this package added —
`judgment: true` on `chimney.stack_plan_in` — and re-rendering gives **all sixteen sheets
byte-identical to the previous commit**. So the entire corpus movement is attributable to that one
flag and to nothing else in the package.

`CORPUS_SHEET_SHA` `535077ae0bca1ea2` → **`c4210345a77b9904`**;
`CORPUS_SHEET_SHA_NO_FRAME` `373d0116be7cecb8` → **`b620afc41d04b412`**.

### And the harness that measured it was wrong once

The first sweep hashed the sixteen rendered files in **filename** order; the test hashes them in
`plans/*.json` order followed by `plans/reference/*.json`, and the two are not the same sequence.
It produced a confident pair of hashes that were hashes of nothing anybody computes, and **they
were written into the test** — `0bcdd2702cc6567b` / `1eefa1e289af46de`, neither of which is a
hash of this corpus in any order the tree computes. The pin was wrong for about ten minutes, and
what was wrong was the instrument, not the numbers I copied out of it faithfully.

**The control caught it**: running the same harness over a `git archive HEAD` checkout and
requiring it to reproduce `535077ae0bca1ea2` / `373d0116be7cecb8`. It did not, so the instrument
was wrong rather than the corpus. **A re-pin whose instrument has not been shown to reproduce the
OLD value is not a measurement, it is a new number** — which is WP-12.4's own lesson, where
*"16 of 16 plates byte-identical"* was true of a comparison that was not the one the pin makes,
and three commits shipped on a red assertion.

## IX. Deliberately not done

- **The `disclosures.py` reconciliation.** Raised as a question with its measurement rather than
  taken here: the two lists are not the same set in either direction, the tone mapping is a
  decision about a surface, and all sixteen sheets move. §VII says why the obvious fix is worse
  than the defect.
- **`scene_version` is not bumped.** Narrowing the schema changes no record's shape, because no
  record could hold a `sweep` or a `lathe`. Stated here rather than left implicit.
- **The 50 unmatchable snapshots are not chased.** Each names a pack that states no rule for its
  slot; whether that is a wrong `source` or a rule the pack owes is a question per parameter, and
  the ratchet holds the count while somebody reads them.
- **The 80 non-judgment snapshots are not re-kinded.** `check_baked_flags` compares `judgment`
  only. Extending it to `range`, `units` or `quantity` would need a ruling on which rule fields
  a snapshot is supposed to reproduce, and inventing that correspondence late is how WP-9.4
  shipped four guards that could not fail.

| round | pristine `4b05806` | working `edb6c1b` |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | FAIL | FAIL |
| 2 | FAIL | FAIL |

**4 of 4 on each, and the two trees agree** — which is precisely what CLAUDE.md records as the
stable property of this intermittent (*"the rate is a property of the machine and the day … what
is stable is that the two trees agree"*). The failure is pre-existing and is not this package's.
The `+ 0.02` tolerance was NOT touched, which that entry forbids in as many words.
