## I. What was asked, and what "places" meant

A plan places when all five of these hold:

1. On `engine="heuristic"`, `geometry_report.refused` is `None`.
2. On `engine="auto"` at the batch budget, `refused` is `None` on five of five runs, with the load
   recorded.
3. `corpus._placed` returns no `refused_placement`.
4. Every drawing kind, the scene and the DXF export succeed, and the roof draws the declared stacks.
5. Every type fact the careful record judges (tiling, stacks, bearing, hearth: all four) is `held`,
   and none becomes `unjudged`.

`plan_check` counts are reported and are not a gate.

**Admissible edits:**

- room sizes inside each room record's own bands;
- the arrangement, inside the parti's topology;
- `stacks_over`;
- breasts centred on their walls;
- flues as the massing states them.

**Laundering, which was refused:**

- deleting or weakening a declaration until a fact reads unjudged;
- moving a breast off centre;
- editing any other record;
- touching anything in `build/`;
- choosing a lucky CP run;
- changing the style, the parti or the massing.

**Stop rule.** Stop at the first of:

- (a) success;
- (b) 12 variants or 6 h of solver time;
- (c) three admissible edits in a row that leave the heuristic's refused set unchanged;
- (d) refusals that trace only to an open question or to an instrument.

Criteria 3 and 4 were never reached. `corpus._placed` solves on `auto` and returns
`refused_response` whenever `geometry_report.refused` is set. That is exactly the verdict that
criterion 2 failed on for every variant.

## III. The fact that decides it, proved by hand

**The premises**, each the corpus's own words:

1. **The passage runs front to back, with two rooms either side of it.**
   `partis/centre-passage-double-pile.json`: *"A through-passage front to back with two rooms either
   side"*. The passage declares its exterior walls S and N, the drawing room S and W, the dining
   room N and W, and the library S and E. "Either side" is read as *wholly* on one side of the
   passage's walls, and the model below encodes the same reading.
2. **The drawing room's and the dining room's fires are on the west gable**, so the west edge of
   each lies on the block's west face. The massing, four-over-four: *"Paired end chimneys serve four
   fireplaces per floor."* The plan record states both fires there, and `typefacts.hearth` reads a
   fire as held only when the room's edge is on its flue wall's face.
3. **The dining room doors the passage**, and a door needs a shared wall (the parti's own `doors`).
4. **The butler's pantry doors the dining room directly, on the dining room's side.**
   `rooms/butlers-pantry.json`: `must_adjoin dining-room`, `direct-door`, hard. The pantry stands
   in the main block, not in the dependency, and the same record says so of a Tidewater plantation
   house: *"the pantry is in the block and the kitchen is in another building"*. The parti puts it
   on the north face, joining the dining room to the link.
5. **The bands.** `rooms/drawing-room.json` has a `width_ft` floor of **16**, which bounds both of
   its sides. `rooms/butlers-pantry.json` has a `length_ft` ceiling of **14** (area ≤ 90, proportion
   ≤ 2.4). `rooms/library.json` has area ≤ 600 and proportion ≤ 1.6.

**The argument.** It needs neither the entrance front nor the hyphen, and it holds wherever along the
gable the drawing room stands.

- The passage runs the full depth from the porch to the rear. It therefore divides the block into a
  west range and an east range, and no room of one touches a room of the other.
- The drawing room, the dining room and the pantry lie in the west range. With the library and the
  stair hall on the other side, they are the only rooms there.
- The dining room touches the west face (premise 2) and must share a wall with the passage
  (premise 3). A rectangle touching both long edges of the west range **spans its whole width**.
- The west range is at least as wide as the drawing room, which is **16 ft** at least.
- Three rectangles tile the west range, and the dining room spans it. The other two therefore lie in
  bands in front of it or behind it. A band holding one room is spanned by that room. A band holding
  two has them side by side, at one depth.
- So the pantry is one of these:
  - **alone in its band**, which gives it a side as wide as the range;
  - **beside the drawing room in one band**, which gives it a side as deep as the drawing room.
- Either way the pantry has a side of at least 16 ft, against the 14 ft ceiling of its own record.

**If the stair hall is put in the west range as well:**

- The pantry can then have a neighbour to share a band with, so the argument above no longer closes
  the case by itself.
- The library is then alone in the east range, so it spans the full depth. Its own bands, area ≤ 600
  at proportion ≤ 1.6, cap that depth at about 31 ft.
- Doing that case by hand across every arrangement of four rooms costs more than it is worth, so
  the model below was asked instead. With the stair free to stand on either side, it answers
  **INFEASIBLE** with and without the pantry's door to the hyphen, and **OPTIMAL** with only the
  drawing room's fire on the gable.

**No arrangement inside the parti holds both west fires with every room inside its own bands.** For
the parti's own grouping the proof is by hand. Where the stair may cross the passage, the model
settles it. The hyphen does not enter the argument, and the pantry's door to the back hall only
makes it worse.

**A solver model reaches the same answer, and its controls show it can answer yes.**

## IV. Fact by fact: house or instrument, and who owns the remedy

**Hearth: the HOUSE.**

- Under the parti, the two west fires and the pantry cannot co-exist inside the rooms' bands (§III).
- The prover holds them only by drawing the drawing room at the back (V3) or outside its floor (the
  model's prover rows).
- The remedy is a ruling on where the dining room's fire stands. The massing says the gable; the
  dining room's own record says the interior wall. That is item 4 of
  `oq/a-room-record-names-the-wall-its-fire-stands-on-and-nothing-compares-it`, which already
  forbids deciding it by moving the fire to clear a finding. It is amended with this evidence.
- `oq/a-shared-flue-cannot-stand-behind-two-centred-breasts` is overtaken upstream, and is amended
  too. Before it is a question of where one stack stands, it is a question of whether the second
  west fire can stand on the gable at all.

**Stacks: the INSTRUMENT, the search.**

- The prover holds every declared stack on V0, V1, V2, V3, D3 and D4.
- On starting point (A), only the search breaks them: 4 of 4 broken on V1, with the landing at 0%
  of its stair.
- On V4 and V5, the composed arrangement, the prover breaks them too. That is not attributed
  separately, because both records also carry the hearth's refusal.
- The search charges a broken stack as a soft term (`STACK_W`), and `geometry.STACK_HARD` ships
  `False`.
- The default is `oq/the-measurement-that-defaulted-the-stacking-rule-has-inverted`'s, and the pool
  is `oq/a-placement-rule-is-free-at-a-pool-the-server-cannot-afford`'s.

**Hearth and bearing on the search: the INSTRUMENT.**

- The search has no hearth term at all. Its only continuity term is `vertical_score`'s transfer
  charge, which asks whether an upper wall stands within 0.75 ft of any ground wall. The type fact
  asks whether an upper **on-grid** line stands on a ground **on-grid** line.
- With strict stacking switched on in-process, the search trades the stacks for the hearth and the
  bearing lines (§V, D1).
- Nothing owns this, so it is `oq/the-search-is-refused-on-type-facts-nothing-tells-it`. That
  question also says what it means for this package: criterion 1 asks the search to hold facts
  nobody tells it about.

**Tiling on the prover: the INSTRUMENT, in two halves. A third part follows the hearth.**

- **The hyphen.** Its residue is exactly two 0.5 ft strips, **7.0 sf**, on every prover placement
  whose residue was read strip by strip: V3, D4, and the careful record's own placement.
  - `geometry_cp._element_boxes` rounds a fractional element inward and says `_absorb` *"grows the
    room back out"* to its true edges.
  - WP-13.3 skips `_absorb` on any level where a bearing or stack fact is held, *"with the tiling
    fact held there is no leftover to absorb anyway"*. That is false for an element the rounding
    moved.
  - `typefacts.tiling` then reads the strips against the true block, at a 0.05 sf tolerance.
  - D3 lands the hyphen and the dependency on whole feet by sizing alone, and the hyphen's residue
    goes **7.0 → 0.0**. That proves the cause, and it is a control and not a candidate.
- **The dependency.** Its residue is **77–118 sf** on the same four placements (V3, D3, D4 and the
  careful record's), including D3's integer box.
  - Offered back alone, its tiling was proven **INFEASIBLE** on V3, D3 and D4.
  - Its box is `dependency_sizes`' derivation from its own rooms plus a grid allowance. The
    prover's own coverage floor is stated against the rooms' declared area for exactly that reason:
    *"asking whether those rooms fill it is asking the box about itself"*.
  - `TILING = 1.0` asks exactly that, element by element.
  - The search tiles the same element on every variant, by drawing rooms past the prover's size
    tolerance: the breakfast room is 259 sf against 168 declared on V1.
  - Both halves are `oq/the-tiling-fact-cannot-hold-on-a-rounded-or-derived-massing-element`.
- **The main block** follows the hearth:
  - 56 sf on V3 and D3, where all three fires are held;
  - **0.0 sf on D4**, where the dining room's fire is off the gable;
  - 46 sf on the careful record's own placement.

**Bearing on the prover: not attributed, and said so.**

DOORS = [("porch", "passage", 3.5), ("passage", "drawing", 3.5), ("passage", "dining", 3.5),
         ("passage", "library", 3.0), ("passage", "stair", 6.0), ("drawing", "dining", 5.0),
         ("dining", "butlers", 2.8)]
band = {r: json.load(open(os.path.join("rooms", t + ".json")))["dimensions"] for r, t in TYPES.items()}
backhall = json.load(open(os.path.join("rooms", "back-hall.json")))["dimensions"]

m = cp_model.CpModel()
seven = m.NewBoolVar("seven_bays")
W = m.NewIntVar(5 * BAY, 7 * BAY, "W")
m.Add(W == 7 * BAY).OnlyEnforceIf(seven)
m.Add(W == 5 * BAY).OnlyEnforceIf(seven.Not())
H = m.NewIntVar(20, HMAX, "H")
hh = m.NewIntVar(int(backhall["length_ft"][0]) + 2, int(backhall["length_ft"][1]) + 2, "hh")
v = {}
for r in ROOMS:
    b = band[r]
    if MODE == "record":
        amin, amax = int(b["area_sf"][0]), int(b["area_sf"][1])
        smin = int(-(-min(b["width_ft"]) // 1))
    else:
        amin, amax = int(0.88 * b["area_sf"][0]), int(1.22 * b["area_sf"][1]) + 1
        smin = max(1, int(0.6 * min(b["width_ft"])))
    x = m.NewIntVar(0, 7 * BAY, "x" + r); y = m.NewIntVar(0, HMAX, "y" + r)
    w = m.NewIntVar(smin, 7 * BAY, "w" + r); h = m.NewIntVar(smin, HMAX, "h" + r)
    a = m.NewIntVar(amin, amax, "a" + r)
    m.AddMultiplicationEquality(a, [w, h])
    m.Add(x + w <= W); m.Add(y + h <= H)
    if SHAPES or MODE == "record":
        mx = m.NewIntVar(0, 7 * BAY, ""); mn = m.NewIntVar(0, 7 * BAY, "")
        m.AddMaxEquality(mx, [w, h]); m.AddMinEquality(mn, [w, h])
        m.Add(100 * mx <= int(round(b["proportion"][1] * 100)) * mn)
    xe = m.NewIntVar(0, 7 * BAY, ""); ye = m.NewIntVar(0, HMAX, "")
    v[r] = dict(x=x, y=y, w=w, h=h, a=a, xi=m.NewIntervalVar(x, w, xe, ""),
                yi=m.NewIntervalVar(y, h, ye, ""))
m.AddNoOverlap2D([v[r]["xi"] for r in ROOMS], [v[r]["yi"] for r in ROOMS])
area = m.NewIntVar(0, 7 * BAY * HMAX, "area")
m.AddMultiplicationEquality(area, [W, H])
m.Add(sum(v[r]["a"] for r in ROOMS) == area)          # with no-overlap: an exact tiling
if "no-fires" not in ARGS and "one-west-fire" not in ARGS:
    m.Add(v["drawing"]["x"] == 0); m.Add(v["dining"]["x"] == 0)
    m.Add(v["library"]["x"] + v["library"]["w"] == W)
if "one-west-fire" in ARGS:
    m.Add(v["drawing"]["x"] == 0); m.Add(v["library"]["x"] + v["library"]["w"] == W)
m.Add(v["porch"]["y"] == 0)
if "entry" in ARGS:
    Pp, Ps = v["porch"], v["passage"]
    m.Add(Ps["y"] == Pp["h"]); m.Add(Pp["x"] >= Ps["x"]); m.Add(Pp["x"] + Pp["w"] <= Ps["x"] + Ps["w"])
if "front" in ARGS:
    m.Add(v["drawing"]["y"] == 0); m.Add(v["library"]["y"] == 0)
    m.Add(v["passage"]["y"] + v["passage"]["h"] == H)
if "sides" in ARGS:
    for r in ("drawing", "dining", "butlers"):
        m.Add(v[r]["x"] + v[r]["w"] <= v["passage"]["x"])
    for r in (("library",) if "stair-free" in ARGS else ("library", "stair")):
        m.Add(v[r]["x"] >= v["passage"]["x"] + v["passage"]["w"])

def share(a, b, need):
    A, B = v[a], v[b]; k = int(-(-need // 1)); lits = []
    for (p, q) in ((A, B), (B, A)):
        for vert in (True, False):
            lit = m.NewBoolVar("")
            if vert:
                m.Add(p["x"] + p["w"] == q["x"]).OnlyEnforceIf(lit)
                s0, l0, s1, l1 = p["y"], p["h"], q["y"], q["h"]
            else:
                m.Add(p["y"] + p["h"] == q["y"]).OnlyEnforceIf(lit)
                s0, l0, s1, l1 = p["x"], p["w"], q["x"], q["w"]
            lo = m.NewIntVar(0, 7 * BAY + HMAX, ""); hi = m.NewIntVar(0, 2 * (7 * BAY + HMAX), "")
            e0 = m.NewIntVar(0, 2 * (7 * BAY + HMAX), ""); e1 = m.NewIntVar(0, 2 * (7 * BAY + HMAX), "")
            m.Add(e0 == s0 + l0); m.Add(e1 == s1 + l1)
            m.AddMaxEquality(lo, [s0, s1]); m.AddMinEquality(hi, [e0, e1])
            m.Add(hi - lo >= k).OnlyEnforceIf(lit); lits.append(lit)
    m.AddBoolOr(lits)

for a_, b_, wd in DOORS:
    share(a_, b_, wd + 2 * JAMB)
if PAIR_PANTRY:                                     # pantry on the west face, in the hyphen's band
    P = v["butlers"]; m.Add(P["x"] == 0)
    need_half = int(round(2 * (2.8 + 2 * JAMB)))    # 3.5 ft, in half-feet
    lo = m.NewIntVar(-4 * HMAX, 4 * HMAX, ""); hi = m.NewIntVar(-4 * HMAX, 4 * HMAX, "")
    p0 = m.NewIntVar(0, 4 * HMAX, ""); p1 = m.NewIntVar(0, 4 * HMAX, "")
    b0 = m.NewIntVar(-4 * HMAX, 4 * HMAX, ""); b1 = m.NewIntVar(-4 * HMAX, 4 * HMAX, "")
    m.Add(p0 == 2 * P["y"]); m.Add(p1 == 2 * (P["y"] + P["h"]))
    if "free-band" in ARGS:
        c = m.NewIntVar(0, 2 * HMAX, ""); m.Add(b0 == c); m.Add(b1 == c + 2 * hh); m.Add(b1 <= 2 * H)
    else:
        m.Add(b0 == H - hh); m.Add(b1 == H + hh)     # centred, as geometry.blocks_for centres it
    m.AddMaxEquality(lo, [p0, b0]); m.AddMinEquality(hi, [p1, b1])
    m.Add(hi - lo >= need_half)
solver = cp_model.CpSolver()
solver.parameters.max_time_in_seconds = float(os.environ.get("T", "120"))
solver.parameters.num_workers = 4
print(solver.StatusName(solver.Solve(m)))

## Addendum, 26 September 2026: item 4 is ruled (WP-14.33)

Item 4 of `oq/a-room-record-names-the-wall-its-fire-stands-on-and-nothing-compares-it` asks whose
statement of the dining fire's wall outranks whose. It was the question this report left for a
ruling, and Lucas ruled it on 26 September: **the style's massing and kit govern.**

- The Tidewater kit's `hearth_position` says *"Hearths are at the gable ends, on the outside walls
  of the end rooms"*.
- So the fire stands on the west gable, which is where both shipped records already put it.

The verdict above therefore stands on a ruled reading rather than an open one: the worked house is
refused on its own facts. The refusal stays the example, and WP-14.28 stays NOT BUILT. No record
was edited. The four-over-four that §III's model found, with only the drawing room's fire on the
gable, puts the dining fire where the style says it does not stand. It is therefore not a house of
this style, and nothing is authored from it.
