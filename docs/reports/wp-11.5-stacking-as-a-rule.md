# WP-11.5 — Stacking as a rule, and the pool that could not serve it

*Phase 11, package 5 of 11. 4 September 2026. From
`docs/reports/tidewater-layout-diagnosis-2026-09-04.md` findings F1, F2, F6 and E3.*

## Two findings before any code, and both changed what the package could be

**1. THE SELECTOR THIS PACKAGE NAMES MATCHES NOTHING.** It asks for the rule to apply to *"any
massing whose `structural_logic` states a stacked plan"*. All 40 massings carry a
`structural_logic` string and **not one says the upper floor repeats the lower**. They state
spans, bearing walls, ventilation, roof framing and materials. `four-over-four`'s reads *"Two
rooms deep requires an interior bearing wall, which the stair hall supplies. Paired end chimneys
serve four fireplaces per floor."* The corpus's one authored sentence to that effect is on a
PARTI — `centre-passage-double-pile`'s description, *"two rooms either side, repeated above"* —
and one instance is not a population a prose reader may be built on, which is
`oq/a-room-records-prose-states-a-floor-its-own-band-does-not`'s 2-of-5 lesson.

**So no massing-level selector exists and inventing one is authoring.** The authored,
machine-readable statement is already there and is per room: `stacks_over`. A room whose author
wrote that claim is the corpus saying this room stacks, and honouring it needs no selector.

**2. A HARD STACKING PIN IN `geometry_cp.py` WAS ALREADY BUILT, MEASURED AND REFUSED (OQ 95).**
Its own comment records that a hard version *"downgraded an authored kitchen wall to satisfy an
inferred stack"*, because the downgrade loop reads
`[key for _t, k, key in core if k == "wall" and key]` and a non-wall pin can never enter it. So a
stack pin outranks every authored exterior wall in the corpus, and a stack-only conflict core
makes the loop `break` and report infeasible. **The blocker is the loop, not the pin**, and that
is recorded here rather than rediscovered a third time.

## What was built

**ONE SPELLING OF THE DECLARED-STACK TEST.** `geometry.declared_stack_breaks` is the single
reader of a `stacks_over` claim against a placement, on `plan_check`'s own rule — strict positive
rectangle intersection — so the search, the charge and the critic cannot convict and acquit the
same house. It was spelled once inside `vertical_score`; this package needed the same test at
candidate-selection time, and a second transcription is the `openings.required_wall_ft` error in a
new place. `declared_stack_census` beside it reports `{claimed, judged, broken, satisfied,
unjudged}` — a claim whose target is not on the level below is **unjudged**, never satisfied.

**TWO INCUMBENTS, NOT A REJECTION.** The search keeps the best candidate overall and the best that
breaks no declared claim, and prefers the second when it exists. It is deliberately **not** a
`continue`: rejecting a breaking candidate outright empties the pool on any plan whose claims the
slicing tree cannot satisfy, and an empty pool is a placement failure where the corpus wants a
stated compromise.

**AND WHEN NO STRICT CANDIDATE EXISTS THE RECORD SAYS SO.** `geometry_report.stacking` carries
`{claimed, rule, cost_points, note}` and names the count it could not satisfy. A hard rule that
quietly becomes a charge is worse than the charge, because a reader then believes the claims were
honoured.

**A NAMED SWITCH, `geometry.STACK_HARD`,** because `_SOLVE_CACHE` is keyed on call arguments and a
sweep over a module constant silently measures the first value — WP-7.4's trap, and a rule nobody
can sweep is a rule nobody can refuse.

## What was measured

**The headline: the cost of the rule is a property of the pool, not of the rule.** One seed on
`spec-builder-colonial`, four pool sizes:

| candidates | placement score | what the rule cost |
|---|---|---|
| 250 (shipped) | 651.5 | **70.5 points** |
| 500 | 605.9 | 24.9 |
| 1,000 | 543.1 | **nothing** |
| 2,000 | 508.9 | **nothing** |

At 1,000 candidates the unconstrained winner already satisfies every claim, so the rule is free.
That is WP-7.4's own finding in a new place — *what makes a term look wrong can be the size of the
pool it re-ranks* — arriving this time on a rule rather than a weight.

**Declared claims, on and off, four seeds:**

| plan | pool | rule off | rule on |
|---|---|---|---|
| spec-builder | 250 | 4 broken | **1** |
| spec-builder | 1,000 | 4 broken | **0** |
| tidewater | 250 | 8 broken | **2** |
| tidewater | 1,000 | 3 broken | **0** |

**The critic's key does not clearly reward it, and that is stated rather than smoothed.** At
1,000 candidates on the spec plan the fatal floor improves by one and the minor floor worsens by
six; on the Tidewater plan the fatal count is unchanged and the serious ceiling worsens by three.
A finding count weights a waste stack landing on nothing the same as a label, and the corpus's own
note on that charge calls a broken claim *"a defect that survives the life of the building"*.

**THE REAL BENEFIT IS IN THE ROOM SIZES AND THE KEY DOES NOT SEE IT.** Measured on
`spec-builder-colonial`, rule off against on:

| | rule off | rule on |
|---|---|---|
| Stair Hall | 36 sf against a 76 sf floor — **53% short** | 64 sf — **16% short** |
| Mud Room | 28 vs 34 — 16% short | 32 — 6% short |
| Study | inside its band | 64 vs 76 — 16% short |
| **total shortfall** | **46 sf over 2 rooms** | **30 sf over 3 rooms** |

Forcing the bath over the laundry forced a better subdivision: the stair hall gains 78% of its own
area and the worst squeeze on the plan more than halves. The minor-finding count went UP while the
house got materially better, which is exactly why this report leads with the rooms.

**The six numbers, eight seeds, the search engine, at the shipped pool of 250:**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| tidewater key | `4–9 / 62–68 / 83–88` | `4–9 / 60–68 / 81–98` |
| tidewater transfers | 5–11 | **4–8** |
| spec key | `6–21 / 89–97 / 90–99` | `13–21 / 90–99 / 92–99` |
| spec transfers | 8–14 | 2–22 |

## The number that decided the default, and it is structural

**On `spec-builder-colonial` the strict candidate introduces two over-capacity clear spans where
there were NONE, the worst 40.0 ft against a 20 ft capacity.** That is a structural defect on a
shipped reference plan, traded for a waste stack. On the Tidewater plan the same rule trades the
other way: over-capacity spans 2 → 4, but the **worst falls 53.9 ft → 36.0 ft**.

| plan | rule off | rule on |
|---|---|---|
| spec-builder | **0 spans** | 2 spans, worst **40.0 ft** |
| tidewater | 2 spans, worst 53.9 ft | 4 spans, worst **36.0 ft** |

Two shipped plans, opposite structural verdicts, at a pool size at which the rule is not free and
which the infrastructure audit already calls the server's bound. **So `STACK_HARD` ships `False`.**
The mechanism is built, guarded by a suite that drives the flag rather than reading the corpus's
own state, and measured both ways. Flipping it is one line and it is a ruling, not a default —
better rooms against worse structure on one plan, and the reverse on the other, is precisely the
kind of trade this project puts to a person.

**This is WP-11.3's precedent with one difference.** That package built a score term, measured it,
and DELETED it, because *"an inert wrong number is an instruction to the next reader"*. A weight
left at zero is an inert wrong number; a named switch at `False` carrying the measurement that set
it, an open question naming the condition to flip it, and a test suite that exercises the other
state is not — it is the shape OQ 51's `delivery: opt-in` flip already uses. The distinction is
that the switch has a stated reason and a reader who flips it gets the numbers, not a silence.

## The one blind guard, and it was the disclosure

Six mutations, five caught on the first pass. The blind one was **the no-strict-candidate
disclosure** — delete it and the suite stayed green, because both shipped plans always find a
strict candidate at 250 candidates and that branch never ran. WP-8.11's lesson again: **a driven
fixture must not accidentally be the shipped corpus.** A pool of ONE forces it — one candidate
cannot satisfy five claims — and the guard now asserts the note names the pool it searched, the
count it could not satisfy and how many the winner breaks, with a complement proving the branch
does not fire at the shipped pool. Six of six on the re-run.

## What was deliberately not done

**THE CP HARD PIN IS NOT BUILT, and OQ 95's refusal stands until the downgrade loop is taught a
second pin kind.** The loop relaxes only `kind == "wall"`, so a hard stack would outrank every
authored exterior wall and a stack-only core would report infeasible rather than relax. Teaching
it needs an ORDERING between an authored `stacks_over` and an authored `exterior_walls`, and that
is a ruling: the corpus already says exterior walls *"are aspirations, not rectangle edges"*,
which argues the stack should win, while OQ 95's measurement of that exact arrangement found an
authored kitchen wall being downgraded to serve a stack. Both engines still charge; only the
search now rules.

**THE TRANSFER-BEAM HALF IS NOT BUILT.** The package also asks for *"every upper partition on a
bearing line below"* as a hard rule. That is 10 transfer beams to zero on the Tidewater plan and
45 across the corpus's five two-level records, and it is a far stronger constraint than declared
stacking. It is not attempted on one measurement's evidence.

**THE POOL IS NOT RAISED.** The measurement says 1,000 candidates makes the rule free, and the
infrastructure audit says one evaluate already consumes ~85% of the server's capacity at 250. Four
times the search on the bench's hot path is a cost decision and not this package's to take.
