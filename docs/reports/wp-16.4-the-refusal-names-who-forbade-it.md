# WP-16.4 — The refusal: what the resolved kit forbids is not drawn, and the sheet names who forbade it

*Phase 16, 30 September 2026. R3, stage 3: refuse what the resolved kit forbids, naming the node
that wrote the ban. A3: a forbidden row's own date range is read against the house's date.*

## I. What was asked

Lucas's ruling R3 of 29 September 2026, asked as a direct question
(`oq/an-inherited-ban-decides-what-the-elevation-may-draw`):

> Decision 4 stands. First, each inherited ban that strips a style wrongly is corrected in that
> style's own kit, case by case. Then the elevation refuses what remains and names the node each
> ban comes from.

WP-16.2 did the first half. This package does the second. Three of Lucas's answers of
30 September 2026 shape it:

- **A3.** A forbidden row's own `applies_when.date_range` is read against the house's date. A dated
  house outside the range draws the feature. An undated house keeps the refusal, and the sheet says
  the date is unstated.
- **A4.** Where the doorcase is refused, the door gets the plain casing, and the refusal names the
  style.
- **A7.** new-urbanist-traditional's own cornice record forbids the modillion cornice, and the
  elevation refuses the drawn modillions and names the style.

WP-16.2 also handed over three things:
- cape-cod-colonial, saltbox-colonial and renaissance-revival-american drew sidelights their own
  kits forbid, because the entrance read whole-slot bans only;
- good-05's `sidelights-as-storefront-glass` cleared on a published 0.0, on a front with no
  sidelights;
- new-urbanist-traditional's modillions.

## II. What was built

**1. One reading of a ban, at a date** (`build/resolve_kit.py`).
- `ban(rec, words, date)` returns None, or the ban: the nodes that WROTE it, whether it is the
  whole slot or rows, the rows the words matched, the date ranges those rows carry, the date read,
  and `date_unstated`.
- A slot bound `forbidden` is forbidden at every date, by its binder. A feature is forbidden where
  every row its words match, among the rows that apply at the house's date, is forbidden.
- A row's date is read by `in_period`, the reader the window surrounds already use. An undated
  house reads every row, so it keeps a dated ban, and `date_unstated` says so.
- `forbidden_by` is the ban's writers, as WP-16.2 left it. `ban_words` is the one sentence every
  surface prints: *"forbidden by georgian-colonial-american's kit for houses of 1700–1780, and this
  house is dated 1765"*, or *"…; this record states no date, so the ban is kept"*.

**2. The entrance** (`build/doorcase.py`, `build/openings.py`, `build/elevation.py`).
- `doorcase.ENTRANCE_BANS` names what the entrance may not draw, in the kit's own ids: the whole
  `transom_sidelight` slot, the sidelights row, the pilaster doorcase
  (`door_surround`'s `pilasters-and-entablature`), and the pilaster. `doorcase.refusals(slots,
  date)` reads them. The placer and the elevation both read it, so the run the placer reserves and
  the doorcase the elevation draws cannot disagree about the sidelights.
- `doorcase.composition` drops the sidelights under either ban. It keeps the transom unless the
  whole slot is banned: a ban on the sidelights row says nothing about the transom.
- `openings._reserve_doorcase` reads the plan's own date. The run it reserves has no sidelights
  where they are refused, and the record says who refused them (`sidelights_refused`) and whose
  doorcase the run was reserved for (`composed_for`).
- A refused pilaster doorcase leaves the door the doorcase's own casing, with its pilasters and its
  entablature taken off. Every figure of the order is withheld: pilaster, column, entablature and
  surround height. §IV.1 says why this is not the kit's `casing` slot.

**3. The envelope** (`build/elevation.py`).
- `ENVELOPE_BANS`: the water table, the belt course, the frieze, the cornice, and the order's
  modillions inside the cornice.
- Each band is refused on its own, because each is its own slot.
  - A refused frieze leaves the cornice springing from the wall head.
  - A refused cornice leaves the frieze to carry the roof.
  - A refused water table leaves the coursing starting at grade.
- The roof stands on what is drawn: the true eave adds only the bands drawn.
- A refused modillion band leaves `eave_cornice` with Gibbs's other members. They are read to the
  same envelope height, which is that function's own rule for the whole order: facade-classical
  fixes a domestic cornice's height, and the order supplies its shape. So the refusal changes the
  cornice's shape and not its height, and the roof does not move. On bad-03 the cornice keeps its
  20.094 in with 6 members in place of 8.
- A refused band has no figures: absent, not zero.

**4. Every surface says it.** The sheet writes *"<FEATURE> NOT DRAWN — FORBIDDEN BY <WRITER>'S
KIT …"* for each refusal. The DXF writes the same lines and carries the bans as `TDL::kit-refused`
XDATA on the door leaf. They go on the leaf, in one record, because an entity holds one XDATA record
per application and the casing already carries the placer's own refusal. The scene models no
refused sidelight or order and names each in `not_modelled`. The elevation record carries
`refused_by_the_kit`.

**5. The stack** (`build/threshold.py`). `hearth_pass` refuses an exterior stack the resolved kit
forbids, where the stack is placed, so the plan, the roof, the section, the elevation and the scene
read one refusal. It is reached by no shipped placement, and a test drives it (§IV.7).

**6. The sidelight fault asks only where sidelights are drawn.**
- The fault is `sidelights-as-storefront-glass`. Its primary, its three secondaries and its three
  exceptions' bounds tests each carry `applies_when count_of_sidelights_drawn_at_the_entrance ≥ 1`.
- The count has three states:
  - 0, where the composition has no pair (the kit refuses it, or the width cap omits it);
  - the drawn spans, where a face draws the entrance door;
  - unmeasured, where the composition has a pair and no face draws the entrance door (§IV.4).
- `sidelight_width_in` is published only where a pair is drawn.

**7. The disclosure.**
- `elevation.READ_FROM_PACKS` and `REFUSED_HERE` are module constants now, so the test that
  partitions the corpus reads the generator's own lists; it used to read a copy.
- `REFUSED_HERE` gains the water table, the belt course, the frieze and the cornice.
- It does not gain `door_surround`: a whole-slot ban on it leaves the door a casing whose width is
  that slot's own pack rule, so the slot is still read, and the record says so (§V).

**8. The census** (`tests/svg_census.py`).
- **V2** reads each ban at the plan's date, and holds the sheet's refusal lines in both directions:
  every refused feature is said, and nothing is said that is not refused.
- **V20, V22, V24 and V25** read the plain casing and the refusal lines.
- **The style sweep** draws each style on the Tidewater house placed with the doorcase run its own
  composition asks for (§IV.3).

## IV. What was found

1. **The plan named the wrong figure for the plain casing.**
   - The plan said the refused doorcase leaves *"the `casing` slot's plain casing"*. On all four
     Italianate styles that slot resolves to georgian-colonial-american's `width_in` of 4.5 in. That
     is trim-classical's `casing_federal`, an INTERIOR Federal architrave, beside a parameter that
     names the interior door. minimal-traditional's states no width at all.
   - Round good-05's 45.9 in leaf, 4.5 in is 0.098 of the leaf against "The Door That Outgrew Its
     Frame"'s 0.14 floor. The house would be convicted on a casing written for another room and
     another period.
   - A4's words are "the plain casing", and the plain casing is the doorcase without its order: the
     composition's own `door_surround` width. The first version said the casing slot's figure on
     the sheet and in the scene while drawing the doorcase's own casing. Both now say what they draw.
2. **A reassociated addition moved a drawing.**
   - The refusal wrote the true eave as `eave × 12 + (frieze + cornice)`. That is the old
     `eave × 12 + frieze + cornice` to within the last bit.
   - On good-03, where the kit refuses nothing, that bit carried three of the DXF's cornice lines
     across a rounding tie at the thousandth. The render digest caught it: the DXF moved and the
     SVG did not.
   - The sum is left to right again, and a test holds it exactly. It asserts equality, not
     approximation, because the property is the order of an addition.
3. **A census check lost its population to the fix it guards.**
   - The sweep draws every style on the Tidewater placement. The placer now reserves no run for
     sidelights the Tidewater kit forbids at 1765, so a swept style whose composition keeps a pair
     drew it over wall the placement never kept.
   - 29 of V24's 43 rows went could-not-evaluate, and the census diff could not see it: that
     compares disagreements, and a could-not-evaluate row is not one. The generated doc's table
     did show it.
   - The sweep now places the house twice: at its own 1765, and at 1790 with the date stamped back,
     where the run holds the pair. The two differ only in what the openings pass seats, and the
     sweep asserts that. Each style is drawn on the placement its own composition asks for.
   - V24 is back at 42 agree and 1 could-not-evaluate (the spec Colonial's unstated bay, as before).
     The mutation that reverts the re-cut turns the doc-currency test red.
4. **The sidelight measurement had three dishonest values, not one.**
   - WP-16.2 named the published 0.0 where the width cap omits the pair (good-05, and good-07 as
     well).
   - Measuring the count found the second. The rule's width was published for sidelights nobody
     draws: 11.482 or 12.758 in on bad-01, bad-05 and good-02, whose entrance door no face draws
     on the search engine. The fault cleared on all three.
   - The garage made a third, and it was found only after the count was built. On bad-02, bad-03
     and bad-07 the rect marked the entrance is the garage door, the widest door on the front
     (WP-14.3), and it carries no composition. The count's first version counted that rect's
     sidelights, so it read 0 and called the fault not applicable: a verdict about the house on
     the strength of which door the drawing picked. The count reads only a door that is not a
     garage's now, and those three are could-not-evaluate. A driven test holds bad-02.
   - A count of 0 where the composition keeps a pair would have called the fault not applicable
     on all six. So the count is unmeasured there, and the fault is could-not-evaluate. It is 0
     only where no pair is composed.
5. **Two comments claimed figures the code did not have.**
   - The doorcase's comment said four styles drew a pilaster doorcase their kits forbid. Five did:
     the four Italianate styles by the row, and minimal-traditional by its whole `door_surround`
     slot, which the elevation had never read.
   - The disclosure's comment said five features were newly refused, the doorcase among them. It is
     four; §II.7 says why `door_surround` is not.
