# WP-11.17 — the entrance front, stated rather than charged

*15 September 2026. Closes `oq/the-search-loses-the-entrance-front-on-a-multi-element-plan`,
raised by WP-11.16 one day earlier. Status: **re-derive these figures, do not quote them** —
every number here is `engine="heuristic"` unless it says otherwise, and the CP figures move with
the hint.*

## I. What was asked for

*"fix the search losing the entrance front."* Two rulings were taken before any code:

- **State the entrance front**, rather than re-rank candidates — give the anchor mechanism the
  entrance face so the porch is on the front BY CONSTRUCTION, leaving WP-11.8's band-first
  acceptance key untouched.
- **Corpus-wide, measure it all** — apply the rule uniformly and re-derive every ratchet and
  digest per plan, reporting which plans moved and why, including any that get worse.

## II. Both of the open question's stated hypotheses were wrong

The question named two mechanisms "worth measuring before anything is changed". Measuring them
was the whole value of the entry, and neither survived:

1. *"`entrance_score` gets no per-element `bounds`"* — true, and irrelevant here. The porch
   stands in the MAIN block, whose element box **is** `(0, 0, 45, 37.24)`, so `bounds=` would
   change nothing for it; and `_touches_wall(rect, "S", W, H)` is `y <= tol`, which does not read
   `H` at all. The missing `bounds=` is real and is named in §VII under its actual symptom.
2. *"a squarer pile makes the front and rear interchangeable"* — the effect is **categorical**,
   not marginal. No charge weighing two faces could produce 0 of 250.

## III. What it was: two independent causes

### Cause 1 — the pool held no candidate with the porch on the front

`hyphen_anchors` returns `{'main': {'W': ['butlers']}}` once the tagging gives the record a
second element, and `flank_slice` lays the butler's pantry as a full-depth strip at
`(0, 0, 4.95, 37.24)`, handing the other six main-block rooms a narrowed `40.05 × 37.24`:

| | porch on S | min porch y | winner y |
|---|---|---|---|
| tagged, as shipped | **0 / 250** | 21.32 | 31.51 |
| tagged, `hyphen_anchors → {}` | 60 / 250 | 0.00 | 27 |
| tags stripped | 159 / 250 | 0.00 | 0 |

The pantry occupies that strip in **250 of 250** candidates, so `entrance_score` charges its 100
points on every one and has nothing to prefer.

### Cause 2 — and the charge is never read anyway

`_key` is `(viol, tot)` and `entrance_score` lives in `tot`. With cause 1 suppressed so a mixed
pool exists: front-porch candidates bottom out at **viol 3**, back-porch ones at **viol 1**, and
the ground score preferred the front by **92.3 points** that the first key never let it speak.
**One extra out-of-band room outranks a fatal-tier entrance violation, unconditionally.** It is
not specific to multi-element plans; on a one-rectangle plan it merely happens not to bite.

This contradicts `entrance_score`'s own docstring — *"weighted heavily enough … that no
candidate with the porch off the entrance wall can win against one that has it right"*.
WP-11.8's band-first ruling silently won, and **cause 2 is not fixed here**: stating the front
means every candidate has the porch on it, so the key is never asked.

## IV. The fix

`build/geometry.py` grows two things and the candidate loop grows four lines.

**`entrance_anchors(plan, blocks, level)`** names the one ground room that must stand on the
entrance front, in `hyphen_anchors`' own return shape so the caller's loop reads one thing. Its
selector is narrow and refuses rather than guessing, and every clause was measured against the
sixteen shipped records:

- `entrance_faces` must be stated — **7 of 16** plans state one.
- `function_class: threshold` **and** declaring one of the entrance walls in its own
  `exterior_walls`. "Every threshold room" is not the rule:
  `good-04-rambling-porch-farmhouse` is a south-facing house whose `north-porch` declares N and
  W, and anchoring it would have pulled a back porch onto the front.
- Where more than one qualifies, **the one that reaches the outside wins**, and if that does not
  decide it the answer is nothing. `spec-builder-colonial`'s porch declares `to: exterior` and
  its foyer does not; `good-02-portico-library-house`'s portico and foyer neither declare it nor
  can be separated by the door graph (they door into each other), so **that plan gets no anchor**.
  Picking the portico by its TYPE would be inventing the answer the record declines to give.
- The face is the intersection of the entrance walls with the room's own declared walls, and
  must be unique. It is, on all six that survive.

**`_partial_flank`** is `flank_slice`'s partial branch. The existing strip spans the WHOLE face,
which is right for a hyphen crossing and wrong for an object on a front: measured, a full-face
strip draws the Tidewater's declared 6 × 12 = 72 sf porch as **45 × 4.95 = 222 sf**, a veranda
across the whole house, and `spec-builder-colonial`'s 24 sf stoop at about 275. The partial
branch makes three guillotine cuts — along the face at `off`, along it again at `off + L`, then
across the middle slab at the anchor's depth — and sizes the anchor's rectangle from **its own
declared area** (`d = acc / L`, the clamps re-deriving `L` so the area survives and the aspect
gives). `partition` grew a stated `frac` for this caller, because `slice_rect` tiles whatever
rectangle it is handed with whatever rooms it is handed and a group whose area does not match its
rectangle is not a smaller room, it is a **stretched** one.

`off` is an rng draw over three positions — low end, centre, high end. A bias rule was written
first and measured: `bias(anchor, along_axis)` is exactly **0 on all six** anchored plans,
because a threshold room on a front declares the entrance wall and then either nothing else or a
symmetric pair (`["S","E","W"]` on both shipped records). An instrument that cannot fire is worse
than none, so the search draws and the score picks.

## VII. What gets worse, and what is named rather than fixed

### The two anchors compete, and three ways of having both were built, measured and reverted

The tagged main block carries both an S entrance anchor (the porch) and a W hyphen anchor (the
butler's pantry, for its door into the back hall). The loop takes the first anchor that lays and
the entrance is laid first, because the entrance is the fatal tier — `entrance_score` charges 100
where `adjacency_score` charges a door, and a house drawn back to front is wrong in a way a door
that cannot be drawn is not. **The price is real and it is doors**, measured on that record:

| | doors the placement cannot draw |
|---|---|
| hyphen anchor alone (the house drawn back to front) | **12** |
| neither anchor | 18 |
| entrance anchor alone (what ships) | **23** |

and corpus-wide 223 → 235, essentially all of it on this one plan. `plan_check` reads one new
fatal out of it — `unreachable: butlers` — against serious 60 → 55 and minor 111 → 105.

It did not have to be: `_partial_flank`'s `P` and `R` slabs stand on the element's own W and E
faces, so a slab receiving the pantry could take the ordinary full-face `flank_slice`. None of
the three routes to that was worth shipping:

- **Letting `partition` decide is inert.** The pantry declares `exterior_walls: ["N"]`, its
  x-bias is exactly 0, and the W slab held it in **0 of 841 draws**.
- **Adding the anchor's stated direction to that bias** — defensible, since which element stands
  beyond which face is as much a fact of this record as a declared exposure — did not move it
  either (`partition` ranks door connectivity above bias and the pantry's other door is to the
  dining room) and cost **6 serious findings**.
- **Putting it in that slab outright**, held out of the split with the slab's target area reduced
  by its own, works and is worse: a pantry laid as a full-face strip down a 16.5 ft slab is a
  worse room than one the guillotine places, and it took the score 672.3 → 753.5, serious 55 → 62
  and doors 23 → 24.

**CORRECTION, WP-11.18 (15 September 2026): the third of those three was measured on a
confounded run, and the conclusion drawn from it does not follow.** Two things were wrong with
it. The nested lay used the **full-face** `flank_slice` rather than `_partial_flank`, so the
pantry became a 184 sf strip inside a 16.5 ft slab — the very shape this package removed one
level up. And the winning candidate had `off = 0`, where **no `P` slab exists at all**, so the
forcing never applied to the placement the number was read off; the attempt considered only `P`
and `R` as hosts and never `B`, which is the rectangle that stands on the low face exactly when
`off` is 0. The figures are not withdrawn — they are what that code did. What is withdrawn is the
reading that the two statements cannot both be kept. **They can**: WP-11.18 keeps both, draws the
pantry at its declared 7 × 12 on the hyphen's own band, places both its doors, and clears
`unreachable: butlers`. Its cost is four draws in 250 of the entrance census, not a door. See
`docs/reports/wp-11.18-the-anchor-that-kept-a-face.md`.

All three were reverted rather than shipped inert or harmful. `partition`'s `frac` survives
because the partial strip needs it; the `pull` parameter and the `also` coupling do not exist.

### The Tidewater spine becomes COULD NOT EVALUATE, and the cause is that a porch is drawn inside the block

The search's axis census read `spine: off-centre` before and reads **`could-not-evaluate`** now.
The passage is drawn `(16.5, 6.0, 12, 23.87)` — directly behind its own porch, which is the
sequence the record describes — and therefore no longer spans S to N, so the axis reader has no
spine to measure. Under the proof it is still `on-centre`, because CP places the passage at
`(21, 0, 10, 37)` and the porch beside it at `(31, 0, 14, 6)`.

**This is a modelling artefact and it is worth naming as one.** The placer tiles the footprint
exactly, so a porch on the S wall takes that stretch of wall from whatever stands behind it — in
a real Georgian house the portico projects in front of the block and the passage runs from its
own front door to its own back one. It is the same question `oq/an-at-grade-appendage-is-drawn-and-not-judged`
asks about the terrace, one room over. `could-not-evaluate` is an honest state and not a pass,
and the guard says so.

### `principal_and_service_score` cannot see a wing room, so service on the front goes unpunished

A dependency room at x = −34 satisfies `_touches_wall(…, "W", …)` (`x <= 0.6`) and can satisfy N,
S or E for no placement whatever. So each service room is charged a flat 2.0 for "not at the
rear" and the 3.0 for *service ON the front* **can never fire**. This is hypothesis 1's missing
`bounds=` under its real symptom, and it is why `test_service_rooms_land_toward_the_rear` stays
split by engine while the entry-porch test folds back. `geometry_cp._score` states the asymmetry
deliberately (*"they measure the main block"*), so changing it is its own package.

### The upper floor moves because the ground floor did

`spec-builder-colonial`'s +7 serious is almost entirely upper-floor churn — `bed2`, `bed3` and
their closets — because `solve_heuristic(level_aware=True)` slices the upper level against the
ground layout. Its porch was ALREADY on the entrance front; the anchor fixes nothing there and
only re-places. The same is true of `good-01` and `good-07`. **Two plans had the defect and were
fixed** (`tidewater-georgian-careful`, `good-03`); one improved (`good-04`, `entrance_score`
300 → 100); three were re-placed with the entrance already right.

### `entrance_score`'s second clause is now unsatisfiable where the anchor serves

It charges 40 for the circulation room a threshold room opens into unless that room ALSO touches
the entrance wall — and a passage standing directly behind its own porch cannot. On `good-07`
the figure went **0 → 40** for exactly this reason, which is cause 2 acting on the clause the
anchor does not state. Whether the clause should read *"reaches the entrance front"* or *"is
entered from the entrance front"* is a question about the rule, not about this placement.

## VIII. What the proof does

CP is untouched — the model does not move and `tests/test_cp_elements.py`'s four proto hashes are
unchanged — but `geometry_cp._hint_heuristic` calls `solve_heuristic`, so the HINT moves. The
tagged record still returns **OPTIMAL**, objective 308.1 → **308.3**, 4 wall pins and 1 shape pin
downgraded, porch at `(31, 0)`, spine on-centre. A different tie, not a different answer.


## IX. The guards: thirty-three repaired, and four of them had lost their subject

`pytest tests/` came back **33 failed, 2121 passed**. Every one was a figure this package moved or
a fixture whose case it dissolved; none was a defect in the change. The four worth knowing:

- **`_hyphen_fixture()` inherited `entrance_faces` from the shipped record**, so the entrance
  anchor pre-empted the hyphen one and four tests about `hyphen_anchors` stopped exercising it
  (`butlers` at x 28.5 instead of 0.00). WP-8.11's rule met again: a driven fixture must not
  inherit whatever the shipped record happens to declare. It drops the field now.
- **`test_facade.py` named the room it drives**, and the room it named — `butlers` — is no longer
  on the front, so the drive changed nothing and `none_` came back empty. Naming the driven room
  is the same mistake as reading the shipped placement, one step later; both facade specimens are
  chosen FROM the reading now.
- **`test_stacking.py`'s landing-over-the-well fixture ran out of room**: the stair well is
  11.68 × 7.2 inside a hall of 12.0 × 7.37, clear strips of 0.32/0.00/0.00/0.17 ft on the four
  sides. The state is driven (a 3 ft strip stated by hand) rather than the assertion deleted.
- **`test_composition.py`'s dining-room clause was a proxy.** It asserted *rear-of-centre* for a
  room declaring **N and W**; the room now takes its W wall instead of its N one, one declared
  wall either way. Corpus-wide, declared `exterior_walls` not reached is **67 of 170 before and
  after** — the anchor costs none of them. The assertion reads the property now.

Two guards did exactly what they were written to do and said so in their own messages:
`test_threshold_pass.py`'s cost test asked to be re-pointed rather than deleted if the search ever
gave the flight to the porch, and `test_axis.py`'s asked for the open question to be closed. Both
were followed.

Also re-pinned, each with the per-plan derivation beside it: three corpus digests, the openings
digest, the furniture ratchets (66/79 → 69/74), the band ratchet (28 → 31), the span figures
(27 → 25 and a charge of 130.5 → 119.7), the stacking floor (3 → 2 kept), the relaxation count
(5 → 6, in all three files that hold it) and two plan-validator counts.

**`test_stacking_rule.py` carries one reversal rather than a re-pin.** With `STACK_HARD` on — not
the shipped setting — the strict candidate used to buy the spec Colonial better rooms and now
costs it 10 sf. The `<` assertion is neither kept nor inverted: keeping it asserts a benefit the
corpus can no longer demonstrate, and inverting it would make a green suite evidence for the
reversal. What is asserted is the magnitude, so the day the cost grows past a room it fails.
