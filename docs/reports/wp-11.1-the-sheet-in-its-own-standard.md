# WP-11.1 — the sheet in its own standard: the wall becomes a body

*4 September 2026. Phase 11, package 1. Branch `claude/georgian-tidewater-plan-analysis-qbwmqs`.*

## Why

Lucas rendered `plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json`, set it beside four exemplar plans — one
pen-and-ink plan and three tan-poché presentation plans — and asked for a diagnosis before being
told what was wrong. The diagnosis is in the plan file for this branch; twenty-eight items, split
into what the placement does (ten) and what the drawing does (eighteen).

The finding that organises this package: **the instrument already owns a written graphic standard
that describes the exemplars almost exactly, and the plan sheet did not obey it.**
`workbench/app/src/theme/tokens.css` carries Graphic Standard No. 1 verbatim — a cream ground, a
warm graphite ink ladder, five named line weights, poché as *"a body: coal skin, salmon flesh"*,
one serif voice in letterspaced roman capitals, and the sentence that decides most arguments here:

> *Duties are exclusive: green never means anything but growth, blue never anything but water,
> salmon never anything but cut construction. Color names things; it never outlines them.*

`build/render_plan.py` was written against a DARK instrument palette and
`workbench/server/svg_theme.py` translated the finished SVG into the standard by literal hex
substitution on the way to the browser. So the sheet had the standard's COLOURS and none of its
GRAMMAR: no poché, no weight ladder, seven room washes doing the work walls should do, and three
typefaces where the standard has one.

The second finding is smaller and sharper. **The sheet Lucas was looking at was set in a font it
never carried.** The exported SVG has no `@font-face`; `svg_theme` rewrites `"Archivo"` and
`"Bodoni Moda"` to `"EB Garamond"`, which resolves in the workbench (it loads webfonts) and
resolves to whatever the machine substitutes anywhere else. The serif title, sans room names and
monospaced dimensions were three faces by accident. That half is WP-11.6's and is named here so it
is not read as fixed.

## What was built

### 1. `build/sheet_style.py` — one spelling of the ink, the pen and the letter

The palette was written in **five places**: identical `PAL` dicts in `render_plan.py`,
`render_section.py` and `render_roof.py`, a superset in `render_elevation.py`, the hex→hex map in
`svg_theme.py`, and `tokens.css`. The comment each renderer gave for the duplication — *"this
file, like every other build/\*.py module in this corpus, is loaded standalone via importlib"* —
is answered by `modcache.load`, which is what every other cross-module read in `build/` already
uses.

`sheet_style.py` holds `DARK` (verbatim), `DARK_ELEVATION`, `DARK_FILL`, and the Drawn Language
itself: `LIGHT`, the five `LW` weights with `INK_FOR` pairing each to its own step of the ladder,
`POCHE`, `FACE`, `MONO`, `TRACK`, `DASH` and the sheet's margins. **`#RRGGBB` literals in the four
renderers: 52 → 0.**

The three renderers that still draw in the dark register are a rename and nothing else, and that
is measured rather than asserted: **all ten sheets `workbench/server/corpus.drawing()` produces —
plan, elevation, section, bearing and roof, on both shipped plans — hash byte-identical before and
after.**

### 2. The wall is a body

`build/structure.py` has computed three wall thicknesses since WP-3.1 and **no drawing had ever
read one.** `wall_thickness(plan)` reads the plan's own `declared.construction_type` against
`construction/wall-assemblies.json`; `wall_lines()` + `bearing_lines()` return every segment on a
level already tagged exterior, bearing or partition with its reason in its own `why`.

| plan | assembly | envelope | bearing | partition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tidewater-georgian-careful` | `solid-masonry-two-wythe`, declared | 15.5 in | 11.0 in | 4.5 in |
| `spec-builder-colonial` | `platform-frame`, **defaulted, and the sheet says so** | 8.0 in | 5.5 in | 4.5 in |

So the sheet now says **which walls carry** — a fact the record has held since WP-3.1 and no plate
had ever shown. On the Tidewater ground and upper plates together: 34 envelope bands, 29 bearing,
30 partition.

**An opening is a hole in the body, not a mark laid over a line.** The gaps come from
`derive_openings` — the same call the leaves and sills are drawn from, so a gap and the door that
sits in it cannot disagree about where the opening is. A window then gets the three lines that make
it read as one: inner face, outer face, glazing on the centre line. Before this, a window was a
2.6 px bar in gilt struck along an unbroken stroke — colour outlining a thing, which the standard
forbids, over a wall that ran straight through its own windows.

Two constructions worth naming because they were found by looking at the sheet rather than by any
test:

- **The envelope is drawn OUTWARD from the block.** `geometry.py` tiles the footprint exactly, so
  the block's edge is the INSIDE face of the exterior wall. Drawing it centred would take half its
  thickness out of every boundary room and quietly shrink the house by a foot and a half.
- **A wall runs to the FACE of the wall it meets.** `wall_lines` returns interior segments running
  to the shared room edge, which is the CENTRE of the wall they butt into, so each end cap was
  drawn half a thickness inside the other wall's body as a dark nub across an otherwise continuous
  face. `_trim_junctions` trims it back. This does not move a wall; it stops drawing a line inside
  another wall's body.

The room fills are gone. The paper is the room.

**One graphic decision was made against the standard's own token, and it is recorded rather than
made quietly.** `tokens.css` gives `--poche-partition: var(--sepia-pale)`, which is right for the
register it was written in — a large-scale detail, where a pale warm field reads as a field. At
the plan scale this sheet draws, a 4.5 in partition is 4.9 px of body between two 3 px cut lines,
so under two pixels of fill survive, and sepia-pale against vellum is a difference of about eight
values. Measured on the rendered sheet, **a partition read as a hollow tube — two parallel lines
with paper between them — beside an exterior wall that read as a solid body.** A drawing that
shows a bearing wall as mass and a partition as an absence is saying something the record does
not. The partition takes `--sepia` instead, the standard's own *"timber & age: wood, masonry
hatch, warm wash"*, which is what a partition in this corpus's frame houses is. It is a choice
BETWEEN the standard's named duties and not a new colour, and `sheet_style.POCHE` keeps
`partition_large` at the token's own value for the register it was written for.

### 3. Two registers, ruled by Lucas 4 September 2026

A **presentation** sheet carries the drawing and its names; a **working** sheet adds the dimension
strings, the divergence `∗` and the relaxation `△` on the field. One renderer, and the title block
says which — a printed plate leaves its page prose behind, which is the argument WP-6.4 made for
naming the ENGINE on the plate rather than beside it.

**`render()` defaults to `working` and `corpus.drawing()` defaults to `presentation`, and the split
is deliberate.** A caller that does not choose gets every disclosure it had:
`tests/test_measurement_honesty.py` counts one triangle per relaxation off a bare
`render(out, path)`, and a sheet that dropped them when nobody chose would be WP-6.1's failure
arriving through a default argument. The workbench is the surface a PERSON reads, so the reader's
sheet is the clean one.

### 4. The diagnostics leave the field and not the sheet

Six lines of disclosure used to be set across the top of the plate, in the drawing's own space.
They are set in the title block below the border now, in the typewritten margin, where a
draughtsman puts a note — **wrapped to the border, because as one line each the undrawable-door
list and the stair's own refusal ran off the right edge and past it.** Nothing is shortened: the
rule the room labels are held to (drawn whole or not at all) applies to a disclosure too, and a
disclosure cut off mid-sentence is worse than a label cut off mid-word, because the reader cannot
see that it was cut.

**One collision was fixed by moving a note rather than by nudging it.** "STAIR NOT DRAWN — SEE
RECORD" was centred on the stair WELL, and a stair well is inside a stair hall, so on the shipped
sheet it was drawn straight through "STAIR HALL" and neither could be read. The disclosure is a
note, so it goes where the notes go; what stays on the field is the well, drawn as the reserved
void it is.

### 5. The rest of the sheet as an object

A ruled border with a hairline inside it; a title block carrying name, style, bay count, clear
dimensions, area, date of representation and register; a compass rose that points up and SAYS what
bearing up has on the ground; a graphic scale in alternating cells a reader can step a dimension
off; and the bay grid **projected past the walls with its figures**, in the construction pen, which
is what that weight is for — the reader sees the discipline the plan was composed on and not only
the marks where it was broken.

Room names are set in roman capitals, letterspaced at the standard's own `--tr-room`. **The
tracking had to enter the fitter**: `.3em` between every pair of letters is three and a half more
ems of width on a twelve-character name, and a fitter blind to it computes a size for a label a
third narrower than the one it draws — the label-through-the-wall defect of 26 August arriving by
a new road.

The plan scale is **13 px per foot, the standard's own `--px-per-ft`.** It is the smallest scale at
which this grammar survives: at the previous 7, a 4.5 in partition is 2.6 px of body between two
3 px cut lines, so the poché this package exists to draw would have been more ink than wall.

## Measured

|---|---|---|---|
| bytes | 26,774 | 32,449 | 38,502 |
| elements | 462 | 745 | 871 |
| render, mean of 5 | 1.6 ms | 6.4 ms | 5.6 ms |
| wall bodies drawn | 0 | 93 | 93 |
| room fills | 24 | 0 | 0 |
| typefaces named | 3 | 2 (one serif, one mono margin) | 2 |
| hex literals in the four renderers | 52 | **0** | 0 |

`plans/spec-builder-colonial.json`: 21,211 → 23,336 (presentation) / 28,482 (working).

**And on `auto`, where CP-SAT answers, the same sheet is a different house** — 31,246 bytes
presentation, `PLACEMENT PROVED (CP-SAT)` in the schedule, **1 undrawable door against the
heuristic's 11**, and a stair actually drawn with its treads and its UP arrow where the heuristic
reports the hall too small to hold one. The sheet Lucas read came from the weaker engine, and the
plate now says which ran on both.

Render cost rises about 4 ms per sheet. `/api/drawings/plan` is dominated by the solve (281 ms on
the heuristic, ~25 s under CP-SAT), so this is under 2% of the route and is not on
`/api/plan/evaluate` at all.

## The guards, which are the durable half

**`tests/test_sheet_poche.py` is new and every one of its six checks was mutation-tested against
the code it guards.** Four mutations, each verified to have LANDED before the suite was believed
(CLAUDE.md's own trap: a mutation that silently does not apply looks exactly like a guard that
works):

| mutation | what went red |
|---|---|
| the wall thickness becomes a constant | thicknesses, and the construction-changes-the-drawing check |
| the envelope is centred on the block instead of outside it | the envelope check |
| the wall runs through its own openings | the opening-is-a-hole check |
| a partition is drawn at the bearing wall's thickness | thicknesses, and partition-thinner-than-bearing |

Three existing guards had selectors this package retired, and each was re-pointed at the property
rather than deleted:

- `test_every_level_plate_shares_one_top_edge` selected the paper-ground rect **by its dark hex**.
  The ground is gone (the room is the paper), so a colour selector would have made the guard pass
  over an empty list. The plate states its own origin now (`data-plate-top`), which is a stronger
  reading of the same thing. Mutation-checked: an 18 px per-level drift turns it red.
- `test_the_building_outline_is_one_rect_per_element_not_one_across_the_gap` read `<rect
  class="wl">`. It reads the envelope bands by `data-block` now — which also drops a circularity
  it had, deriving px-per-foot by dividing the widest drawn rect by the widest declared block.
  Mutation-checked: one envelope across the whole drawn extent turns it red.
- `test_every_room_is_named_on_the_sheet` pinned room names in mixed case. It compares
  case-insensitively now; pinning the case would have made a typographic ruling enforceable by a
  test written about something else.

**And the widened palette guard found a hole in itself.** `test_retokenize_is_total_over_renderer_
palettes` scanned four renderer sources; once their palettes moved it would have judged an empty
set. Widened to scan `sheet_style.py` and to discharge a colour's duty if it is already a Drawn
Language token — and then mutation-tested, which showed that `LIGHT` **vouches for itself**: adding
`"smuggled": "#123456"` to it left the widened test green. `test_the_light_register_quotes_the_
standard_and_does_not_invent_it` closes that by holding every `LIGHT` value against `tokens.css`,
which is the artefact `LIGHT` claims to quote. A new ink is now a change to the standard, which is
a ruling, not a literal in a renderer.

## What was deliberately not done

- **No furniture.** The corpus holds 278 items in 59 rooms with footprints, clearances and an
  authored `placement`, and OQ 92 carries Lucas's ruling that arrangements *"should be placed
  accordingly"* — but every rule that would place a bed or a table is editorial and belongs in a
  grammar file with a `basis` sentence, written onto the placed record by a pass. That is WP-11.4.
  Drawing furniture from the catalogue at render time would be inventing plan content in the
  renderer, which is the fault WP-6.1 exists to have removed.
- **No chimneys, steps or columns.** `roof.chimney_positions()` gives two stacks at the gable ends
  and the kit gives their 22 in plan; `steps_and_stoop` gives 3–6 risers at 6.875 in; `porch_type`
  gives one portico bay and forbids a column without an answering pilaster. All of it is drawable
  and none of it is on the placed record yet. WP-11.5.
- **No font embedded.** WP-11.6, and until it lands the exported sheet is set in whatever serif
  the reader's machine has. The title block does not yet say so; that is the first thing WP-11.6
  owes.
- **`svg_theme.retokenize` still runs over the plan** and is now a no-op for it, because the sheet
  emits the Drawn Language directly. It is left in place rather than special-cased: the map is
  still total over the other three renderers and the totality test is what keeps it honest.
- **Nothing about the placement.** Twenty-three of twenty-five rooms are still drawn at a size the
  record does not declare, the passage still sits west of centre, the kitchen "dependency" and back
  hall "hyphen" are still rooms of one flat rectangle, nothing stacks, and the terrace is still not
  placed. The sheet states each of them more legibly and changes none of them. They belong to
  `oq/the-parti-dissolved-its-own-dependencies`, OQ 54 and OQ 55, to Phase 11's B line, and to
  `oq/a-massing-element-is-placed-and-nothing-below-the-placer-knows-it` (kept on one line: a slug
  broken across two is a dangling citation to `check_citations.py`, which caught this one).

## Raised

`oq/the-placement-carries-no-wall-bands` — the rooms tile the footprint exactly, so a centred
partition takes half its thickness out of each of the two rooms it divides and the sheet draws a
wall the placement does not allow for. The room's stated figure is the record's and is not
adjusted; the schedule says so on every sheet. Whether the placer should carry wall bands, and what
that does to the exact-tiling identity `over_band()` rests on, is the geometry layer's to answer.
