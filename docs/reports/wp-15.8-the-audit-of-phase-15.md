# WP-15.8 — the audit of Phase 15

*Phase 15, the plates drawn. 28 September 2026.*

Lucas asked for an audit of Phase 15 before it is called done: an attempt to find what is wrong,
not a re-read. The phase answered three of the four defects in his review of the drawn Tidewater
front (27 September):

- **WP-15.5**: the exterior stacks stopped at the roof line; they stand on the ground now.
- **WP-15.6**: nothing kept a window off the doorcase; the placer reserves the doorcase and half a
  pier beside it now.
- **WP-15.7**: the cornice was drawn as a flat band; it is drawn with its members now.

The fourth, the Gibbs Ionic capital, waits on its plate. Every host that holds one answers 403, so
WP-15.1 through 15.4 are blocked (§VII).

The audit's deliverable is a verdict and an itemized list. The verdict is §I; the list is §III.

## I. The verdict

**The verdict waits on the whole build, which was running on the final head when this was
committed; its verdict replaces this paragraph.** What was already established when it was
committed:

- none of the five auditors found anything that blocks deployment: every shipped drawing agrees
  with its record, and what they found is fixed or measured and stated (§III);
- the walk on the final head is 701 green, none failed, and the same 6 could-not-evaluate as
  before the audit;
- the test count reconciles by name (§VI).

## II. How it was run

- **Five auditors worked `07178dc..3b083d9`**, the whole of Phase 15, each from its own angle:
  - **A**: regressions, and every consumer of a changed field;
  - **B**: whether each new guard can fail, by planting defects in real sheets;
  - **C**: every published figure, re-derived by running the code;
  - **D**: the drawings, including cases no shipped plan reaches;
  - **M**: 47 mutations of the phase's code.

  Four read and one mutated, each in its own tree. **Every finding was reproduced with the
  auditor's own probe before anything was touched.**
- **The fixes are seven commits, each measured against a worktree of its parent**:
  - every sheet the corpus draws, hashed with `tests/render_digest.py` (128 sheets);
  - the census diff against the pinned known disagreements;
  - the placement and openings digests;
  - the composer's `family-georgian` set.
- **Every new guard was mutation-checked**, each mutation asserted to have landed and each file
  restored by its sha256: 79 mutations over the seven commits, all red in the end (§V).
- **A sixth auditor, read-only, took the audit's own diff** (`3b083d9..3a1bb85`). The audit of
  Phase 14 broke a gate row with its own fix, so this one was checked too (§VI).
- **The whole build** ran in a fresh `git worktree` of the final head, with `ezdxf`, `ifcopenshell`
  and `fastapi` installed, and every red was attributed by test id. Then the walk, then CI, also
  by test id (§VI).

## III. Every finding: what it was, where it came from, and what was done

certifies a defect. **Worth fixing** means a real defect that no shipped plan shows, or a guard
that cannot fail. **Minor** means prose, or a small instrument defect.

## IV. The two findings that were measured and not applied

### A front the drawn layer refuses and the elevation measures

Auditor A found it, and both halves were reproduced.

- **On the shipped Tidewater plan** (heuristic), the drawn layer says *"Facade symmetry could not be
  judged: 4 declared window unit(s) on the entrance front were not drawn"*. Beside it sit *"The Bay
  That Broke the Symmetry: 3 against at-most 0"* and *"Windows That Do Not Stand On Each Other:
  90.0 against at-most 2.0"*, both fatal.
- **On the composer's `living-hall-picturesque`** for `family-georgian`, the front read not-mirrored
  on `e8668e6`: one opening unmatched, one declared unit undrawn. WP-15.6's doorcase then refused
  the porch's south window, and at `2467585` the front reads mirrored: none unmatched, two
  undrawn. The unmatched opening was the window that vanished, so the fatal cleared.

**The planned fix** was one reader, `axis.front_complete`, for both layers, with the elevation
withholding the five figures wherever it answered no. **Its gate was set before measuring**: apply
it only if the symmetry and alignment faults alone move, and only on fronts the drawn layer already
calls incomplete. It was measured on a clean worktree over three populations:

- the sixteen plans on the heuristic;
- the declared records;
- the composer's set with the repair budget lifted.

| | plans that move | the composer's returned set |
|---|---|---|
| before | — | side-hall-townhouse, living-hall-picturesque, courtyard-and-portal, ranch-tripartite |
| the fix as planned | 6 of 16 | side-hall-townhouse, centre-passage-double-pile, five-part-palladian, tower-villa |
| narrowed to where the drawn layer refuses | 2 of 16 | centre-passage-double-pile, side-hall-townhouse, living-hall-picturesque, courtyard-and-portal |

**It failed the gate three ways.**

1. **It acquits.** With the offset and the matching count withheld,
   `storeys-out-of-vertical-alignment` has one test left to run: `bay_count_on_the_principal_front
   at-least 3`. That test passes, so the fault goes from PRESENT to CLEAR, not to unjudged, on four
   plans (two under the narrow variant). The measurement is what found this.
2. **It reaches plans the drawn layer never judges.** On `bad-03`, `bad-05`, `good-02` and
   `good-05` the front is incomplete too, but their diagrams want no centre bay, so the drawn layer
   judges no symmetry there at all.
3. **It reorders the composer.** The native `centre-passage-double-pile` comes back into the set
   by losing two fatal findings. One becomes unjudged, which `_sort_key` reads as none; the other
   becomes the false clear. It is the right house for a Tidewater Georgian brief, returned for the
   wrong reason.

So nothing was applied, and the product code is unchanged.

### A clear that rests on nothing

The acquittal has a cause older than all of this. `core._judge` returns `clear` when every test
that RAN passed. A test that wanted a measurement nobody supplied did not run, and does not count.
Measured with `tests/fault_clears.py` at `2467585`, on the sixteen plans on the heuristic, as the
bench checks them:

- **550** fault verdicts read clear.
- **142** of them (26%) never ran their governing test, every one for want of a measurement.
  - 48 are on fatal faults, 62 serious and 32 minor.
  - They fall on 19 faults and 15 plans.
  - **70 of the 142 cleared on a secondary that is `build/elevation.py`'s own constant**, so about
    half rest on nothing measured at all.
- **284** (52%) left some applicable test unrun, counting secondaries.

The plainest case is `storeys-out-of-vertical-alignment`. It reads clear on seven one-storey plans,
on a floor on the bay count that says nothing about alignment, where the honest verdict is not
applicable. OQ 84 met one instance of this in August and closed it by supplying the measurement.
The rule that made the instance possible was never raised as a question.

**Three rulings come first**, and the question states them:

- whether a secondary may stand in for the primary; no record says which secondaries are the same
  rule;
- whether a clear needs the governing test (142 move) or every applicable test (284 move);
- what an unjudged fatal costs a candidate.

## IX. Traps worth carrying

- **A gate set before the measurement is what stopped a fake pass.** The front-complete fix read as
  plainly right: one reader, two layers, a contradiction removed. Measured against the gate written
  before it was built, it turned a fatal fault into a clear on four plans. Written after, the gate
  would have been fitted to the result.
- **Withholding a measurement does not unjudge a fault.** The fault is clear on whatever test is
  left. Before withholding a figure, find out which tests read it and which do not.
- **A mutation proves a guard only against the tests it was run against.** Run it against the
  files that hold the property, not the one test written for it.
- **Read a figure off the element that states it, after waiting for that element.** The walk's
  race read a page's text before the span existed.
