## I. What was asked

WP-16.5 raised three questions on its way out, and Lucas answered them the same evening (B1–B4).
Each is recorded as two acts, the question as put and the answer, in its question's own file.

- **B1, on `oq/the-roof-is-drawn-side-gabled-whatever-the-style-says`.** A plan record that names no
  roof form takes the canonical `roof_form` of its style's resolved kit, after its own declaration
  and before its massing's default. The kit's form goes through a closed table onto the forms the
  roof layer draws. Inherited roof records a style contradicts are first corrected in its own kit,
  as WP-16.2 did the bans. A style with no canonical form keeps the fallback, and the sheet says
  so. The composer's candidates move too, and the ranking is re-measured before it lands.
- **B2.** `return-that-never-returns` gains no exception. It is not applicable where no gable end
  is drawn, and a house drawn with a gable end is judged, whatever its kit says of the return.
- **B3, on `oq/the-rake-is-drawn-as-an-edge-and-carries-no-member`.** The Cardboard Gable's own
  rake: a 6–8 in board 1¼ in proud, carrying the eave's bed mould and crown (no corona, no
  modillions) at 0.5–0.7 of their heights, 4–8 in beyond the wall. It is drawn at the midpoints,
  and is a judgment withheld from the fault. The styles the fault excepts keep the edge, and a
  pediment keeps its own rule.
- **B4, on `oq/a-return-permitted-only-over-a-deeper-cornice-is-drawn-over-a-shallower-one`.** Where
  a style's kit states a minimum cornice for its return, the cornice is drawn at least that deep.

**3. The rake (B3, B5, B10, B25–B30, T11).**

- **`resolve_kit.rake_at(rec, date)` is the one reader** of a style's resolved `rake_condition` at
  the house's date. It reads eight states, each naming who said it:
  - `forbidden`: the slot, or a forbidden fault row on a record settling nothing else;
  - `fault`: a canonical row IS The Cardboard Gable's own rake, `boxed-rake-with-return`;
  - `plain`: the record states a plain trim's width;
  - `undrawn`: a canonical bargeboard, coped parapet or close verge, which this generator does not
    draw;
  - `unjudged`: a canonical row the reader does not know;
  - `unread`: the record states its rake only in words, quoted and never parsed;
  - `overhang` (T11): the record states the rake's overhang and no member;
  - `silent`.
- **`elevation.rake_for` turns the reading into what is drawn:**
  - the fault's rake, a judgment: a 7 in board, the eave's crown and bed mould at 0.6 of their
    heights, 1¼ in proud and 6 in beyond the wall;

  - or the roof's edge, with the reason.

  **A plain trim drawn at one measured figure is a measurement** (B29: minimal-traditional's
  5½ in "is a measurement rather than a judgment"). A band's middle, or a maximum drawn at its
  figure, is a judgment, and the sheet says which.
- **`elevation.rake_marks` is the one spelling the sheet and the DXF draw.**

  - Each stops where it meets the top of the eave cornice, as the Georgian kit's own rule has it:
    "it must die into the cornice return rather than meeting it in a mitre".
  - The two slopes meet on a plumb line.
- **Where it is not drawn, each surface says so.**
  - An eave face says the rake's overhang past its corners is not drawn there, nor on the roof plan
    or the model, because the roof record stops at the gable wall.
  - The roof plan says that at each gable end the roof is drawn to the wall, because the record
    states no rake past it (`disclosures.roof_stops_at_the_gable_wall`). It claims no rake: a kit
    may keep the edge flush.

- **The styles the fault excepts** keep the edge where their kit states the fault's rake or

- **Withheld from the faults:**

## IV. What was found

**1. The rake the kit asks for has no slope to run along on eight of the ten drawn shipped plans
with a gable end.** A rake runs along the gable's slope.

- roof.py leaves the ridge height unjudged where no pitch is migrated for the style, "rather than
  computed off an invented pitch". The gable end's outline is then the eave line alone.
- **26 of the 41 drawn styles have no migrated pitch, and on 16 of them the kit asks for a rake.**
- On the shipped plans, the rake is drawn on tidewater-georgian and good-03 only. The other eight
  say *"NOT DRAWN — THE ROOF JUDGES NO RIDGE (NO ROOF-PITCH CONSTRAINT IS MIGRATED FOR
  COLONIAL-REVIVAL), SO THE GABLE END HAS NO SLOPE TO CARRY IT"*, naming their own style. Their eave
  faces do not say the gable ends draw a rake.
- `oq/the-rake-the-kit-asks-for-is-drawn-only-where-a-pitch-is-migrated`.

**2. The cause was first worded "NO PITCH IS STATED", and that was false.** The second independent
check found it.

- Every style reaching that state on the shipped plans states a pitch in its kit's `roof_pitch`
  slot. colonial-revival and georgian-revival read queen-anne-american's 9:12 minimum, for
  instance. roof.py reads only a migrated constraint.
- The words now say what roof.py lacks, and never that no pitch is stated.

**3. The first independent check said the opposite of finding 1, and was wrong.** It reported that
the fault's rake "is now drawn" on the colonial-revival plans. It is not: those roofs judge no
ridge.

- The census then held every gable face that asks for a rake to draw one. That would have convicted
  16 honest faces.
- `_rake_held` reads the ridge now, and a gable face with none must say so.
- This was found by drawing the plans, not by reading the check.

**4. The census's MEASURED/JUDGMENT clause read the whole sheet.** So it passed a fault's rake said
MEASURED, because some other line on the same sheet said A JUDGMENT. The second check found this.
`_rake_held` reads the face's own rake notes now, and the mutation that says MEASURED of the fault's
rake turns it red.

**5. The elevation labelled minimal-traditional's measured 5½ in board a judgment, against B29.**
The first check found it. A plain trim drawn at one measured figure is now labelled measured; a
band's middle or a maximum is a judgment.

**6. An overhang with no member was going to be drawn as The Cardboard Gable's rake (T11).**

- ranch-style's c03 and neo-eclectic's defining characteristics each state the rake's overhang and
  no member.
- `rake_at` read such a record as silent, and silence draws the fault's rake: a member the kit never
  stated, under a band it did.
- `overhang` is its own state now: the edge is kept, and the band is said.
- *Read with it, not taken by analogy:* a record forbidding the fault's own rake and settling
