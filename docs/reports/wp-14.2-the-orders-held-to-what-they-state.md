# WP-14.2 — the orders held to what they state

*Phase 14, the ink held to its plates. Built 27 September 2026 in seven commits, one per step of
the plan: `5377e5e`, `8cc891f`, `ef48257`, `dacde10`, `a80f4cb`, `26ffb13` and `b128455`. This
report is the eighth.*

WP-14.1 built the instrument and took the first reading. It found 452 disagreements between what a
drawing shows and what its record states, pinned by identity and by figure. This package covered the
orders and the mouldings:

- the 73 profile plates;
- `dist/orders.html`;
- the workbench's Proportions plate;
- the elevation's cornice inset;
- the order stack the engine builds for all of them.

It fixed each defect in the step the plan gave it and landed each fix with the guard that proves it.
**The census went from 452 to 255 known disagreements, and every check on the orders and mouldings
now reads zero.** None of the 197 was waved away. Each was removed from the drawing, the record or
the instrument, and each removal is named below with its figure. The 255 that remain belong to the
elevation, section, roof, DXF and plan, which are WP-14.3's and WP-14.4's.

The rule this package worked to is the phase's: **draw what the record states, say what it does
not, and invent nothing.** Most of what it removed was one of three things:

- a missing figure drawn as a measured zero;
- a second copy of an arithmetic that disagreed with the first;
- a sentence on a plate describing a different drawing from the one beside it.

## I. What was fixed, step by step

The census counts are the known-disagreement list before and after each step.

### Step 1 — the column the plate is drawn at, and the relief its ink draws (452 → 435)

- **Twelve Palladio plates** printed "at a 24″ column" over a drawing made at 12″. The footer and
  the manifest's review note both computed `module_in * 2`. That is a column only where the module
  is the semidiameter, and Palladio's module is the whole diameter. Both now read `dimension()`'s
  own `totals.lower_diameter_in`.
- **Five plates** printed "1.00″ of relief" over no relief at all. The figure was the layout's scale
  floor. Relief is now measured from the ink. A zero is said as one of two things: no member
  publishes a projection, or every published face is flush.

### Step 2 — a projection nobody published is not a projection of 0 (435 → 416)

- **Absent figures became zeros.** `dimension()` read a missing `projection_parts` as 0. **94 of 934
  members**, every torus, scotia and plinth among them, reached every surface as faces measured
  flush. It carries `None` now, as `width_parts` has since WP-5.11, and the schema admits null.
- **An unpublished member is a ghost at its naked.** No shape is constructed, and a dashed bracket
  marks the member. The fill closes over it and the ink does not stroke it. Fourteen tori and scotias
  had been drawn bitten into the shaft they stand on, and two of them ran off the plate. Every plate
  counts its ghosts, and a complete one says it is complete.
- **The datum can be unjudged.** OQ 78's reading took the missing figures as evidence, so nine
  assemblies were read "naked" on the strength of their own silence. The rule now reads published
  figures only.
- **The die carries the plinth**, read on the base's own datum. Benjamin's Corinthian die had stood
  2.8″ inside the plinth it carries. Where the base publishes nothing, the die had been 1.2 × R, a
  figure no record states. It is now unjudged, stands on the column's radius, and says so.
- **The 90 written zeros were classified from their notes.** 46 are real zeros and are pinned:
  35 datum planes, 9 origins of a relief sequence and 2 stated as zero. 2 are placeholders,
  Vignola's Doric and Tuscan apophyges, and are now null.
- **Looking at the first plate found two more defects.**
  - The ink stroked the silhouette's closing edge, which runs up the naked across every ghost.
  - The border was `box_h + 26` tall while the ink starts 36 px below its top, so the foot of the
    ink ran through the bottom rule on all 73 plates. Re-measured from the plates committed at
    `5377e5e`, it overran by 9.6 to 10.3 px on every one. The border is `box_h + 42` now, and on
    all 73 it clears the ink by 5.7 to 6.4 px.

### Step 3 — a shape nobody can construct is drawn as its envelope (416 → 406)

- **Words and ink disagreed on ten capital plates.** They drew volutes and acanthus as a swelling,
  beside words saying these were "NOT DRAWN AS SOMETHING PLAUSIBLE". They are drawn as their dashed
  envelope now, and the words say so.
- **A part nobody recorded is named.** Vignola's Ionic capital records the volute's channel and
  fillet and no volute, and four of the five Ionic packs inherit it. The plate says "NOT RECORDED:
  THE VOLUTE". The rule reads member names, never notes, because the shaft's astragal note
  mentions the volute's eye and a shaft owes no volute.

### Step 4 — a curve drawn as a line is counted and said (406 → 403)

25 curved members are handed the same face twice and drawn as vertical lines. Each is now marked
and said. Why they have no run has two causes, each a question about how the record is read, so it
is put to a ruling: `oq/which-end-of-a-moulding-its-projection-names`.

### Step 5 — confidence is marked on every drawing of a member (403 → 338)

- **Low confidence went unsaid.** 65 of 73 plates, the inset and the DXF were silent about a member
  apportioned at low confidence. Each such member is now outlined dashed over its own height, its
  label says its level, and the DXF carries the level as XDATA.
- **An unstated confidence is not a high one.** It travels as None.
- **Holding the marks to each member's height found a construction defect nothing else had.** The
  scotia's arcs dug 0.07″ into the fillet below at a 12″ column, in every base in the corpus. Each
  half is a quarter-ellipse now, and every member's ink is held to its own band.

### Step 6 — the orders page and the Proportions plate draw what Python serves (338 → 268)

- **The orders page's port of the engine is deleted.** It agreed with Python on all 156 cases
  measured. It went anyway: a copy that agrees today is the one nobody re-checks when the engine
  moves, and step 7 moved the engine. The page now scales what `build/render_orders.py` serves.
- **The flutes were drawn in the wrong places.** The page drew `round(n/2.4)` lines at evenly
  spaced fractions of the radius. The lines are Python's now, at r(y)·sin((k+½)·2π/n). The count
  is the record's. The setting-out, with a flute on the axis, is a convention, and the page says
  so.
- **An unjudged invariant printed FAIL.** An invariant the engine could not evaluate now prints
  N/EV with the reason.
- **Two packs could not be reached.** greek-doric and moorish-arch were in the page and no button
  led to them. The harness now finds reachability by clicking every button the page renders.
- **The Proportions plate computed its own figures in JavaScript**: its datum, frame, die and
  unpublished count. Three of them were wrong:
  - its frame was narrower than its ink on 12 packs;
  - it called 13 packs' flush members unpublished, and was silent about 9 packs' real ones;
  - on 13 packs, its caption named one datum over assemblies Python had read on the other.

  It reads Python's figures now.
- **The agents' tool and the nav serve the same figures.** `tdl_get_proportions` serves the datum
  per assembly, the unpublished members and the ink's extent. The nav's overlay badge reads the key
  the packs carry.

### Step 7 — the stack each pack states (268 → 255)

- **An overlay's own column sets its shaft.** Five overlays drew the base authority's shaft under
  their own column. At a 6″ module, Benjamin's Corinthian drew 120″ against a stated 132″, and
  Gibbs's Ionic 110″ against 108″. The shaft is now the column less the base and capital drawn,
  which is `_synth_shaft`'s own rule. The inherited members stay; only the shaft's body takes the
  difference. Three of the overlays also state their shaft: Benjamin's Corinthian and Tuscan, and
  Gibbs's Ionic. For all three the derived shaft is that stated figure to the part, which is the
  evidence the authors wrote the rule in.
- **An inherited entablature no longer replaces one the overlay states and contradicts.** Four
  overlays drew Vignola's finer architrave, frieze and cornice, 30″ against Benjamin's stated
  24″. They draw their own whole entablature now. Where the inherited triplet sums to the pack's
  own total it is kept, because it is a division of that total. So Gibbs's Doric keeps its
  triglyphs and modillions. The first version of the rule threw those away.
- **Benjamin's subplinth is an alternative, not a layer.** It was stacked on the pedestal. His own
  arithmetic sets it out in a whole with no pedestal in it: 28/2 = 14 diameters = column 11 +
  entablature 2 + subplinth 1. It carries `alternative_to: "pedestal"` with that quotation, and the
  stack takes one or the other.
- **Every surface says what the stack left out.** `dimension()` returns `stack_notes`, and the
  orders page, the Proportions plate and `tdl_get_proportions` all say them. O11 holds the page to
  them and R4 holds the plate.
- **The plates keep the triplet on purpose.** The profile plates enumerate the triplet stack, so
  the plate set stays at 73 and each inherited cornice plate is still drawn, saying whose it is.
- **The elevation inset names the pack it draws.** It hard-coded "GIBBS IONIC".

## III. What the instrument got wrong

- **E4 could not see the fix it demanded.** Where the base publishes nothing, it read the record
  and *asserted* what the geometry did: "the die is drawn at 1.2 × R". Step 2 removed that
  stand-in, but E4 never looked at the output, so it went on disagreeing through two pins. It reads
  the drawn die now.
- **E1 demanded a base the record says a Greek Doric column does not have**, and reported both
  baseless orders COULD NOT EVALUATE. A column stated without a base is judged as shaft plus
  capital.
- **R2 counted the column's own body** as an unpublished member on two Palladio packs that leave it
  null. The body is the column, and R2 finds it from the record's heights.
- **R4 first compared the plate's words with the notes the server served.** If the server stopped
  serving them, both sides would be empty and would agree. This was proved rather than argued: the
  mutation that drops the notes from `tdl_get_proportions` was GREEN against that R4, with 0 rows.
  R4 now compares against the engine's own stack, and the same mutation is red.
- **Two guards could not fail as written.**
  - P10 was satisfied by its own footer sentence (step 5).
  - The plate tests only ever saw a scale factor of 1, because the server builds geometry at the
    module it is asked for. Dropping `* f` passed until a test asked for twice the module.
- **Two runs sharing a machine are not a control.** Step 7's attribution first ran the composer on
  the control and on the new tree at the same time. The fourth candidate read 70.6 against 67.4.
  Run one at a time, twice each, the two trees agree exactly. The difference was load, not code.
- **The walk's plate reader measured the pane, not the plate.** Step 6 extended the Proportions
  check (⑩) from three packs to all 26 and read each band's corners through `getCTM()`. That matrix
  ends in the root's viewport, which is in CSS pixels, while the check compared it with the
  viewBox, which is in inches. The check was committed unrun. Its first run was red on 77 checks:
  every pack with a stack read about 600″ tall against a stated 108″ to 192″, which is the height of
  the pane the plate is fitted to. The band's screen matrix, undone by the root's, is the viewBox's
  own space.
- **And one of its checks had no third state.** "The capital stands clear of the shaft" read
  Palladio's Doric and Tuscan capitals as narrower than their shafts. Their records publish no
  projection for any member, so since step 2 the plate draws each capital at its naked, dashed,
  and says so. How far such a capital stands out is not a fact the record holds, and the walk now
  says COULD NOT EVALUATE with that reason.
- **A shell variable set inside one background job does not reach the job beside it.** A command
  meant to run one test on each tree set its paths inside the first job. The second job therefore
  ran a bare `pytest` in the main checkout: the whole suite, including the six files that rewrite
  repository data and restore it in a `finally`. It was stopped with SIGINT, so those `finally`
  blocks ran. The checkout was then proved byte-identical to the intended state before anything
  else was done. The two census runs that had overlapped it were run again on the settled tree.

## IV. What was deliberately not done

- **Palladio's detailed Corinthian cornice is not drawn in the stack.** The stack draws his own
  whole entablature at the grain its record states it: three members, none publishing a
  projection. So the orders page shows three brackets where it drew Vignola's mouldings. His own
  invariant ties his detailed cornice to the whole entablature's cornice member. A record field
  saying so would let the stack substitute it; inferring the substitution from an invariant
  expression would be inventing a rule.
- **Benjamin's Tuscan keeps Vignola's triplet**, because it sums to Benjamin's own 3.5 modules.
  Benjamin's own division of that total is 1 : 1 : 1½ modules (30 : 30 : 45 parts), all
  apportioned at low confidence, and it differs from Vignola's 1 : 1⅙ : 1⅓.
- **The flutes' setting-out** is drawn as a convention and not ruled.
- **moorish-arch on the workbench is still scaled by a column diameter it does not have.** Asked
  for a 12″ column, the server dimensions it at a 6″ module against its own stated 24″, because
  `diameters_per_module` falls to its 0.5 default for a pack with no column. The orders page draws
  it at its own module and says so.
- **`gibbs-doric` states 24 flutes and inherits a shaft body named "twenty flutes".** The drawing
  uses the record's 24. The name belongs to its base's count, and it is left for the source leg.

## VII. Corrections (WP-14.6, 27 Sep 2026)

WP-14.6's adversarial audit re-derived every figure this report publishes. Four did not
reproduce. Each is corrected here and the text above is left as it was written. All four were
re-derived from the corpus, not taken from the audit's word.

- **Step 5's "65 of 73 plates ... were silent about a member apportioned at low confidence"
  counts the wrong quantity.** 65 of 73 plates carry a member the plate outlines dashed, which is
  one whose confidence is medium, low or unstated (`profiles.is_weak`). **16 plates carry a member
  at LOW, 56 members in all**: ten cornices, three bases, two capitals and one entablature,
  over Benjamin, Chambers, Gibbs, Palladio and Vignola. The fix did what it said,
  because the dashed outline is drawn on every weak member. The sentence named one population
  and counted the other.
- **Step 3's "four of the five Ionic packs inherit it" is three.** Four of the five Ionic
  CAPITALS draw no volute, and one of the four is Vignola's own. Only Benjamin, Chambers and
  Palladio inherit it; Gibbs's Ionic states its own capital, `cap_volute` included. Counted off
  the plates (`NOT RECORDED: THE VOLUTE` on four capital plates) and off each overlay's own
  assemblies.
- **Step 2's "90 written zeros" counts each zero once per pack that resolves it.** The raw order
  packs held **48 distinct written zeros** before this package, and the report's own split
  accounts for exactly 48: 46 real (35 datum, 9 origin, 2 stated) and 2 placeholders. 90 is how
  many resolved members carry one across the 26 packs, because an overlay inheriting its base's
  assembly inherits its zeros too. Classification is of the record, so 48 is the number. It is
  46 now, and `tests/test_unpublished_projections.py::WRITTEN_ZEROS` holds every one of them.
- **§III's "a stated 108″ to 192″" starts at 87″.** At the 12″ column the walk asks for,
  `greek-doric`, which has no base, stacks 87″. `benjamin-doric` is the lowest pack with one, at
  108″. `moorish-arch` states no stack and draws no plate.
