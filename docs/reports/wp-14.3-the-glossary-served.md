# WP-14.3 — the glossary served, and the one sentence a stranger can read

*24 September 2026. Branch `wp-14.3`, built on `b4172f8` (WP-14.0, 14.1, 14.2, 14.5, 14.6 and
14.7 merged; 121 records in `glossary/`). Built in parallel with WP-14.4 on the same two server
files, so every addition to `corpus.py` and `app.py` is a self-contained block and nothing
existing in either was moved or reformatted. The contracts are
`docs/prd/phase-14-the-dossier-and-the-journey.md` §C, §D, §E.6, §J and §K; the analysis they
answer is `docs/reports/ux-first-principles-2026-09-24.md`. Every figure below was measured in
this worktree on this tree, and each "before" was measured on a `git archive` of `b4172f8` rather
than remembered. Cite this report by filename.*

## What it is for

Ruling 2 of 24 September puts every definition the workbench shows in a `glossary/` record the app
never writes, and ruling 8 lets exactly one of those records be read before signing in. WP-14.1
and WP-14.2 wrote and checked the records; nothing served them. This package serves them, makes a
term citable and findable, lets a `style:` citation name a dossier section, and opens the one
ruled path — without moving a byte of what tranche one keeps still: every MCP payload,
`core.overview()`, `/api/health` and `workbench/server/rail.py`.

## I. What was built

**`mcp_server/core.py` — `_data()` only.** `glossary/*.json` is loaded into
`core._data()["glossary"]`, keyed by id, by exactly the loop that loads `faults/`. No other line of
`core.py` moved: `overview()` has no glossary key and no glossary count, and no MCP tool reads the
key.

**`workbench/server/corpus.py` — one block, placed before the search index it feeds.**

- `glossary_payload()` is §C.1 in code: `version` (`schema_version + "+" + digest`), the schema's
  own `version`, the 16-hex SHA-256 digest of the records **as authored** (sorted by id, no
  `reads`), `count`, `terms` in (family enum index, `order` or +∞, id) order each carrying `reads`,
  `by_family` over every family of the schema enum in its order, and `by_field` over all seven
  fields. Nothing is re-spelled: the family order is the schema's enum through `core.schema`, the
  seven fields are `build/check_glossary.py`'s own `FIELDS`, and `reads` is
  `check_openings.GLOSSARY_REC_RE.findall(basis)`, de-duplicated in order of first appearance —
  exactly the population `check_basis` opens and verifies, so the provenance line a popover prints
  is the file the checker read and never a second regex's opinion of it.
- Cached in `_GLOSSARY_PAYLOAD`, cleared by `reset_glossary_payload()`, which `invalidate()` calls
  beside `reset_search_index()`. Measured, not imitated: a warm rebuild costs **7.9 ms** and the
  payload is **140,908 bytes** raw; it is returned SHARED, as the search index is, and every record
  in it is a copy so no caller can reach `core`'s own.
- `glossary_term(term_id)` is §C.2: the record with `reads`, its `confusable_with` resolved in
  declared order to `{id, term, sense}`, each `see` cite named from the search index by its `cite`
  (`null` where the kind is not indexed — a `kit:`, `brief:` or `constraint:` cite), and the set's
  `version`. A miss is `{"error": "no glossary term '<id>'", "did_you_mean": [...]}`, where
  `did_you_mean` is `core.get_fault`'s rule verbatim, and `_ok()` makes it a 404.
- `search_index()` appends one `term` entry per record outside the families `surface`, `section`
  and `nav-group` (`GLOSSARY_FAMILIES_NOT_INDEXED`), in the payload's order, shaped exactly as
  §C.5 gives it. **The haystack is the term, the id, the akas and the sense; the definition is
  never in it** and travels as `short`. The payload's own `indexes` / `does_not_index` fields say so.

**`workbench/server/app.py`.** `GET /api/glossary` and `GET /api/glossary/{term_id}`, both through
`_ok()`, in a block of their own after the search index route. The module docstring's WHAT THE
UNGATED SURFACE CARRIES paragraph gains the sentence §C.3 asks for, in this commit: exactly one
API path is open, the gate compares whole paths, and what that body can carry is bounded by the
RECORD — `check_glossary` rule 9 forbids `about-tdl` a `see`, a `confusable_with`, a `binds` and a
`surface`, so it resolves no other record.

**`workbench/server/auth.py`.** `OPEN_PATHS` gains exactly `"/api/glossary/about-tdl"`.

**`workbench/server/citations.py`.** `DOSSIER_SECTIONS` is the tuple §D.1 fixes, the server's one
spelling; `_known_ids("term")` returns `core._data()["glossary"]`; and the fragment rule is §E.6's
— a `style:` fragment is a slot id or a section, a `kit:` fragment a slot id only, `term` has no
fragment rule (as `fault` and `pack` have none). **`REF_RE`, `ID_CHARS` and `FRAG_CHARS` are
untouched**, and a test now says so in terms. A refused style fragment reads *"unknown slot or
section fragment"*, because a reader told no should learn which two vocabularies it was checked
against; a refused kit fragment still reads *"unknown slot fragment"*.

**`build/check_counts.py`.** Three rows, so the search-index figure is policed everywhere prose
states it (see §II.2). The figures were rewritten by `check_counts.py --fix` and by nothing else —
four of them, including the one in `CLAUDE.md`, 666 → **762**.

**Tests.** `workbench/server/tests/test_glossary_routes.py` (new, 15 tests), and extensions to
`test_zz_auth_leak_guard.py` (+4), `test_search_index.py` (+2, and two re-cut), `test_citations.py`
(+4), `test_grammar_agreement.py` (+3) and `test_compression_and_caching.py` (the named-cache test
names the new global). One root test re-cut, `tests/test_check_glossary.py` (§II.1).

**Prose.** `glossary/README.md` (§II.1), `docs/deployment.md` (the gate paragraph said only
`/api/*` is gated, which a reader would now take to mean all of it), `docs/workbench.md` and
`workbench/README.md` (what the index holds, and the served size re-measured by hand and dated,
because no checker computes it).

## II. What was found

### II.1 A guard written "until the server accepts one", and this package is the server accepting one

`tests/test_check_glossary.py::test_citations_are_held_to_the_grammar` asserted that
`style:tidewater-georgian#lineage` does NOT resolve, under the comment *"the tranche-one rule: no
dossier-section fragment until the server accepts one"*, and `glossary/README.md` told authors
*"the server does not accept that fragment yet"*. Both were WP-6.4's *"until X lands"* shape, and
WP-14.3 is X: the moment `citations.validate` learned the sections, the assertion became false and
the README sentence became a false instruction. **The checker was right and the test was about a
date.** Re-cut to the property it was always about — the fragment rule is the SERVER's and the
checker defers to it rather than spelling a fourth grammar — so the test now drives all three
halves through the checker: a bad style fragment refused as *"unknown slot or section fragment"*,
a section on a `kit:` refused as *"unknown slot fragment"*, and the section on a `style:` accepted.
The README says what the rule is rather than what it is not yet.

### II.2 One figure in seven places, and the checker watched one of them

The search index's size was claimed in `CLAUDE.md`, and policed there. It was also written into
`workbench/README.md` (665), twice into `docs/workbench.md` (666 in one sentence, 665 in the
next), and into comments in `workbench/app/src/components/Spotlight.jsx` (665, twice) and
`workbench/app/src/search/match.js` (665). **Run on a `git archive` of `b4172f8`, the three new
rows report two of the three prose copies STALE against a live 666 before this package changes
anything** — which is `check_counts.py`'s own *"a number stated twice needs claiming twice"*
broken by the number that rule was written about, and WP-12.8 had already corrected one of these
by hand. All four prose copies now read 762 and all four are claimed.

**The two JavaScript comments are named and NOT claimed.** `Spotlight.jsx` is "unchanged by
design" in tranche one and leaves in tranche two (PRD §J.1, §L), and `match.js` is owned by
WP-14.13; a claim on either would make `--fix` edit a file this package may not touch, which is an
edit by another route. **`match.js:3` is a copy the PRD does not name** — §L names only
Spotlight's — so it is reported here for the package that owns the file.

### II.3 A test's proxy for "findable by name" cannot hold for a word with two senses

`test_search_index.py::test_entry_shape` asserted `name.lower() in hay` for every entry. §C.5 gives
a term with a `sense` the name `term (sense)` — two records say "variant" and a list of two
identical names answers nothing — and the parenthesised whole is not a substring of a haystack
built from the parts. The property was never "the whole name is in the haystack": `match.js`
compares the typed query with the NAME before it reads the haystack, so the whole name finds itself
anyway. Re-cut to the property in its parts for a term — the word is in the haystack, the sense is
in the haystack, and the name is exactly `term (sense)` or `term` — and unchanged for every other
kind. The mutation that drops the sense from the name goes red on it.

### II.4 A comment claimed a guard that did not exist until now

`workbench/app/src/citations.js` (WP-14.5) says its `DOSSIER_SECTIONS` line is *"held to"* the
server's tuple *"by test_grammar_agreement.py … by READING it"*. At `b4172f8` neither the server's
tuple nor the test existed. It is a forward reference to this package, written into a merged file
in the present tense; it is true from this commit, and it is recorded because it is the class this
corpus keeps meeting — a sentence claiming a check before the check exists reads exactly like one
that has it. The test reads the one-line form §D.1 fixes, refuses anything but exactly one such
line, and says to restore the form rather than loosen the reader if it is reformatted.

### II.5 What the one open path carries, stated rather than assumed

The ungated body is the `about-tdl` record — its definition, its basis quoting `VISION.md`, its
readers and its `is_not` lines, all `VISION.md`'s words — plus `reads: ["VISION.md"]`, two empty
lists, and the set's `version`, which carries a digest of the WHOLE glossary. That digest says a
signed-out visitor can tell THAT the gated glossary changed and nothing about what it holds; it is
§C.2's shape, and it is written into the docstring so it is a stated property rather than a
discovered one. Measured: 3,097 bytes, 3.7 ms warm. A miss behind the gate returns a
`did_you_mean` list of real ids, so a miss must be gated too, and the leak guard asks one.

### II.6 Two rules for one citation, both by contract

The server accepts `term:about-tdl#anything` — §E.6 adds no fragment rule for `term`, as none
exists for `fault` or `pack` — while `check_glossary` refuses a `term:` citation carrying a
fragment. Both follow their contracts and neither is changed here; the disagreement is recorded so
the next reader does not take the checker's refusal for the server's.

### II.7 The index is a sixth heavier, and the reason is the display text

96 of the 121 records are indexed (12 surfaces, 9 sections and 4 rail groups are places the rail
already names): **666 → 762 entries, 206,053 → 241,518 bytes served**, +17%, almost all of it the
definitions carried as `short`. The palette fetches the index once and keeps it, so this is paid
once a session; it is §C.5's shape and is stated rather than netted off.

## III. What was deliberately not done

- **`api/client.js` gains nothing.** §J.3 lists `glossary()` and `glossaryTerm(id)` there for
  this package; the lead reassigned the app half, so the routes exist and nothing in the app calls
  them yet. The Gate's fetch of `about-tdl` is the app package's.
- **No MCP tool, no `overview()` key, no `/api/health` field** — all asserted, and
  `core.overview()` was compared byte for byte against `b4172f8` (identical, in insertion order and
  with sorted keys). `workbench/server/rail.py` hashes identically (`ac7f5815…`); the rail's
  `SYSTEM` prompt lists no `term` kind, so a term chip will not be offered by the model until the
  assistant package teaches it, even though the validator now accepts one.
- **No fragment rule for `term`**, per §E.6.
- **No check on the served size in KB.** The figure in `docs/workbench.md` is re-measured and dated
  by hand; a checker for a byte count would be policing the wire format, which is FastAPI's.
- **No new open question.** Every finding above has an owner or is by contract.
