# WP-11.10 — the depth floor, derived from the fault's own rule and refused as a cap

*7 September 2026. Branch `claude/georgian-tidewater-layout-diagnosis-sq78o4`.*

**Three framings of this problem were falsified in a row, each by measurement and none by
reading.** That is the whole shape of the package, so it is the structure of this report.

---

## I. The three framings, and what killed each

### Framing 1 — "build the per-element roof, then item 3"

Ruled on my own account of the blocker. **Falsified**: the truss fatal that keeps the re-authored
`centre-passage-double-pile` at rank 9 is on the **main block's** roof, so a per-element ridge
relation never reaches it. The main block comes out **45 x 28.89 ft** against
`PILE["double-pile"] = 36.0`.

### Framing 2 — "the blocker is a missing depth floor at the pile"

Mine, and put to Lucas, who ruled *depth floor first*. **Falsified by the first measurement taken
after the ruling**: a floor at the pile moves `spec-builder-colonial`, a shipped reference plan,
from 30.75 to 36.0 ft — **262 sf of empty floor, 17.1% more footprint than programme** — to satisfy
a rule that is not convicting it. A pile is a TYPICAL depth. This corpus removed 35 proportion
floors on 3 September for exactly the error of reading a typical value as a hard one.

**And the sentence I used to justify it was itself wrong.** I wrote that `spec-builder-colonial`
"passes the truss fault today". It does not pass — **it is UNJUDGED**. Its style has no migrated
`roof_pitch_rise_per_12` constraint, so `structure.roof_heights` returns `grade_to_ridge_ft: None`
and the fault has no number to test. *Unjudged is not passed*, and I said passed.

### Framing 3 — "derive the floor from the fault's own rule"

Mine, and the one Lucas asked for. **Built, and then falsified by its own output.** The derivation
is sound; what it exposes is that the fault it inverts cannot carry a placer constraint. §III.

## II. What was built

`build/depth_floor.py`, a **LEAF** — `structure.py` loads `geometry.py`, so anything `geometry`
reads may not reach back into `structure`; `build/storeys.py` carries the same rule and the same
reason. It inverts the fault's own test:

    roof_height_eave_to_ridge / wall_height_grade_to_eave  at-least  T
    =>  min_outside_span >= T * wall_height_ft * 24 / pitch

- **T is READ from `faults/truss-flattened-pitch.json`**, never transcribed. A checker carrying a
  copy of the number it checks is one rule in two places — WP-11.7's `facade_share` defect, and
  this file would have been the obvious next instance. `tests/test_depth_floor.py` parses the
  module's **AST** and fails if the threshold appears as a numeric constant anywhere in the code,
  and separately doubles the threshold *in a temp copy of the record* and requires the span to
  double.
- **The wall height is the sum of the STOREY heights**, from `build/storeys.py`, not the ceiling
  heights. §IV.2.
- **Four verdicts**, and `unjudged` is never collapsed into `ok`.

It is reported by `build/diagnose_sheet.py` — the instrument — and read by **nothing else**.

---

## III. Why it is a disclosure and not a cap

Three measurements. Any one is sufficient; the first is decisive.

**1. A `good-*` reference plan is convicted FATALLY by this fault today, through a licence that
names its sibling.** `good-05-lobby-gallery-mansion` reads **0.2509 against 0.45**. Its style is
`italian-renaissance-revival`; the fault's `exceptions[]` name `italianate-american`. The licence
never reaches it — `oq/a-licence-matches-the-style-id-exactly-and-never-its-descendants`,
recorded and unruled. **The floor that conviction implies is 69.31 ft of depth against a drawn
38.64** — an 80% deeper house, demanded of a villa whose low pitch is the point of the style.

A finding a reader can weigh is one thing. A hard floor in the placer is another: it would make a
known-broken licence into a constraint that silently reshapes every house of that family, with the
reader never seeing the fault it came from.

**2. Only 3 of the 16 plan records have a style with a migrated pitch constraint at all.** On the
other 13 the floor is COULD NOT EVALUATE — the right answer, and the same refusal
`structure.roof_heights` already makes. But a cap would then govern a fifth of the corpus and be
silent on the rest, which is not a rule; it is a coincidence of which styles have been migrated.

**3. The pile alternative is worse**, measured in §I.

`oq/the-depth-a-roof-needs-is-known-and-cannot-be-enforced` carries all three, what a ruling must
decide, and what must not be done to close it.

## IV. What the building of it found — four defects, all mine, none found by reading

### IV.1 The third copy of a shared reading was wrong two ways, on its first run

`_style_roof_pitch` is spelled identically in `structure.py` and `roof.py` — roof.py's own comment
says *"byte-for-byte the same logic"*. This file needed a third because it must stay a leaf. My
first version **walked `constraints/*.json` filtering on `applies_to_styles`** where the real one
reads the constraints **on the style node**, and it **dropped the `direction == "between"` branch**
entirely. It returned `None` for `tidewater-georgian` where structure returns `8.0`.

Caught by comparing against the function it was copied from, not by reading it. The guard is now
agreement on **all 164 styles**, which is `test_grammar_agreement.py`'s mechanism for the citation
grammar's three spellings.

### IV.2 The signature invited the wrong number, and its own CLI supplied it

`wall_height_ft` first took a list of heights, and the CLI passed `floor_to_ceiling_ft` — **21 ft
on the Tidewater plan where the denominator wants the storey heights, 23.44** — an 11.6% error in
the direction that makes the floor look smaller and the house look better. It takes the **plan**
now and derives the heights itself. *A signature that lets a caller supply the wrong number is a
signature that will be handed the wrong number.*

### IV.3 The inversion disagreed with the forward rule, and the reason is the third test location

The only real correctness test is to solve the rule both ways and compare. On its first run:
**2 agree, 1 DISAGREE** — `good-03-parlor-drawing-room-house` read `ok` while the fault convicted
it at *"22.6 against between 33.7 and 39.8"*, a band in different units entirely.

`greek-revival-american` carries an **`exceptions[].bounds_test`**, which `core.check_measurements`
**SUBSTITUTES for the primary**. CLAUDE.md states it exactly: *a fault's tests live in three places
and the third is the one that bites*. The floor was right about the primary and the primary was not
the rule in force. It refuses with that reason now: **2 agree, 0 disagree, 14 unjudged.**

### IV.4 The tests wrote to a git-tracked corpus data file

To prove the threshold is read rather than transcribed, the first version **mutated
`faults/truss-flattened-pitch.json` in place** and restored it in a `finally`. A `finally` does not
run if the process is killed — and **this session alone had two container restarts mid-run**. The
failure mode is the worst kind this repository has: a corrupted threshold sitting in the corpus,
looking exactly like an authored change, in the file every other reader of that fault trusts.

`_FAULT` is a module-level constant precisely so it can be redirected. The tests now build a
mutated **copy** under `tmp_path` and monkeypatch the constant; nothing writes inside the
repository. Both mutations still bite, re-verified after the change.

**The refusal is tested, not asserted.** `TestItIsNotEnforced` fails if `geometry.py`,
`geometry_cp.py` or `compose.py` so much as names the module, and separately pins both shipped
plans' footprints byte-identical. An unenforced figure that quietly starts steering the placer is
the failure this package refused to risk, and there is no behaviour to observe — so the absence is
checked directly.

**Hot path**: the WP-11.9 aspect block adds **0.166 ms** to `plan_check.check()` over 25 rooms —
**0.049%** of the 338 ms evaluate the infrastructure audit measured as the whole server's bound.
`_load` goes through `build/modcache.py`, so the module is executed once per process, not per call.

## VI. What was deliberately not done

- **The cap.** §III. It becomes available when the licence question is ruled, and that ordering is
  the cheapest route — ruling it would except `good-05`, remove the 69 ft floor, and make a cap
  discussable on its merits.
- **`geometry_report.depth_floor`.** The number is in the instrument, not the record. Promoting it
  is a one-line change and is held until the cap question is settled, because a figure in the
  placer's own report is one import away from being read by the placer.
- **The three shape terms WP-11.10's package text names** — `WIDTH_W`, the flat 12, and the
  direction-to-the-square. `WIDTH_W` is already swept and refused at 0.0 with its measurement in
  a comment, and that comment ends *"Raise the weight only with a fresh sweep ON `auto`."* The
  flat 12 is a bare literal (`if got < lo * 0.85: s += 12`) and cannot be swept until it is
  named. Both are real work and neither is this package: the container question came first by
  ruling, and it consumed the package.
