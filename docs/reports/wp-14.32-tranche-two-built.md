## II. What was found, across the packages

The findings in each package's own report are not repeated here. What follows are the ones a later
reader is most likely to trip on.

1. **The worked house is refused by its own statement about a fire** (WP-14.21).
   - The massing puts the dining room's fire on the west gable. The dining room's own record puts it
     on an interior wall.
   - Followed as the massing says, the butler's pantry is forced to a side of at least 16 ft
     against its own 14 ft ceiling. A small CP-SAT model and a hand proof agree.
   - The remedy is a ruling, not code.
2. **The kit had two resolvers, and the MCP tools read the wrong one** (WP-14.26).
   - They disagreed on 38 of 15,908 (style, slot) pairs.
   - `tdl_get_slot` listed 228 styles that FORBID a slot as specifying it, and missed 6,380
     resolved specifiers.
   - One authority now; every moved pair is named in a fixture.
3. **A trim pack answered the machine client with nothing and a false column** (WP-14.18).
   - 0 assemblies for 27 stackless packs.
   - A 228 in "lower diameter" on packs with no column.
   - Two readings of one ceiling, 108 against 114.
4. **The fault card had labelled the wrong field "how it lives"** (WP-14.17). It was `frequency`.
5. **The assistant's prompt typed its own figures, and they had rotted** (WP-14.22).
   - It said 209 faults and 13 citation kinds.
   - The server read a key the client never sent, and would have cut its JSON mid-object.
6. **A hollow mark on the map took clicks only on its outline** (WP-14.27).
   - Three of six let a centre click fall through and pan the map.
   - This was found by re-cutting a walk check that could not fail.
7. **The copy ratchet's readers were blind to four shapes before it was emptied** (WP-14.31).
   - The shapes were a `title:` property, an irregular plural, adjectives before the noun, and a
     share.
   - Emptying the list first would have published a ban that did not hold.
8. **The width floor moved a button out of reach on the one surface it was not built for**
   (WP-14.30). On the bench at 1280, `re-solve` sat behind a scrollbar. The strip wraps now, fixed
   at the merge.

## III. What this package found by putting the tranche together

1. **Five parti descriptions carry build history, and the new record pages print them.**
   - The record page for `centre-passage-double-pile` shows *"WP-13.5 states the container … so
     `geometry.blocks_for` lays …"* to a reader.
   - It is the record's own prose: 5 of 21 partis, 0 of 17 groupings, 60 rooms or 40 massings.
   - Rewriting a record's description is an authorial decision, so it is filed:
     `oq/five-parti-descriptions-carry-build-history-a-reader-now-sees`.
2. **One real regression, and no lane could have seen it.**
   - WP-14.19 made `core.compose` ask the composer to check a brief's named records.
   - `tests/test_revise.py`'s fake composer lacked the method, so a test about MCP bounds went red
     with an `AttributeError` about its fixture.
   - WP-14.19 did not run that file. The integration run found it, and the fake now answers as the
     real composer does for a brief naming no parti.
3. **A shard run in a `git archive` extract turns every test that asks git a question red.**
   - The four shards ran in extracts, and seven tests plus `check_ids.py` read git's population
     (`git ls-files`, `git grep`) and failed there.
   - The seven tests are `test_duplicate_keys`, `test_duplicate_json_keys`, three in
     `test_citations`, and two in `test_open_question_ids`. The server suite's band-file guard
     failed the same way.
   - All of them pass in the real checkout, and were re-run there.
   - **Shard in `git worktree`s, not in archive extracts.** An extract is right for a byte-level
     control of an old tree and wrong for a run of the suite.
