# WP-14.9: the plate before the proof

*25 September 2026. Branch `wp-14.9`, taken from `0ad8b49`. Scope: PRD
`docs/prd/phase-14-the-dossier-and-the-journey.md` §E.4, §H.1–H.2, §I.6, §I.9, §I.10 and §J.1,
and `PLAN-OF-ACTION.md`'s WP-14.9 package. Every figure below was measured in this worktree,
most of them against a server started from it. Cite this report by filename.*

## What it is for

Lucas opened `trim-classical` on the Proportions surface and found "a bunch of text". The pack
holds three wall sections and three casings, 71 profiled members between them, and the page drew
none of them. The only plate it had was the order stack. The page also opened on *invariants ·
proved against the data* and put the pack's own conflicts at the foot.

A practitioner reads a pack the other way round:

1. what the pack is;
2. the drawing;
3. what goes wrong with it today;
4. the figures at my building;
5. whose figures they are;
6. who uses the pack;
7. where it comes from;
8. last, how it was checked.

This package puts the page in that order. It draws every pack whose record holds assemblies, and
it refuses out loud what the record cannot support.

## I. What was built

### The wall-datum plate

`components/AssemblyPlate.jsx` draws the plate. `plate/assemblyPlan.js` lays it out: pure, with no
React. It is built to four refusals.

- **It constructs nothing.** Each band is a face the server served (`build/profiles.py`, in model
  inches from the wall plane, y up). It reaches the page as that face's own `path` string and is
  placed by one `translate(…) scale(k, −k)` on its parent group, which is `OrderPlate`'s idiom.
  - There is no arc arithmetic, no sweep flag and no curve built in JavaScript (OQ 83).
  - `path[data-asm]` counts equal the served faces: **71 of 71** on `trim-classical`, drawn as
    53 in the wall-section frame and 18 in the casing frame.
- **It writes no word a glossary record should.** The in-frame key's three words are the records
  `member`, `wall-plane` and `part`, reached through `wordOf`, because a `Term` is a button and
  cannot sit inside an SVG. The same three stand as `Term`s over the numeral key. The foot line is
  the two records `figure-drawn-from-record` and `figure-drawn-upright`. Member names and heights
  on the leaders are the payload's, in `feetInches16`.
- **It draws what the record holds and nothing the record holds only as prose.** Every assembly
  stands upright from the wall plane, casings included. A casing is measured across its face, and
  the pack says so only in a member note
  (`oq/casings-are-measured-across-and-drawn-upright`). The foot line says *drawn upright*.
- **It lays out nothing itself.** `plate/assemblyLayout.js` (WP-14.6) decides the frames and
  their scale. `plate/assemblyPlan.js` decides every pixel inside a frame:
  - One leader per face, elbowed at a knee common to the assembly, so no two leaders cross. The
    screenshot harness measures **0 crossings** on both frames at both widths.
  - A label is lettered where the member is tall enough and its name fits the column at 9 px or
    more. Every other member takes a numeral that the key below names.
  - Where labels cannot all fit, lettering gives way first and the frame grows last. Measured:
    **0 labels outside a frame and 0 overlapping**, on both frames at both widths.
  - Part ticks run up the wall where there are sixty or fewer parts.
  - The scale bar is in the engine's notation.
  - Each frame sits in its own loupe (`PlateViewer`, labelled *the plate*).

### The page's decisions

`proportions/page.js` holds the page's decisions as pure functions.

- **`PAGE_SECTIONS` is one list and the page renders by walking it:** head, plate, conflicts,
  rules, authorities, used-by, sources, proof.
- **`pageSections` leaves out a section only when it has nothing to show.** The proof is never
  left out.
  - The proof is folded through prefs key `proof` and counted in all three states while folded:
    *invariants: 10 passed · 0 failed · 0 unjudged* on `trim-classical`.
- **`holds: null` is UNJUDGED.** `invariantMark` reads it through `judgment.js`, never on
  truthiness.
  - `ruleState` gives these no verdict: a rule the sources leave to the reader, a rule never
    evaluated, and a rule `out_of_calibration`. The chair rail reads *yours to judge · out of
    calibration*.
- **Everything the reader sets lives in the address.** `FILTER_SPEC` covers `q`, `ceiling`,
  `opening` and `diameter`.
  - The three measures are marked `widens`, so the strip does not count them as narrowing
    filters.
  - `requestFor` sends only the measures the address names.
  - `sliderAt` rests a slider at the address, else at the served value, clamped to its own range.
- **A bare `#/proportions` is an index.** No remembered or default pack is drawn. The walk reads
  six kind headings, each the glossary's word for that kind.

### The surface

`surfaces/Proportions.jsx` is re-cut to walk that list.

- **Conflicts come before the rules.** Their heading carries the payload's count.
- **The rules table has a header row:** *slot · dimension*, *derived rule*, *value*, *range*. It
  uses a fixed layout and fits its pane (finding 4).
- **The authorities heading counts the route's rows.** Its `data-authorities-count` is read from
  `/api/authorities/<order>`, never typed.
- **Users are grouped by how the pack reaches them:** *bound by this style*, *opted in*, and
  *named in the pack's applies_to, not reached by it*. Each relation is a glossary record.
- **`OrderPlate` and its `inches()` are byte-identical to `0ad8b49`**: 15,937 characters,
  compared, not eyeballed.

### Refused out loud

- **`data-refused="zones"`.** No 4 + 12 + 3 dimension string divides a wall into zones. The pack

## IV. Findings

### 1. The pack filter never matched an authority

The filter says *"Filter the proportion packs by name, kind or authority"*. It handed the list
route's `authority` to `matches`, which stringifies a field. The list route serves that field as
an object, so every pack's authority read as `[object Object]`. Measured on the live list route
with the parent's own `search/match.js`:

- **the query `object` matched all 57 packs**;
- **130 of the 146 distinct words in the packs' stated authorities reach more packs** once the
  authority is read as words.

This was pre-existing. `packGroups` reads the authority through `authorityWords` now, and a test
filters on an author's name.

### 2. The route serves `authority` in two shapes, and my first cut blanked the index on it

- **The list route serves an object on all 57 packs**: `{source, author, year, strength, note,
  url?}`.
- **The detail route serves a string on all 57.**

`d9a39c9` rendered `{p.authority}` in the index. React threw #31 on the first pack, so a bare
`#/proportions` drew nothing at all.

**The unit fixture agreed with the defect.** It carried `authority` as a string, which is the
detail route's shape and not the one the index receives. It is in the list route's shape now,
with a comment saying why.

`authorityWords` is the one reading of both shapes: the author, else the source, then the year.

- Three packs state no author and show their source: `brick-course`, `sash-light` and
  `storey-graduation`.
- Five state `author: "compiled"`: `trim-classical`, `trim-craftsman`, `room-vernacular`,
  `facade-classical` and `facade-picturesque`. The index prints that one word under their
  names, because it is the record's word. Whether "compiled" is a byline at all is a data
  question, and it is not changed here.

### 3. The page typed "five authorities" over a table the route fills with four on the Composite

`/api/authorities/<order>` serves 5 rows for Tuscan, Doric, Ionic and Corinthian, and **4 for
Composite**. The parent's heading was typed JSX.

The parent's walk made it worse: it waited on the text `five authorities` as its readiness
signal, which pinned the false count.

The heading now carries `data-authorities-count` read off the rows. The walk checks it against
the route: 5 of 5 on `gibbs-doric` and 4 of 4 on `vignola-composite`.

### 4. The rules table ran off its pane

`RecordLink`'s `.tdl-record-note` is `white-space: nowrap` (WP-14.8's token), so a slot's id
cannot wrap. Setting a fixed layout alone left the dimension header colliding with the next
column.

- Slot and dimension are one column now: the slot's link, and the dimension on the line below.
- The expression cell may break anywhere.
- **0 overflowing cells at 1440 and at 1280.** That was measured cell by cell on every lower
  section: conflicts, rules, used-by, sources and proof.

### 5. The detail route serves no `sources`

**55 of 57 pack records carry a `sources` list.** `trim-classical` has 11. The two without are
`greek-doric` and `moorish-arch`.

`GET /api/proportions/<id>` returns none of them: its keys are `assemblies`, `at`, `authority`,
`conflicts`, `derived_rules` and so on, with no `sources`.

So the page's sources section can show only the pack's authority lines. It is headed by the
glossary word *authority*, not "sources", so it does not claim a list it does not have. Serving
the list is a server payload change and is for the lead (§VII).

### 6. The shell is wider than a 1280 px window

At 1280×800, `document.documentElement.scrollWidth` is **1,380**. That is 100 px of sideways page
scroll, and the right rail is clipped. It holds on the Kit and the Fault Corpus as well, which
this package does not touch.

The Proportions pane sits inside it (`main` 800 px) and itself overflows nothing. It is the
shell's, and WP-14.13 is the shell.

### 7. Width sets the plate's scale, not the height

Three assemblies stand side by side, each with a label gutter, so the frame's width sets the
scale:

| Window | Wall sections (px/in) | Casings (px/in) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1440 | 3.39 | 44.1 |
| 1280 | 3.00 | 38.9 |
| 1680 | 4.56 | 64.8 |

At 3 px/in a 3/8" listel is about a pixel tall. That is why, on the wall-section frame at 1440,
**5 of the 53 members are lettered and 48 take a numeral**.

- It is correct behaviour: the numerals are keyed, and the loupe magnifies.
- A reader who expected names on the drawing should know the reason is the scale.
- Stacking the three sections vertically would buy scale at the cost of the side-by-side
  comparison WP-14.6's layout was ruled for.

### 8. The census

57 packs:

- **25** draw the order stack.
- **27** draw assemblies, over **235** served faces.
- **5** have no drawing (§I).

The walk holds all 27 band for face, *none short*.

### 9. The plate's base board is not the baseboard rule, and that is correct

The Georgian section letters *Base board (the plinth of the order) 6 7/8"*, while the rules
table says the baseboard is 9".

The rule is the whole base: plinth 6.9 + cap 1.62 + fillet 0.48 = **9.0**. The plate names the
plinth member by its own name.

Recorded because it reads like a disagreement on the page, and the first reader will ask.

### 10. The old walk block read JSX copy

It waited on `five authorities`, tested `/material modules/` and `/conflicts with building
today/` against `main`'s text, and read the first `main svg[role="img"]`. On a page carrying
several plates, that is whichever plate the DOM puts first.

The new block:

- takes kind headings from `/api/glossary`;
- takes counts from the API;
- scopes every plate read to `[data-pack-page=<id>] [data-section="plate"]`.
