# WP-14.2 — one house, one verdict

*Phase 14, package 2. 20 September 2026. Ruling of the same day: **the verdict is about the
PLACED house.** Cite this report by filename; a WP number names more than one package here.*

## What it is

`build/compose.py`'s per-candidate verdict — `counts`, `disqualified` and every score axis —
was measured on a record with its placement **stripped**. The revision loop that runs three
statements earlier is measured on the **placed** record. Both numbers were printed on one card
and nothing said which was which.

    -  declared = OP.strip_placement(copy.deepcopy(plan2))
    -  res2     = PC.check(declared, C)
    +  res_before = rv["critique_before"]["check"]
    +  res2       = rv["critique_after"]["check"]

`score_before` moves with it, computed from the loop's own BEFORE critique, so the invariant the
old code stripped to protect — that `score` and `score_before` are one instrument — survives. It
is a **different** instrument from the one earlier reports published, and every figure this
function emits moves with it.

## The defect, as the loop's own log states it

Measured on `briefs/family-georgian.json`, `candidates=2`, `revise=True`,
`revise_engine="heuristic"`, `revise_rounds=2`, `revise_budget_s=60.0`:

    side-hall-townhouse
      drawn key   [11, 55, 79, 22]  ->  [1, 58, 83, 22]
      card, before this package:  fatal 1   DISQUALIFIED — 1 fatal finding

**The loop cleared ten fatals and the card went on reading `fatal 1` and `DISQUALIFIED`, because
the number it read was one the loop never touches.**

And the stripped reading is not merely a different date on the same house. `plan_check.py:2583`:
with a placement the section, roof and elevation come from it; **with the placement stripped,
`build_elevation(plan)` solves a fresh throwaway heuristic placement inside the critic.** So the
verdict a reader acted on was computed on a fourth house — not the sheet's, not the loop's, not
the export's. The two elevations disagree on one record: `truss-flattened-pitch` is FATAL at
0.4488 against at-least 0.45 on the stripped reading and **absent** on the placed one;
`storeys-out-of-vertical-alignment` reads 38.556 in against 41.52 in.

`plan_check.py`'s own comment names this as WP-9.1's *two buildings in one verdict* — fixed for
records that carry a placement, and reintroduced here by a caller that strips on purpose.

**The reconciliation is the deliverable**, and it is exact on both candidates:

    counts        == drawn_key_after[:3]    [1, 58, 83]  and  [2, 65, 95]
    counts_before == drawn_key_before[:3]   [11, 55, 79] and  [3, 62, 91]

`tests/test_one_verdict.py` asserts it element for element. That is the assertion the old code
cannot satisfy, and it is the one to keep.

## The prediction in the approved plan was wrong, and the correction is the point

The plan said *"expect the fatal counts on the cards to RISE (declared 3 → drawn 11 on both
measured candidates)"*. **On the set this brief actually returns, they did not move at all** —
1 and 2 on both trees. That earlier 3 → 11 pair was measured on `centre-passage-double-pile` and
`foursquare-quadrant`, which this tree no longer returns
(`oq/the-composer-returns-a-set-that-satisfies-neither-must-have-room`). What moved hugely is
**serious, 23 → 58 and 30 → 65, and minor, 69 → 83 and 75 → 95**.

Publishing the predicted pair would have been quoting a figure about a returned set that no
longer exists. **Re-derive; do not re-read.**

## The cost, published rather than netted off

`score` FELL on both candidates, 56.5 → 50.7 and 52.8 → 49.7. **The houses did not get worse.**
The score started reading the placed house, whose drawn findings charge the `connections` axis
and which the stripped reading could not see. Any comparison of a score published before this
commit with one published after it is a comparison of two instruments.

**It costs no extra solve on the revised path.** `critique()` returns its full `check` and
`revise()` hands back both ends of it, so the placed reading was already in hand at both dates
and two `PC.check` calls were removed. One run each on a shared box: 44.4 s against 46.1 s —
enough to establish that nothing was added, not enough to claim 1.7 s was saved.

**It does cost one placement per BUDGET-SKIPPED candidate.** A candidate the set's budget does
not reach used to be returned with the declared verdict; it now gets `RV.revise(rounds=0,
place=True)` so that its card is of the same house as everybody else's. That is a real placement
— up to a 25 s proof on `auto`, a fraction of a second on the search — and it is the honest price
of the ruling: the alternative is a returned set in which some cards are of one house and some of
another, which is the defect this package removes wearing a budget's clothes.

## What is deliberately NOT done

**`compose(revise=False)` still publishes the declared verdict**, and says so in
`verdict_basis`. There is no placement to read on that path, and placing every candidate to
judge it is a cost nobody has measured.
`oq/the-opt-out-compose-still-judges-a-house-nobody-placed` records it with the three things a
ruling must decide.

**Selection is still on the declared reading, and `result["selected_on"]` now says so.** The
first `_sort_key` runs over all ~13 instantiated candidates before any of them is placed;
placing thirteen houses to choose two would pay for eleven placements nobody sees. So the
SELECTION is a judgement about the diagram and the VERDICT is about the house — two questions,
two readings, deliberately, and the result states both rather than leaving a reader to infer
that one instrument did both.

## What this package surfaced and did not cause

**The score cannot see ten fatals clear.** On `side-hall-townhouse` the loop went from eleven
placed fatals to one and **all eight axes came back bit-identical** — `connections` did not move
by one part in forty-two. `SEV_CREDIT` gives `fatal` and `serious` the same 0.0 and
`_axis_from_layers` takes the worst finding per room, so a room whose fatal clears while it
still carries a serious contributes exactly what it did before.

That behaviour is four packages old and `compose.py`'s module header states it as the design.
What is new is that a reader can see it: before this commit the card read `counts 1 → 1` beside
`score 56.5 → 56.5` and nothing looked strange. `oq/the-score-cannot-see-ten-fatals-clear`, with
the axis table and the three readings a ruling must choose between. **Not fixed here**: moving
`SEV_CREDIT` would reopen a stated ruling on one session's reading.

## Addendum — the fourth reader, found by the build (20 Sep 2026)

This report says four comments this package falsified were corrected in the commit that
falsified them, "three of them found by grepping the tree for `score_before` and
`declared record`". **There was a fourth site and it was not a comment — it was an
assertion**, and the full `check_all` found it fifty minutes in:

    tests/test_compose_revision.py::TestThePlacedLoopOnTheReturnedCandidates
        ::test_score_and_score_before_are_one_instrument
    AssertionError: assert 23 == 58

It re-checked the returned plan STRIPPED of its placement and required `counts` to match,
under a docstring reading *"Both are score_candidate on the DECLARED record; a drawn finding
never enters the composite"* — the retired contract, stated and enforced.

**It was the loudest hit either grep would have produced.** That file carries `score_before`
seven times and the phrase `declared record` in the very line that was wrong. The sweep did not
miss it for want of a matching phrase; it missed it because the sweep was read as a sweep for
PROSE and the hits it wanted were comments. *Verify every consumer means every one* (WP-12.9),
with `tests/` inside the word every.

**Attributed before it was touched**, on a `git archive` checkout of `a0c4fca`, this phase's
own base: it PASSES there, so it is this package's. (The same control run cleared the other
unattributed red of that build — `test_refusal.py::test_the_dxf_selftest_tells_a_refusal_from_a_failure`
fails character-identically on the base and is pre-existing.)

**RE-CUT, not re-pinned.** The subject — score and score_before are one instrument — is
unchanged; only which house the instrument reads moved. So:

- the AFTER end is re-derived independently: re-check the returned plan **as it stands** and
  match `counts` element for element. Measured on `family-georgian`, both candidates reproduce
  exactly (fatal/serious/minor/info **1, 58, 83, 26** and **2, 65, 95, 24**) and the stripped
  reading does not (1, 23, 69, 28 and 2, 30, 75, 24);
- the BEFORE end is held by `drawn_key_before[:3]` against `counts_before`, the cheapest
  independent reader of a record that no longer exists by the time the test runs. The two ends
  have different strengths and the docstring says so, rather than letting one green tick read
  as two.

**AND THE LINE ABOVE THE ONE THAT FIRED WAS VACUOUS.** The old body asserted the fatal counts
agree before asserting the serious ones do, and on this brief the two readings agree on fatal
for both candidates — 1 and 2 — so only the `serious` line could ever have reported the ruling.
That is WP-11.15's *a fixture where both branches return the same number guards neither*, one
line from the assertion that caught it. The whole `counts` dict is asserted now, and **the
premise that the two readings differ at all is asserted too**, with a message naming the remedy:
where they coincide, the re-derivation passes under either instrument and the guard is
distinguishing nothing.

Two mutations, both INJECTING the defect rather than disabling the guard — `counts` reverted to
the stripped reading, and `counts_before` taken from the AFTER critique — each asserted to have
LANDED before its colour was believed and each restored byte-for-byte under a hash check. Both
bite, **and on different lines** (the re-derivation and the before-end key reconciliation), which
is what says the two ends are guarded separately rather than one shielding the other.
