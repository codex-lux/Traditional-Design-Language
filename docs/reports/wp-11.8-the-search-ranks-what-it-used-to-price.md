# WP-11.8 — the search ranks what it used to price

*5 September 2026. Ruled by Lucas on four questions: teach the heuristic the band hard; split
the CP budget into a batch one and an interactive one; build the N-level placer (a later
package); keep `auto` in the fixture generator and correct its README.*

WP-11.7 made the room's own proportion band a hard pin in the PROOF. This asks the question
`PLAN-OF-ACTION.md` reserves for WP-11.8 — whether the search follows or is demoted — and the
answer is that it follows, by ranking rather than by refusing.

---

## I. The measurement that framed it

The search has read `shape_band` since WP-9.4, as a soft charge: `SHAPE_W = 6.0` per ratio point
past a room's own ceiling. A candidate can win while paying it, which is the same shape as
`level_score`'s flat-12 width charge that CLAUDE.md records as *"the search will place a room
below the floor of its own band and say nothing."*

Measured over the sixteen shipped plans, `engine="heuristic"`:

> **77 of 219 placed rooms — 35% — were drawn outside the band their own record states.**

Worst: a dining room at **5.0 : 1** against a ceiling of 1.8; a guest bedroom at 3.33 against
1.35; a library at 3.42 against 1.6.

---

## II. Rejection alone is worthless here, and that was measured before it was written

The obvious reading of "hard" is to reject a candidate that leaves any room outside its band.
Measured on the Tidewater ground floor over 250 slicings:

| rooms out of band | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| candidates | 18 | 21 | 45 | 87 | 57 | 18 | 3 | 1 |

**Zero of 250 conform.** The best has three. So a hard rejection has nothing to accept, and
"hard" cannot mean "refuse" in an engine that has no conflict set and cannot decline to place.

What it can mean, and what was built: **the band is the FIRST key of the acceptance and the
score is the second.** A candidate leaving fewer rooms outside their band beats one that scores
better, always. The band governs the CHOICE rather than the feasibility.

One detail is load-bearing. The candidate loop carries a span-charge prune —
`if part >= best["_raw"]: continue` — and that prune is only sound WITHIN a violation tier. Left
as it was, a candidate with fewer rooms out of band would be skipped for scoring worse, and the
first key would not be a key at all. It is tiered now, and a test says so.

---

## III. What it costs, and the third key that was built to avoid the cost and refused

| | out of band | under band | fatal | serious | minor | relax | spans over capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 77 / 219 | 15 | 113 | 745 | 1031 | 64 | 13 (worst 40.0 ft) |
| ceiling only, then score | 28 / 219 | 21 | 129 | 695 | 1006 | 91 | — |
| **both bands, then score** | **30 / 219** | **13** | **136** | **681** | **992** | 86 | **25 (worst 60.0 ft)** |
| doors, ceiling, score | 68 / 219 | — | 131 | 780 | 1022 | 82 | — |

The second row is what this package first built and the third is what it ships; §IV is why.
Against the baseline: slivers fall by 61%, rooms under their own area floor by 2, serious
findings by 64 and minor by 39 — and **fatal findings rise by 23.**

**Every one of the twenty-three is `unreachable` in the drawn layer.** Adjacency fatals (38) and
fault fatals (17) do not move at all. The cause is geometric and obvious once seen: a squarer room
shares less wall with its neighbours, so its declared doors lose the run they need.

**And the same geometry has a second, structural price, which is the sharpest thing in this
report and is NOT a finding the critic reports.** Over-capacity clear spans go **13 → 25 and the
worst 40.0 → 60.0 ft**; relaxations 64 → 86. A squarer room puts fewer cuts on the bay module,
and the only way this slicer creates a bearing line is to cut on it — WP-7.4 established exactly
that, in the other direction, when its span term pulled cuts ONTO the grid and took relaxations
9 → 7. So a first key that rewards squareness works against WP-7.4's own mechanism. Two things
are worth saying plainly about it: the capacity itself and `bearing_lines`' 0.75 ft tolerance are
UNTOUCHED, which CLAUDE.md forbids moving in as many words; and `plan_check` has no span finding
at all, so none of these twelve appears anywhere a reader of the sheet would see them — that is
OQ 98, and it means this cost is disclosed here and nowhere else.
It is a real argument against the ranking and it is recorded as one rather than netted off.

So a third key was built to protect the more serious fact — a count of doors the placement cannot
seat, ranked FIRST, above the band. It is the third row of that table: **worse on every axis at
once.** The reason is that the count is a PROXY for the drawn layer's rule and not the rule: the
drawn layer seats openings through `openings.place`, which reads walls and obstructions, while
the proxy compared a shared run against `required_wall_ft`. Optimising a proxy optimises the
proxy. It was deleted rather than kept as a reported number, because a figure that looks like the
drawn layer's and is not would be read as the drawn layer's.

### What settled the trade instead

Not a weight, and not a third key — a fact about what the corpus can DO with each defect:

```
unreachable + adjacent_placed  ->  add-the-grammar-door
drawn-proportion-above-band    ->  None  ("a placement outcome; the record already
                                          states the right size")
```

**All 81 of the resulting `unreachable` fatals carry `adjacent_placed`** — 58 of 58 before and
81 of 81 after, re-derived after the key changed rather than carried over — so
`critique._intended_move` answers every one with a named move, and the revision loop is on by
default wherever a product is made (the 1 Sep ruling). A room drawn outside its own band has
**no move at all**.

The change therefore trades **49 defects nothing can fix for 23 the corrective loop is built to
clear.** That is the argument, and it is a fact about the move registry rather than a preference.

## IV. The key counts the band in both directions, and a WP-7.4 guard is why

The key shipped as the proportion CEILING alone and the full suite refused it — not on a pin, on
an assertion WP-7.4 had left behind in as many words:

> *"the dining room is under band again on the hill-climb; WP-7.4's score terms put it in band
> (under_band 2 → 1, the stair hall taking its place) and something has undone that"*

It had. Ranking the ceiling above the score took the corpus's under-band rooms **15 → 21** and put
`spec-builder-colonial`'s dining room back at 87 sf against a 122 sf floor, 29% short. A ranking
that honours one band by breaking the other is not the room's own record winning; it is one term
winning, and the guard that caught it exists because a previous package had already paid to fix
exactly that room.

So the key counts both — `band_violations` for the proportion ceiling and `under_band`, the same
reader the report uses, for the area floor. The third row of §III's table is the result: two more
rooms over their ceiling, eight fewer under their floor, and fewer serious and minor findings than
the ceiling-only key on top of that. **Under-band is now better than the baseline as well as
better than the intermediate**, which the ceiling-only key could not say.

Cost: about 7% of the search's wall clock (0.35 → 0.38 s on the Tidewater plan at 250
candidates), and seven more fatals, all `unreachable` and all carrying `adjacent_placed`.

**The intermediate is kept in the code comment and in this table on purpose.** It is a correct
measurement of a wrong key, and a reader who deletes the area half will reproduce it.

## V. The residual is disclosed, by both engines, from one place

No pool of 250 has ever reached zero, so *"held every band"* and *"held as many as it could"*
look identical in a drawing. `geometry_report.shape_band` states the count and what it means —
and it lives in `_disclose`, the one function both record writers call, because a figure added to
one writer and not the other is the exact defect that function exists for (OQ 40's disclosure
shipped in `write_record` alone for two phases; WP-11.6 found it).

It reads differently on the two engines and both readings are honest: on CP a non-zero count
means shape pins were downgraded and `downgraded_shape_pins` names them; on the search it is the
best the pool offered. Room types stating no band are unjudged and are not counted.

## VIII. Shape was not bought with area, and the sheet is what said otherwise

The obvious suspicion about a first key that rewards squareness is that it buys shape with area:
a room made square by growing it is not the room the record declares. The suspicion arrived by
LOOKING at the sheet — the Tidewater upper passage is drawn 26′-10″ × 20′-1″, 539 sf, against a
declared 12 × 30 = 360 — and the report very nearly went out saying so.

Re-derived instead of re-read (WP-9.5's rule), on `engine="heuristic"`, over the sixteen shipped
plans, against a pristine `git archive HEAD` checkout for the BEFORE:

| | rooms with a declared area | drawn ≥10% off it |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 231 | **164** |
| ceiling only, then score | 231 | 159 |
| both bands, then score | 231 | **148** |

**Area divergence FALLS.** And the very room that raised the suspicion is the sharpest case
against it:

| upper passage | drawn | area | aspect |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 14.65 × 40.08 | 587 sf | 2.74 : 1 |
| after | 26.85 × 20.08 | **539 sf** | **1.34 : 1** |

It was 587 sf before and is 539 sf now — closer to its declared 360 and less than half as long.
The 539 was read off the new sheet, compared against the record, and attributed to this package;
it is 48 sf of an overrun this package REDUCED. **A number read off the deliverable is not a
measurement of the change**, and nothing but running the old code on the same plan says which.

What is true and is not this package's to fix: the divergence is large and still large. 148 of
231 rooms are drawn a tenth or more off their declared area, because the declaration
reaches the search as a scalar in `level_score` and nothing bounds a side — Part IV.B's fact (i),
`WIDTH_W = 0.0`, unchanged here. The first key ranks SHAPE and the corpus states the ceiling; no
key ranks SIZE and no record states a tolerance. That is B2's question, not this one's.

The ground passage is the other room worth naming, because it looks like a defect and is not:
drawn 9.9 × 40.1, **4.05 : 1**, and counted as conforming — `centre-passage` states a ceiling of
5.0 in its own record, which is the corpus's own reading of what a passage is. The key does what
the corpus says, including where the corpus is permissive.

## X. Five guards fell, and not one of them was a pin to bump

The full suite refused this package seven times, over two full runs. One was a real defect, one forced §IV's design,
and three were guards pinning an OUTCOME the placer is free to change. None was fixed by moving a
number.

**A furniture rectangle was judged at full precision and written rounded.** `_overlaps` and
`_inside` tested the computed rectangle; the record then wrote `round(v, 3)`. On
`good-03-parlor-drawing-room-house` the foyer's two hall chairs were computed to abut a
coat closet exactly and were WRITTEN 33.574 against 33.573 — one thousandth of a foot of overlap,
in the record, on a sheet. A second record of one fact, which is what this file's own WP-7.4 note
two functions below is about. `furniture.to_record()` now rounds ONCE, before the collision and
containment tests, so what was judged is what is written; corpus-wide **overlapping items 1 → 0**
and the chairs are a named refusal instead of a drawn collision (placed 545 → 544, unplaced
120 → 121). It had been latent since the pass was written and only a placement that moved
exposed it.

**Four guards pinned an outcome, and each said so in its own comment while doing it.**

| guard | pinned | what actually happened |
|---|---|---|
| `test_the_fixture_run_turns_the_corner_before_refusing` | four fixtures on ≥2 walls of `spec-builder-colonial`'s primary bath | the bath is drawn 20.00 × 12.44 rather than 9 × 18; one 20 ft wall holds all four honestly |
| `test_spec_builder_plan_flags_a_span_over_capacity` + the bearing diagram's `class="bad"` | that plan flagging an over-capacity span | that plan has none now (21 wall lines, 13 bearing, 0 over) while the corpus has 25 |
| `test_every_figure_in_needs_is_the_figure_the_sentence_states[tidewater]` | that some refusal states a figure | every refusal on that plan is now "no shared wall", which states none |
| `test_the_two_new_claims_land_and_the_landing_check_finally_fires` | `landing` kept, `landing-off-well` firing | 3 of 5 claims still land; the SET changed, and `landing-off-well` hangs off the kept list by design |

The span pair is the one to read twice, because the honest fix looks exactly like the forbidden
one: CLAUDE.md says *"do not loosen the 20 ft capacity or `bearing_lines`' 0.75 ft tolerance to
make the number smaller"*, and neither moved. The plan got better and the corpus got worse; the
guards now pin both halves — the mechanism must fire somewhere, and `spec-builder-colonial` must
stay clean — so a regression in either direction shows.

Each is now written against the behaviour instead of the outcome: the corner is driven on a
synthetic 9 × 14 ft room no placement can outgrow; the figure test reports COULD NOT EVALUATE for
a plan whose refusals carry no number, with a new pair-wise guard so both plans cannot go quiet at
once; and the landing is moved onto its stair by hand — into the strip of the hall clear of the
well, which is the case `plan_check.py`'s own comment says the rule exists for — so the rule is
exercised whatever any engine does. The landing's broken claim is asserted BROKEN, so the trade
cannot reverse unnoticed in either direction. Every replacement was mutation-checked with the
mutation asserted to have landed.

**And one assertion was retired rather than replaced.** `test_the_spec_colonials_squeezed_dining_
room_is_named` required the worst under-band room to be ≥20% short — a FLOOR on how bad the engine
is, written when the engine was that bad. It is 6% now, on one room instead of three. The
invariant that class exists for is that the trade is REPORTED; the size of it on any one plan is
the placer's to change, so the figure is pinned as a ceiling that fails on a regression and not on
an improvement.

## XI. What this package does NOT do

**The budget split reaches `solve()` and stops there.** `build/revise.py` and
`build/critique.py` each carry their own `time_limit_s=25.0` default and neither was moved: they
are the revision loop's knobs, they are bounded by `revise_budget_s` above them, and changing what
a compose costs is not this package's to do. Named here rather than left to be discovered, because
after this package "the budget" is two numbers and those two call sites are a third.

`SHAPE_W` is unchanged at 6.0 — the ranking replaces the need to sweep it, and a sweep would have
been the alternative ruling. The slicer is untouched: it still produces the same 250 candidates
and the package only chooses better among them, so the ceiling on this approach is whatever the
pool contains (best of 3 on the Tidewater ground floor, and 30 corpus-wide). Teaching
`slice_rect` to AIM at the band is the obvious next lever and is not built. CP is untouched
except for the budget constant. The N-level placer, ruled to be built, is its own package.
