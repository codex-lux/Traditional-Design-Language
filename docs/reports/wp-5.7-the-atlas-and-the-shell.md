# WP-5.7 — The atlas, and giving the shell's proportions back to the reader

*26 August 2026. Branch `claude/map-ui-layout-improvements-spu56a`.*

## What was asked for

Raised by Lucas against a screenshot of the Phylogeny's map reading, zoomed in:

> the map is VERY crude and doesn't take well to zooming in since the resolution does not
> scale up as you zoom in.
>
> There should be a button to make the atlas temporarily full screen to get the maximum
> viewing space.
>
> the rail should have a collapse/expand option as should the navigation bar on the left of
> the screen to get better access to screen real estate.
>
> Allow windows to be adjusted by pulling at the margins to provide the optimal
> customizable screen experience

Four asks. All four are built. The first turned out to be two separate defects wearing one
symptom, and the second of them is the more interesting.

---

## 1. "The resolution does not scale up as you zoom in"

### 1a. The pen was being scaled with the drawing

The screenshot shows a dark band running diagonally across the plate and pale slabs behind
it. Those are not a coastline rendered coarsely. **They are a coastline and two lineage
arcs rendered at the correct shape and the wrong width.**

`MapView` set `vectorEffect="non-scaling-stroke"` on the `<g>` wrapping the land paths and
on the `<g>` wrapping the arcs. **`vector-effect` is not an inherited property.** It
reached neither. So `strokeWidth={0.7}` on the land was 0.7 *degrees of longitude* of ink,
and `strokeWidth={1.5}` on a cascade-carrying arc was 1.5 degrees — about a hundred miles.
At the home view that is two or three pixels and looks deliberate. At the six degrees the
old `MIN_W` allowed, it is a seventy-pixel shoreline and a hundred-and-fifty-pixel arc:
the drawing stops being lines and becomes bands, which is exactly the image that was
reported.

Every zoom multiplied the pen. The fix is one attribute per element, not per group.

This is the **third** instance in this codebase of a per-element SVG property set
somewhere it does not reach — `tests/test_drawn_labels.py` exists because a `font-size`
presentation attribute loses to a class rule, and both plan renderers had the same class of
bug. The walk now asserts the general form for the map: **no stroked mark in the atlas may
carry a `stroke-width` without a `vector-effect` of its own.**

### 1b. And then, genuinely, there was only one outline

Underneath that, the original complaint was also true. The basemap was Natural Earth 110m
simplified with Ramer-Douglas-Peucker at **0.55 degrees** — 888 points, one outline, held
at every scale from a hemisphere to a county. A vector that cannot gain detail as it grows
is a raster with extra steps.

So there are three outlines now, generated by `workbench/scripts/make_coastlines.py` and
selected by `app/src/surfaces/phylo/coastTiers.js`:

| tier | source | tolerance | rings | points | raw | gzip | fetched |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| coarse | Natural Earth 110m | 0.35° | 102 | 1,946 | 21 KB | 9 KB | eagerly, in the main bundle |
| medium | Natural Earth 50m | 0.06° | 478 | 14,442 | 154 KB | 60 KB | below 70° of longitude |
| fine | Natural Earth 10m | 0.012° | 2,262 | 116,623 | 1.17 MB | 363 KB | below 16° of longitude |

The finer two are dynamic imports, so Vite splits them into their own chunks and a reader
who never zooms never pays for them. `MIN_W` moved from 6 to 3 — set by what the finest
outline can honestly draw (about five screen pixels of error across a 1,200px pane), not by
taste. `MAX_W` moved from 300 to 340, and the clip to the North Atlantic is gone: 274
gazetteer entries span the globe, and the old box stopped at 52°E, so any mark east of it
sat over blank paper.

**The honesty this needed.** While a finer tier is in flight, or if it failed to arrive,
the map is drawing something coarser than the scale it is set to. `useCoastline` reports
`drawn`, `wanted`, `pending` and `failed` as four separate things, and the legend prints
the difference: *"Fetching the fine outline for this scale; what is drawn is still the
medium one"*, or *"The fine outline could not be fetched, so this is the medium one at a
scale it cannot carry — the facets are the simplification, not the shore."* A coarse
coastline shown where a fine one was asked for is a drawing that has not been evaluated at
this scale, and reporting it as the fine one would be the same error the fault corpus
exists to prevent.

**Culling, because a sharper coastline must not be a slower one.** The generator writes a
bounding box per ring and `visibleRings` drops what the view box would clip anyway — at the
fine tier, a zoomed-in view mounts a handful of paths instead of 2,262. The boxes are
rounded *outward*: drawing one ring too many costs nothing, and the other error is a hole in
the world.

**The graticule follows the scale too.** It was fixed at ten degrees, which is two lines at
continental zoom and — once three-degree views became reachable — none at all. `graticule.js`
picks the coarsest step that still puts five lines across the view. It shipped inverted in
this very package (it took the coarsest step whose count *fitted*, which is always the
coarsest rung, which drew nothing) and a browser probe counting `<line>` elements caught it;
`src/graticule.test.mjs` catches it now.

**And the wheel actually prevents default.** React attaches `wheel` at the root as a passive
listener, so `preventDefault()` inside a JSX `onWheel` never worked — the map zoomed *and*
the page scrolled under it. It is a native non-passive listener now. There are `+` and `−`
buttons as well: a trackpad with no wheel gesture, a touch screen and a keyboard had no way
to zoom at all.

### What the generator found, and what the test found

Delta-encoding the paths (relative `l` commands on a quantised grid) took the fine tier from
1.9 MB to 1.17 MB and its gzip from 658 KB to 363 KB. The first version dropped the comma
between a pair whenever the second number began with a decimal point — correct after
`-2.58`, **wrong after `2`**, because `l2.36` is one number and not two. It would have
shipped a coastline whose every point after the first integer delta was displaced.
`src/coastlines.test.mjs` parses the deltas back into points rather than pattern-matching
the bytes, and it caught it before the build did.

Two other things the generator handles rather than draws: Natural Earth cuts Eurasia and
Antarctica at the antimeridian, which leaves a segment stepping from +180 to −180 *inside*
an ordinary ring (a hairline ruled straight across the plate); and Antarctica is dropped
outright, because it is a third of the ink in a world outline and there is no gazetteer
entry south of 42°.

## 2, 3, 4 — The shell's proportions

The left rail was 236px and the AI rail 344px at every window size and on every errand.
That is 580px of permanent furniture — a third of a laptop screen — held whether you were
navigating or reading a map.

`state/layout.js` is a fifth external store (after `planDoc`, `session`, `draftDoc`, `nav`),
holding a width and a folded flag per pane, in localStorage. **Deliberately not in the URL:**
`#/kit/craftsman/cornice` is a citation, and a citation that also carried the sender's rail
width would be handing the reader the sender's monitor.

It holds **eight panes**: the two shell rails, the Phylogeny's taxon record, and the five
surface index panels that were fixed numbers written into their own JSX — the Fault Corpus's
list at 330, the Kit's record at 340, the Plan Workbench's findings at 430, the pack list at
250, the Transcription record at 360. Every one of those was chosen against one window, and
is exactly the column a reader wants narrower while reading a plan and wider while reading a
fault. `components/PullPane.jsx` applies the splitter to them, as a component rather than
five copies because the order is easy to get backwards: the handle belongs on the pane's
inner edge, so it goes after a left-hand pane and before a right-hand one, and `grows` has to
agree with which side it is on.

**The five index panes pull but do not fold, and `PANES` says so with `foldable`.** Folding
was asked for on the rails, where the pane is chrome you may not want at all. A surface's
index is the surface's own subject: a Fault Corpus with the fault list folded away is not a
decluttered Fault Corpus, it is a broken one. The refusal is stated in `setCollapsed` as well
as in `dragTo`, because `toggle`, the key map and a stored preference from a hand-edited
localStorage all reach the first without going through the second.

- **Fold.** Both rails, and the Phylogeny's taxon record. A folded pane becomes a 26px
  spine with its name turned up it and a chevron — not nothing. A fold whose opener lives
  somewhere else is a trapdoor.
- **Keys.** `[` and `]`. `keys.js` said a fourth key would need a reason; 580px of
  furniture between the reader and the drawing is one, and it is written down there.
- **Pull.** `components/Splitter.jsx` is a real `role="separator"`: pointer-captured drag,
  arrow keys to nudge, shift-arrows to stride, Home to reset, Enter to fold, double-click
  for the shipped width. Ten pixels of grab over a one-pixel rule — the ink the drawing
  wants and the target the hand wants are not the same object. Hauling a pane below 60% of
  its floor folds it and *keeps* the width, so unfolding returns the size that was chosen.
- **Clamp on read, not just on write.** A width stored on a 2560px display must not strand
  the nav off the side of a laptop, so `clampAll` runs on mount and on every window resize:
  no pane over a third of the window, and the two rails together never over half.
- **Full screen.** An `ActionChip` on the atlas that takes the masthead *and* both rails,
  and asks the browser for its own full screen as well — two different wins, and the
  browser's may be refused without costing the shell's. Escape leaves; so does F11, because
  `fullscreenchange` is watched and the shell follows it. `layout.full` is the one piece of
  layout state that is **not** persisted, which is the whole meaning of "temporarily".

One thing found while looking: the map's legend is `flex: none` and says a great deal, so on
an 860px window it took 395px — the drawing, whose whole subject is extent, got less than
half its own surface. The prose is load-bearing and none of it is cut; it is capped and
scrolled, and the drawing gets the guaranteed share.

## What was deliberately not done

- **No map library, no tiles, no network at run time.** The app still has two dependencies.
  The tiers are generated once, offline, and vendored.
- **The gazetteer did not move an inch.** It is still interface furniture keyed on prose
  region names, and not one number in it may migrate into `styles/*.json`. A finer coastline
  is a finer *drawing aid*; it does not make a placement any better sourced than the prose it
  came from, and the legend still says so.
- **No fourth tier.** Natural Earth 10m is a 1:10,000,000 dataset; simplifying it less would
  buy bytes, not accuracy, and `MIN_W` is set where the data stops rather than where the
  reader's patience does.
- **`layout` is not in the URL, and filters are not in `layout`.** WP-5.6's rule stands:
  filters live in the query string, places live in the hash, preferences live in
  localStorage. Three stores, three lifetimes, no overlap.
- **Folding was not extended past the three panes that are chrome.** The five surface index
  panels pull and stop at their floor. See above: a fold is right for a rail and wrong for a
  subject.
- **Nothing was made to remember a per-surface layout.** One width per pane, shared across
  every visit. A reader who wants the Kit's record wide and the Fault Corpus's list narrow
  gets that, because they are different panes; a reader who wants the *rail* wide on the
  Kit and narrow on the map does not, and nothing suggests they would.

## New open question

**OQ 72** — the fine coastline tier is 363 KB gzipped, fetched whole the first time a
reader crosses 16° of longitude, and 2,262 rings of it exist to serve a corpus whose hearths
sit in perhaps forty places. A per-region split would fetch a fiftieth of it, at the cost of
clipping rings at sector boundaries — which draws a stroked seam through open water unless
the clip is done properly. Whether that is worth building, or whether one 363 KB fetch per
session is simply the right answer for a self-hosted instrument, is not obvious and is not
decided here.
