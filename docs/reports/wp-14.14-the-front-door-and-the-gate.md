# WP-14.14: the front door and the Gate

*25 September 2026. Branch `wp-14.14`, taken from `81e04fb` (WP-14.13's site map on top of
`0ad8b49`). Scope: PRD `docs/prd/phase-14-the-dossier-and-the-journey.md` §0, §C.3, §F, §I.1–I.4,
§I.10, §I.12 and §J's Overview row. Every figure below was measured in this worktree. Cite this
report by filename.*

## What it is for

Phase 14's first finding was that nothing said what this is. A practitioner arriving at the
workbench met a paragraph written for an AI agent and eleven buttons whose labels and
descriptions were typed into the page. This package makes the landing say what the corpus is,
who it is for and what it is not, show the two ways in, draw the whole site map once, and label
the worked example, stopping where the corpus stops. **Every word on it is a glossary record's
and every figure is the API's.** The password screen now says one sentence too, taken from the
one record a signed-out visitor may read. The page says nothing when that read fails.

## I. What was built

**The front door, `surfaces/Overview.jsx`** (the filename is kept). It is drawn from three
readers, and it adds no sentence of its own beyond the search button's label and `⌘K`:

- **What this is.** `about-tdl`'s term and definition.
- **Who it is for.** `about-tdl.readers`: the production builder, the plan-development lead, and
  the architect and the classicist. That is VISION IX's practitioner list, and it has no
  homeowner line. §J forbids one, citing `VISION.md:359`, and the walk asks the page as well as
  the record.
- **Two entrances.** These are navModel's own `style` and `brief` items, each with its surface
  record's `what`. Beside the first, the style in hand is **offered** as a link (`in-hand`, the
  guided example when nothing is held) and never opened (§I.10, *memory only offers*). Beside the
  second, where a house is under way, the journey's own resume link sits under the
  `on-the-bench` term.
- **The map.** `components/TwoSpineMap.jsx`. The two spines and the library are drawn as ruled
  rows of links, with one row per navModel group and navModel's own ids, hrefs, step numbers and
  live figures. It is built from anchors, not an SVG engine: a real `<a href>` can be opened in a
  new tab, copied and read by a screen reader.
- **The worked example.** It shows `guided-example`'s definition, its two citations as
  `RecordLink`s (the Tidewater Georgian dossier and the example brief), and **where it stops, in
  the record's words** (§II.1).
- **What it holds.** One row per `counts.by_rank` key, named by the record the `style.rank`
  binding gives, then slots, proportion packs (summed over their kinds), faults, massings and
  groupings, each named by its own term. The ontology version stays in the section's eyebrow and
  the search button stays in main.
- **What it is not.** `about-tdl.is_not`.

`prefs.markSeen('front-door')` runs on mount. The key is the one `ColdLinkBanner` reads (§I.12,
WP-14.13): once the front door has been seen, the banner stops offering it. `DOORS` is retired,
and so is the landing's use of `core.overview().what_this_is`. The API still serves that string
to the MCP tools and to anyone else who reads it; it is just no longer this page's paragraph.

**Three pure modules** hold the page's logic so that it can be tested without React:

- `frontdoor/frontDoor.js` provides `aboutView`, `guidedView`, `rankRows`, `total`,
  `entranceItems` and `resumeView`. Each has a loading state and a missing-record state as well
  as the ready one.
- `frontdoor/spineMap.js` provides `mapRows(model)`. It is a flattening of navModel's output
  that copies ten named fields from each item and adds `depth` and `parent`. It holds no list of
  its own.
- `frontdoor/aboutLine.js` provides `lineOf` and `aboutLine`. Together they give the Gate's
  sentence, which is either the record's definition or `null`. The function never rejects.

**The Gate, `Gate.jsx`.** One effect reads `api.glossaryTerm('about-tdl')` through
`aboutLine`, and `{about && <p data-about-tdl>{about}</p>}` draws it under the heading. No other
route is asked while signed out. When the read fails the Gate says nothing: no error text, and no
fallback sentence.

**One glossary record gained a field.** `glossary/guided-example.json` gains `more`: *"The
example stops at the brief. Its house has no plan that places: the plan written for it is
refused, because its placement breaks facts of its own house type, so no sheet or export of it is
drawn and the plan step names the conflict instead."* Its basis now quotes two more sentences,
from `build/revise.py` and `build/typefacts.py`. `check_glossary.py` verifies 480 quotations,
and this record's count went from 3 to 5.

**Tests.** `src/frontDoor.test.mjs` has fourteen tests, uses `node:fs` only, and imports no
React. `src/glossary.test.mjs` learns one more shape, `glossaryTerm('…')`, so the id the Gate
asks for is seen by WP-14.8's scanner. There is a fixture row for that shape. The app suite goes
from **384 to 398**, all green.

**The walk.** The Overview block is re-cut to §J.1 and a front-door-and-Gate block is appended.
Both are described in §III.

## II. What was found

### 1. "Record words" for where the example stops had nowhere to live, and one place they could not go

The brief asks the front door to say plainly, **in record words**, that the worked house has no
plan that places. No record said so. The measurement lives in
`oq/the-worked-house-has-no-plan-that-places`. `check_glossary.py`'s rule 4 refuses a glossary
basis that names `docs/open-questions.md` or anything under the register, and it is right to:
an open question is a question, not a source.

The sentence is therefore a new `more` on `guided-example`. Its basis quotes the code that makes
it true:

- `build/revise.py`: *"refused -> refused on a lower key is accepted, which is the Tidewater
  plan's own case"*
- `build/typefacts.py`: *"a placement that breaks a hard fact of the type is REFUSED, not"*
  drawn

The sentence reaches the front door and the Glossary's term page in the same words.

**The cost, stated in the question's own amendment.** The record is now a second place where the
answer to that question lives, and nothing ties the two together. `check_basis` checks that the
quoted sentences are in those modules. That stays true whether or not the house ever places, so
the `more` could become false without any check noticing. Whichever answer closes the question
must revise this record in the same commit.

The answer the front door gives is the question's own answer 3: *the refusal is the example*. It
is said in advance, so the page offers no tour that promises a house it cannot show. That
promise is one of §J's must-nots.

### 2. The copy ratchet could not go down from this package, because the copy it retired was invisible to it

The brief asks for the ratchet's floor to be lowered to whatever this package reaches.
`copy_ratchet.test.mjs` had **zero Overview rows** in its baseline, both titles and counts, so
there was nothing to lower.

What this package removed was the old `DOORS` table. That was 28 app-written strings: eleven
labels, eleven descriptions, three group names and three blurbs. None of them matched either of
the ratchet's two patterns, which are a `title=` of eight words or more and a number followed by
a plural. The removal reached no instrument.

The guard that does reach it is new. `frontDoor.test.mjs`'s *"the front door writes no sentence
of its own"* reads every JSX text run in `Overview.jsx` and `TwoSpineMap.jsx` and requires the
list to be exactly `['Search the corpus', '⌘K']`. It also requires the one template literal in
the page to be `ontology …`. A count literal written into the page turns **both** that test and
the copy ratchet red (mutation M09 in §IV). The ratchet's own baseline is unchanged.

### 3. The Gate is not the only thing that asks the server while signed out

§J says that signed out, the page makes no `/api/` call besides health, login and about-tdl.
Measured on the gated server, it makes one more:

    /api/health 200, /api/overview 401, /api/glossary/about-tdl 200

The `/api/overview` call is `App.jsx`'s boot probe. It asks one real route to learn whether the
browser already holds a session. It answers 401 and carries no corpus text, so nothing becomes
public, but it is a fourth call, and `App.jsx` belongs to WP-14.13. I did not edit it.

The walk names the probe instead of hiding it. The check requires that no call was made besides
the three allowed ones and at most one `/api/overview`, and that the `/api/overview` call
answered 401. A second call, or a probe that answered with corpus text, fails the check.

Whether the shell should learn the session state some other way, such as `/api/health` reporting
it, is WP-14.13's to decide. I raise it here and not as a question, because it is a matter of the
shell's design, not an open fact.

### 4. At 1280 px the front door's content fits, and the page will not until the shell releases it

With `#root[data-reflow]` set, the front door has **0 px** of sideways overflow at 1280 px. That
holds both for the page and for the front door's own scroller. The walk measures it by setting
the attribute for the measurement and then removing it.

On this tree nothing sets the attribute on the Overview. That job is WP-14.13's `App.jsx`
effect, so a reader at 1280 px still meets the shell's 1380 px floor and a 100 px sideways
scroll. The walk records this as COULD NOT EVALUATE by name, not as a pass. Once WP-14.13 lands,
the same block judges the page as a reader meets it.

### 5. The example brief's link opens the Brief surface, not the example brief

`guided-example` cites `brief:family-georgian`. `citations.js`'s `routeCite` sends every `brief`
citation to `{surface: 'brief', selection: {}}`, because *"there is a single brief"*. So the
front door's link names the example brief and lands on an empty Brief Intake. Loading the
example there belongs to WP-14.10 (`BriefIntake`, `JourneyBar`, `session.js`), which I did not
touch. The walk checks that the link exists and is drawn by `RecordLink`, and does not claim the
brief loads.

### 6. The site map offers no Kit and no Style Record items, and the map follows it

The retired `DOORS` offered `kit` and a separate Style Record. navModel's `NAV` does not: the
dossier carries the kit. The front door's map therefore has thirteen items and no `kit` item. It
draws what the site map says, and a place missing from it is the site map's to add. Nothing is
typed in here.

2. writes the mutation and asserts that it LANDED;
3. runs the named suite;
4. restores the original bytes and asserts the SHA-256 matches.

## V. Screenshots

Screenshots were taken with Playwright at 1280×800, 1440×900 and 1680×1050, of the front door
(against an open server) and of the Gate (against a gated one). They are in the session
scratchpad under `shots-14.14/`, not in the tree.

**What a practitioner arriving with no idea what this is understands in ten seconds.** At
1440×900 the first screen holds three things:

- **What this is.** The name, then *"A pattern book for traditional houses, written so that a
  machine can read it and a person can still argue with it: each style's parts, the proportions
  that size them, and the named errors a fluent eye would catch."*
- **Who it is for**, in three short columns: the production builder, the plan-development lead,
  and the architect and the classicist. Each has one line of what they get.
- **Two ways in**, framed as cards. *Find a style* (164), with the guided example offered beneath
  it. *Brief*, step 1 of a house, "to do", with its surface's one-line what.

So a builder, a plan lead or an architect can find their own line and a door within a glance, and
the search bar sits directly under the doors.

One scroll down is the whole workbench as four ruled rows: start; styles; a house in its five
numbered steps, with *Trace a drawing* under Plan; and the library. Each place carries the live
figure or status the rail shows. Beside the rows is the guided example, and the sentence under it
tells the reader, before they try, that the example stops at the brief and why. Below them are
the inventory and the five things it is not. At 1680 the map and the example sit on the first
screen too.

**What it does not yet say well.** The inventory is labelled only `ontology 0.7.0`, and the
five *"Not …"* lines have no heading. No glossary record names either section and the page writes
none, so the reader infers *"what it holds"* from the rows and *"what it is not"* from the
sentences. That inference works, but it is not a heading. A record for each would give them one.

**At 1280×800** as this tree ships, the shell's 1380 px floor cuts off the right of the masthead
and the rail. That is the 100 px of sideways scroll in §II.4. With `#root[data-reflow]` set, the
same page fits the window, and the readers reflow to two columns and one.

**The Gate** at all three sizes shows the eyebrow, the heading, the one definition sentence in
body ink, the shared-password line, the field and the button. With the about-tdl read failing,
it is the same card with the sentence simply absent: no error, no substitute. On all three sizes
its calls were `/api/health 200, /api/overview 401, /api/glossary/about-tdl 200`.

## VI. What was deliberately not done

- **`App.jsx`, `Chrome.jsx`, `tokens.css`, `navModel.js`, `crumbs.js`, the palette and
  WP-14.9–14.12's files are untouched.** The `data-reflow` effect, the session probe (§II.3) and
  the cold-link banner that reads `seen['front-door']` are all WP-14.13's.
- **No `PageHead` on the front door.** The page is itself the explanation of every place, and a
  head above it naming `surface-overview` would be a second what-this-is over the first. The map
  already describes the overview item through its record.
- **The example brief is not seeded into Brief Intake** (§II.5). That is WP-14.10's.
- **The copy ratchet's baseline is not edited** (§II.2): it held no row this package could
  remove.
- **No new open question.** The worked house's question is amended (§II.1), and the session probe
  is WP-14.13's design (§II.3).
- **`walk.mjs`**: only the Overview block and the appended front-door-and-Gate block were
  edited. `NAV` and `spawn` are imported dynamically inside those blocks, so the file's header,
  the rail block and every engineClaim-pinned line are unchanged.
