# WP-11.18 — the anchor that kept a face and threw away the extent

**Status.** Complete. Re-derive every figure below rather than quoting it; the placement of six of
sixteen plans moved and `engine="heuristic"` is the only engine any of these numbers describes.

## I. What was wrong

WP-11.17 stated the entrance front and shipped one named cost — `unreachable: butlers` on
`plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json`, with doors the placement cannot draw going 12 → 23 on
that record. Its own report called the two anchors genuine competitors and recorded three
recoveries built, measured and reverted.

The butler's pantry was drawn at **x 28.5–45.0, y 27.0–37.24** — the *east* end of the main block.
The hyphen it doors into stands at **x −7–0, y 9.62–27.62**, on the *west* face; the dining room
its other door names is at x 0–16.5, y 0–18. The pantry was diagonally opposite both, so both
doors failed and the room was unreachable. It is not a hyphen-crossing problem. The room was in
the wrong corner.

**`hyphen_anchors` computes `face_toward(a, b)` with the neighbouring element's whole rectangle in
hand and returns only the FACE, discarding the extent.** So the anchor knew the pantry belonged on
the west wall and not that it belonged *opposite the hyphen*. Measured over the three positions
`_partial_flank` draws on that 37.24 ft face for a 12 ft run:

| position | y | overlap with the hyphen band |
|---|---|---|
| low | 0.00–12.00 | 2.38 ft |
| centre | 12.62–24.62 | **12.00 ft** |
| high | 25.24–37.24 | 2.38 ft |

`openings.required_wall_ft` asks 3.50 ft for that leaf. **Only the centre position can ever place
the door**, and two draws in three threw it away.

**And the pre-WP-11.17 state did not set a bar.** The full-face strip drew the pantry
**4.95 × 37.24 = 184 sf against a declared 84 — 119% over.** Spanning the whole depth it
necessarily met both neighbours, so both doors placed: the old placement bought them by drawing
the room at more than twice its size, which is the veranda defect `_partial_flank` exists to
remove. A correct fix has to beat both states.

## II. What was built

1. **`face_toward` lifted to module level; `anchor_span` beside it.** Two rectangles in, a face
   out; the span is the perpendicular overlap that function already computes to pick the face, so
   deriving it anywhere else would be a second spelling of one rule.
2. **`hyphen_anchors` returns `{element: {face: ([ids], band)}}`**, `entrance_anchors` the same
   shape with `band = None`. The outdoors has no extent and any position on a front is a
   legitimate entrance; pinning one would be `centre_bay_score` arriving through the back door,
   which WP-11.3 built, swept and deleted. **Two neighbours past one face → band `None`**, not a
   union and not a midpoint: choosing between two extents is inventing an answer the record does
   not give.
3. **`_partial_flank(span=)` and `_band_off`.** With a band the position is derived and no draw is
   taken — centred on the band, clamped to the face, snapped off a sub-module slab — with the two
   end positions kept as candidates and the largest overlap winning, because a centred position
   needs two end slabs and each slab needs a room. `span=None` is today's three-way draw exactly.
4. **`run=` for a single-room hyphen anchor.** A multi-room anchor keeps the full-face strip:
   `run` is a room's own longer declared dimension and several rooms have no single such figure,
   so inventing one (a sum, a maximum) would be reading one quantity as another, which is OQ 48's
   error. The dependency's own `{E: [powder, cellarstair, kitchen]}` is unmoved.
5. **The chain.** `_host_for` picks the rest-rectangle whose own face IS the element's, offered
   `B`, `P`, `R` — **`B` first because it is what exists when `off` is 0**, which is one of the two
   reasons WP-11.17's third recovery attempt was inert. The later anchor's rooms are held out of
   the split, the host's own area target is reduced by theirs, and the host is decomposed in turn.
6. **`partition(frac=)` stops at the closer side, not at the first overshoot.**
   The growth loop runs `while acc < target`, so the room that **crosses** the target decides how
   far past it the group lands. Where the share is a coin flip that costs nothing — the share
   moves. Where the share is **stated** the caller has already chosen the rectangles, so an
   overshooting group is a stretched room and its neighbour is a shrunk one. Measured on the
   tagged record: a 495.9 sf target took `passage` (408) **and** `drawing` (396) — 804 into a
   567.9 sf rectangle, 62% over — and left `stair` alone in the 567.9 sf slab above it, **drawn
   567.9 sf against 126 declared, 4.5×**. Scoped to `frac is not None`, so a caller that draws its
   share is untouched.

## IV. The pantry, which is the subject

- Drawn **7.00 × 12.00 = 84.0 sf at (0.00, 12.62)** — its declared rectangle exactly, against
  16.5 × 10.24 = 169 sf at WP-11.17 and 4.95 × 37.24 = 184 sf before it.
- It stands on the main block's west face over **12.00 ft** of the hyphen's own 9.62–27.62 band,
  against the **3.50 ft** `openings.required_wall_ft` asks.
- **Both its doors place.** `butlers ↔ backhall` was the subject; `butlers ↔ dining` was left to
  `adjacency_score` under the ruling to measure rather than state it, and it lands adjacent.
- `unreachable: butlers` is cleared.

## V. The cost, measured and not closed

**Porch-on-the-entrance-front is 246 of 250 on the tagged record**, against WP-11.17's 250 of 250;
the other five anchored plans are unchanged at 250 of 250. A hosted anchor is best-effort: a host
holds two or three rooms rather than the element's seven, so the cuts a partial lay needs are not
always there, and where they are not the host is sliced ordinarily and the entrance can leave the
front. The winner is still drawn at y = 0.0 and the four are charged the 100 points
`entrance_score` has always charged.

**Two ways of closing those four were built and reverted, and both restore the census:**

- **Making the chain atomic** — a refused hosted anchor places nothing, so the caller falls through
  to WP-11.17's entrance-only lay — gives 250 of 250 and **loses the door**: the chain is refused
  on the candidates that win, so the pantry goes back to the east end. fatal 7 → 9, serious
  57 → 62, unplaced doors 20 → 32, `unreachable: butlers` back. Without the closer-side rule as
  well: fatal 9, serious 60, doors 25.
- **Falling back to the full-face strip inside the host** gives 250 of 250 and **the winner then
  takes it**: the porch is drawn 45 × 4.95 = 222.8 sf against a declared 72, serious 57 → 65. The
  assumption behind trying it was that `drawn-vs-declared` would rank a veranda out. It does not.
  That is precisely what WP-11.17 removed the strip for, arriving one layer down.

## VI. Three more refusals, each with its number

- **Laying the entrance anchor first and chaining the pantry** (WP-11.17's order kept) is worse
  than doing nothing: fatal 10, serious 57, doors 24, and the pantry lands at (0.00, **30.72**) —
  in the west slab and *above* the band — so both doors still fail. The band needs the rectangle
  with the most rooms in it, and that is the element, not a slab of it. **That is the reason the
  banded anchor is laid first, and it is a property of the band rather than a preference between
  the two statements.**
- **Letting the anchor take the host's full depth** where there is no room for a slab behind it —
  keeping its area and giving up its aspect — is fatal 10, doors 24. Reverted.
- **Ranking the growth loop by which room best fits the remaining target**, for a stated share, is
  **byte-identical**: connectivity dominates the key and the choice never changes on this corpus.
  Deleted rather than left inert at the bottom of a tuple, because an instrument that cannot fire
  is worse than none.

## VII. The correction WP-11.17 owes

Its report §VII and the `CLAUDE.md` entry say that forcing the pantry into the slab "works and is
not worth it", at a score of 672.3 → 753.5 with serious 55 → 62 and doors 23 → 24. **That run was
confounded two ways and the conclusion does not follow from it.** The nested lay used the
**full-face** `flank_slice` rather than `_partial_flank`, so the pantry became a 184 sf strip
inside a 16.5 ft slab; and the winning candidate had `off = 0`, where **no `P` slab exists at
all**, so the forcing never applied to the placement the number was read off. The attempt also
considered only `P` and `R` as hosts, never `B` — which is the rectangle that stands on the low
face exactly when `off` is 0.

The numbers are not withdrawn: they are what that code did. What is withdrawn is the reading that
the two statements cannot both be kept. They can, at the cost in §V.

## VIII. What the guards are

`tests/test_element_awareness.py::TestTheFlankIsStatedRatherThanSearchedFor` carries all of it.

- **`anchor_span`**, per face, clipped to the element's own face, `None` on no overlap, and the
  two-neighbour case returning `None` rather than a union or a midpoint.
- **`hyphen_anchors`**, in the pair shape, with the band **read off the hyphen block's own
  rectangle** rather than transcribed — so the assertion says "the neighbour's own extent"
  instead of pinning two literals that go stale the next time `blocks_for` moves a wing.
- **`_band_off`**: the band-centred position when the rooms allow it, an END when they do not (a
  centred position needs two slabs and each slab needs a room), and `None` when no position the
  rectangle can hold overlaps the band at all.
- **`_host_for`**: which rest-rectangle stands on the next anchor's face, and the room-count
  refusal.
- **The shipped record's pantry**, drawn 7.00 × 12.00 at x = 0.00 with ≥ 3.50 ft of shared wall
  against the hyphen and both its doors placed — the rectangle AND the overlap, because either
  alone can be right for the wrong reason.
- **`partition(frac=)`** stopping at the closer side, with the drawn-share path asserted
  identical rather than merely left alone.
- **The `off = 0` case, driven.** The corpus cannot reach it, so it is a hand-built element with
  the rng seeded to the draw that makes `off` zero — WP-11.10's stated precedent for a guard
  against a record this corpus does not yet have.

**Five mutations, each asserted to have LANDED before its colour was believed, and the file
restored and hash-checked after each.** All five bite: dropping the band, dropping `run=` on a
single-room hyphen anchor, dropping the chain, dropping the closer-side rule, and removing `B`
from `_host_for`'s offer. **The fifth was GREEN on the first sweep** — the mutation moves the
CALL SITE and the `_host_for` unit test drives the function directly with its own tuple, so the
two never met. That is a guard that runs where the bug cannot occur, and the answer was the
driven `off = 0` case rather than a source-reading assertion about an argument list.

## IX. What this does not do

- **It does not touch `geometry_cp.py`.** The prover has no anchor mechanism and proves this
  record OPTIMAL already; `corpus._placed()` solves on `auto` and takes the proof, so nothing
  shipped is drawn by the search.
- **It does not close `oq/the-search-loses-the-entrance-front-on-a-multi-element-plan`'s second
  cause.** `_key` is still `(viol, tot)` and still never reads `entrance_score`; stating the
  front means the key is not asked, which is WP-11.17's own framing and is unchanged.
- **It does not rule on `STACK_HARD`.** Both halves of the argument that defaulted it `False`
  have now moved in opposite directions — the spans say the rule helps, the rooms say it hurts —
  and `oq/the-measurement-that-defaulted-the-stacking-rule-has-inverted` carries both. Neither is
  evidence about the rule: what moved both times is the placement the comparison is made against.
