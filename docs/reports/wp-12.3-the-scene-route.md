# WP-12.3 — The scene route, and the budget rather than the clock

*9 September 2026. The brief is `docs/prd/phase-12-the-sheet-in-the-round.md` §8. The package is
small and its whole content is a decision: the Round needs a scene and six plates, and the
question is whether that is one call or seven. The PRD did not ask; the review that adopted it
(§10, item 8) said the package owed the measurement before WP-12.4 wires the overlay. The
measurement was taken and it decides it — not on the clock, which says the plates are nearly free,
but on the metered budget, which says seven calls per record change buys a reader eight edits an
hour.*

---

## What was built

- **`workbench/server/corpus.py::scene(plan, parti=None, candidates=250, plates=True)`** — one
  `_placed` placement, then `build_section(geometry_result=placed)`, `build_roof(section=…)`,
  `build_elevation(section=…, roof=…)` and `scene.build_scene`, exactly the chain WP-12.0 made
  the drawing set take. Returns `{scene, plan, solver, plates, plates_refused}`.
- **`SCENE_PLATES`** — the six named views that carry a plate: `plan`, the four elevations
  `S N E W`, and `roof`. **The APPROACH view is deliberately absent**, because a perspective
  dimension is never true and the ruling of 8 Sep withholds its dimensions; a view with no true
  dimensions has no plate to be held to.
- **`POST /api/scene`** behind `_heavy(request)` and `_plan(body)`, like every sibling that
  drives the solver since WP-10.1. `plates` must be a bool or the route answers 422 naming it.
- **`api.scene(plan, opts)`** in `workbench/app/src/api/client.js`, beside `api.drawing`.
- **`workbench/server/tests/test_scene_endpoint.py`** — 8 tests, 77.0 s, all passing.

## The measurement that decided the shape

`plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json`, `engine="auto"` (which is what `_placed` takes), this
container. The solve is budget-bound at `BUDGET_BATCH_S = 40.0` and came back at the same figure on
both runs; the plate and scene figures are from a warm process, measured twice.

| | seconds |
|---|---|
| a cold solve, `_SOLVE_CACHE` cleared and a fresh `plan["id"]` | **37.48** |
| a record that already carries `geometry` — the `is_placed` short-circuit | **0.00** |
| `build_scene` on the placed record | **0.00** |
| all six named views' plates, on the placed record | **0.38 / 0.39** |
| the whole call end to end, fresh process | **38.46** |

The last row is a second, independent reading and it is the one to quote: the section, the roof,
the elevation, the scene, the six plates **and** the first-call module loads together are the
**0.98 s** between it and the solve — **2.6% of the placement**.

Payload, `json.dumps(...)` of each part of one response. The raw counts reproduce exactly across
runs; the gzipped ones move by about 1.5%, because `auto` is not reproducible under a wall-clock
budget and the placement inside the record really does differ:

| | raw bytes | gzipped |
|---|---|---|
| the scene record | 64,967 | 6,918 |
| the six plates | 249,425 | 50,808 |
| the placed plan record | 57,948 | 12,976 |
| **the whole response** | **376,574** | **70,702** |

So: **about 71 KB on the wire, and everything but the placement is a fortieth of it.**

**The clock does not decide this and the budget does.** `limits.heavy_calls_per_hour()` is 60 per
identity and every route that solves is `_heavy`. Six plates fetched one at a time beside the
scene is seven calls per record change: **eight record changes an hour** for a surface whose
entire subject is moving between views of one house. One call is sixty. That is the argument, and
it is an argument about a meter rather than about latency — **a reader who profiled this route
would conclude the plates were free and reach the opposite design.**

**The placed record comes back for a second reason, and it is not a cache.** `_placed` returns the
record with its `geometry` untouched, so a client that keeps this one and posts it to a later
export or evaluate pays 0.00 s where a fresh record pays 37.48 s. That is the drawing set's own
rule (WP-6.4) rather than an optimisation: one placement, and every surface reading it is reading
the same building. `geometry._SOLVE_CACHE` is a different mechanism and is not what this measures —
the 0.00 s row is taken on a record that cache has never seen.

## What the tests hold

The three things this route can get wrong that nothing else would notice:

1. **The scene and the plates are different buildings.** Asserted on
   `solver.drawn_by.input_digest`, which `_placed` stamps from the record it was HANDED, before it
   solves — WP-11.8's J6, which exists precisely so two sheets of "the same house" that disagree
   can be told apart by their input. Not on the SVG bytes: two plates of one house are
   legitimately different drawings, and what must not differ is what they were drawn from.
2. **A plate that could not be drawn goes missing rather than being named.** To a viewer an absent
   key and a view not yet fetched are the same thing. Both shipped plans draw all six, so the
   guard reads the contract out of the source rather than out of a corpus that happens never to
   exercise it — WP-8.11's fixture rule.
3. **A caller-supplied parti reaches the solver as a record.** WP-9.4 measured 114 bays of half a
   foot arriving that way through two bench routes. Both branches driven: a dict is refused by
   name, an id is not.

And one about the route rather than the record: **it is not a sixth drawing kind.**
`test_m3_drawings.py::test_unknown_kind_names_the_kinds` posts `axonometric` and expects a 422
naming the five kinds, and that stays true only while no camera is smuggled into that enum. This
file repeats the assertion against `scene` and `axonometric` both, so a later reader adding one
fails beside the route that exists instead.

## Findings

### 1. Two names that read as module-level and are per-function locals

The first draft of `scene()` borrowed `B = _os.path.join(ROOT, "build")` from the neighbouring
`export_cad`. Both halves are wrong: `B` is a local in each of the three functions that need it,
and `_os` is an alias each of them makes with its own `import os as _os` — the module imports `os`
plainly. Two `NameError`s, on the tests' first two runs.

**Neither would have been caught by reading**, and that is the finding rather than the typo. The
surrounding functions read exactly as though both names were module-level; the only way to see it
is to run the function.

### 2. The parti was passed as a record to a function that takes an id — found by reading

This one was caught the other way, and is the more serious of the two. The first draft forwarded
the RESOLVED parti record `pt` to `drawing()`. But `drawing()` calls `core.load_parti(parti)`
itself, and that function takes a caller's **string**: handed a dict it does
`os.path.basename(str(dict))`, finds no such file, and returns `None`. So every plate would have
been drawn **with no parti** while the scene beside it used one — two different buildings in one
response, which is the exact defect WP-12.0 removed one layer up, reintroduced by the package that
depends on WP-12.0.

It would have been invisible: the plates would still have been drawn, still been valid SVG, still
carried a digest — because the digest is stamped on the record `_placed` was handed, and that
record is the same one either way. **The digest guard cannot see this**, which is worth saying
plainly beside the test that relies on it. It was found by reading `load_parti`'s signature, and
the fix is that `scene()` forwards the id string and refuses a caller-supplied record outright.

### 3. A refused elevation is content, and `build_scene` was already written for it

Outside the classical-front family `build_elevation` declines. That is not an error here: the
scene then carries one `not_modelled` entry saying no face states an opening, which is
`scene._build_from_plan`'s own reading of the same refusal. Passing `None` rather than propagating
the error is deliberate and is stated in the code — a house whose elevation cannot be composed
still has walls, slabs, datums and a roof, and refusing the whole scene for it would report
could-not-evaluate about six things on the strength of one.

### 4. The first published plate figure was 0.70 s and the reproducible one is 0.38

The number taken while building the package measured the six plates in a process that had not yet
loaded the drawing pipeline, so it carried the module loads. Re-derived in a warm process it is
0.38 s twice; the loads are real and are accounted for in the 0.98 s end-to-end remainder above,
where they belong. Neither figure changes the decision — the argument is about the meter — but a
number that does not reproduce is a number the next reader cannot check.

## What was deliberately not done

- **No ETag or conditional fetch.** The PRD floats it; it cannot help while `_heavy` counts the
  request rather than the response, and teaching the meter about a cache is a change to the meter.
  Returning everything at once achieves the same end with no new mechanism.
- **No `axonometric` or `approach` kind on `/api/drawings/{kind}`.** See above; the negative
  fixture that keeps that enum honest is now asserted from two directions.
- **The scene is not cached server-side.** `core.py` is pure functions and the server holds no
  plan state (`docs/workbench.md`); the record the client keeps is the cache, and it is the
  client's.
- **No walk assertion.** The Round does not exist yet. WP-12.4 owns the browser-side checks, and
  a walk step against a route with no surface would be a guard that cannot fail.

## Reproducing the measurement

```
python3 - <<'EOF'
import gzip, json, sys, time
sys.path.insert(0, ".")
from workbench.server import corpus

plan = json.load(open("plans/tidewater-georgian-careful.json"))
t = time.time()
res = corpus.scene(plan)
print(f"{time.time() - t:8.2f} s  the whole call")
for k in ("scene", "plates", "plan"):
    b = json.dumps(res[k]).encode()
    print(f"{k:8} {len(b):8} raw {len(gzip.compress(b)):8} gzip")
whole = json.dumps(res).encode()
print(f"{'all':8} {len(whole):8} raw {len(gzip.compress(whole)):8} gzip")
EOF
```

For the split figures, clear `geometry._SOLVE_CACHE` and give the plan a fresh `id` before the
cold row, or it measures the cache — `docs/workbench.md`'s own benchmarking trap, to which this
repository has published a 43x-wrong figure before.

## Addendum, 9 September 2026 (WP-12.8): the size row is wrong twice over

Left as written above, and corrected here, because this project does not overwrite a
measurement — a figure is a statement about the tree it was measured on.

**The time claim stands.** Re-measured on the same plan on the post-WP-12.7 tree, the whole
call is **39.00 s** against a solve that is almost all of it. Everything but the placement is
still a fortieth of the clock, and the argument for one metered call is untouched.

**The byte figures do not, for two independent reasons.**

| | raw | gzip 4 | gzip 6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| published above | 376,574 | — | 70,702 |
| the same response, re-measured | 621,870 | **97,442** | 92,934 |

1. **The published 70,702 was gzip level 6, and `app.GZipExceptSSE` deploys level 4.** The wire
   figure was never the server's. That distinction has been in `CLAUDE.md` since the
   compression audit — *"never pass only `minimum_size` to `GZipMiddleware`"* — and it was not
   applied to the measurement.
2. **The scene grew.** WP-12.6 dressed the envelope and WP-12.7 added the doorcase and the
   stoop: 101 solids became 500, and 350 of the 500 are muntins. The scene record alone went
   64,928 → 270,029 raw.

**And the sentence most likely to be quoted next is the one to be careful with.** *"Everything
but the placement is 2.6% of it"* is true of the CLOCK and inverted for the PAYLOAD: the placed
record is 57,957 of 621,870 raw bytes, so **ninety per cent of this response is everything but
the placement**. One number, two questions, opposite answers — which is why the route's
docstring now says which question each row answers.

Composition of the 500, for whoever is next asked where the bytes went:

```
muntin 350 · wall 49 · opening-frame 40 · sash 39 · deck 4 · hearth 3
surround 3 · entablature 3 · slab 2 · roof-plane 2 · gable 2 · chimney 2
```

The muntin count is bounded by pack data and not by the caller: `elevation.GLASS_MODULE_BANDS`
floors the glass module at 7.0 in and the shipped plan takes 9.0, so a hostile
`context.date_of_representation` buys about 30% more lights across and nothing like an
amplifier.
