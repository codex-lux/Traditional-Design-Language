# WP-12.8 — The adversarial audit of Phase 12's last three packages

*9 September 2026. The diff audited is `3d697c4..cad17dd` — WP-12.6 (the envelope dressed) and
WP-12.7 (the entrance and the porch, in two commits) — plus the WP-12.8 work reported here.*

Lucas asked for a genuine adversarial pass rather than a re-read: four read-only auditors on
different angles, every finding ranked and attributed, everything blocking or worth fixing
fixed, and a yes/no on deployment readiness.

**Four blocking defects were found and all four are this session's own.** Three of them are one
sentence apart in kind — *a coordinate read in the wrong frame* — and none of them was visible
to any assertion in the tree, because **every test written for WP-12.6 and WP-12.7 read a
member's in-plane extent and not one read the third coordinate.**

---

## I. How it was run

Four auditors, dispatched together, none of them the agent that wrote the code:

| angle | what it was given |
|---|---|
| call chain and regressions | every consumer of `build_scene`, `scene["bounds"]`, `scene["solids"]` and every reader of a solid id |
| second-order risk | payload, CPU, browser memory, injection, degenerate camera inputs |
| second occurrences | the five defect patterns WP-12.6 and WP-12.7 had already fixed once |
| whether the tests can fail | an isolated git worktree, 60 mutations, each with a landed-assertion and a verified restore |

Two rules were kept throughout, and both earned themselves. **Every figure an auditor reported
was re-derived here before it was acted on** — one headline was wrong (below), one was
overstated, and one was a whole order of magnitude out in the mechanism if not the size.
**And a mutation that merely disables a guard was never accepted as evidence**: where a defect
could be injected instead, it was.

## II. What blocks deployment — all four introduced by this session

### 1. Every dressed member on the north and east faces was drawn INSIDE its own wall

`_face_extrude` returns `at` = the **low** face of the wall, and that is exactly right for the
opening *frame*, whose thickness is the whole wall. It is exactly wrong for anything thinner.
On S and W the low face *is* the outside face; on N and E it is the **inside** one.

Measured on `spec-builder-colonial`, whose north wall runs y 30.750 → 31.417:

```
S-0-ground-bar-v1        muntin     at -0.667   the outer face — right
W-0-ground-shutter-l     shutter    at -0.667   the outer face — right
N-0-ground-bar-v1        muntin     at 30.747   the INNER face, 8 in behind
E-0-ground-shutter-l     shutter    at 49.997   the INNER face
N-2-door-surround        surround   at 30.747   the INNER face
```

**That plan's entrance front is the N face**, so its door surround, both sidelights and its
flush frieze were inside the house, with only the two projecting mouldings outdoors above a
blank wall. Corpus-wide, re-derived here rather than taken on report — the auditor said
1,763/1,679 and this counts the projecting entablature members out, since those were already
face-aware and did not move: **1,769 dressing solids on the wrong side against 1,713 on the
right, of 3,482.**

It is WP-12.2's own defect with the sign reversed — that one put `at` on the *outside* face with
an always-positive thickness and stood the N and E openings *proud* of their walls — and it
survived for the same reason: no number in the record disagrees with it, and only a picture of
the north side shows it. `_on_the_outside_face` is the one spelling now, and the projecting
entablature members, which were already face-aware, are unchanged.

### 2. `_chimneys` read a key nothing in this repository writes

```python
top = pos.get("grade_to_cap_ft") or (z_ridge + CHIMNEY_ABOVE_RIDGE_FT)
```

`grade_to_cap_ft` has no writer anywhere in the tree. `roof.py` writes
`total_height_grade_ft`, and `render_elevation.py` reads it. So the `or` fired on **every stack
on every plan** and the editorial 2.0 ft always won. On `tidewater-georgian-careful` the record
states a ridge of 39.03, a `height_above_ridge_ft` of 8.0 and a total of **47.03**; the model
drew the axis to **41.03**.

Three consequences, each one this phase exists to prevent:

- an **invented** number displacing a stated one, in a layer whose whole rule is that it invents
  none;
- a stack standing 2.0 ft above the ridge **against the style constraint
  `chimney_height_above_ridge_ft` that the same roof record judges `at-least 6, ok: true`** — the
  model broke a rule the record passes;
- the plate and the model **six feet apart about one stack**, which is WP-12.0's own subject.

`CHIMNEY_ABOVE_RIDGE_FT` is deleted; a missing height is refused by name and never guessed.
**And the literal guard could not have caught it**: `tests/test_scene.py` exempts 2.0 as a
structural value, so an invented dimension that happens to equal an index is invisible to it.
That limitation is now written into the test.

### 3. `_entrance_agreement` compared two different coordinate frames

`drawn` is the elevation's `u`, which runs from the **outside** face's left edge. `placed` is
`axis.door_bay`'s `position_ft`, a **clear**-frame plan coordinate. `_openings` applies the shift
between them (`ox = oy = -t_ext`) on every rectangle it draws; this comparison did not.

So WP-12.7's headline figures are each short by one exterior wall — and, far worse, **a house
whose two records agree exactly would have been reported as disagreeing by `t_ext`.** A
disclosure that convicts a record that agrees is worse than no disclosure.

Re-derived in one frame, corpus-wide:

| plan | published | true |
|---|---:|---:|
| `spec-builder-colonial` | 21.33 ft | **22.00 ft** |
| `tidewater-georgian-careful` | 5.42 ft | **6.71 ft** |
| `bad-03-narrow-lot-townhome` | — | 5.00 ft |
| `bad-07-octagon-dinette-colonial` | — | 1.02 ft |

**And the test ratified the defect.** `test_the_two_records_of_the_front_door_disagree_and_the
_scene_says_so` recomputed `drawn` with the same wrong expression, so it agreed with the code by
construction. It reads the surround solid the scene actually drew now, and asserts that the
difference between the two readings is the exterior wall and nothing else.

### 4. A caller-supplied record could build half a million solids

`_porch` draws one solid per riser and `plan.threshold.steps[].riser_count` is an unbounded
`integer` in the plan schema, reached by `POST /api/scene` from any caller. The loop's `break`
on `inner - lo <= 0` is defeated by a tread that is negative or vanishing, because the difference
then *grows*.

Measured directly:

```
tread 13 in, riser_count 200,000   ->      2 solids     (the flight's own depth stops it)
tread -1.0 in, riser_count 100,000 -> 99,999 solids
tread 1e-9,   riser_count 500,000  -> 499,999 solids
```

**The first report of this said one caller-supplied integer was the amplifier, and it is not** —
re-derived here, `riser_count` alone is bounded by the flight's stated depth whatever its value.
It takes a second poisoned field. That does not make it less of a hole: a 56 KB body, on a route
the infrastructure audit measures as one serialised core, behind a rate limit that is a *call*
limit and not a *work* limit.

Three bounds now, and only the last is a guard: the record's own riser count; the treads that fit
the flight's stated depth; and `_MAX_DRAWN_RISERS`, which refuses the excess **by name** —
because a stoop drawn with 64 of its 100,000 risers and no note is a drawing that lies about its
record. Non-positive treads, rises and rectangles are refused with their own reason.

## III. Worth fixing — and one of them is a whole layer that was never reached

### 5. `_chimneys` was nested inside `_roof`'s success path

Found while writing the guard for a *different* finding, which is why it is worth stating
separately: `_roof` returns early on a form it cannot dimension and on a roof with no judged
ridge, and `_chimneys` was called from its tail. So on `spec-builder-colonial` — a
four-over-four whose hearth is `gable-end-paired`, whose massing therefore calls for two stacks —
the chimneys were not undrawn but **unconsidered**, and the layer said nothing at all. The roof
record carried the reason in full:

> *Placement source calls for gable-end chimneys …, but the main roof has no judged ridge height
> to measure a chimney's total height against — unjudged, not placed.*

A stack stands on its own record and is read beside the other layers now, not behind another
layer's guard. The reason is republished rather than composed, which is `_porch`'s rule for
`plan.threshold.unplaced` one layer over.

### 6. `bounds` stopped being the envelope and `modelAt` was not told

`frame.js::modelAt` registers a flat elevation plate by taking `scene.bounds` to be the outside
face of the exterior wall — which it *was*, while WP-12.1 stated it off `section.footprint`.
WP-12.7 made `bounds` the union of everything drawn, and the stoop stands 3.458 ft clear of the
house.

```
tidewater  before: S 0.000  N 0.000  E 0.000  W 0.000
tidewater  after : S 0.000  N 0.004  E 2.166  W 0.004     <- the E plate 2.17 ft out
spec       after : S 0.000  N 0.004  E 0.000  W 0.459
```

`plateTransform` fits the whole plate off two points, so the error is a rigid slide of the entire
drawing and looks like nothing at all. The scene publishes `bounds.envelope` beside
`bounds.min/max` now — two quantities that had become one field — and `modelAt` **refuses** a
record stating no envelope rather than falling back on the frame, because a silent fallback puts
the misregistration back on exactly the records that cannot say otherwise.

### 7. The viewer had no pen for the `construction` ink

`schema/scene.schema.json`'s ink enum is `cut / profile / seen / fine / construction`.
`three-scene.js`'s `PEN` and `INK` maps carried `hidden` and `grid` — neither of which a solid
may declare — and **not `construction`**, which is the ink WP-12.6 gives the two chimney axes,
the only `judgment` solids in the whole corpus. Both lookups fall back with `||`, so a judgment
drew at `draw-seen` / `lw-medium`: pixel for pixel a measured edge, in the one place a reader
most needs to tell a decision from a measurement. The two dead keys are what made the map *look*
complete.

Three vocabularies (the schema's enum, the two maps, `Round.jsx`'s `TOKEN_NAMES`) and no
assertion relating any pair of them. There is one now, and it holds all three — including the
direction that would have caught the obvious wrong fix, since mapping `construction` to a new
token name without adding it to `Round.jsx`'s list leaves `tokens[...]` undefined and falls back
to `draw-seen` again, identically silent.

### 8. Dragging the approach view moved the sun and not the house

The orbit handler builds its next pose as `{...p, azimuthDeg, elevationDeg}`, and `setPose`'s
perspective branch reads neither — it is driven by `eye`, `target` and `fovDeg`. So on the
approach the picture did not move while `reshade` swung the sun across it, `isNamed` went false,
and the caption dropped to FREE VIEW on a view the reader could no longer steer, because
`poseFor('free')` returns null.

**Refused rather than orbited.** The approach's whole content is a person standing at 5′-6″, the
caption prints that height, and a dragged eye would make the caption false. Extending it to a
walk-round is a feature and belongs in a package.

### 8b. And the picture found one the auditors did not: every hearth was two feet underground

**The eighth package running in which rendering the sheet and looking at it found what nothing
else did** — and this time the *looking* only raised the question. `hearths.breast` is a PLAN
rectangle and carries no z; `_hearths` wrote `0.0`. On `tidewater-georgian-careful`, whose
ground floor is 2.0 ft above grade, that put all three fires **two feet under the house, lying
on the ground outside it**.

It is invisible in every named orthographic view — a flat rectangle beneath the floor slab is
hidden by the elevation, or reads as part of the plan cut — and plainly visible in the approach
as three pale slabs on the lawn at the foot of the west and east walls. **Pre-existing since
WP-12.1**, and only drawable at all because WP-12.7 added the view that looks at the base of
the house from a person's height.

**And the picture was not what settled it.** Three shapes at the wall foot could have been the
stoop, the water table, or a rendering artefact. Projecting the breast corners through
`frame.js`'s own approach camera put the drawing and dining hearths at screen x 484–546 and the
library hearth at 1053 — which is where the marks were, and where the stoop (824–878) is not.
WP-12.5's rule: *looking at the sheet finds defects; it does not adjudicate them.*

The breast stands on its own storey's floor now, which the section states. **The height stays a
zero**: the projection is a judgment and this fixes only the datum.

### 9. Four more, briefly

- **`_hearths` and `_spaces` read `lv.get("level")`** where the plan schema states `index` under
  `additionalProperties: false`. Every hearth solid was tagged `level: 0`, an upper-storey fire
  included; `_spaces` fell back to the enumerate position, right by coincidence on all sixteen
  plans and wrong for the cellar the schema documents at `index: -1`. **Pre-existing (WP-12.1)**,
  and the third occurrence in this layer of *a field read off the wrong record* after
  `shutters_carried` and `dormers`.
- **The sill refusal was gated on a dict that is always truthy** — `if rects_by_face:` is
  `if {"S": [], "N": [], "E": [], "W": []}` — so it would file *"the sills under every window"*
  against a house with no windows. That is WP-12.6's own dormer defect in the function
  immediately below the one it was found in, in the same commit. Latent: the five shipped plans
  that draw no opening refuse their elevation earlier.
- **`_entrance_agreement`'s three exits shared one `what`**, and the `what` is what a plate
  prints. Over the sixteen plans that is **eleven entries reading as eleven disagreements when
  four are disagreements and seven are could-not-evaluate**.
- **`_porch` was silent on a record with no `threshold` block at all**, guarded four fields where
  its body read seven (`KeyError: 'y_ft'` out of `build_scene`), could emit duplicate solid ids
  for a room with two front doors, and never named the `platform` band the record states.

### 10. And three the audit found in the viewer's own housekeeping

- **`clear()` never disposed a line geometry.** `load` pushes each `LineSegments2`'s *material*
  into `lineMats` and its `LineSegmentsGeometry` nowhere; `group.clear()` detaches from the scene
  graph and three.js does not free on removal. One orphaned WebGL buffer per solid per load, and
  the Tidewater scene went **101 solids at WP-12.1 to 500** — that count is the published one
  and this measurement's own, not an auditor's. **Pre-existing, five-fold amplified.**
- **`reshade` ran on every frame**, rewriting a colour attribute per solid and setting
  `needsUpdate` on each — one buffer upload apiece, so five times as many as at WP-12.1. The
  arithmetic was never the cost; the driver round-trips are. It short-circuits when the two angles and
  the solid count are unchanged, which makes a resize, an explode, a clip change and a re-load
  free.
- **`poseFor` could throw during React render.** `namedViews` is called inside a `useMemo` with
  no error boundary above it; before WP-12.7 it read only `scene.storeys` and could not throw,
  and the approach put `targetFor`'s `const { min, max } = scene.bounds` on its path. It returns
  null for a missing or malformed frame now, and for a non-string face — which is what its own
  comment always promised.

## IV. Every solid now says which storey it stands on

`solid.level` is what `three-scene.js` picks an explode offset with, and only slabs, walls and
hearths carried one — so `explode: levels` lifted the structure and left every window, sash bar,
shutter and doorcase behind. The storey is a fact the elevation had already resolved to lay the
opening out; the join had to be made by hand because `opening_rects` names a storey by id and
`solid.level` is an integer.

**Tidewater: 57 of 500 solids carried a level; 494 do now.** The six that do not are the
chimneys, the gables and the roof planes, which are not on a storey — and naming them in the
guard is what stops the test being satisfied by defaulting.

## V. What was NOT done, and why

- **The chimney's plan size is still read twice** — `elevation.py` from the proportion pack,
  `threshold.py` from the resolved kit cascade. They agree at 22 in today by construction.
  Reconciling them is a data question about which record owns the figure, not a drawing change.
- **The model draws the stack as an axis and the plate draws it at 22 in.** Both are correct
  disclosures of one judgment and they are not the same disclosure. Rather than change either,
  the scene's `judgment` entry now *says* the plate draws the mass, so a reader comparing the two
  is not left to read a deliberate difference as a disagreement about the house.
- **`sweep` and `lathe` remain unmeasurable by `_extent`.** They are in the schema's `oneOf` and
  nothing emits one; when something does, it owes that function a rule rather than a silent skip.
  `xy` **was** admitted, because the schema's plane enum carries it and `round/solids.js` builds
  it — a record three spellings called valid was one the frame refused to measure.
- **`geometry.py`'s CLI conflict printer reads `conflict`/`requirements` where the writer states
  `infeasible`/`conflicts`**, so the named-conflict printer prints nothing in exactly the case it
  exists for. Pre-existing, CLI-only, and outside this diff.
- **`moves.py:578` reads two dead grouping keys.** Pre-existing, harmless, outside this diff.

## VI. The measurements this audit corrected

**A figure is a statement about the tree it was measured on**, and three of Phase 12's are no
longer statements about this one.

| published | re-derived | why |
|---|---|---|
| `three` in a **544.7 KB** chunk, entry **478 KB** | **564 KB**, entry **477 KB** | WP-12.6 and 12.7 |
| `POST /api/scene` **376,574 raw / ~71 KB gzipped** | **621,870 / 97,442** | 101 solids → 500, **and the published figure was gzip level 6 where the server deploys level 4** |

The last one is `check_counts.py`'s own rule — *"a number stated twice needs claiming twice"* —
broken by the number the rule was written about.

**And WP-12.3's "everything but the placement is 2.6% of the call" needs its question named.**
It is a **time** claim and it is intact: 39.00 s end to end against a solve that is almost all of
it. Read as a **size** claim it is inverted — the placed record is 57,957 of 621,870 raw bytes,
so ninety per cent of that response is everything but the placement. One number, two questions,
opposite answers. Both docstrings say so now.

## VII. Whether the new tests can fail

**Twenty-four mutations, and every one is red from a green baseline** — sixteen in
`build/scene.py`, eight in the app. Each asserts its own landing (the occurrence count, an
`ast.parse`, and a read-back from disk) and restores in a `finally` with a byte-for-byte check,
because *a mutation that silently does not apply looks exactly like a guard that works*.

**Two of my own guards were blind on their first run and both are recorded here** rather than
quietly fixed:

1. `test_an_entrance_that_cannot_be_compared_is_not_an_entrance_that_disagrees` ran over the two
   shipped plans, and **both are in the disagreement state** — so a mutation giving both states
   one `what` again left it green. It reads `good-02-portico-library-house` now, a real corpus
   record in the other state; 7 of the 16 plans are.
2. The `reshade` short-circuit guard searched the whole function head for `shaded.length`, and
   that string also appears in the line that *records* the cached pose — so dropping it from the
   condition left the guard green. It reads the `if (…) return;` condition and nothing else now.

**And the mutation auditor found four guards from WP-12.6 and WP-12.7 that could not fail:**

- `test_a_stack_the_roof_cannot_carry_is_refused` had a **purely negative body** and a docstring
  claiming *"the refusal must say that rather than a stack silently not appearing"*. Deleting the
  refusal, and deleting `_chimneys` entirely, both left it green — and the refusal it named was
  not even the branch that record takes.
- `test_shutters_are_drawn_iff_the_storey_window_carries_them` could not tell which half of
  `shutters_carried and lw_in` was holding, because the two are **perfectly coextensive on both
  shipped plans**. Removing the `shutters_carried` half left the whole suite green. It is driven
  by a record stating a leaf width and declining to carry shutters now.
- `test_the_composition_is_the_door_two_casings_and_two_sidelights` passes with `_entrance`
  deleted entirely: its arithmetic is asserted on the elevation record, not on the scene. Its
  docstring's claim to the contrary is corrected; four sibling tests do catch the deletion.
- The portico source guard sliced the file from `def _porch(` onwards, so reading `portico_bays`
  200 lines earlier in `_entrance` — a plausible home for doorcase logic — passed.

## VIII. The verdict

**Yes — deployment-ready, with the four blocking defects fixed, verified and guarded.**

The honest qualifications, because a verdict with none is not a verdict:

- **Everything blocking was this session's own**, and three of the four were one kind of mistake
  — a coordinate read in the wrong frame — repeated in three places by three different routes.
  Nothing in the tree could see any of them, and the reason is exactly reproducible: **every
  assertion written for these two packages read an in-plane extent, and the defects were all in
  the third coordinate.** The lesson is not "test more"; it is *when a package adds a dimension,
  assert on that dimension*.
- **The browser walk was run on this tree and its FAILED list is exactly three**: the engine
  caption and the Pantry label spill twice — the three `CLAUDE.md` already records as red on
  `main`, and identical to the reference run taken before any of this session's changes.
  Nothing this audit touched appears in it.
- **`tests/test_shape_pins.py`'s known intermittent is unchanged** and is not this work's.
- Six items are explicitly deferred in §V, each with its reason. None of them changes what is
  drawn.
