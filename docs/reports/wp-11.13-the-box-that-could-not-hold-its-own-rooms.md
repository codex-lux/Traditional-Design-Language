# WP-11.13 — the box that could not hold its own rooms

*7 September 2026. Branch `claude/georgian-tidewater-plan-analysis-qbwmqs`. Every corpus figure
is `engine="heuristic"`, which is deterministic; every CP figure names CP and its budget. The
shipped corpus is BYTE-IDENTICAL across this package and that is checked, not asserted: 0 of its
16 plan records carry a `block` tag, so nothing here is reachable from it.*

---

## I. What was wrong

`geometry.dependency_sizes` sized a non-main massing element's box to **exactly** its rooms'
declared area — `H = need / W`, zero slack by construction. `geometry_cp._element_boxes` then
rounded that box **inward** to the model's integer grid: `ceil` the low edge, `floor` the high
one. That rounding is deliberate and right — WP-11.11 chose it so that nothing CP proves sits
outside the mass the record states — and `blocks_for` centres a dependency on the main block's
axis, so its origin is a half-foot and **both** roundings bite.

Composed, the box the proving model worked in was strictly smaller than the rooms it had to
hold. On a hand-tagged `tidewater-georgian-careful`, against a coverage floor of 0.97:

| element | integer box | its own rooms | coverage needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| dependency | 690 sf | 701 sf | **1.016** |
| hyphen | 105 sf | 112 sf | **1.067** |

The rooms could not fit *at all*, at any floor.

**And beside it, the floor and containment read two different boxes.** The coverage loop
computed the element's box with `int(round(...))` while every room's containment was bounded by
`_element_boxes`. One quantity, two roundings — so the model demanded 97% of the larger be
packed inside the smaller. On the hyphen that is **108.6 sf into a box holding 105**: infeasible
by construction, before a single declared fact was read. That is why every attempt to tag this
record came back with the same useless core — *"the rooms cannot tile any footprint this parti
and lot allow, even with every declared requirement dropped"*. The engine was right and it was
answering a question about arithmetic, not about architecture.

## II. What was built

**One rounding, in the leaf both readers load.** `elements.integer_box` is the rule now, called
by `geometry_cp._element_boxes` and by `geometry.multi_element_disclosure`. A second
transcription of an inward rounding is precisely what this package found wrong, so it is not
available to make again.

**The box is sized so its rounded form still holds its rooms.** `grid_allowance_ft()` is two
quanta per axis, one per inward-rounded edge, and it is *derived* — solving
`(W − 2g)(H − 2g) ≥ need` for `H` — rather than chosen. The hyphen takes the same allowance in
DEPTH only: its width is the gap between two masses, a real dimension of the house, and its
origin is integral by construction.

**The floor is stated against what the box was derived from.** The main block's box is grown
independently by `derive_footprint` until the programme fits, so "fill 97% of your box" is a
real question about the rooms. A dependency's box is derived *from* its rooms, so asking whether
those rooms fill it is asking the box about itself — and the quantised answer cannot be made to
land, because the floor needs the box within 3% of the rooms' area and one foot of a 30 ft
dependency is 5%. For such an element the floor is stated against **the rooms' own declared
area**, which is the guarantee the floor was for (rooms may not shrink and leave the element
half empty) in a form the grid cannot falsify. `COVERAGE` itself is **unchanged at 0.97**.

**It is on the record.** `geometry_report.multi_element.element_capacity` carries, per element,
the stated box, the box the proving model works in, its rooms' own declared area, the coverage
each implies, and whether they fit — plus `elements_too_small_for_their_own_rooms` when any does
not. Before this the condition surfaced only as a CP infeasibility whose core blamed the tiling.

## III. What it bought

The hand-tagged Tidewater — service programme in a west dependency, back hall as the hyphen — is
**proved OPTIMAL in 12.3 s with zero pins downgraded**. That is Part I.A item 4 of the Phase 11
diagnosis (*"the dependency and the hyphen are inside the block"*), and the first time this
corpus has proved a multi-element house that is not a selftest fixture.

The conflict set became diagnostic rather than instrumental, and the ladder is the deliverable:

| tagging | CP conflict core |
|---|---|
| butlers **in** the dependency (WP-11.11's) | *"Dining Room and Butler's Pantry share a door"* |
| butlers in the block, direct kitchen door kept | *"Butler's Pantry and Kitchen share a door"* |
| that door dropped | **OPTIMAL** |

## IV. The corpus said where the butler's pantry goes, a fortnight before anyone tagged it

WP-11.11 tagged the butler's pantry into the dependency and CP refused. **The refusal was right
and the tagging was wrong**, and `rooms/butlers-pantry.json` had said so since 24 August, in the
`via` clause OQ 59 added to its `must_adjoin kitchen`:

> in a plan whose kitchen is in a dependency — a five-part Palladian, **a Tidewater plantation
> house**, any arrangement with a detached or hyphenated service wing — **the pantry is in the
> block** and the kitchen is in another building, and the connecting gallery is the connection.
> Read without this, the rule asked the pantry to be in two buildings at once.

The two crossings are not equivalent, and that asymmetry is what decides the tagging rather than
taste: `must_adjoin dining-room` is hard with **no** `via`, so a pantry in the dependency must
cross and cannot; `must_adjoin kitchen` is hard **with** `via: [back-hall, gallery-corridor]`,
and the plan already carries `butlers → backhall → kitchen` in full.

**The shipped record is still not tagged**, deliberately. Authoring it means dropping a declared
door and moving a shipped reference plan's placement, and this package already fixes six layers'
worth of arithmetic; WP-11.9's own lesson is that a package which does two things can only be
reasoned about as one. It is a record question now rather than an engine one —
`oq/the-parti-dissolved-its-own-dependencies`.

## V. WP-11.11 published "2 of 16 crossing pairs" and it is 1

The second was `breakfast ↔ terrace`, and the terrace takes no rectangle at all (WP-11.10), so
it stands in no element and cannot cross a boundary. The first instrument written here made the
same mistake — it read "no element" as "the main block", which is the exact defect the whole of
WP-11.9 exists to remove, reproduced inside the package auditing it. Corrected before it was
believed, by filtering on `stacking.takes_a_rectangle` the way every other reader does.

## VI. Three guards were re-cut against behaviour, and none was bumped

Each failed on a change that had nothing to do with what it was written to protect — WP-11.8's
four-guard lesson, a package later:

- `test_a_dependency_is_sized_single_pile_not_against_the_main_blocks_pile` pinned
  `(W, H) == (20.0, 20.0)`. Its discriminator is the **width** — 2 bays of 20 under the
  single-pile rule against a 10 ft splinter under the main block's — and the depth merely
  follows. It asserts the width and that the box holds its 400 sf.
- `test_the_recorded_block_is_measured_on_the_rooms_the_placer_places` pinned `depth_ft == 22.0`
  as a proxy for "the unplaced terrace's 600 sf is not counted". It now asserts the property:
  the box is sized for 440 sf and not for 1040.
- `test_the_coverage_floor_is_per_element` was a **source grep** for
  `">= int(COVERAGE * _eW * _eH)"` — the very expression that carried the second rounding. A
  guard reading a selector rather than a property goes blind on the next edit; this one went red
  on the *right* edit instead, which is the same fault wearing the other sign, because it can
  neither survive a rewording nor tell a fix from a regression. It measures each element's
  drawn fill against the floor that element is actually held to.

## VII. The audit of this package's own guards

Six mutations, each **asserted to have landed** before the suite ran; all six bite.

**One is weaker than it looks and the test file says so.** Deleting the depth term from
`dependency_sizes` leaves the *width* half of the allowance in place, and on this plan the
actual rounding then loses less than the worst case — so the dependency row still fits and only
`test_the_allowance_is_derived_from_the_rounding_and_not_chosen` goes red. That one asserts
`(W − g)(H − g) ≥ need`, the guarantee; the other asserts one plan's luck. Both are kept because
they fail on different mutations.

**And `capacity_report` was wrong in this corpus's own worst direction twice before it was
right**, both caught by running it rather than reading it:

- It returned `fits: true` on an **empty sum**. Not hypothetical: `footprint.blocks` on a placed
  record carries no room list, so the first run of the disclosure reported every element as
  `rooms: 0, fits: true` — unjudged reported as passed, in code written for a package about
  disclosure. Membership is the geometric join (`elements.element_of`) now, and an element with
  no members is `null`.
- It judged the **main block** on the integer grid, and convicted it on every heuristic
  placement — `geometry_cp._snap_fpd` makes the footprint integral before any element is built,
  so a grid figure computed from an unsnapped record describes a box CP never uses. The main
  block reports `fits_on_the_proving_grid: null` with that reason.

## IX. And the proved sheet was rendered and looked at, which found a seventh layer

`build/render_plan.py` draws an exterior door — and its sill, and its swing — on the
**footprint's** wall rather than on the wall of the room's own massing element:

```python
px, py = (p_, 0.0 if wl == "S" else H) if horiz else (0.0 if wl == "W" else W, p_)
```

On the tagged sheet that puts two doors in open space north of the whole building, attached to
nothing. The record is right and `openings.place` seated both correctly on their own element's
boundary (WP-11.9's `_boundary_walls`); the drawing throws the answer away. The interior branch
three lines above is already right and is the model for the fix.

| door | its room's own north face | drawn at |
|---|---|---|
| `backhall → exterior` (hyphen) | 30.0 ft | 42.0 ft |
| `kitchen → exterior` (dependency) | 33.02 ft | 42.0 ft |

`workbench/app/src/sheet/derive.js` has the same construct and states it as intended behaviour in
its own docstring. On a one-rectangle house the two lines coincide, so the corpus is untouched.

**Not fixed here, and the reason is a real obstacle rather than scope discipline alone.** The
honest fix writes the room's own face coordinate onto the exterior entry in `derive_openings`,
where the rectangle is already in scope — the marks go on the record and the renderers derive
nothing (WP-11.3). That changes `derive_openings`' output shape in both spellings, and
`tests/fixtures/sheet_symbols/` holds the two to one contract against a frozen `expected` — a
fixture whose generator re-solves on `auto`, which `oq/the-frozen-fixture-is-regenerated-by-solving`
records as eight hundred lines of solver noise round the real change.
`oq/an-exterior-door-is-drawn-on-the-footprints-wall-and-not-its-rooms` carries the location in
both languages, the two rulings it needs and the sweep it should start from.

**Six packages running, this technique has found what nothing else did.**

## X. What this package does NOT do

- **It does not tag the shipped record.** §IV; that is a record edit and its own package.
- **It does not change `COVERAGE`.** The question asked whether 0.97 was the right number for a
  non-main element and the answer is that the number was the wrong variable.
- **It does not model a hyphen as anything but an element.** The Tidewater back hall has five
  doors and a hyphen has two ends; that is `oq/the-parti-dissolved-its-own-dependencies`, and
  the tagging that proves here puts three of those five inside the dependency it opens onto.
- **The renderer's exterior doors**, §IX — named, located in both spellings, and left.
- **No new checker**; `TOTAL_CHECKS` does not move.
