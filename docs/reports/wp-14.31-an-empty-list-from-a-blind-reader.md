# WP-14.31: an empty list from a blind reader

*25 September 2026. Phase 14, tranche 2, wave 5: the last pass on type. The contract is
`docs/prd/phase-14-tranche-2.md` (§F gives this package all of `workbench/app/src`, and §G is what it
may not do) and the standing rule is tranche 1's: every definition is a glossary record, and the app
writes none. Cite this report by filename; a Phase 14 number does not disambiguate a package in
this repository.*

## What it is for

Tranche 1 made the rule and ratcheted what it could not yet remove. `src/copy_ratchet.test.mjs`
held the app's long tooltips and typed counts BY IDENTITY, so none could be added and each could
only leave. This package was to take that baseline to zero, move the text still set in the faint
inks onto a readable one, and meet the other sentences the app writes to a reader: the palette's
labels, the dossier's fault headings, the Plan Workbench's could-not-evaluate panel, the
Phylogeny's shift-click note and the Export page.

Three commits: `a4f7ea2` (the inks), the copy and records, and this report.

## I. What was built

by the property that holds it: text (`color`, or `fill` on SVG text), a mark (borders, backgrounds,
strokes, shape fills), or unknown (a table entry, a fallback, a prop handed on). It holds them BY

### 5. The findings a reader meets

**The Plan Workbench's could-not-evaluate panel said one thing about three states.**

- **What the old panel did.** Its heading read *"could not evaluate · N of this style's constraints
  · M faults"*. Under it the panel drew rows in three marks:
  - unjudged;
  - yours to judge, a hard rule with no test handed to the reader;
  - not applicable.

  N was the constraint summary's `unjudged`. That is exactly the unjudged constraint rows, so the
  check-by-hand rows under the same *could not evaluate* were in no count at all. **It also cut the
  not-applicable faults at four with nothing saying so.** That is the fourth state, the one WP-5.13
  added because a state absent from every list reads as clear.
- **What it does now.** The panel is one group per mark, each headed by its own record
  (`judgment-unjudged`, `judgment-yours-to-judge`, `judgment-not-applicable`). Each group's count is
  the length of the lists it draws from. A group caps its fault rows at eight and states the rest as
  *"and N more"*. A group with nothing in it is not drawn. The corpus-wide footnote went, because a
  count typed in the page was the thing being removed.
- **And the first version of the fix put two numbers under one word, which only the screenshot
  showed.** It headed the unjudged group *"unjudged · 125"* while the masthead above it read
  *"4 unjudged"*. The masthead counts the constraint summary, the style's constraints alone. The
  group holds those 4 AND the corpus's 121 unjudged faults. The old heading had named the two
  populations and the first draft merged them. The walk was green over it: its check was about the
  groups, and nothing held the panel against the masthead.

  So each group now names its parts with the `constraint` and `fault` records. A new walk check
  holds the unjudged group's constraint part equal to the masthead's count, and the two parts equal
  to the group's. Where the masthead states no count, that check says it could not evaluate rather
  than passing.

**The Style Dossier's Faults section** is headed by `fault-group-here`, `fault-group-lineage` and
`fault-group-universal`, each with its count, and it links to the corpus by the `surface-faults`
record's word.

**The Phylogeny's shift-click note** is the `shift-click-to-compare` record.

**Brief Intake's pointer to the conflict set** reads the `conflict-set` and `on-the-bench` records
and shows the `conflict-set` definition. The old note said in the app's own words that the solver
proves a plan and not a brief, opening *"The CP-SAT solver landed (WP-2.3)"*. The record says the
same thing without the history: a conflict set is found for a plan on the bench, never for a brief.

## II. What was found

1. **An empty list from a blind reader is not empty.** The ratchet's readers were written against
   the shapes the tree held on the day, so a baseline could reach zero while the rule was still
   broken in eight places. Emptying it without widening it first would have published a ban that
   did not hold. The widening is the half of this package that matters most, and it is the half a
   reviewer would not have asked for.
2. **A heading that counts some of the rows under it is a false heading, and so is one that counts
   more than the figure beside it.** The Plan Workbench's old panel printed *could not evaluate*
   over rows in three states and counted one of them. Its not-applicable rows were cut at four in
   silence, which repeated inside the panel the exact collapse the fourth state exists to prevent.
   The first fix merged two populations under one word, so the page said *"4 unjudged"* and
   *"unjudged · 125"* a few inches apart. Both were true, and nothing on the page said why they
   differed. **A check about a panel cannot see what the panel says against its neighbour.** This
   one was found by looking at the screenshot, and it is guarded now by a check that reads both.
3. **A typed count and its obvious replacement can disagree.** 262 was right. The nearest API figure
   a later hand would reach for, the resolved packs, is 340. The route now states the raw count per
   row, so the page sums what the figure always meant.
4. **The ratchet is at zero and the app still writes paragraphs.** A deliberately crude census finds
   48 JSX text runs of twelve or more words in 19 files. Ten are code fragments. Of the 38 that are
   prose:
   - about half disclose the payload in hand;
   - about half explain the product whatever record is on screen;
   - one of the explanations is a corpus fact typed as prose. The Phylogeny's *"Japanese, Islamic,
     South Asian and African traditions are absent"* will be silently false the day a node for any
     of them is added, and the count scanner cannot see it.

   Where the line falls between a definition and a disclosure is a ruling, so it is a question:
   `oq/the-app-still-writes-paragraphs-the-ratchet-does-not-read`.
